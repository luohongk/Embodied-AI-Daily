Teleoperator-Aware and CBF-Based Adaptive Nonlinear MPC for Shared Autonomy in Obstacle Avoidance of Legged Robots 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Safe and effective collaboration between humans and autonomous legged robots is a fundamental challenge in shared autonomy, particularly for teleoperated systems navigating cluttered environments. Shared autonomy for legged robots remains challenging due to uncertain human commands, collision-avoidance requirements, and the need to coordinate high-level planning with dynamically feasible locomotion. This paper presents a teleoperator-aware, control barrier function (CBF)-based, adaptive nonlinear model predictive control (ANMPC) framework for shared autonomy of quadrupedal robots in obstacle-avoidance tasks. The framework employs a prescribed arbitration weight between human and robot actions while modeling human inputs using a noisily rational Boltzmann model, whose parameters are adapted online through a projected gradient descent (PGD) law based on observed joystick commands. CBF constraints are incorporated into a computationally efficient NMPC formulation to promote obstacle avoidance while accounting for predicted human actions. The hierarchical architecture integrates high-level CBF-based ANMPC (10 Hz), mid-level dynamics-aware NMPC (60 Hz) enforcing single rigid body (SRB) dynamics, and low-level nonlinear whole-body control (500 Hz). Human-in-the-loop hardware experiments with ten participants on a Unitree Go2 quadruped demonstrate real-time feasibility and collision-free navigation across 50 trials. Shared autonomy reduced average human control effort by 51% and joystick activity from 88% to 42% of trial time, with comparable obstacle clearance and significantly higher perceived assistance than manual operation. Simulation-based ablation studies demonstrate improved obstacle clearance across operator rationality levels and up to a 66% reduction in median human-action prediction error through online adaptation.

World Models for Ocean Engineering: A Critical Review of Maritime Autonomy, Adaptive Latent Prediction, Digital Twins, and Biodiversity Monitoring 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Marine robots face partial observability, non-stationary dynamics, and sparse communication, motivating WMs that learn compact predictive states for planning. This review develops a functional taxonomy of WMs for ocean engineering, focusing on Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) and AdaJEPA's test-time adaptation within model predictive control, mapping them to navigation, adaptive sampling, digital twins, and biodiversity monitoring, and separating demonstrated evidence from prospective applications. The synthesis identifies key Gaps: benchmarks, uncertainty calibration, safe adaptation and proposes a research agenda for physics-informed, deployment-adaptive marine WMs validated through simulation and field missions.

Learned Network Dynamics for Model Predictive Traffic Signal Control under Incidents 2026-11-20
📝 13 pages main manuscript, 26 pages supplementary material (39 pages total)
Show abstract

Model predictive control (MPC) of urban traffic signals relies on a model of how queues respond to signal decisions, which is hard to specify and calibrate in heterogeneous networks, particularly when incidents change capacities and turning shares. Measurements add a second limitation: the controlled lanes at the stop line are often short, so queues extending upstream of them fall outside lane-level measurements. We propose GNN-MPC, a receding-horizon controller that plans with action-conditioned network dynamics learned by graph neural networks. Phase sequences are coordinated across intersections by approximate best responses, with an acceptance test that keeps the predicted cost no higher than that of the initial plan. In microscopic simulation under non-recurrent incidents, GNN-MPC reduces travel time and increases completed trips relative to all tested controllers, on a homogeneous grid and a heterogeneous 21-intersection network. Controlled comparisons on the heterogeneous network distinguish the roles of observation and prediction. With nearly as many vehicles entering the network, GNN-MPC reduces travel time by 13% relative to MaxPressure with the same upstream measurement, and GNN-MPC without the upstream representation does not reproduce its performance. Under the same traffic measurements, planner and objective, replacing the learned dynamics with calibrated store-and-forward predictors increases travel time by 31-34%. Observing hidden demand and predicting its response to control are thus complementary requirements of network-level predictive control.

Acceleration of Sparse and Irregular Workloads through Energy-Efficient Stream Extensions 2026-11-20
📝 Doctoral Thesis; abstract abridged to fit arXiv character limit
Show abstract

The slowdown of Moore's law and Dennard scaling has motivated computer architects to uphold performance scaling through architectural specialization. So far, most efforts have focused on highly regular workloads, which exhibit good data locality, ample reuse, and a data-independent execution flow. As this specialization hits diminishing returns, attention shifts towards sparse and irregular workloads, which are essential to applications like sparse neural networks, multiphysics simulations, and graph analytics. However, they lack the convenient properties of highly regular compute that modern architectures are optimized for, resulting in very low (<10%) functional unit utilization on traditional CPUs and GPUs. The most promising approaches to their general hardware acceleration leverage memory streams, which decouple data movement from execution. In this thesis, we explore energy-efficient hardware stream extensions that significantly accelerate sparse and irregular workloads in general-purpose systems without specializing to specific application domains or compromising on regular-workload performance. We design a flexible, core-coupled stream extension supporting affine and indirect streams as well as intersection and union of sparse tensors. We integrate it into an energy-efficient RISC-V compute cluster, achieving significant performance and energy efficiency benefits on general sparse linear algebra, and further use it to accelerate stencil computations, spiking neural network inference, and model-predictive control. We evaluate it at scale in a 432-core 2.5D RISC-V system fabricated in 12nm FinFET, achieving leading compute utilization and energy efficiency on sparse and irregular workloads while remaining competitive on regular workloads. Finally, we extend chip-level interconnects with end-to-end irregular streams, improving performance, energy efficiency, and bus utilization.

Should Belgians Install an Advanced Energy Management System in Their Electrified Dwelling ? 2026-11-19
📝 Full version including appendices A-C, which are not part of the 11-page proceedings version. Published at ACM BuildSys '26 under CC BY 4.0. 17 pages, 10 figures, 13 tables. Data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18417982
Show abstract

As dwellings electrify and more complex electricity pricing spreads in Belgium, the question arises: What are the potential benefits of installing an advanced energy management systems in an electrified Belgian dwelling ? To answer this question, we simulate 500 representative Belgian dwellings in which fossil systems are replaced by controllable electric technologies: space heating via air-to-air heat pumps, domestic hot water via electric boilers, and private transport via electric vehicles with home chargers. Photovoltaic systems are assigned according to current adoption rates. We evaluate two advanced energy management systems (EMS): model predictive control (MPC) and TreeC, a machine learning method using decision trees to model the EMS strategy. These are compared to a rule-based controller (RBC) that mimics default operation and an MPC with perfect forecasts (MPC P) as an upper bound. Performance for these EMSs is assessed for 2023 under a real contract with hourly prices and a monthly peak tariff. Average annual savings versus RBC are 82.5 EUR, 116.6 EUR, and 177.4 EUR for TreeC, MPC, and MPC P, respectively. All EMSs deliver similar thermal comfort (26-31 Kh). MPC saves more, while TreeC achieves higher EV charge completion (93.3% vs 88.8% for MPC, with a max charge of 95%) and a smoother aggregate load across the 500 dwellings. Savings remain modest relative to EMS purchase (280-680 EUR) and subscription (0-360 EUR/year) costs which is not favourable towards widespread adoption. The data and code to reproduce this study are available at https://github.com/EVERGi/should_belgians_install_advanced_ems_paper.

How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods 2026-11-19
📝 PhD thesis, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, defended 25 February 2026. 178 pages. Promotors: Maarten Messagie and Thierry Coosemans. Chapters 4-5 are based on Knowledge-Based Systems 309 (2025) 112756, doi:10.1016/j.knosys.2024.112756; chapter 6 on Energy and AI 18 (2024) 100448, doi:10.1016/j.egyai.2024.100448; chapter 7 on BuildSys '26, pp. 405-416, doi:10.1145/3744256.3812575
Show abstract

The transition from fossil fuels presents significant challenges in energy management. To ensure a sustainable energy future, it is crucial to reduce energy consumption when possible and to synchronize electricity consumption and storage with the production from sources with low greenhouse gas emissions. This thesis explores a variety of energy management methods used to manage batteries, heat pumps, and other energy-related systems. The thesis presents three simulation cases alongside one real-world experiment to compare energy management done through simple rules, optimization, and machine learning methods. Furthermore, this thesis introduces TreeC, a novel machine learning-based method that learns understandable yet efficient energy management strategies, applicable to many cases. The three key contributions of this thesis can be summarized as follows: 1) The developed TreeC method demonstrates competitive performance compared to a variety of energy management approaches, such as model predictive control and reinforcement learning, across a diverse set of energy management problems. The understandability of TreeC's energy management strategies greatly improves the transparency and trustworthiness of machine learning-based energy management. 2) The real-world experiment highlights the need to validate energy management methods on actual hardware and not solely on simulations. Both TreeC and model predictive control performed worse than in simulations, due to, respectively, insufficiently representative training data and the real-time input data not being reliable enough. 3) Energy management algorithms reduce costs in electrified Belgian dwellings, particularly in poorly insulated dwellings, electric vehicle chargers, and photovoltaic installations. However, on average, these cost savings do not yet outweigh the current costs and effort required to implement such algorithms.

Stuck in Place: Stall Diagnosis and Remedies for JEPA Planners 2026-11-19
📝 10 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables
Show abstract

Joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) world models plan from pixels with model predictive control, yet often fail on tasks their model should handle. Test-time adaptation attributes such failures to model error under distribution shift and updates the weights on the current episode, without checking this attribution against the planning loop itself. We follow the executed planning loop on pushing tasks. In most settings, with and without distribution shift, nearly all failures (at least 96%) are stalls, where the object stays in place while the model predicts progress. The warm-started gradient-descent (GD) planner then returns nearly the same plan, a fixed point where the agent mostly idles. We propose Unstick, which pairs on-policy fine-tuning before deployment with a fixed-weight loop breaker: a residual memory of the episode's prediction errors or random re-initialization at every replan. With GD, Unstick recovers most of the adaptation gain on pushing tasks with the memory, and with re-initialization most of it on shape shifts but much less on visual shifts. A large part of what limits these planners under GD is thus a fixed point of the planning loop, present with and without distribution shift, which a planner with fixed weights can break. Code is available at https://github.com/jhlee0619/unstick.

Differentiable Hydrodynamics for Whole-Body MPC of Underwater Robots 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Underwater vehicle-manipulator systems distribute actuation across thrusters and joint motors. Whole-body model predictive control (MPC), rather than separate vehicle and arm controllers, can coordinate both to extend the workspace and exploit the system's full kinematic capability. Doing so, however, requires a hydrodynamic model and derivatives throughout the prediction horizon. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) captures geometry-dependent forces and moments for such a model, but is too expensive to evaluate online. We present a differentiable analytical hydrodynamic model combining smooth spherical-harmonic force and moment fields with a coupled added-inertia matrix identified from CFD. It compactly represents velocity and acceleration-dependent loads and provides their derivatives in closed form. These derivatives are propagated through free-floating vehicle--manipulator dynamics for whole-body trajectory optimization and receding-horizon control, jointly optimizing thruster forces and joint torques for reaching and continuous tracking. Held-out CFD tests validate the principal translational-force and rotational-moment fields and the added-inertia response. The analytical Jacobians evaluate faster and avoid the step-size sensitivity of central finite differences. Closed-loop Stonefish experiments demonstrate whole-body MPC in an independent marine simulator. By making geometry-dependent hydrodynamics directly available to gradient-based control, the model incorporates water forces and their sensitivities into motion allocation between the vehicle and arm, rather than treating them only as disturbances.

MoRE: Mixture-of-Experts for Multi-Physics Robotic World Models 2026-11-19
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026. 19 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

A robotic world model should predict how objects evolve under robot actions and physical dynamics. While recent 3D world models have shown strong predictive capabilities, they still struggle with the heterogeneous dynamics arising from diverse object interactions. To address this in a unified framework, we decompose robotic interactions into distinct physical regimes and assign each to a specialized motion expert. We introduce MoRE, a 3D world model with a mixture of motion experts organized around rigid, deformable, and prehensile dynamics. This design enables a single model to capture diverse physical behaviors while preserving regime-specific priors. Trained on mixed simulation and real-world data, MoRE integrates with Model Predictive Control (MPC) to support diverse manipulation tasks. Experiments show that MoRE improves prediction accuracy across physics regimes, leading to stronger real-world MPC performance. Code, data, and project website are available at https://alwaysleepy.github.io/MoRE-WM-site/.

From Self-Play RL to Constrained Control: Distilling DiffMPC Students with DAgger 2026-11-18
📝 18 pages, 10 main content pages, 3 figures, L4DC submission
Show abstract

Self-play reinforcement learning (RL) produces strong driving policies, but these policies are hard to verify or interpret. Differentiable model predictive control (DiffMPC) can enforce explicit constraints, but it is usually trained by imitating logged data or with costly RL. To add constraints to an already trained policy, we keep a PPO teacher's backbone and replace its action head with a DiffMPC that enforces road boundary half-planes and agent ellipses. We then distill the student with DAgger in the teacher's self-play simulator. Over 9 seeds, the constrained student of a smaller teacher matches its teacher on nuPlan and beats it on CARLA and on out-of-distribution Waymo scenes. For a stronger teacher, the student has fewer failures on nuPlan and Waymo, while staying level on CARLA. Its distillation takes no more than 30% of the teacher's training time.

Dual-Actuation Emulation of Scaled Relative Orbital Dynamics on a Free-Flyer 2026-11-18
📝 7 pages, 4 figures. Presented at the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Space Robotics (iSpaRo); to appear in IEEE Xplore
Show abstract

Ground-based free-flyer testbeds reproduce the planar motion of a free-floating spacecraft using air bearings, but not the relative dynamics of spacecraft in orbit. We present a dual-actuation architecture that physically emulates Clohessy-Wiltshire dynamics on an open-source free-flyer. Two model predictive controllers run in parallel: one drives the solenoid thrusters with an orbital dynamics model, representing the spacecraft under test; the other drives bidirectional propellers that physically apply the orbital accelerations, continuously updating its reference to account for thruster-induced velocity changes. A dynamic similitude maps the orbital scenario onto the limited floor area and actuation rates of the testbed, letting the spacecraft controller run directly at orbital scale. We validate the architecture on hardware and against a high-fidelity orbital simulation, where the orbital accelerations instead arise naturally, both driven by the identical spacecraft controller. Their agreement indicates that the testbed reproduces the relative orbital motion the controller would encounter in flight.

Real-Time QP Solvers: A Concise Review and Practical Guide Towards Legged Robots 2026-11-18
📝 12 pages, 1 figure, 2 tables
Show abstract

Quadratic programming (QP) underpins real-time robotics by enabling efficient, constrained optimization in state estimation, motion planning, and control. In legged locomotion and manipulation, essential modules like inverse dynamics, Model Predictive Control (MPC), and Whole-Body Control (WBC) are inherently QP-based, demanding reliable solutions amid tight timing, energy, and computational resources on embedded platforms. This paper presents a comprehensive analysis and benchmarking study of QP solvers for legged robotics. We begin by formulating the standard convex QP and classify solvers into principal algorithmic approaches: interior-point methods, active-set strategies, operator-splitting schemes, and augmented Lagrangian/proximal approaches, while also discussing solver code generation for fixed-structure QPs. Each solver is examined in terms of algorithmic structure, computational characteristics, and its ability to exploit problem structure and warm-starting. Performance is reviewed using publicly available benchmarks, with a focus on metrics such as computation time, constraint satisfaction, and robustness under perturbations. Unified comparison tables yield practical guidance for solver selection, underscoring trade-offs in speed, accuracy, and energy efficiency. Our findings emphasize the synergy between solvers, tasks, and hardware -- e.g., sparse structured IPMs for long-horizon MPC and dense active-set for high-frequency WBC to advance agile, autonomous legged systems, with emerging trends toward ill-conditioned, conic, and code-generated deployments.

How Uncertain Is My Action? Self-Correcting Visual Planning with Frozen World Models 2026-11-17
Show abstract

A frozen latent world model combined with sampling-based model predictive control is a visual planner. At deployment, the executed action can depart from the commanded one, and the frozen model has no way to notice. We show that the model can be run in reverse: for any hypothesized actuation shift, it predicts what it should have observed under that shift, and comparing this prediction with what was actually observed quantifies the shift and lets the planner correct itself. Unlike the residual energy, which mixes the shift with the model's own error, the score keeps only the part of the residual that a shift can produce. This shift score detects the shift with a locally optimal test, estimates it with Cramer-Rao error bars, names the dominant generator, and compensates the planner once an alarm fires. On PushT, TwoRoom and OGBench-Cube with a frozen latent world model, measured detection follows the prediction to within 0.05 AUROC. Self-correction raises success from 0.650 to 0.862 on PushT and from 0.378 to 0.432 on the harder Cube task, levels with an oracle that is told the true shift at the same block, better than five common test-time adaptation methods. On a real robotic arm, the score identifies a base-joint offset that no joint encoder registers and reduces the reach error from 36 mm to 3-5 mm, and under shifts drawn at random and sealed before the session, estimates recover 92-94% of the error reduction achieved with the true shift. The broader implication is that a frozen world model can act as a filter by predicting the future, testing action-interface uncertainty and validity, and finally correcting failures.

Feedback-Linearised Trajectory Tracking via Model-Informed Robust Geometric DDPC 2026-11-17
📝 This manuscript has been submitted to the 9th Annual Learning for Dynamics & Control Conference, taking place in Stockholm, Sweden, in June 2027
Show abstract

Data-driven predictive control (DDPC) predicts system responses from recorded trajectories, but nonlinear dynamics can limit the accuracy of fixed linear predictors. This paper studies geometric robust DDPC for trajectory tracking of a fully actuated manipulator using model-based feedback linearisation. A mechanical model maps virtual acceleration commands into joint torques, while recorded data capture residual dynamics arising from imperfect model parameters. The controller minimises worst-case prediction error over an open Grassmannian ball centred at the fitted trajectory subspace. Simulations on a planar 2R manipulator under gravity compare the proposed method with double-integrator model predictive control (MPC) and direct regularised data-enabled predictive control (DeePC). Results show improved tracking over double-integrator MPC despite increased torque effort, while performance relative to DeePC depends on the reference trajectory.

CADENCE: Learning Acyclic Gaits for Model-Based Quadruped Locomotion 2026-11-17
📝 18 pages, 8 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Model-based quadruped controllers usually step on a cyclic gait, which lifts the legs in a fixed order on a fixed period, wherever the safe footholds are. We present CADENCE, a learned step planner with a small action space. Every 40 ms, it picks one of at most 65 discrete choices: no step, or one leg's step to a safe foothold. It also predicts a swing time. We train CADENCE by reinforcement learning on top of a convex model predictive controller (MPC). Nothing prescribes a gait: there is no gait schedule, and the reward has no gait term, only a cost per step. The one structural constraint is that at most two legs may swing at once. The policy we analyzed developed an acyclic gait, with no fixed period or stepping order, yet it still paired diagonal legs. An inverse footstep model (IFM), fitted on a controller's logged steps, turns each foothold into a command. With only the IFM refitted, on fewer than 20,000 steps, CADENCE ran frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC. In simulated trials on a range of terrains (26% to 49% voids) and speeds (0.1 m/s and 0.2 m/s), it succeeded in 85.1% (mean of five seeds), against 67.8% for OCS2-Trot, 65.5% for OCS2-Single, and 39.6% in a static walk ablation of CADENCE. At 49% voids, it succeeded in 72.9%, against 43.0% for OCS2-Trot.

Adapting Generalist Vehicle Models for High-Speed MPC Across Terrains 2026-11-17
📝 https://amrl.cs.utexas.edu/optcar/ Selected at CORL 2026
Show abstract

High-speed off-road autonomy requires precise closed-loop control for a target vehicle while remaining robust across changing terrains. Recent forward kinodynamic (FKD) prediction foundation models suggest a promising path, starting from a generalist model and specializing it to the target platform. However, effective specialization remains challenging, as it often requires substantial real-world data, and models adapted to one setting can still overfit to specific terrains or driving regimes. We present OptCar (Optimized Car), a recipe for bridging the gap from generalist to specialist FKD models that preserves cross-terrain generalization while optimizing performance for a specific vehicle. OptCar introduces a transformer FKD architecture that uses FiLM to condition multi-step predictions on a single dynamics context token summarizing recent state-action history. It then specializes the generalist model using limited real-world data and targeted synthetic rollouts from environment-specific system identification. In closed-loop model predictive control (MPC) experiments across three terrains and an out-of-distribution cart-pulling task, the largest gains appear at 6 m/s, the highest speed evaluated and the regime in which slip dominates tracking error. On vegetation + dirt, the most slip-diverse terrain, OptCar reduces 6 m/s trajectory tracking error by roughly 55% relative to AnyCar fine-tuned on real data alone, and remains the most accurate even when an unseen cart payload changes the dynamics. With 5 minutes of real data per terrain, OptCar is competitive on road with a specialist trained on 30 minutes of road data and outperforms it when the terrain changes. Sideslip-Conditioned Drift Trajectory Planning with Flow Matching and Control Barrier Functions 2026-11-17
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables. Under review. Demonstration video: https://youtu.be/nskukDD1MFg
Show abstract

Drifting, the deliberate control of a vehicle at large sideslip angles, is relevant to both motorsport and recovery from unexpected oversteer. Existing autonomous drifting methods typically determine drift intensity through predefined reference trajectories, optimization objectives, or learned policy rewards, rather than accepting a desired sideslip angle as an explicit input. We propose, to the best of our knowledge, the first generative drift trajectory planner explicitly conditioned on a requested peak sideslip angle. A conditional flow matching model is trained on demonstrated drift laps with hindsight-derived sideslip labels and refined through reinforcement fine-tuning. A control barrier function (CBF) correction is then applied to satisfy model-based road, tire friction, and actuator constraints at the planning waypoints. In closed-loop experiments in the racing simulator Assetto Corsa, a nonlinear model predictive controller tracks the generated plans for six requested sideslip angles from 20 deg to 45 deg, with 30 laps (approximately 75 km) per request. The median peak sideslip angle measured across drift-corner passes follows the requested angle, with a mean absolute error of 1.2 deg across the six conditions. Ablation experiments show that reinforcement fine-tuning improves conditioning accuracy, while CBF correction substantially reduces off-road and spin events.

Direct Dynamic Retargeting for Humanoid Imitation Learning from Videos 2026-11-17
📝 https://gepetto.github.io/robot-motion/ddr/
Show abstract

Imitation Learning from monocular video demonstrations provides a scalable approach for teaching complex skills to humanoid robots. However, translating human motion to humanoids requires overcoming significant morphological mismatches. So far, dynamic retargeting methods obtain physically feasible references by tracking an intermediate geometric retargeting in simulation, usually through its joint angles. We show that this intermediate step biases the solution through two factors: the space in which the reference is tracked, joint angles rather than task-space keypoints, and the tracked reference itself, the keypoints of the geometric retargeting rather than those of the human demonstration. We propose Direct Dynamic Retargeting (DDR), which bypasses both: it tracks the human keypoints exclusively in task space with a sampling-based Model Predictive Control solver inside a physics simulator, without contact annotations. DDR is evaluated in simulation against joint-space dynamic retargeting under controlled conditions (identical solver, budget, and warm start), on three humanoids and on motions from monocular videos and motion capture. The tracking space explains most of the gain in end-effector accuracy and robustness, and tracking the human demonstration directly prevents the transmission of errors from the intermediate geometric reference. This advantage carries over to Reinforcement Learning imitation policies, which reproduce the human demonstration more faithfully than policies trained on joint-space dynamic references. These policies are deployed zero-shot on two physical humanoids of very different sizes, H1-2 and R1, including contact-rich motions. We release the source code and a dataset of dynamically feasible references covering all the flat-ground motions of AMASS on the three humanoids.

Manufacturing Differential Sparsity: Planner-Hardware Co-Design for Efficient World Model Planning 2026-11-17
📝 14 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

JEPA-style latent world models let embodied agents plan toward new goals with model predictive control, but they are still too slow for real-life embodied control: sampling-based MPC repeats predictor inference tens of thousands of times per plan, and even on high-end GPUs one planning step can take tens of seconds. We observe substantial latent locality in this repeated-inference workload: the predictor maps close actions to close latents, and inside a quantized predictor this closeness takes a form that hardware can exploit, differential bit-level sparsity. Unlike an input stream, whose temporal similarity is given, a planner itself decides which candidates it evaluates together, so the planner can choose its own references and, with reference forest sampling, manufacture more sparsity: it couples the sampled action sequences of each group to a shared root, with competitive planning quality. We build the accelerator on the insights of the rich line of bit-level sparsity accelerators and design it and the planner tightly coupled as a single co-design: the planner increases sparsity and exposes structure for hardware scheduling, and the bit-exact hardware effectively uses the sparsity that the planner creates. On three JEPA world models, our co-design is 1.28-1.80x faster than a dense accelerator and uses 38-52% less energy, and it is 1.22-1.58x faster than prior bit-sparse design and uses 16-36% less energy, with the same area. To our knowledge, our planner is the first planner that manufactures differential bit-level sparsity across its candidates and exposes its structure for hardware scheduling, and our co-design is the first algorithm-hardware co-design for JEPA-style world-model planning.

GP--RL--MPC: Uncertainty-Aware Reinforcement Learning-Augmented Model Predictive Control with Gaussian Process Postdecision State-Value Functions 2026-11-17
📝 19 pages including references and appendices, 3 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to L4DC 2027
Show abstract

We propose Gaussian Process Reinforcement Learning-Augmented Model Predictive Control (GP--RL--MPC), which uses a learned critic as the MPC terminal penalty to account for costs beyond the prediction horizon. Gaussian process regression (GPR) enables data-efficient critic learning from limited, noisy rollouts within optimistic approximate policy iteration (OAPI), with soft updates mitigating policy chattering. A tunable risk coefficient weights GP epistemic uncertainty in the terminal cost, regulating sensitivity to uncertain critic predictions. The postdecision state formulation shifts disturbance averaging offline to account for aleatoric uncertainty from the terminal transition onward. Under matching objectives and admissible actions, a one-step horizon recovers greedy value-space RL. After fitting GP hyperparameters by exact marginal likelihood, we construct a sparse deterministic training conditional (DTC) posterior using inducing points selected by pivoted Cholesky (PC), reducing online evaluation costs as the training dataset grows. In a continuous stirred-tank reactor (CSTR) subject to exogenous disturbances, GP--RL--MPC with an appropriately selected risk coefficient and policy screening reduces the sum of squared errors (SSE) by an average of 80.52% relative to an otherwise matched baseline MPC across five random seeds (sample standard deviation: 3.48%).

WM-Quant: Rollout-Aware Post-Training Quantization for JEPA-Style World Models 2026-11-16
📝 11 pages, 5 figures, and 7 tables
Show abstract

We present WM-Quant, to our knowledge, the first post-training quantization (PTQ) framework designed specifically for JEPA-style world models. Although world models typically feature relatively few parameters, action planning algorithms often require thousands of inferences, which can quickly become computationally expensive. PTQ is a natural way to reduce this cost. However, we show that in world models using autoregressive model predictive control (MPC) rollouts, quantization error can compound with each predictor step, resulting in action rankings from the quantized model becoming near-random. WM-Quant addresses this with three components that build on existing PTQ methods: early-encoder precision, control-path preservation, and rollout-aware scaling. Early-encoder precision protects accurate latent state and goal information to mitigate long-term error propagation. Control-path preservation allows the world model predictor to distinguish between fine-grained actions, significantly improving action rankings and execution. Rollout-aware scaling jointly calibrates encoder and per-step predictor activation ranges with respect to an entire rollout rather than a single prediction, reducing the cumulative rollout error. Across multiple world models, environments, and PTQ methods, WM-Quant substantially improves planning success at low bit-widths, approaching full-precision performance at W4A4.

Data-Driven Predictive Control for Autonomous Vehicle Racing under Varying Friction 2026-11-16

Data-driven predictive control (DPC) predicts future outputs by combining recorded input-output trajectory data such that the combined trajectory approximately matches a measured initial trajectory and tracks a provided reference.
