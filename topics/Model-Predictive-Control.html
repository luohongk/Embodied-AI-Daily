Learned Terminal Costs for Short-Horizon Model Predictive Control of Autonomous Trucks in Interactive Highway Traffic 2026-10-29
📝 13 pages, 2 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Autonomous highway driving for heavy-duty trucks is a challenging control problem. The greater mass, dimensions and intricate dynamics increase the requirements for cooperation from surrounding traffic in critical maneuvers, requiring a careful balance between safety constraints and efficient long-horizon decision making. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides rigorous constraint satisfaction but relies on terminal cost which can limit its effectiveness at short, computationally affordable horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) captures long-term driving behavior but offers no constraint guarantees and tends toward overly conservative policies when trained under sparse collision penalties. In this paper, we propose a hybrid MPC and RL framework for highway driving in autonomous trucks, where a finite-horizon MPC, solved via dynamic programming, uses a pre-trained RL model for terminal cost estimation. We evaluate the framework in a simulation environment with reactive surrounding traffic and compare it against pure MPC with zero terminal cost, rollout with terminal cost estimation using a base policy, and pure RL. Results show that the hybrid approach achieves zero collisions with the lowest cost of any configuration.

Teaching a Tree to Recover: Online Distillation of an LLM Supervisor with Counterfactual Replay 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Autonomous mobile robots commonly pair a global planner with a local model predictive controller (MPC), but nonconvex obstacle avoidance and a limited horizon can make the robot freeze in complex layouts. Deciding whether to replan, wait, or call for help is a supervisory decision. Recent work queries a large language model (LLM) supervisor on every anomaly, which is slow and costly. We instead distill the LLM online into a decision tree. After each training episode, counterfactual replay labels recoveries as needed, unnecessary, or harmful. These labels retire unhelpful rules. In simulation, on 20 unseen layouts where the MPC alone freezes, the tree handles at least 18 correctly across all seeds, either by reaching the goal or by calling for help when no feasible path exists. The tree matches both an always-query LLM supervisor and expert-designed rules, while issuing no LLM queries at test time. Handwritten rules cannot anticipate the long tail of situations a physical robot will encounter. Online distillation is a promising remedy: the tree handles familiar cases, and the LLM is consulted only for new ones, whose validated recoveries become new rules. Language models can thus also serve as designers of supervisors rather than only as runtime supervisors.

Safe and Fuel-Efficient Motion Planning for Lunar Propulsive Drones 2026-10-28
📝 This work has been submitted to the 2027 IEEE Aerospace Conference
Show abstract

Propulsive drones are a promising new technology for generating high-resolution terrain models of the Moon. In future lunar missions, propulsive drones will complete a series of hops across the lunar surface while capturing images with downward-facing cameras. We present an autonomous motion planning framework that uses a vision-guided optimal control strategy for performing safe, efficient hops between specified waypoints. The drone plans parabolic hops between waypoints generated by a global-level planner; if a nominal waypoint is unsafe or unreachable, the drone then completes a diversion maneuver to safely land. To evaluate the validity of potential landing sites, we develop a custom semantic segmentation model for identifying obstacles from aerial images, and we introduce an approach for rapidly calculating reachable terrain. We then use model predictive control (MPC) to robustly track hop trajectories that guide the drone towards safe landing sites. We test the optimal hop planner in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. Our results demonstrate that our unified motion planning framework yields safe, fuel-efficient hop trajectories which can be tracked rapidly and with low error via MPC.

A Multi-Layer Navigation System for Smart Wheelchairs with a Limited Input Interface 2026-10-28
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Presented at IROS 2026 (IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems); to appear in the conference proceedings
Show abstract

Smart wheelchairs are robotic devices that gather information from the surroundings and allow people to safely drive through various environments. They usually rely on the interpretation of high-level user's intentions, typically decoded through human-robot interface (HRI) or brain-machine interface (BMI). However, navigating in an unknown environment and accurately understanding user commands remain significant challenges. Misinterpretation of commands or the environment can lead to user frustration, distrust of the system, or even unsafe situations. In this paper, we present a novel Multi-Layer Navigation System (MLNS) for a smart wheelchair that enables navigation in unknown environments while interpreting and safely contextualizing user's intentions even in the case of limited and faulty commands. The stack relies on a Virtual Sensors-Driven Potential Fields (ViSPF) method for obstacle detection that accounts for the wheelchair's actual size, a Model Predictive Control (MPC) module for trajectory generation, and an open-vocabulary computer vision algorithm for target identification. Finally, a system based on Priority Queue (PQ) was implemented for target selection. We validated our system on a real smart wheelchair with experiments involving 10 participants without disabilities, including scenarios with faulty input interfaces. The results demonstrate the robustness and effectiveness of our system in real-world environments, proving its potential for practical applications in assistive technologies.

Convex-SKM-Net: Low-Latency Block Halfspace Corrections for Neural Convex Constraint Satisfaction 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Fast inference and strict constraint satisfaction are essential for learning-based decision-making. Existing methods often rely on constraint-specific projections or feasible-set parameterizations, while iterative repair can be costly. We introduce Convex-SKM-Net, a convex constraint-satisfaction layer that forms linearization halfspaces from residual and (sub)gradient oracles without constraint-specific inequality projectors. It applies fixed-depth dual projected-gradient descent (dual PGD) to minimum-displacement block problems using GPU-friendly matrix operations. We jointly learn the predictor and a positive-definite mobility, adapting the correction geometry to improve objective quality. We prove convergence of the outer iterates at fixed dual depth and linear convergence in distance under Slater's condition and bounded subgradients. All our test outputs on three second-order-cone model-predictive control (SOC-MPC) horizons and six power-system convex relaxations satisfy a $10^{-3}$ feasibility tolerance. Comparisons with the latest constraint-satisfaction methods show inference speedups of up to $144.3\times$ on SOC-MPC and $7.7\times$ on a convex quadratically constrained quadratic programming (QCQP) benchmark with joint training. On QCQP, the mean objective gap is $0.012\%$, with full test feasibility at $10^{-4}$. Learning mobility reduces the five-seed affine-task mean gap by $22.4\%$ and improves gaps in matched power-system comparisons.

Neural Compression of Long ADMM Trajectory for Multiparametric Quadratic Program 2026-10-27
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/SOLARIS-JHU/TQPNet
Show abstract

Solving large-scale multiparametric quadratic programs (mpQPs) in real time often requires thousands of iterative optimization updates, creating a major computational bottleneck in model predictive control and learning-enabled systems. This paper proposes TQPNet, a self-supervised neural compression framework that learns to compress long ADMM optimization trajectories into a compact neural warm start followed by a few refinement iterations. We derive a low-dimensional ADMM scheme operating on the primal-dual variables $(x,β)$ and show that its iteration admits an equivalent ReLU-layer representation, termed ADMM(ReLU). Building on this insight, TQPNet combines a multilayer perceptron with ReLU activations (MLP(ReLU)) and a small number of unrolled ADMM(ReLU) refinement layers. The network is trained offline using an optimality-condition-informed residual loss, eliminating the need for solver-generated labels. The proposed residual-based framework enables both self-supervised training and online solution certification through adaptive ADMM(ReLU) refinement. Experiments on large-scale mpQPs and real-time MPC applications demonstrate that TQPNet achieves high accuracy, strong generalization, and substantially faster inference than conventional optimization-based methods.

Beyond Single-Episode Optimization: Sliding-Window Aware Generative Auto-Bidding for Long-Term Advertising Effectiveness 2026-10-27

Show abstract

Auto-bidding systems optimize bids to maximize value under efficiency constraints such as Cost-Per-Action (CPA). Existing methods treat each day as an independent episode. However, many advertisers produce value so sparsely that per-day efficiency ratios become statistically unreliable, undermining advertiser retention. Platforms therefore evaluate window-level efficiency over sliding windows of $W{=}7$ days, ensuring fair evaluation and long-term advertising effectiveness. This creates cross-episode coupling: each day's bidding decisions affect up to $W$ overlapping windows, so setting daily targets requires anticipating future market conditions. We propose SWAG-Bid (Sliding-Window Aware Generative Auto-Bidding), a hierarchical framework decomposing this challenge into episode-level planning and step-level execution. The planner uses a Masked Trajectory Model to forecast markets and generate candidate plans, scored across all overlapping windows by Multi-Window Model Predictive Control Sampling (MWMS) with exponential confidence decay. The controller adjusts reliance on this guidance through a state-adaptive gate, Per-Step Gated Adaptive Layer Normalization (PSG-AdaLN), complemented by Return-to-Go and Cost-to-Go channels carrying budget and constraint information. Experiments on AuctionNet-Sparse and online A/B tests on AliExpress show that SWAG-Bid achieves competitive constraint satisfaction and value acquisition under sliding-window evaluation.

Nonprehensile Robotic Catching Using Virtual Constraints 2026-10-26
📝 9 pages, 6 figures; an abridged version of this article has been submitted to the 2027 American Control Conference (ACC)
Show abstract

The problem of nonprehensile robotic catching is addressed using virtual nonholonomic constraints (VNHCs); the motion of the robot end-effector is designed and controlled directly in terms of the states of the object before it is caught on the surface of the end-effector. In contrast to contemporary approaches, our approach avoids the need for object trajectory prediction, and consequent time-varying robot trajectory generation. With a focus on the planar case, we demonstrate that our VNHC approach can be used to catch an object on an end-effector at a desired height, with minimal relative normal velocity at the contact instant. Moreover, the VNHC function depends only on the desired catching height, and the same VNHC can be used for a range of initial conditions. We use model predictive control (MPC) to bring the object and end-effector to rest at a desired location after contact is made. The approach is validated in simulation.

Learning Action-Conditional and Object-Centric Gaussian Splatting World Models for Rigid Objects 2026-10-26

World models enable intelligent agents to predict the consequences of their actions on the environment. In this paper, we propose Multi Rigid Object Gaussian World Model (MRO-GWM), a novel model that learns action-conditional dynamics of rigid objects in 3D. Using object-centric Gaussians, we can represent arbitrary object shapes and multi-object scenes. We develop a novel temporal-spatial transformer architecture that predicts future rigid body motion from a history of object Gaussians and future actions. Objects are represented by Gaussians in an object-centric frame, which allows to describe object motion as a rigid body transformation. Our model is trained on reconstructions from multiple viewpoints, which requires the model to also handle partial observations of objects due to occlusions. We analyze prediction performance of our approach on synthetic datasets composed of typical household objects with multi-object dynamics and interactions by a robot end effector. We also evaluate our model in model-predictive control for non-prehensile manipulation in simulation and for image-based tracking and prediction of object motion in simulated and real-world scenes.

Distributed Predictive Flocking for a Swarm of Miniature Vibration-Driven Robots in a Confined Arena 2026-10-26
📝 Submitted to Robotics and Autonomous Systems
Show abstract

Coordinating many miniature robots in a confined arena, with no leader, central planner, or global map, is a core challenge for swarm robotics. As the swarm grows denser, collision avoidance becomes the limiting factor, and the number of safely coexisting robots depends on the formulation of the inter-robot safety constraint. We present a distributed predictive flocking controller in which each robot solves its own nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) problem, optimizing swarm cohesion and alignment costs, inter-robot and obstacle safety, and a task objective, while exchanging only predicted trajectories with local neighbors. Within this generalized controller architecture, solved in real time, we compare six barrier and control-barrier-function (CBF) safety formulations on a digital twin of an open-source vibration-driven bristlebot in a $0.64\,\mathrm{m^2}$ arena. A look-ahead discrete-time CBF performs best overall, with the fewest collisions and the widest inter-robot margins across nearly every swarm size tested. We deploy it on phototaxis, phototactic orbiting, and cohesion without a stimulus tasks, using swarm sizes starting from ten robots and up to the geometric packing limit. The swarm holds well: collisions stay low in small swarms, rising steeply as the packing limit approaches, while coordination and cohesion degrade gently rather than collapsing. The swarm is found not to trade safety for order, keeping both across the confined arena, with local alignment high at every swarm size.

VisGeo-PipeTrack: A Visual Geometric Servo Control Framework with MPC-RCAPID Control for UAV-Based Dynamic Pipes Tracking 2026-10-26
📝 Submit to IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems IEEE TITS
Show abstract

While unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly deployed for pipeline inspection, prevailing research predominantly assumes static pipes. In real-world industrial environments, pipes often exhibit dynamic movements, such as vibrations. This imposes more stringent requirements on UAVs' adaptive control and computational efficiency than in static scenarios. Therefore, this paper proposes a servo control framework named VisGeo-PipeTrack, which utilizes geometric constraints to enable UAV tracking of dynamic pipes. To achieve efficient UAV positioning, YOLOv5 is employed to detect labels on the pipe. The positions are derived from visual geometric constraints based on these detected labels to enable precise localization. To address tracking control challenges, a model predictive control based real-time corrected adaptive PID (MPC-RCAPID) controller is proposed. This controller employs the Recursive Least Squares method for real-time model estimation and integrates rolling horizon optimization to dynamically adjust PID parameters. Experiments conducted on both the AirSim simulator and the Tello UAV platform validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. Simulation comparisons with standard PID, fractional-order PID, adaptive PID, and fuzzy PID demonstrate that MPC-RCAPID achieves the shortest settling time (3.26 s) and the lowest average tracking error (3.52%). In physical experiments on the Tello platform, compared with standard PID control, MPC-RCAPID reduces the average tracking error by 55.52%, 26.72%, and 26.52% across the three test dimensions. These results demonstrate that VisGeo-PipeTrack facilitates efficient autonomous tracking in dynamic pipe environments.

Learning Loco-Manipulation From SMPC Demonstrations With Sparse Offline-to-Online RL 2026-10-26
📝 CoRL 2026 Camera-ready version
Show abstract

Integrating locomotion and manipulation is essential for robot autonomy, but scaling standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) to complex tasks is severely bottlenecked by the slow, manual process of dense reward shaping. To bypass this limitation, we leverage Sample-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) entirely in simulation as an automated, rapidly tunable expert to generate massive offline datasets. Because this data solves the fundamental exploration problem, we can train an off-policy RL agent using purely sparse task rewards, drastically reducing the time required to learn new skills and eliminating the need for manual tuning. Integrating this high-level agent with a low-level dynamic stability controller yields more optimal behaviors that strictly align with true task objectives, ultimately allowing the learned policies to surpass the original optimal control teacher. We validate the robustness of this sim-to-real framework by successfully deploying complex loco-manipulation skills across different morphologies, including an arm-equipped Spot quadruped and a G1 humanoid.

PlayPolicy: Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-26

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present PlayPolicy: Dexterity from supervised play (DSP), a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. Quantile-Based Command-Reserve MPC for Current-Command Gimbal Pointing 2026-10-26
📝 10 pages, 4 figures. Supplementary hardware trial data and recomputation scripts included as ancillary files. Submitted to Journal of the Franklin Institute
Show abstract

This paper studies the constrained line-of-sight pointing control problem of a two-axis current-command gimbal subjected to mismatched mechanical disturbances. Friction, torque ripple, current-response lag, model mismatch, and limited drive commands make accurate pointing more challenging yet interesting. A quantile disturbance predictor is proposed to compensate predictable acceleration residuals and describe their remaining uncertainty, with the aid of learning median and outer quantiles from state, command, and reference histories. A tube-inspired model predictive controller is proposed to reserve command headroom according to prediction uncertainty, with the aid of calibrated radii and stage-dependent input margins. The median improves the nominal prediction, while the radius adapts the command reserve without adding optimization variables. A finite-horizon tube inclusion result is established for a specified linear error model and fixed ancillary policy; its conditions are distinguished from the approximations used in hardware. Native command bounds and a final command limiter keep the sent commands within their prescribed range. On a two-axis embedded platform, four controller configurations are compared in seven repeated trials each. The proposed configuration achieves a two-dimensional pointing root-mean-square error of $3.94 \pm 0.10$ mrad with no command-limit samples in the evaluation windows. Its performance supports the combined use of residual prediction and adaptive command margins in the tested operating condition. Solver times and logged loop overruns are reported separately for the nominal 100 Hz implementation.

Physics-Informed and Data-Driven Composite Disturbance Separation and Predictive Control for Airborne Electro-Optical Gimbals with Multiple Constraints 2026-10-26
📝 20 pages, 8 figures. Includes supplementary derivations and proofs. Demonstration video included as an ancillary file
Show abstract

This paper studies the constrained pointing control problem of airborne electro-optical gimbals subjected to composite periodic and aperiodic disturbances. Under carrier vibration, input delay, and actuator constraints, achieving line-of-sight stabilization and tracking is challenging. A disturbance-separated model predictive control (MPC) scheme is proposed to compensate these heterogeneous disturbances according to their physical characteristics. An adaptive periodic observer predicts multi-harmonic vibrations over the MPC horizon using linear-regression frequency estimation. A linear observer estimates the aperiodic disturbance, and a bounded long short-term memory (LSTM) network predicts its change over the input delay. Embedding periodic predictions into the MPC model while applying aperiodic compensation outside the optimizer limits amplification of aperiodic estimation errors and improves pointing accuracy under frequency changes and parameter mismatch. On the stated headroom envelope, input-set tightening provides feasible commands within the prescribed limits, and a regional practical tracking bound is established under explicit input-sequence and predicted-state margin conditions. A UAV-mounted two-axis gimbal platform is assembled for comparative indoor hovering experiments, complemented by simulations under frequency changes and parameter mismatch. In the reported trials, the proposed method achieves yaw and pitch relative-angle root-mean-square errors (RMSEs) of 0.770 degrees and 0.350 degrees, reducing tracking errors by 25.1% and 45.9% compared with linear active disturbance rejection control.

Quadrotor interceptors equipped with strapdown cameras offer a low-cost solution for physically countering unauthorized UAVs. We present interception-oriented spherical-bearing nonlinear model predictive control (ISB-NMPC), which couples spherical-bearing prediction with nonlinear quadrotor dynamics to jointly optimize target-directed closing motion and visibility. Autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs) offer an efficient solution for environmental cleanup as well as search and rescue operations in open waters. Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Step Lookahead Information Via Adaptive Batching 2026-10-25
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

We study tabular finite-horizon reinforcement learning problems with multiple steps of lookahead information. Before acting, the learner observes $\ell$ steps of future transition and reward realizations: the exact state the agent would reach and the rewards it would collect under any possible course of action. While it has been shown that such information can drastically boost the value, finding the optimal policy is NP-hard, and it is common to apply one of two tractable heuristics: processing the lookahead in chunks of predefined sizes ('fixed batching policies'), and model predictive control. We first illustrate the problems with these two approaches and propose utilizing the lookahead in adaptive (state-dependent) batches; we refer to such policies as adaptive batching policies (ABPs). We derive the optimal Bellman equations for these strategies and design an optimistic regret-minimizing algorithm that enables learning the optimal ABP when interacting with unknown environments. Our regret bounds are order-optimal up to a potential factor of the lookahead horizon $\ell$, which can usually be considered a small constant.

Energy-Based Fault Detection and Isolation Framework for Flat Port-Hamiltonian Mechanical Systems 2026-10-24
Show abstract

Fault diagnosis during regulated motion requires informative signals even when feedback attenuates tracking deviations. This paper develops an energy-based diagnostic framework that constructs mechanical port-power residuals without a dedicated dynamic observer. Spectral Hamiltonian Algebraic Power Evaluation (SHAPE) combines port-Hamiltonian variables and differential flatness to generate nominal power references from planned motion for fully actuated mechanical systems with collocated actuation. Subtracting these references from commanded effort multiplied by measured flow yields residuals whose final evaluation requires neither recursive observer updates nor measured-state dynamics calculations. Continuous wavelet transform (CWT) scalograms and a shared-encoder convolutional neural network are used to distinguish nominal operation, actuator loss of effectiveness, anomalous friction, and vibration from complete trajectories. Evaluation uses a simulated six degree of freedom Kinova manipulator under model predictive control and fixed plant-model mismatch, with single faults at a specified joint. Across five training seeds and 3000 held-out test runs, SHAPE-CWT achieves a mean accuracy of 84.69 percent, versus 81.67 percent for torque residuals and 97.17 percent for the generalized momentum observer under common CWT processing. The respective sample standard deviations are 0.70, 0.70, and 0.37 percentage points. These results demonstrate useful fault-type discrimination from algebraic power residuals, while severity-excitation profiles and offline velocity perturbations reveal limits to their diagnostic capability.

ZiMPedance: Impedance-Aware ZMP Modeling and Control for Payload Carrying with Quadruped Robots 2026-10-24
Show abstract

Load transportation with quadruped robots is strongly affected by the dynamics of the physical interface between the robot and the load. Passive spring-based arms provide a lightweight and mechanically compliant alternative to active manipulators, but their dynamics can store and release energy during locomotion, producing oscillatory interaction forces that reduce robot stability. This paper develops a passive-arm-aware ZMP model for a quadruped carrying a payload through a passive arm. The reduced analytical model relates passive-interface stiffness, damping, and payload mass to the ZMP response under both external payload forcing and locomotion-induced base excitation, showing that lightly damped passive modes can overlap with gait harmonics. We then propose an arm-aware MPC (ARMPC), a passive-arm Model Predictive Control approach based on extended Single Rigid Body Dynamics that combines reduced floating-base dynamics with passive-arm joint states, internal torsional springs and dampers, and base-arm inertial coupling. Simulation ablations show that ARMPC consistently reduces ZMP-margin violations and prevents failures across all tested conditions, while Nominal MPC, which neither models nor compensates the passive arm, and Baseline MPC, which compensates it through an estimated wrench, exhibit higher violation percentages and failures under disturbances. Hardware pull-release experiments with a 2 kg payload show that the proposed ARMPC completes all longitudinal and diagonal disturbance trials without failure, whereas Baseline MPC fails in 7/15 and 8/15 trials, respectively. The same model also enables passive-arm end-effector tracking while maintaining a positive ZMP margin.

A Tutorial on Physics Constrained Machine Learning for Hybrid Modeling, Optimization, and Control 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Physics-constrained machine learning (PCML) integrates domain knowledge, first-principles, and physical constraints into AI/ML models. In this work, we present a step-by-step tutorial of a modular PCML framework, KKT-HardNet, and illustrate how it could be used for hybrid modeling, process simulation, optimization and model predictive control in a single platform. KKT-HardNet combines fast inference capabilities of neural networks with guaranteed satisfaction of algebraic constraints through differential projection. It can handle heterogeneous data and enforce both linear and nonlinear equality and inequality constraints. The modular design supports custom neural-network backbones and user-defined projection mechanisms for PCML. To enable efficient end-to-end training, KKT-HardNet exploits implicit function theorem to backpropagate through the projection layer. This avoids computationally expensive automatic differentiation. This work also introduces an exact reduced-order Fischer-Burmeister reformulation and a primal-dual interior-point method as an alternative projection solve strategy. We also provide a diverse set of benchmarking problems for PCML in engineering applications. KKT-HardNet achieves orders of magnitude better performance in terms of both constraint satisfaction and inference time. The KKT-HardNet code and the benchmark datasets are available at https://github.com/souls-tamu/kkt-hardnet .

Metro-WM: Long-Horizon Latent Planning with Realisable Sub-Goals 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Model-predictive control with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) provides a strong zero-shot goal-reaching planner, but it is only effective over short planning horizons. Hierarchical extensions attempt to bridge this gap by learning a macro planner to predict intermediate latent sub-goals to guide the micro planner. In this work, we demonstrate that unconstrained latent sub-goal prediction is fundamentally flawed. A rigorous evaluation reveals that leading hierarchical macro planners routinely emit physically unrealisable sub-goals. To resolve this, we introduce Metro-WM, a hierarchical framework that issues sub-goals by retrieving genuine states from prior experience rather than generating ungrounded latent vectors. Specifically, Metro-WM constructs a graph whose vertices are observed frames from offline expert demonstrations or random-action trajectories, allowing frames from different episodes to be connected and stitched into routes to the goal. Planning over the full graph also makes the system highly robust to execution errors: if the micro planner drifts off course, Metro-WM instantly finds a new optimal path from the current state. Our experiments show that Metro-WM achieves superior long-horizon success rates of up to 37.33 percentage points over the strongest hierarchical baseline on the same world model while being up to 10.9 times faster, requiring both 13-56 times less offline compute and fewer tuned hyperparameters. Additional analysis reveals that Metro-WM frequently finds shorter paths than the offline demonstrations, outperforms an oracle relying on the query's own demonstration on most benchmarks, and maintains robust performance under extremely sparse dataset conditions.

Robust Offset-Free Lane-Keeping Control Framework for Vision-Only Automated Vehicles 2026-10-23
📝 13 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Achieving accurate, real-time lane-keeping control during high-speed cornering on large-curvature roads while ensuring robust vehicle stability is a critical challenge in autonomous driving. To address this challenge, this study developed a robust offset-free model predictive control (OF-MPC) framework for vision-based autonomous vehicle lane-keeping. We present Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models (HWM), an architecture and planning paradigm for hierarchical model predictive control directly on visual world models trained solely via next-latent prediction. HWM learns world models at multiple temporal scales within a shared latent space, so predictions from the long horizon model serve as subgoals for the short horizon model via latent matching, without task-specific rewards or hierarchical policies. To keep long-horizon search tractable, HWM learns an action encoder that compresses primitive action chunks into latent macro-actions. On real-world Franka manipulation, HWM solves pick-and-place from a single goal image at 70% success vs. 0% for single-level planning. Across simulated push manipulation and maze navigation, HWM consistently improves performance on long-horizon tasks while requiring up to 3x less planning compute.

Safety evaluation in sampling-based stochastic model predictive control often requires numerical estimation of exit functionals. The approximation of first-exit times and exit indicators is therefore a key numerical bottleneck, and discretization error in these quantities directly affects the resulting controller. This paper studies how existing higher-order methods for strong approximation of exit times can be brought into safe control. Two cases are highlighted. For general noncommutative dynamics, an adaptive order-1 Milstein discretization is used together with Lévy-area simulation via Wiktorsson's method. For commutative dynamics, an adaptive order-1.5 construction achieves a stronger exit-time rate. Under a local anti-concentration condition on the exit-time law, we show that strong exit-time approximation transfers to strong approximation of the failure indicator. The methods are then studied in the context of chance-constrained path integral control, which provides an exact continuous-time representation of safety through exit events. Reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive control (MPC) offer complementary strengths, yet existing MPC-RL frameworks commonly rely on gradient-based optimization and therefore cannot handle the non-differentiable dynamics and objectives common in RL. We propose Soft MPCritic, an MPC-RL framework that learns a soft value function and uses sampling-based planning both for online control and for generating value targets. Specifically, Soft MPCritic instantiates MPC with model predictive path integral control (MPPI) and learns a terminal Q-function through fitted value iteration. Because the value targets are computed by the same planner used for control, the learned value function is aligned with the planner and implicitly extends its effective horizon. To keep target generation tractable, we introduce an amortized warm-start strategy that reuses the open-loop action sequences planned during online control to initialize batched MPPI computations of value targets, reducing computational cost without sacrificing solution quality. For robustness to model error, Soft MPCritic plans over scenarios drawn from an ensemble of dynamics models trained for next-step prediction. On both classical and constrained control tasks, Soft MPCritic learns effective policies with non-differentiable objectives, particularly where finite-horizon MPPI alone falls short. Soft MPCritic thus offers a practical blueprint for synthesizing MPC policies directly from black-box objectives, without differentiable surrogates.

Task-Space Motion-Force Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for Precise and Compliant Control under Uncertainties 2026-10-12

In Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), the control law is implicitly determined by the solution of an Optimal Control Problem (OCP). Consequently, OCP design is often tailored to specific platforms and tasks, demanding extensive expert knowledge and tuning effort. To address this, we propose a unified NMPC framework that directly plans and controls the robot joints subject to task-space motion and force requirements and is capable of seamlessly generalizing across multiple robotic platforms and diverse tasks. Furthermore, optimization-based methods often treat interactions as rigid contacts, making the torque solution sensitive to the modeling errors of the robot dynamics and the environment. We study the intrinsic task-space impedance of our proposed controller, its partial contraction for differential stability and differential passivity under unmodeled interactions. As a result, our framework enables closing the force-feedback loop in the high-level planning layer to achieve precise motion-force tracking performance, addressing the long-standing challenge of force-feedback NMPC. Finally, we validate our framework on physical hardware, demonstrating hybrid motion-force control on a redundant manipulator and compliant locomotion on a quadruped, while preserving core NMPC advantages such as hard constraint satisfaction, optimal redundancy exploitation, and real-time planning under unforeseen interactions.
