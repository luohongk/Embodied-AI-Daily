Design of an Underwater Vehicle With Orientation-Adjustable Thrusters and Adaptive Tracking Control 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs) are essential for marine exploration and research. However, conventional designs often struggle with limited maneuverability in complex and dynamic underwater environments. This paper presents an orientation-adjustable thruster UUV (OAT-UUV) with vector thrusters, enabling full six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) motion. and composite maneuvers. To overcome challenges associated with uncertain model parameters and environmental disturbances, a vision-based tracking control system based on a novel feedforward adaptive model predictive controller (FF-AMPC) is proposed to achieve robust object tracking, which integrates realtime state feedback with adaptive parameter updating. Extensive experiments, including closed-loop tracking and composite motion tests in real-world environment, have been conducted. The obtained results demonstrate that the orientation-adjustable propulsion design significantly improves the underwater maneuverability, while the FF-AMPC controller achieves a 49.1% hovering improvement compared to conventional controller.

Beyond Single-Episode Optimization: Sliding-Window Aware Generative Auto-Bidding for Long-Term Advertising Effectiveness 2026-11-03
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Auto-bidding systems optimize bids to maximize value under efficiency constraints such as Cost-Per-Action (CPA). Existing methods treat each day as an independent episode. However, many advertisers produce value so sparsely that per-day efficiency ratios become statistically unreliable, undermining advertiser retention. Platforms therefore evaluate window-level efficiency over sliding windows of $W{=}7$ days, ensuring fair evaluation and long-term advertising effectiveness. This creates cross-episode coupling: each day's bidding decisions affect up to $W$ overlapping windows, so setting daily targets requires anticipating future market conditions. We propose SWAG-Bid (Sliding-Window Aware Generative Auto-Bidding), a hierarchical framework decomposing this challenge into episode-level planning and step-level execution. The planner uses a Masked Trajectory Model to forecast markets and generate candidate plans, scored across all overlapping windows by Multi-Window Model Predictive Control Sampling (MWMS) with exponential confidence decay. The controller adjusts reliance on this guidance through a state-adaptive gate, Per-Step Gated Adaptive Layer Normalization (PSG-AdaLN), complemented by Return-to-Go and Cost-to-Go channels carrying budget and constraint information. Experiments on AuctionNet-Sparse and online A/B tests on AliExpress show that SWAG-Bid achieves competitive constraint satisfaction and value acquisition under sliding-window evaluation.

Scaling Nonlinear Optimization: Many Problems One GPU 2026-11-03
📝 9 pages, 6 figures, ieeeconf style, submission for RA-L
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Modern GPUs and software frameworks have made sampling-based methods, such as reinforcement learning and model predictive path integral control, increasingly popular in robotics. However, the logic these methods can execute per sample is limited to what has been implemented on the GPU. Nonlinear programs (NLPs) are one of the most expressive tools in robotics, reliably minimizing a cost while exactly enforcing general constraints. NLP optimizers were among the field's main workhorses, but were not designed for modern GPU hardware. Embedding NLPs such as trajectory optimization and inverse kinematics directly within each sample would be highly valuable, yet the only current option is to call a CPU solver per sample, possibly in parallel on a multi-core CPU. This bottlenecks the CPU, requires GPU-to-CPU-to-GPU transfers when the rest of the pipeline resides on the GPU, and leaves the GPU intermittently idle. To address this issue, in this work we present jaxipm, the first GPU-batched solver for generally constrained nonlinear optimization problems. Our approach is a JAX implementation of the battle-tested IPOPT algorithm, facilitated by eliminating control flow through heterogeneous iteration fusion, and minimizing GPU idle time through iteration-level batching. Our implementation enables solving hundreds of NLPs in parallel on a single GPU. We validate jaxipm on a range of quadrotor nonlinear model predictive control benchmarks, where on a single GPU we achieve between 30.7% and 69.9% of the throughput of 64 IPOPT instances running in parallel on a 64-core CPU. open-source codebase: https://github.com/johnviljoen/jaxipm

Safe-Ballbot: Tilt-Consistent MPC with Control Barrier Functions for Ballbot Navigation 2026-11-02
📝 Under-review
Show abstract

Ball-balancing robots (ballbots) must lean to accelerate, and the lean is limited by the wheel torques and by safety. Planners that use the full robot model are expensive, while planners that ignore the balance dynamics produce references that cannot be followed. This paper presents a two-layer navigation scheme for a three-omni-wheel ballbot. The upper layer is a model predictive controller on a \emph{jerk model} of the ball position, with discrete-time control barrier functions for static, moving, rotating and concave obstacles. The lower layer is a hierarchical sliding mode controller with an extended state observer and a robust exponential control barrier function on the tilt (HSMC-ESO-ECBF). The layers are linked by an acceleration bound derived in closed form from the largest steady tilt that the barrier function admits, so that the plan never asks for a lean the tracker refuses. For obstacles whose motion is only predicted, the obstacles are inflated by conformal prediction radii that bound the prediction error with a prescribed probability. In MuJoCo, with omni-wheel roller contacts and torque saturation, the scheme keeps at least $10$\,cm of clearance, tracks the plan within $2$\,cm RMS, keeps the tilt below the limit and plans in $9$--$12$\,ms, $10$--$15$ times faster than a full-model nonlinear MPC that enters the inflated obstacles; with predicted obstacles, the conformal inflation removes the collisions of a constant-velocity predictor in $30$ random crossing scenarios and reduces them in an endless run on a closed path.The source code and video illustrations are available at https://safe-ballbot.github.io

From PID to Reinforcement Learning in Building HVAC Control: A Control-Architecture Review of Tuning Strategies, Formulations, and Deployment Trade-offs 2026-11-02
📝 52 pages, 6 Figs
Show abstract

This paper reviews control architectures for building heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) systems, addressing the tension among energy demand, occupant comfort and equipment operability, and situating proportional-integral-derivative (PID) regulation within a layered control stack that also includes rule-based and model-based supervisory control and data-driven reinforcement learning (RL). It examines the thermostat-PID layer from two-position and scheduled setpoint control through proportional, PI and PID regulation and their tuning, then formulates RL for building control as a Markov decision process. Its central contribution is a taxonomy of where RL meets PID: RL as a gain tuner for an existing loop, as a direct replacement for the regulatory controller, as a hybrid with model predictive control, and as supervisory setpoint and schedule generation. Across these classes, the review establishes that the physical loop remains invariant while the intervention point changes, and that the classes differ in their requirements for actuation authority, state information, modelling, commissioning and constraint handling. It further establishes that PID is inexpensive and well codified but limited by nonlinearity, time variation, discrete mode switching, loop interaction and slow plant dynamics, while RL's admissible position must be argued rather than assumed. The paper concludes that these architectures are not competing philosophies of control but nested points of attachment on one HVAC plant, with hybrid implementations possible, and that deployment trade-offs must be assessed according to the specific control interface and scaffolding required.

Interpretable Allocation and Model-Predictive Rebalancing of Equity Portfolios, Tested on Real and Synthetic Markets 2026-11-02
Show abstract

A live equity portfolio poses three problems: which stocks to hold, when to trade, and whether the strategy holds up beyond the observed market. We built one system for all three and tested it on 373 US equities, calibrated on 2014-2024 and traded from January 2025 to September 2026. A selection rule chose stocks by score, by a bandit, or at random, and one of seven weighting rules, four consumer-theory utilities, and three standard portfolios, set share counts under limits on budget, single-stock weight, portfolio volatility, and turnover, with each stock's preference weight a bounded score of its trend, market beta, and the market's recent direction. A model-predictive controller forecasts portfolio value with a multi-asset geometric Brownian motion and re-planned at its horizon, when the value left its forecast band, or at a drawdown stop. To probe deeper declines than the real window held, we also ran the system on simulated markets from per-asset hidden Markov generators with jumps, linked by market and sector factors. On real prices, the stocks chosen mattered more to terminal wealth than the weighting rule, and the early re-plans shrank the worst drawdown in most rule combinations against a fixed calendar without consistently raising wealth. In the deepest simulated declines, every combination, random picks included, ended above simulated SPY and the equal-weight universe in the median, even without early re-plans, so the margin came from shared parts of the system, not from stock selection or re-plan timing. A market hedge that tightened a beta cap as the market weakened cost wealth on real prices but paid off in the most sustained simulated declines, and the generator's persistent trends favored score-based selection more than the real market did, which suggests a synthetic test judges stock selection fairly only if its generator does not build in that persistence.

When to Probe and When to Act: Clarity-Aware Risk-Adaptive Control for Interactive Driving 2026-11-01
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to ICRA 2027. Youran Wang and Devodita Chakravarty contributed equally
Show abstract

Safe navigation in interactive traffic requires autonomous vehicles to reason about unobservable human characteristics and the reciprocal nature of multi-agent interactions. Traditional architectures often decouple prediction from planning, leading to the "Frozen Robot Problem" or unsafe maneuvers under high ambiguity because they treat risk assessment and information acquisition as independent objectives. We argue that the notion of safety should not remain static but must instead dynamically co-evolve with the system's internal confidence: the robot should actively clarify ambiguity when necessary while also recognizing the diminishing returns of information, and naturally transition toward task execution. We introduce a unified belief-space Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that couples multistep information planning with a clarity-adaptive Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) barrier mechanism. By treating clarity, a scalar informational state, as an explicit planning variable, the controller co-designs its probing strategy with its safety specification, tightening conservatism under high ambiguity and relaxing constraints as confidence improves. We further provide a performance analysis showing that the cost of information seeking and the expected planning-cost gap to a controller that knows the true human characteristic are bounded by residual ambiguity and solver error. We evaluate the framework in lane-merging and unsignalized-intersection scenarios against representative interactive-planning baselines. Across 5,000 simulations, the proposed method achieves the highest success rates (94.6% and 93.2%) and the lowest collision rates (1.2% and 1.4%) among the compared methods, providing a principled foundation for robust decision-making in human-centric environments.

BarrierFormer: Transformer-Guided Predictive Barrier Enforcement for Safe Robot Control 2026-10-30
📝 23 pages, 2 figures, Accepted at 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026), Austin TX, USA
Show abstract

Control barrier functions (CBFs) have become one of the most popular tools for encoding and enforcing state constraints in safety-critical robotics. Standard CBF approaches are inherently myopic in nature as they enforce safety only at the current time step. Consequently, the system can be driven toward the boundary of the safe set where no feasible safe control exists at a future timestep. Model predictive control (MPC) based approaches address this by enforcing state constraints over a receding horizon. However, such approaches generally require the model to be known for solving a constrained optimization problem at every step, which is computationally expensive for real-time deployment. We propose BarrierFormer, a barrier-supervised transformer framework that addresses these limitations by encoding rollout-level CBF constraints in learning a model-free safe policy. A causal transformer encodes observation-action history, autoregressively generates a predictive rollout through the dynamics head to replace the model, and provides a residual correction to a nominal controller through the action head to replace the online computation. A barrier critic operating on local observations evaluates CBF constraint violations along this rollout, and a safety teacher computes barrier-consistent actions satisfying these constraints as direct supervision targets for the learned control policy. During inference, the policy maps observation-action history to control actions without any online optimization or model knowledge, enabling real-time model-free predictive safety enforcement. Evaluations across linear and nonlinear, 2D and 3D dynamical systems for safe goal-directed navigation demonstrate that BarrierFormer outperforms existing reinforcement learning (RL)-based, diffusion-based, MPC-based, and transformer-based approaches in safety rate and inference latency.

Online Learning of Heterogeneous Locomotion Dynamics under Reinforcement Learning Policies for Precise Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-30
📝 18 pages, 15 figures. Supplementary video: https://youtu.be/H-u8buk86C0
Show abstract

How to reuse a pretrained locomotion policy for precise loco-manipulation is still an open problem. We propose an online method to learn the closed-loop dynamics under a frozen locomotion policy. The learned model predicts floating-base motion and improves loco-manipulation accuracy. However, unobserved latent conditions of the policy introduce heterogeneous responses, meaning that the same observed state will lead to different motions. Direct learning without accounting for this heterogeneity introduces bias and fluctuations into dynamics estimates, degrading floating-base prediction and end-effector tracking. To address this problem, a contaminated Gaussian model is proposed to approximate the effects of latent conditions. The model uses two Gaussian components to approximate predictable and unpredictable responses under the same observed state. Then, the contaminated Gaussian model is combined with a Gaussian process to construct an online learner using random Fourier features (RFFs). The learner estimates each response's probability of belonging to the predictable component and uses it to weight online updates, capturing predictable responses while reducing bias and fluctuations caused by heterogeneous observations. With consistent and unbiased dynamics estimates, model predictive control (MPC) predicts floating-base motion accurately and enables precise loco-manipulation. A finite-sample error bound is established to ensure uniform convergence in online learning. Simulation and hardware experiments demonstrate consistent end-effector tracking across different manipulator configurations and tasks, with errors of 0.01 m.

Interaction-Aware Sampling-Based Motion Planning for Autonomous Surface Vessels in Urban Waterways 2026-10-30
📝 This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication
Show abstract

Autonomous surface vessels operating in urban waterways must coordinate with other vessels in confined spaces while complying with traffic regulations, often without knowing their intentions. We present a decentralized motion planner based on model predictive path integral control, a sampling-based model predictive control scheme. The planner jointly computes a trajectory for the ego vessel and a prediction of the trajectories of the other vessels, and therefore accounts for their interaction. Traffic regulations are encoded as discontinuous costs, which the sampling-based formulation admits without a smooth approximation. We evaluate the method in maps taken from real sections of Amsterdam's canals, in encounters between two vessels and in scenarios with four interacting vessels. Across 780 randomized runs the method reaches every goal without collisions or deadlocks, and outperforms an optimization-based planner designed for the same domain, as well as a version of the proposed planner that does not account for the interaction. We further validate the planner on two scaled vessel prototypes in an outdoor environment, where planning and localization run onboard at 10 Hz, and head-on and crossing encounters are resolved without collisions or rule violations.

AC-SMPC: Differentiable Actor--Critic MPC with State Constraints for Safe and Fast Robot Navigation 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Achieving safe navigation in confined dynamic environments remains a challenging problem, as robots must balance navigation efficiency with safety. Existing actor--critic MPC architectures typically employ differentiable MPC layers, but this way cannot readily accommodate explicit state constraints, making it intractable to involve collision avoidance within learning-based planning. To address this issue, we propose an actor--critic model predictive control framework with state constraints. We first use vertices to represent the robot and obstacles, and half-spaces to represent the workspace. Based on these, the nonlinear robot dynamics is locally linearized and the hyperplane-based collision avoidance constraints are convexified into the differentiable MPC layer, and then the actor exploits closed-loop feedback to adjust the MPC objective parameters that shape local motion behavior, while the critic evaluates long-term task performance. We further relax the convexified constraints with bounded and penalized slack variables to form an elastic quadratic program, accommodating cases where the local constraint approximations cannot be satisfied simultaneously. Simulation tests and experiments, involving dynamic collision avoidance for multi-robot in narrow cases, illustrate the advantages of the proposed framework in adaptively adjusting the learned policy and improving navigation efficiency over existing approaches while maintaining the motion safety.

CuPIQP: A Batched and Differentiable QP Solver on GPUs for Robot Learning and Control 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Quadratic programs (QPs) are fundamental building blocks of many robotic tasks such as inverse kinematics, whole-body control, and model predictive control. Extending these methods to large-scale robot learning and control requires solving many QPs concurrently and, for end-to-end learning, differentiating through their solutions. Efficient batched optimization is therefore essential to bringing established model-based methods into GPU-based robotics pipelines. We present \textsc{cuPIQP}, a batched and differentiable QP solver on GPUs based on a proximal interior-point method, with unified support for dense, sparse, and multistage problems. The solver exploits parallelism across independent QPs and their internal structure while preserving per-problem numerical updates and termination decisions. Benchmarks with up to 16384 QPs per batch demonstrate solve-time speedups of up to $18.8\times$ over \textsc{Moreau} and $4.26\times$ over eight-core \textsc{PIQP}. In an end-to-end learning task, \textsc{cuPIQP} accelerates training by $4.6\times$ over \textsc{QPAX} and $7.8\times$ over \textsc{Moreau}. By reducing the computational cost, \textsc{cuPIQP} provides efficient, reusable infrastructure for integrating model-based optimization into large-scale robot learning and control.

Learned Terminal Costs for Short-Horizon Model Predictive Control of Autonomous Trucks in Interactive Highway Traffic 2026-10-29
📝 13 pages, 2 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Autonomous highway driving for heavy-duty trucks is a challenging control problem. The greater mass, dimensions and intricate dynamics increase the requirements for cooperation from surrounding traffic in critical maneuvers, requiring a careful balance between safety constraints and efficient long-horizon decision making. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides rigorous constraint satisfaction but relies on terminal cost which can limit its effectiveness at short, computationally affordable horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) captures long-term driving behavior but offers no constraint guarantees and tends toward overly conservative policies when trained under sparse collision penalties. In this paper, we propose a hybrid MPC and RL framework for highway driving in autonomous trucks, where a finite-horizon MPC, solved via dynamic programming, uses a pre-trained RL model for terminal cost estimation. We evaluate the framework in a simulation environment with reactive surrounding traffic and compare it against pure MPC with zero terminal cost, rollout with terminal cost estimation using a base policy, and pure RL. Results show that the hybrid approach achieves zero collisions with the lowest cost of any configuration.

Teaching a Tree to Recover: Online Distillation of an LLM Supervisor with Counterfactual Replay 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Autonomous mobile robots commonly pair a global planner with a local model predictive controller (MPC), but nonconvex obstacle avoidance and a limited horizon can make the robot freeze in complex layouts. Deciding whether to replan, wait, or call for help is a supervisory decision. Recent work queries a large language model (LLM) supervisor on every anomaly, which is slow and costly. We instead distill the LLM online into a decision tree. After each training episode, counterfactual replay labels recoveries as needed, unnecessary, or harmful. These labels retire unhelpful rules. In simulation, on 20 unseen layouts where the MPC alone freezes, the tree handles at least 18 correctly across all seeds, either by reaching the goal or by calling for help when no feasible path exists. The tree matches both an always-query LLM supervisor and expert-designed rules, while issuing no LLM queries at test time. Handwritten rules cannot anticipate the long tail of situations a physical robot will encounter. Online distillation is a promising remedy: the tree handles familiar cases, and the LLM is consulted only for new ones, whose validated recoveries become new rules. Language models can thus also serve as designers of supervisors rather than only as runtime supervisors.

Safe and Fuel-Efficient Motion Planning for Lunar Propulsive Drones 2026-10-28
📝 This work has been submitted to the 2027 IEEE Aerospace Conference
Show abstract

Propulsive drones are a promising new technology for generating high-resolution terrain models of the Moon. In future lunar missions, propulsive drones will complete a series of hops across the lunar surface while capturing images with downward-facing cameras. We present an autonomous motion planning framework that uses a vision-guided optimal control strategy for performing safe, efficient hops between specified waypoints. The drone plans parabolic hops between waypoints generated by a global-level planner; if a nominal waypoint is unsafe or unreachable, the drone then completes a diversion maneuver to safely land. To evaluate the validity of potential landing sites, we develop a custom semantic segmentation model for identifying obstacles from aerial images, and we introduce an approach for rapidly calculating reachable terrain. We then use model predictive control (MPC) to robustly track hop trajectories that guide the drone towards safe landing sites. We test the optimal hop planner in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. Our results demonstrate that our unified motion planning framework yields safe, fuel-efficient hop trajectories which can be tracked rapidly and with low error via MPC.

A Multi-Layer Navigation System for Smart Wheelchairs with a Limited Input Interface 2026-10-28
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Presented at IROS 2026 (IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems); to appear in the conference proceedings
Show abstract

Smart wheelchairs are robotic devices that gather information from the surroundings and allow people to safely drive through various environments. They usually rely on the interpretation of high-level user's intentions, typically decoded through human-robot interface (HRI) or brain-machine interface (BMI). However, navigating in an unknown environment and accurately understanding user commands remain significant challenges. Misinterpretation of commands or the environment can lead to user frustration, distrust of the system, or even unsafe situations. In this paper, we present a novel Multi-Layer Navigation System (MLNS) for a smart wheelchair that enables navigation in unknown environments while interpreting and safely contextualizing user's intentions even in the case of limited and faulty commands. The results demonstrate the robustness and effectiveness of our system in real-world environments, proving its potential for practical applications in assistive technologies.

Convex-SKM-Net: Low-Latency Block Halfspace Corrections for Neural Convex Constraint Satisfaction 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Fast inference and strict constraint satisfaction are essential for learning-based decision-making. Existing methods often rely on constraint-specific projections or feasible-set parameterizations, while iterative repair can be costly. We introduce Convex-SKM-Net, a convex constraint-satisfaction layer that forms linearization halfspaces from residual and (sub)gradient oracles without constraint-specific inequality projectors. It applies fixed-depth dual projected-gradient descent (dual PGD) to minimum-displacement block problems using GPU-friendly matrix operations. We jointly learn the predictor and a positive-definite mobility, adapting the correction geometry to improve objective quality. We prove convergence of the outer iterates at fixed dual depth and linear convergence in distance under Slater's condition and bounded subgradients. All our test outputs on three second-order-cone model-predictive control (SOC-MPC) horizons and six power-system convex relaxations satisfy a $10^{-3}$ feasibility tolerance. Comparisons with the latest constraint-satisfaction methods show inference speedups of up to $144.3\times$ on SOC-MPC and $7.7\times$ on a convex quadratically constrained quadratic programming (QCQP) benchmark with joint training. On QCQP, the mean objective gap is $0.012\%$, with full test feasibility at $10^{-4}$. Learning mobility reduces the five-seed affine-task mean gap by $22.4\%$ and improves gaps in matched power-system comparisons.

Neural Compression of Long ADMM Trajectory for Multiparametric Quadratic Program 2026-10-27
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/SOLARIS-JHU/TQPNet
Show abstract

Solving large-scale multiparametric quadratic programs (mpQPs) in real time often requires thousands of iterative optimization updates, creating a major computational bottleneck in model predictive control and learning-enabled systems. This paper proposes TQPNet, a self-supervised neural compression framework that learns to compress long ADMM optimization trajectories into a compact neural warm start followed by a few refinement iterations. We derive a low-dimensional ADMM scheme operating on the primal-dual variables $(x,β)$ and show that its iteration admits an equivalent ReLU-layer representation, termed ADMM(ReLU). Building on this insight, TQPNet combines a multilayer perceptron with ReLU activations (MLP(ReLU)) and a small number of unrolled ADMM(ReLU) refinement layers. The network is trained offline using an optimality-condition-informed residual loss, eliminating the need for solver-generated labels. The proposed residual-based framework enables both self-supervised training and online solution certification through adaptive ADMM(ReLU) refinement. Experiments on large-scale mpQPs and real-time MPC applications demonstrate that TQPNet achieves high accuracy, strong generalization, and substantially faster inference than conventional optimization-based methods.

Show abstract

The problem of nonprehensile robotic catching is addressed using virtual nonholonomic constraints (VNHCs); the motion of the robot end-effector is designed and controlled directly in terms of the states of the object before it is caught on the surface of the end-effector. In contrast to contemporary approaches, our approach avoids the need for object trajectory prediction, and consequent time-varying robot trajectory generation. With a focus on the planar case, we demonstrate that our VNHC approach can be used to catch an object on an end-effector at a desired height, with minimal relative normal velocity at the contact instant. World models enable intelligent agents to predict the consequences of their actions on the environment. In this paper, we propose Multi Rigid Object Gaussian World Model (MRO-GWM), a novel model that learns action-conditional dynamics of rigid objects in 3D. Distributed Predictive Flocking for a Swarm of Miniature Vibration-Driven Robots in a Confined Arena 2026-10-26
📝 Submitted to Robotics and Autonomous Systems
Show abstract

Coordinating many miniature robots in a confined arena, with no leader, central planner, or global map, is a core challenge for swarm robotics. As the swarm grows denser, collision avoidance becomes the limiting factor, and the number of safely coexisting robots depends on the formulation of the inter-robot safety constraint. We present a distributed predictive flocking controller in which each robot solves its own nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) problem, optimizing swarm cohesion and alignment costs, inter-robot and obstacle safety, and a task objective, while exchanging only predicted trajectories with local neighbors. Within this generalized controller architecture, solved in real time, we compare six barrier and control-barrier-function (CBF) safety formulations on a digital twin of an open-source vibration-driven bristlebot in a $0.64\,\mathrm{m^2}$ arena. A look-ahead discrete-time CBF performs best overall, with the fewest collisions and the widest inter-robot margins across nearly every swarm size tested. We deploy it on phototaxis, phototactic orbiting, and cohesion without a stimulus tasks, using swarm sizes starting from ten robots and up to the geometric packing limit. The swarm holds well: collisions stay low in small swarms, rising steeply as the packing limit approaches, while coordination and cohesion degrade gently rather than collapsing. The swarm is found not to trade safety for order, keeping both across the confined arena, with local alignment high at every swarm size.

VisGeo-PipeTrack: A Visual Geometric Servo Control Framework with MPC-RCAPID Control for UAV-Based Dynamic Pipes Tracking 2026-10-26
📝 Submit to IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems IEEE TITS
Show abstract

While unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly deployed for pipeline inspection, prevailing research predominantly assumes static pipes. In real-world industrial environments, pipes often exhibit dynamic movements, such as vibrations. This imposes more stringent requirements on UAVs' adaptive control and computational efficiency than in static scenarios. Therefore, this paper proposes a servo control framework named VisGeo-PipeTrack, which utilizes geometric constraints to enable UAV tracking of dynamic pipes. To achieve efficient UAV positioning, YOLOv5 is employed to detect labels on the pipe. The positions are derived from visual geometric constraints based on these detected labels to enable precise localization. To address tracking control challenges, a model predictive control based real-time corrected adaptive PID (MPC-RCAPID) controller is proposed. High-speed off-road autonomy requires precise closed-loop control for a target vehicle while remaining robust across changing terrains. Recent forward kinodynamic (FKD) prediction foundation models suggest a promising path, starting from a generalist model and specializing it to the target platform. However, effective specialization remains challenging, as it often requires substantial real-world data, and models adapted to one setting can still overfit to specific terrains or driving regimes. Inertially Significant Humanoid Teleoperation via Whole Body Model Predictive Control and Online Parameter Estimation 2026-10-15

The effective deployment of humanoid robots in practical tasks of interest will require a capability of sensing and adapting to variations in inertial parameters, as occurs during heavy tool use or when carrying significant payload. Such payloads constitute a significant proportion of the combined payload-robot system, the effects of which cannot be ignored by delegating to feedback control. This paper showcases a general solution to this problem by integrating a whole body model predictive controller (WB-MPC) with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online estimation of fully physically consistent time-varying inertial parameters. These estimates are fed through to the WB-MPC's full rigid body dynamics model, resulting in more accurate feedforward torques for task demands under external load. Additionally, a virtual reality (VR) interface is provided that enables an operator to provide task-specific references without regard to payload while delegating dynamic balance to the WB-MPC. The framework is validated with simulation and hardware experiments on a humanoid system.

CQDC-Warp: GPU-Accelerated Differentiable Quasi-Dynamic Simulation for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Efficient synthesis of contact-rich manipulation trajectories is essential for downstream robot learning. Gradient-based planning offers a promising approach, but jointly optimizing motion and contact decisions remains challenging. Meanwhile, many existing planners run on CPUs with limited throughput. We present CQDC-Warp, a GPU-accelerated differentiable quasi-dynamic simulator for batched model predictive control (MPC). We use it to study contact-guided trajectory optimization, in which intended robot--object contacts are specified before planning and encouraged through soft costs, while the simulated dynamics determine contact geometry and frictional response without a prescribed contact-mode schedule. Across $17{,}408$ trials on the $17$-object Allegro in-hand rotation benchmark, our framework achieves $79.8\%$ success, compared with $73.7\%$ for CFree and $65.2\%$ for IMPACT, and reduces mean trajectory-generation time by factors of $27.0$ and $50.2$, respectively. On a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with $2{,}048$ parallel worlds, forward and backward throughput reach $2.06\times10^5$ and $2.25\times10^5$ world-steps per second, respectively. Further experiments cover manipulation with LEAP and Shadow hands, environmental contact exploitation, bimanual manipulation, whole-body control, and real-world trajectory replay on a Sharpa hand.
