Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-15
Show abstract

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a multifingered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world experiment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-specific demonstrations or sim-to-real transfer.

Safe and Fuel-Efficient Motion Planning for Lunar Propulsive Drones 2026-10-15
📝 This work has been submitted to the 2027 IEEE Aerospace Conference
Show abstract

Propulsive drones are a promising new technology for generating high-resolution terrain models of the Moon. In future lunar missions, propulsive drones will complete a series of hops across the lunar surface while capturing images with downward-facing cameras. We present an autonomous motion planning framework that uses a vision-guided optimal control strategy for performing safe, efficient hops between specified waypoints. The drone plans parabolic hops between waypoints generated by a global-level planner; if a nominal waypoint is unsafe or unreachable, the drone then completes a diversion maneuver to safely land. To evaluate the validity of potential landing sites, we develop a custom semantic segmentation model for identifying obstacles from aerial images, and we introduce an approach for rapidly calculating reachable terrain. We then use model predictive control (MPC) to robustly track hop trajectories that guide the drone towards safe landing sites. We test the optimal hop planner in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. Our results demonstrate that our unified motion planning framework yields safe, fuel-efficient hop trajectories which can be tracked rapidly and with low error via MPC.

Inertially Significant Humanoid Teleoperation via Whole Body Model Predictive Control and Online Parameter Estimation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

The effective deployment of humanoid robots in practical tasks of interest will require a capability of sensing and adapting to variations in inertial parameters, as occurs during heavy tool use or when carrying significant payload. Such payloads constitute a significant proportion of the combined payload-robot system, the effects of which cannot be ignored by delegating to feedback control. This paper showcases a general solution to this problem by integrating a whole body model predictive controller (WB-MPC) with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online estimation of fully physically consistent time-varying inertial parameters. These estimates are fed through to the WB-MPC's full rigid body dynamics model, resulting in more accurate feedforward torques for task demands under external load. Additionally, a virtual reality (VR) interface is provided that enables an operator to provide task-specific references without regard to payload while delegating dynamic balance to the WB-MPC. The framework is validated with simulation and hardware experiments on a humanoid system.

CQDC-Warp: GPU-Accelerated Differentiable Quasi-Dynamic Simulation for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Efficient synthesis of contact-rich manipulation trajectories is essential for downstream robot learning. Gradient-based planning offers a promising approach, but jointly optimizing motion and contact decisions remains challenging. Meanwhile, many existing planners run on CPUs with limited throughput. We present CQDC-Warp, a GPU-accelerated differentiable quasi-dynamic simulator for batched model predictive control (MPC). We use it to study contact-guided trajectory optimization, in which intended robot--object contacts are specified before planning and encouraged through soft costs, while the simulated dynamics determine contact geometry and frictional response without a prescribed contact-mode schedule. Across $17{,}408$ trials on the $17$-object Allegro in-hand rotation benchmark, our framework achieves $79.8\%$ success, compared with $73.7\%$ for CFree and $65.2\%$ for IMPACT, and reduces mean trajectory-generation time by factors of $27.0$ and $50.2$, respectively. On a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with $2{,}048$ parallel worlds, forward and backward throughput reach $2.06\times10^5$ and $2.25\times10^5$ world-steps per second, respectively. Further experiments cover manipulation with LEAP and Shadow hands, environmental contact exploitation, bimanual manipulation, whole-body control, and real-world trajectory replay on a Sharpa hand.

MR-MPCC: Model Predictive Contouring Control for Robotic Manipulator Tracking under Moving Reference Frames 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted by The IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems 2026 (IROS 2026)
Show abstract

Maintaining small path-tracking errors under moving reference frames is essential for robotic manipulation tasks with tight execution tolerances, such as machining and surgical automation. Conventional time-indexed controllers, including tracking Model Predictive Control (MPC), rely on a fixed temporal correspondence with the reference trajectory and may therefore accumulate path-relative error under abrupt reference-frame motion or timing mismatch. This paper presents MR-MPCC, a Model Predictive Contouring Control (MPCC) framework for velocity-controlled robotic manipulators with 6-DoF end-effector pose regulation under Moving-Reference disturbances. A hierarchical control architecture is adopted, in which an upper-level MPCC optimizes path progress while penalizing contouring and orientation errors, and a constrained resolved motion rate control (RMRC) layer maps the commanded Cartesian twist to feasible joint velocities under joint-level and safety constraints. MR-MPCC is implemented in a ROS2--MuJoCo pipeline and validated on a UR5e platform under moving-reference disturbances, where the path frame is perturbed and tracked by electromagnetic sensors. Compared with PI control and time-indexed tracking MPC under matched disturbance, MR-MPCC achieves lower geometric path-following error and smaller deviations in the moving path frame, demonstrating improved path-following performance under reference-frame motion.

Plan Before You Trade: Inference-Time Optimization for RL Trading Agents 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning agents for portfolio management are typically trained and deployed as static policies, with no mechanism for policy adjustments using price forecasts at inference time. We propose FinPILOT (Financial Plugin Inference-time Learning for Optimal Trading), a plugin inference-time policy optimization framework inspired by Model Predictive Control (MPC). Under the price-taker assumption, portfolio actions do not affect future market prices, allowing a multi-step price forecaster to provide imagined market trajectories without iterative action-conditioned rollouts. At each decision step, FinPILOT uses the forecaster's predicted price trajectory to construct an allocation-based imagined return objective and optimizes the pretrained policy at inference time before executing one step of the trade. Our framework is compatible with any pretrained policy and adapts the policy using the forecaster's predictions without modifying the original RL training pipeline. Evaluated with five pretrained policies (PPO, SAC, A2C, TD3, DDPG) across DJ30 equities and foreign exchange (FX) over three rolling held-out test years (30 settings in total), FinPILOT improves Total Return in 28 of 30 settings and Sharpe ratio in 27 of 30, with gains spanning both stochastic and deterministic policies. Direct single- and multi-period portfolio optimizers supplied with the same forecasts do not reproduce these aggregate gains under our evaluation protocol. Further, using controlled forecasts at calibrated quality levels, we show that both the magnitude and consistency of FinPILOT's performance increase as forecast error is reduced.

Planning to Learn: Real-Time Robust Adaptive Control on the GPU with Guaranteed Future Learning 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Robust adaptive control is vital for keeping robots safe while actively reducing uncertainty in unfamiliar settings. However, traditional methods are often conservative since they fail to account for guaranteed future parameter learning during planning and struggle to scale to high-dimensional systems. In this work, we present A-SLS, a learning-aware robust adaptive model predictive control (RAMPC) framework based on system-level synthesis. A-SLS forecasts future parameter uncertainty and preserves correlations between process disturbances, parameter error, and estimate updates to construct tight, guaranteed reachable sets that account for future information gain. We also develop a GPU-accelerated A-SLS solver to enable real-time performance. Across nonlinear robotic systems, A-SLS reduces reachable-tube conservativeness, maintains real-time performance, and scales to whole-body control for a quadruped with 61 states, 8 uncertain parameters, and 12 control inputs. We further demonstrate the approach on hardware, where a physical Crazyflie drone learns an uncertain dynamics parameter online to stabilize on the fly.

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing 2026-10-14
📝 13 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles
Show abstract

Vehicles that lose control at the handling limit through icing, hydroplaning, or an abrupt evasive maneuver fall outside the operating envelope of production safety systems such as electronic stability control, which focus on preventing instability rather than recovering from it. Expert drivers regain authority in these conditions by drifting. Existing autonomous recovery approaches are validated on idealized scenarios, define "recovered" states empirically and inconsistently, or rely on offline-planned trajectories rather than acting online. We address these gaps by first training a racing policy with reinforcement learning that learns drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, then adapting it to recovery through continued training with a minimum-time reward. We define recovery relative to a phase-plane-grounded stable equilibrium and introduce Gym-Khana for controller training and evaluation. Across 294 initial loss-of-control states, the transferred policy improves recovery by 186.8% over a Stanley path tracker and outperforms both a nonlinear model predictive controller and a recovery-only policy trained from scratch, while also recovering faster on states the baseline can already handle. An ablation shows that racing pretraining, rather than broader initial-state exposure alone, drives this gain. Deployed on a physical 1:10-scale F1TENTH vehicle using system identification, actuator-lag modelling, and domain randomization, the transferred policy outperforms Stanley on every tested loss-of-control state, including recovering 50% of the time from a hard state the baseline never recovers from.

Bellman Conformal Inference: Calibrating Prediction Intervals For Time Series 2026-10-14
📝 36 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

We introduce Bellman Conformal Inference (BCI), a framework that wraps around any time series forecasting models and provides calibrated prediction intervals. Unlike the existing methods, BCI is able to leverage multi-step ahead forecasts and explicitly optimize the average interval lengths by solving a one-dimensional stochastic control problem (SCP) at each time step. In particular, we use the dynamic programming algorithm to find the optimal policy for the SCP. We prove that BCI achieves long-term coverage under arbitrary distribution shifts and temporal dependence, even with poor multi-step ahead forecasts. When the raw forecast intervals are approximately calibrated, we prove that BCI is near optimal for its surrogate model predictive control problem. We find empirically that BCI avoids uninformative intervals with infinite lengths under our main experimental configuration and generates substantially shorter prediction intervals when the raw forecast intervals are poorly calibrated.

Learning-Based Adaptive MPC for Magnetorquer-Only Satellite Attitude Control 2026-10-14
📝 8 pages, 8 figures, 1 table, Accepted for publication at the International Conference on Space Robotics (iSpaRo) 2026
Show abstract

Nanosatellites (e.g., CubeSats) have the potential to carry out useful science and exploration activities for outer solar system exploration, but their small size leads to tradeoffs between attitude control and science capabilities. Magnetorquers offer a unique compromise as compact actuators that enable three-axis attitude control, but suffer from a loss of control authority and require full knowledge of the planetary magnetic field. In this work, we present a learning-based adaptive model predictive control (MPC) algorithm that plans rotational trajectories for the satellite while accounting for this loss of control authority. Key to our approach is the inclusion of a learned magnetic field model that is trained in simulation using low-fidelity magnetic field data. Using measurements collected online, our proposed algorithm adapts this learned magnetic field model using a simple last-layer update scheme, allowing the MPC controller to quickly adapt to the true planetary magnetic field model and more effectively plan attitude trajectories. We demonstrate the results of our proposed approach for two use cases, (1) Earth-orbit and (2) Uranus-orbit, and show that our learning-based adaptive MPC algorithm improves controller success for these system simulations by 5% and 61%, respectively.

Adapting Generalist Vehicle Models for High-Speed MPC Across Terrains 2026-10-14
📝 https://amrl.cs.utexas.edu/optcar/
Show abstract

High-speed off-road autonomy requires precise closed-loop control for a target vehicle while remaining robust across changing terrains. Recent forward kinodynamic (FKD) prediction foundation models suggest a promising path, starting from a generalist model and specializing it to the target platform. However, effective specialization remains challenging, as it often requires substantial real-world data, and models adapted to one setting can still overfit to specific terrains or driving regimes. We present OptCar (Optimized Car), a recipe for bridging the gap from generalist to specialist FKD models that preserves cross-terrain generalization while optimizing performance for a specific vehicle. OptCar introduces a transformer FKD architecture that uses FiLM to condition multi-step predictions on a single dynamics context token summarizing recent state-action history. It then specializes the generalist model using limited real-world data and targeted synthetic rollouts from environment-specific system identification. In closed-loop model predictive control (MPC) experiments across three terrains and an out-of-distribution cart-pulling task, the largest gains appear at 6 m/s, the highest speed evaluated and the regime in which slip dominates tracking error. On vegetation + dirt, the most slip-diverse terrain, OptCar reduces 6 m/s trajectory tracking error by roughly 55% relative to AnyCar fine-tuned on real data alone, and remains the most accurate even when an unseen cart payload changes the dynamics. With 5 minutes of real data per terrain, OptCar is competitive on road with a specialist trained on 30 minutes of road data and outperforms it when the terrain changes.

Full-Order Safety for Nonlinear Reduced-Order MPC 2026-10-13
📝 8 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Reduced-order models (ROMs) make model predictive control (MPC) of high-dimensional nonlinear systems tractable, but the resulting modeling error can lead to constraint violations on the real system. We introduce CoRE-MPC, a robust MPC formulation that plans with a nonlinear ROM and guarantees recursive feasibility and constraint satisfaction for the full-order system. It can be applied with a broad class of nonlinear model reduction techniques and the resulting MPC does not require evaluating the full-order model online, thus ensuring computational efficiency. The key insight is in how the ROM affects the dynamics of the prediction error. We account for this with two error radii that are propagated alongside the ROM and provide a guaranteed bound on the prediction error. The approach is validated in simulation on an 80-state oscillator chain, a Burgers equation, and a 9,768-state finite-element soft robot, where a two-state ROM with data-driven bounds keeps all 1,628 nodes clear of a wall at a median solve time below 1 ms for a 20 ms control period. Across these examples, CoRE-MPC is 13 to 61 times faster than full-order robust MPC at only 27 to 35% higher closed-loop cost, whereas existing certified schemes are either infeasible or at least twice as conservative. Koopman DCM: Unstable Eigenfunctions as Data-driven Representations for Legged Balancing 2026-10-13
📝 14 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

In legged locomotion, divergent components of motion (DCMs) have emerged as characteristic states for balance control. They isolate the unstable mode of the dynamics but, in existing formulations, apply only to reduced models such as the linear inverted pendulum. In this study, we show how DCMs can be more generally formulated as Koopman eigenfunctions. Whereas Koopman analysis typically targets eigenvalues near zero, which capture conserved or slowly varying quantities, our investigation leads us to deliberately search for unstable eigenpairs with large eigenvalues. The resulting Koopman DCMs are data-driven observables trained using only real-robot data. On a real biped, DCMs learned from one hour of robot data improve tracking of reference walking patterns. We further show how learned DCMs provide state-based viability constraints when combined with model predictive control.

Beyond Safe Execution: Safe Online Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Driving with Value Calibration and Adaptive Guidance 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Online reinforcement learning (RL) enables driving policies to adapt to changing deployment conditions, but requires safe exploration throughout training. Safety filtering alone does not ensure stable policy improvement or reduced dependence on corrections. We propose Safe Online Adaptive Refinement (SOAR-Flow), a framework for safe and stable online fine-tuning of offline-initialized Flow-based driving policies. A control barrier function (CBF)--model predictive control (MPC) filter corrects potentially unsafe actions before execution. To support stable policy updates, we introduce Gaussian-return-aware value calibration based on calibrated Q-learning (Cal-QL), using recorded behavior returns and predicted return variability to smooth conservative critic updates and mitigate policy degradation. Paired corrective learning teaches feasible action adjustments, while an intervention-to-go predictor encourages decisions requiring fewer future corrections. Its predictions also regulate guidance strength, allowing recovery supervision to weaken as predicted dependence decreases. Together with gradually reduced calibration regularization, this supports continued task improvement while preserving hard execution constraints. We analyze conditions for safe execution at control steps and diminishing auxiliary learning influences. We evaluate SOAR-Flow in simulation and on an uncrewed ground vehicle (UGV). The results demonstrate improved driving performance and reduced reliance on safety corrections during protected online fine-tuning. The demonstration video and project overview are available at https://lzqw.github.io/SOAR-Flow/.

Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models 2026-10-12
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. 27 pages, including references and appendices. Project page: https://kevinghst.github.io/HWM/
Show abstract

World models are a promising path to zero-shot embodied control through planning. However, existing world model planners struggle on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks: prediction errors compound and naive search is exponential in the planning horizon. Hierarchy mitigates both by decomposing tasks into shorter, tractable subproblems; yet prior hierarchical approaches either amortize control into task-specific policies (hierarchical RL) or assume low-dimensional states and known dynamics (classical hierarchical MPC). We present Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models (HWM), an architecture and planning paradigm for hierarchical model predictive control directly on visual world models trained solely via next-latent prediction. HWM learns world models at multiple temporal scales within a shared latent space, so predictions from the long horizon model serve as subgoals for the short horizon model via latent matching, without task-specific rewards or hierarchical policies. To keep long-horizon search tractable, HWM learns an action encoder that compresses primitive action chunks into latent macro-actions. On real-world Franka manipulation, HWM solves pick-and-place from a single goal image at 70% success vs. 0% for single-level planning. Across simulated push manipulation and maze navigation, HWM consistently improves performance on long-horizon tasks while requiring up to 3x less planning compute.

Higher-Order Approximation of Exit Functionals in Sampling-Based Stochastic Model Predictive Control 2026-10-12
📝 Accepted for publication in the proceedings of the 2026 IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC 2026). Minor correction to Assumption A4 and a typo fix relative to the conference version. Results are unchanged
Show abstract

Safety evaluation in sampling-based stochastic model predictive control often requires numerical estimation of exit functionals. The approximation of first-exit times and exit indicators is therefore a key numerical bottleneck, and discretization error in these quantities directly affects the resulting controller. This paper studies how existing higher-order methods for strong approximation of exit times can be brought into safe control. Two cases are highlighted. For general noncommutative dynamics, an adaptive order-1 Milstein discretization is used together with Lévy-area simulation via Wiktorsson's method. For commutative dynamics, an adaptive order-1.5 construction achieves a stronger exit-time rate. Under a local anti-concentration condition on the exit-time law, we show that strong exit-time approximation transfers to strong approximation of the failure indicator. The methods are then studied in the context of chance-constrained path integral control, which provides an exact continuous-time representation of safety through exit events. Numerical experiments compare the two cases in terms of strong exit-time error, failure-indicator error, and closed-loop constraint satisfaction, showing improvement over Euler-Maruyama and thereby enabling existing and future techniques whose applicability depends on improved strong approximation.

Soft MPCritic: Value-Based Reinforcement Learning for Non-Differentiable Predictive Control 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive control (MPC) offer complementary strengths, yet existing MPC-RL frameworks commonly rely on gradient-based optimization and therefore cannot handle the non-differentiable dynamics and objectives common in RL. We propose Soft MPCritic, an MPC-RL framework that learns a soft value function and uses sampling-based planning both for online control and for generating value targets. Specifically, Soft MPCritic instantiates MPC with model predictive path integral control (MPPI) and learns a terminal Q-function through fitted value iteration. Because the value targets are computed by the same planner used for control, the learned value function is aligned with the planner and implicitly extends its effective horizon. To keep target generation tractable, we introduce an amortized warm-start strategy that reuses the open-loop action sequences planned during online control to initialize batched MPPI computations of value targets, reducing computational cost without sacrificing solution quality. For robustness to model error, Soft MPCritic plans over scenarios drawn from an ensemble of dynamics models trained for next-step prediction. On both classical and constrained control tasks, Soft MPCritic learns effective policies with non-differentiable objectives, particularly where finite-horizon MPPI alone falls short. Soft MPCritic thus offers a practical blueprint for synthesizing MPC policies directly from black-box objectives, without differentiable surrogates.

Task-Space Motion-Force Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for Precise and Compliant Control under Uncertainties 2026-10-12
Show abstract

In Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), the control law is implicitly determined by the solution of an Optimal Control Problem (OCP). Consequently, OCP design is often tailored to specific platforms and tasks, demanding extensive expert knowledge and tuning effort. To address this, we propose a unified NMPC framework that directly plans and controls the robot joints subject to task-space motion and force requirements and is capable of seamlessly generalizing across multiple robotic platforms and diverse tasks. Furthermore, optimization-based methods often treat interactions as rigid contacts, making the torque solution sensitive to the modeling errors of the robot dynamics and the environment. We study the intrinsic task-space impedance of our proposed controller, its partial contraction for differential stability and differential passivity under unmodeled interactions. As a result, our framework enables closing the force-feedback loop in the high-level planning layer to achieve precise motion-force tracking performance, addressing the long-standing challenge of force-feedback NMPC. Finally, we validate our framework on physical hardware, demonstrating hybrid motion-force control on a redundant manipulator and compliant locomotion on a quadruped, while preserving core NMPC advantages such as hard constraint satisfaction, optimal redundancy exploitation, and real-time planning under unforeseen interactions.

Tube-Based Robust Control Strategy for Vision-Guided Autonomous Vehicles 2026-10-12
📝 15 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

A robust control strategy for autonomous vehicles can improve system stability, enhance riding comfort, and prevent driving accidents. This paper presents a novel interpolation-tubebased constrained iterative linear quadratic regulator (itubeCILQR) algorithm for autonomous computer-vision-based vehicle lane-keeping. The goal of the algorithm is to enhance robustness during high-speed cornering on tight turns. Compared with standard tube-based approaches, the proposed itube-CILQR algorithm reduces system conservatism and exhibits higher computational speed. Numerical simulations and vision-based experiments were conducted to examine the feasibility of using the proposed algorithm for controlling autonomous vehicles. PRB-WM: Physics-Regularized Beta World Models for Predictive Link Adaptation on Real Time-Varying Underwater Acoustic Channels 2026-10-12
📝 Submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. 15 pages, 13 figures, 3 tables, 2 algorithms
Show abstract

Adaptive modulation and coding (AMC) improves the spectral efficiency of underwater acoustic (UWA) links, but its feedback is inevitably stale: sound propagates slowly, and by the time channel state information (CSI) arrives, the fast time-varying multipath channel has moved on. Predictive link adaptation therefore needs more than a point forecast: it needs a distribution over each candidate action's consequences. We propose PRB-WM, a physics-regularized Beta world model for predictive per-subcarrier link adaptation on real time-varying UWA channels. A persistence-anchored stochastic latent-space dynamics model learns how the channel evolves; a counterfactual outcome head evaluates, for every candidate modulation and coding scheme (MCS), a Beta-binomial distribution of bit errors on shared imagined futures; and a chance-constrained receding-horizon model predictive controller maximizes expected spectral efficiency subject to a bounded probability of violating the bit error rate (BER) target. To the best of our knowledge, PRB-WM is the first world model for UWA communications. In closed-loop replay of six measured sea-trial links from the public UWA Channel Library against a 24-method pool spanning the full methodological spectrum, PRB-WM attains a mean goodput 87% above the strongest baseline, ranks first on all six time-varying links at the longest lookahead, and is the only method whose realized frame risk decreases systematically with lookahead, meeting the BER-violation contract where no baseline does. Ablations confirm that rollout sampling dispersion is essential to the chance constraint and that the physics anchor is necessary for trainability.

Learning Reachability-Based Safety Representations for Real-Time Robot Navigation in Unknown and Dynamic Environments 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Safe robot navigation in unknown, dynamic environments requires more than geometric collision checking: a robot still far from an obstacle may be unable to avoid it because of its speed, orientation, or the obstacle's motion. Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis captures this in a value function whose sign separates states that admit a safe continuation from those where collision is unavoidable, but computing it online for every newly observed environment is too expensive. This dissertation trains neural implicit representations offline on reachability solutions to give optimization-based controllers this information in real time. Since the value function never exceeds the constraint, it equals a directly computed constraint, such as a signed distance, minus a nonnegative dynamics correction. Learning only this correction keeps represented obstacles outside the predicted safe set despite approximation errors. The first two of five methods use the learned function as a terminal constraint in model predictive control, for static local environments and predicted obstacle motion. The next two use it as a barrier candidate in control barrier function safety filters, for static environments and, through pairwise barriers in relative coordinates, for a varying number of moving obstacles. Simulations and hardware experiments on ground and aerial robots show better collision avoidance than geometric and hand-designed baselines, especially for short horizons and moving obstacles. The fifth method replaces the grid-based offline solver, which limits the first four to low-dimensional models. For control-affine dynamics with polytopic inputs, the HJ Hamiltonian is maximized at a vertex, so a parallelizable tree search over vertex inputs can generate training labels. With a loss from the HJ variational inequality, these labels yield barrier candidates for systems with up to 13 states.

Imitation Learning of Avoidance Modes for Efficient Multimodal Model Predictive Control 2026-10-12
📝 This work has been submitted to an Elsevier journal for possible publication
Show abstract

Multimodal model predictive control (MM-MPC) avoids local minima among moving obstacles by solving one optimal control problem (OCP) per combination of avoidance sides, so its computational cost grows exponentially with the number of decision-critical obstacles. We propose SideNet-MPC, which replaces this enumeration with a mode decision learned by imitating MM-MPC as an expert. A graph neural network is trained to predict the expert's minimum-cost mode, one avoidance side per obstacle. Its edges encode, for each obstacle pair, the gap predicted for the moment the vehicle first reaches either obstacle, so a neighbor that blocks a preferred passage can reverse the proposed side. The expert's selection rule compares its minimum-cost mode with the previously executed mode; SideNet-MPC keeps this rule and substitutes the network proposal for the minimum-cost mode. Hence at most two OCPs are solved per control period, and every applied input still comes from an OCP with control barrier function constraints rather than from the network. In $100$ seed-matched closed-loop Monte Carlo runs of mixed traffic, SideNet-MPC reaches $93\%$ of the expert's success rate and needs \SI{18.6}{ms} per control period on a single thread, 48\% less than the expert's \SI{35.5}{ms} on sixteen parallel threads. In a controlled gap sweep, its proposed side reverses in the same gap interval as the expert's, whereas proposers without pairwise edges, trained on the same data, never reverse and all deadlock at the blocked gates of a deterministic scene.

Geometry-Aware Predictive Control for Walk-to-Run Bipedal Obstacle Crossing 2026-10-11
Show abstract

This paper presents a geometry-aware predictive control framework for bipedal obstacle crossing across walking and running regimes. The proposed approach integrates a phase-structured trajectory generator with a constrained model predictive control (MPC) tracking layer to generate coordinated pelvis and swing-foot motions during obstacle traversal. The crossing process is represented through five interpretable phases: pre-crossing, leading-leg clearance, straddle, trailing-leg clearance, and recovery. Within this structure, swing-foot elevation is adapted as a function of obstacle height, while lateral stance widening and pelvis repositioning are introduced as functions of obstacle depth to improve transverse clearance and body alignment. A continuous gait-parameter evolution enables smooth walk-to-run transition without discrete controller switching. The generated references are tracked using MPC to regulate motion smoothness, clearance feasibility, touchdown behavior, and lateral admissibility. The framework is evaluated in reduced-order simulation over five progressively difficult obstacles under walking and running conditions. The robot successfully crosses the first four obstacles, achieving minimum body-obstacle clearances of 0.036, 0.036, 0.059, and 0.017 m, respectively. The fifth and most demanding obstacle leads to collision, identifying the current operating boundary of the proposed reduced-order formulation. Scenario-based MPC is an attractive strategy for chance-constrained motion planning that approximates uncertainty via a finite set of sampled scenarios. As a sampling-based method, scenario-based MPC is sensitive to distribution mismatch. We address this problem in the context of Safe-Horizon Model Predictive Control (SH-MPC) with obstacles governed by switching dynamic modes. From finite mode observations, we construct a confidence set for the unknown categorical mode law and derive a multiplicative domination bound that transfers a Safe-Horizon collision-risk certificate from a selected scenario-sampling distribution to every law in the confidence set. Wasserstein geometry is used to regularize probability reallocation among modes according to the similarity of their induced trajectory predictions, while a collision-risk surrogate biases sampling toward dangerous modes. Robust Nonprehensile Object Transport with Quadruped Robots 2026-10-05

In this paper, we present a robust nonprehensile object transportation framework for quadruped robots. An uncertainty-aware trajectory optimization method generates object motions with minimal closed-loop sensitivity to uncertain parameters. The resulting reference trajectory is tracked using a coupled convex model predictive controller that jointly predicts the CoM dynamics of the quadruped and the payload followed by a whole-body QP that enforces ground reaction constraints. The approach is evaluated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments under variations in the object's inertial parameters. Its performance is compared with fixed-orientation and straight-line trajectories as baseline. The results show that the optimized object motion reduces the sliding by approximately 50% compared with the fixed-orientation baseline and 30% compared with the straight-line baseline, while also achieving lower robot CoM tracking errors.

Time-series Foundation Models for Predictive Control: The Role of Excitation 2026-10-05
📝 Accepted at the TS-LIMITS Workshop at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Deploying model predictive control (MPC) requires constructing or identifying a predictive model for each target system. Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) offer an attractive option thanks to strong zero-shot forecasting capabilities across systems. However, low forecast error does not guarantee that a TSFM captures the system's response to the alternative actions considered by the controller. We study this gap using residential heat-pump control as a test bed, measuring the agreement between predicted and ground-truth effects of control interventions. Importantly, we find that TSFMs can recover the system's input-response relationship when the context contains sufficient independent control excitation. Common fine-tuning pipelines and feature smoothing reduce, but do not eliminate, the need for in-context excitation. Our results indicate that current TSFMs used for predictive control require sufficiently informative control variation in the inference context. Initial closed-loop results show promise for shorter context windows.

Learning Over-Relaxation Policies for ADMM with Convergence Guarantees 2026-10-05
Show abstract

The Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) is a widely used method for structured convex optimization, and its practical performance depends strongly on the choice of penalty and relaxation parameters. Motivated by settings such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), where one repeatedly solves related optimization problems with fixed structure and changing parameter values, we propose learning online updates of the relaxation parameter to improve average performance on problem classes of interest, while guaranteeing that asymptotic convergence is not compromised for the worst-case realization of such problems. This choice is computationally attractive in the Operator Splitting Quadratic Program (OSQP)-like architectures, since adapting relaxation does not trigger the matrix refactorizations associated with penalty updates. We establish convergence guarantees for ADMM with time-varying penalty and relaxation parameters under mild assumptions, and show on benchmark quadratic programs that the resulting learned policies improve both iteration count and wall-clock time on average over baseline OSQP.

Structured Representation Learning for Behavior Cloning: How can we learn to safely control a nuclear power plant? 2026-10-05
Show abstract

Learned models for industrial control are usually judged by aggregate accuracy, but accuracy at the component level does not guarantee safety once it is embedded in the system it is meant to serve. We study this gap on a behavior-cloning task: imitating an expert Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) policy for load-following of a Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR), an industrial system with tight safety constraints. We propose a structured architecture encoding variables from each timescale into separate latent spaces, reflecting the physical decomposition of the system, before training a controller to imitate the expert on the product latent space. On long-horizon rollouts, separated embeddings improve both accuracy and feasibility compared with a shared-embedding baseline. Sensitivity analysis further shows that our model yields interpretable representations aligned with the system's physics. However, standalone deployment still leaves several percent of trajectories infeasible regardless of the architecture. Using our method to warmstart the NMPC optimizer rather than acting standalone, we recover full feasibility and near-optimal cost while still cutting computation time by $\sim$15% relative to the expert controller, and even more for abrupt operating changes.

Trajectory tracking performance of Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) degrades during an agile high-speed flight due to the depletion of the battery and subsequent loss of maximum available thrust. In applications such as drone racing, this leads to a failure to complete the race due to possible collisions with obstacles. In this paper, we present a novel method for integrating battery and propulsion system models into a Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) framework to enable real-time prediction of the voltage, current, power, and maximum available thrust of the platform. Our proposed approach achieves lower trajectory tracking error as a result of its real-time thrust awareness, and with the help of trajectory replanning, it allows the UAV to fly in time-optimal regime throughout the mission. The accuracy of this proposed model was verified in real-world flight experiments, while the effectiveness of the replanning algorithm was evaluated in simulation. By the end of the battery capacity, compared to an unaware controller, our novel controller achieved a 25% reduction in mean position error without replanning, and an 88 % reduction with replanning.

MagServo: Uncertainty-Resilient Hierarchical Magnetic Servoing via Learned Latent Representations 2026-10-05
Show abstract

Magnetic navigation provides contact-free and line-of-sight-independent feedback for robotic systems, yet existing approaches typically rely on explicit pose estimation or direct use of raw magnetic measurements, making accurate control susceptible to modeling errors, measurement noise, and disturbances. This work presents MagServo, a hierarchical learning-based framework for robust 6-DoF magnetic servoing directly using the learned latent magnetic feature. MagServo learns uncertainty-resilient magnetic representations through masked reconstruction and captures state-dependent interaction dynamics between robot motion and latent magnetic transitions without analytical magnetic models or explicit Jacobian supervision. Based on the learned dynamics, a hierarchical controller combines nonlinear model predictive control for coarse approach with local Jacobian inversion for precise fine regulation. Extensive physical experiments demonstrate submillimeter and subdegree accuracy, achieving mean terminal errors of 0.386 mm and 0.479 degree for 6-DoF pose reaching. MagServo further outperforms a localization-based control baseline in complex trajectory tracking and maintains robust performance under unseen magnetic-source configurations without retraining. Optimal Control with Learned Critics under Unmodeled State Dependencies 2026-10-04
📝 Keywords: Model Predictive Control, Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, iLQR, Value Function Approximation. Presented at 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026), Austin TX, USA
Show abstract

Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides a structured and constraint-aware mechanism for decision-making, but its reliance on optimization-friendly analytical dynamics models limits its use in tasks with contacts and other hard-to-model state dependencies. Model-free reinforcement learning avoids explicit modeling assumptions but typically requires large amounts of interaction data. We present a learning-based MPC framework that combines the data efficiency and structure of local model-based planning with learned components that compensate for incomplete dynamics and finite-horizon myopia. The method augments a nominal analytical model with a residual dynamics network that learns missing state-dependent effects from data and combines the resulting planner with a learned action-value critic that injects long-horizon MDP structure into the local iLQR optimization. To make this practical at reinforcement-learning scale, we develop a GPU-accelerated batched iLQR solver that evaluates learned dynamics and critic networks inside the optimal-control loop and solves thousands of trajectory-optimization problems in parallel. The complete system is integrated into a robotics simulator, enabling scalable model-based reinforcement learning under incomplete dynamics. Experiments on biased and incompletely modeled control tasks show that the approach improves closed-loop control performance while preserving the model-based structure needed for efficient constrained trajectory optimization.

RVC-NMPC: Nonlinear Model Predictive Control with Reciprocal Velocity Constraints for Mutual Collision Avoidance in Agile UAV Flight 2026-10-04

This paper presents an approach to mutual collision avoidance based on Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) with time-dependent Reciprocal Velocity Constraints (RVCs). Unlike most existing methods, the proposed approach relies solely on observable information about other robots, eliminating the need for excessive communication. The computationally efficient algorithm for computing RVCs, together with the direct integration of these constraints into the NMPC problem formulation at the controller level, allows the whole pipeline to run at 100 Hz. This high processing rate, combined with modeled nonlinear dynamics of the controlled Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), is a key feature that facilitates the use of the proposed approach for agile UAV flight. The proposed approach was evaluated through extensive simulations emulating real-world conditions in scenarios involving up to 10 UAVs and velocities of up to 25 m/s, and in real-world experiments with accelerations up to 30 m/s$^2$. Comparison with the state of the art shows 31% improvement in terms of flight time reduction in challenging scenarios, while maintaining a collision-free navigation in all trials.

A Unified Dynamics Framework for Reinforcement Learning and Classical Control of a Six-DOF Pipeline-Tracking ROV in NVIDIA Isaac Sim 2026-10-04
📝 26 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning controllers for underwater vehicles are usually trained against one physics representation and deployed against another, so reported performance does not always describe behavior outside training. This paper presents a pipeline-tracking architecture for a six-degree-of-freedom remotely operated vehicle (ROV) in which one Universal Scene Description (USD) scene supplies the real BlueROV2-Heavy mass, added-mass, damping, buoyancy, and thruster parameters to both halves of the system: a vectorized NumPy implementation of Fossen's marine-craft equations, and an interactive NVIDIA Isaac Sim deployment applying the identical equations as PhysX forces at every step. The Coriolis-centripetal term is the primary dynamics model in both branches; a controlled ablation on PPO and TRPO shows that including it does not destabilize either algorithm and modestly improves tracking, about 19 percent lower standoff RMS error for TRPO. Five reinforcement learning algorithms, PPO, soft actor-critic, TD3, DDPG, and TRPO, are trained against one environment, reward, and randomized evaluation harness through a checkpoint-compatibility layer scoring any policy with the same code. The pipeline is extended with six classical baselines, PID, sliding-mode, fuzzy logic, feedback linearization, model predictive control, and an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system, driven by the same guidance geometry and thruster allocation as the learned policies. Under Coriolis-enabled dynamics, PPO, TRPO, and feedback linearization reach the strongest combination of 100 percent success and competitive accuracy; PID, fuzzy control, and the neuro-fuzzy baseline also reach 100 percent success with looser tracking; DDPG and TD3 each show a specific, explainable failure mode rather than a general weakness of off-policy learning; and classical control remains a strong baseline against the best learned policies.
