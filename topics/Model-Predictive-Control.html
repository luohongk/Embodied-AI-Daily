$μ$MPC: Microsecond-Scale Nonlinear and World Model Control from Edge to GPU via Koopman-Linearized Precompilation 2026-11-25
📝 9 pages, 4 figures. Project Website: https://www.deveshnath.com/projects/micrompc/
Show abstract

We present $μ$MPC, a fast model predictive control (MPC) framework that reduces online computation using learned Koopman-linearized dynamics. By precomputing finite-horizon control-to-output maps, $μ$MPC eliminates lifted-state trajectories, yielding an action-only Koopman-Compiled QP (KC-QP) with $Hn_u$ decision variables, independent of lifted dimension. A fixed, precomputed primal operator enables efficient primal-dual iterations using only matrix-vector products and projections, accommodating changing goals and constraints without refactorization. This efficiency also enables control-aware dynamics learning through differentiable KC-QPs in the training loop and batched DAgger-style data aggregation. $μ$MPC achieves solve times as low as $23μ$s on a desktop CPU and 24 ms onboard a 168 MHz Crazyflie microcontroller. It scales to 4096D quadruped control in under 5 ms, visual planning with 2048D world models in 0.4 ms, and collision-aware planning for 200 quadrotors at over 30 Hz on the GPU. Across these benchmarks, $μ$MPC achieves up to $108\times$ faster constrained MPC and $1907\times$ faster visuomotor planning than competing methods.

Reference-Model-Guided Distributed MPC for UAV Swarm Formation Flying Under Switching Directed Graphs 2026-11-25
📝 14 pages, 13 figures
Show abstract

Distributed formation control of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms in complex environments faces several challenges, including switching inter-agent connectivity, limited communication, external disturbances, and collision avoidance. To address these challenges, we propose a reference-model-guided distributed model predictive control (MPC) framework for UAV swarm formation. Each UAV uses relative-position information from its neighbors as input to a local reference model, whose output serves as the formation guidance for the onboard MPC controller. Meanwhile, a Kalman filter is utilized to predict neighboring UAV motions, enabling the construction of inter-UAV collision-avoidance constraints solely from instantaneous relative-state information. By decoupling MPC optimization from distributed coordination, this control architecture preserves the look-ahead planning and constraint-handling capabilities of MPC while enhancing the swarm's adaptability to switching directed graphs. Moreover, eliminating the exchange of neighbors' predicted trajectories reduces communication bandwidth demands, offering strong scalability for large-scale UAV swarms. To improve robustness and safety, disturbance-observer-based feedforward compensation is employed, while the bounded residual disturbance is explicitly considered in the control barrier function (CBF)-based obstacle-avoidance constraints. Finally, the proposed framework is validated through both simulations and real-world flight experiments. Specifically, the flight verification is conducted on a fully distributed multi-UAV platform without external global localization.

Decision-Focused Learning for Multistage Stochastic Programs, with Application to Power Network Repair Scheduling 2026-11-25
📝 Code and data: https://github.com/DavidKrame/DFL_for_MSP__Application_to_power_network_repair
Show abstract

Multistage stochastic programs (MSPs) with discrete decisions are hard to solve due to the curse of dimensionality, non-convex value functions, and scenario trees that grow exponentially with the horizon. We develop a decision-focused learning (DFL) framework for MSPs in which a neural network predicts scores for the part of the decision that drives the state transition. Under structural conditions that make this sub-decision enter the stage objective linearly, the network is trained on labels from offline single-scenario problems, using the Fenchel-Young loss with perturbed optimizers, which need only an optimization oracle. We apply the framework to repair scheduling in power networks under stochastic, time-varying component failures, with and without a per-stage repair budget. On a synthetic 5-bus network and on the IEEE 14-bus network at two repair-cost scales, the learned and baseline policies are compared on common evaluation paths. Without the budget, DFL is the cheapest policy on the 5-bus network; on the 14-bus network it does not outperform a near-sighted model-predictive control (MPC) baseline, but it beats repairing every failed component at the higher scale. Under a binding budget, DFL is the cheapest policy evaluated on both networks and at both scales, 14% to 23% below the next-best policy. On the 5-bus network, the mean cost of DFL is 1.7% (without the budget) and 1.8% (with it) above the exact optimum computed by dynamic programming. Within days of computation, sample average approximation yields costlier policies than DFL and the repair-everything heuristic, and stochastic dual dynamic integer programming remains far from convergence. DFL computes its decisions about as fast as the trivial heuristics and orders of magnitude faster than MPC. Since these conditions are not specific to power networks, the framework extends to other MSPs that share this structure.

Should Service Requirements Be Scalarized or Constrained When Minimizing Costs? A Case Study on HVAC Control 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Automated decision systems, from learned policies to model-based optimizers, often minimize costs such as energy or resource use while meeting a service requirement, for example the quality of service (QoS) specified in a service level agreement (SLA), a comfort band, or a safety margin. The requirement can be scalarized with the costs into a single objective, with a fixed or an adaptively tuned (Lagrangian) weight, or it can be imposed as a constraint. We study this choice in the Smart Buildings Challenge, in which a single policy controls the heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) of previously unseen buildings and is evaluated by a weighted sum of comfort, energy cost and carbon emissions. With all other components fixed, we compare the two treatments in model-based optimization, represented by model predictive control (MPC) with the requirement as a hard or a soft (l1-penalized) constraint, and in learned policies, represented by proximal policy optimization (PPO) with a fixed or a Lagrangian penalty in the reward or a projection layer on the action. In MPC, the soft constraint reproduces the hard one once its weight exceeds the requirement's shadow price, which the challenge's comfort weight exceeds approximately fourfold; below this weight, comfort is sacrificed, and under a metric that values comfort below its shadow price the soft constraint outperforms the hard one by up to 28%. A weight tuned on the training buildings is 3.9% worse than the hard constraint on 48 unseen buildings, because the shadow price varies across buildings. In PPO, the projected policy satisfies the requirement from the outset and leads early in training; with longer training, the Lagrangian policy overtakes it while the projected policy deteriorates, and the fixed penalty does not learn to satisfy the requirement.

Model Predictive Control of a Multirotor UAV with a Cable-Suspended Payload: A Comparison of Shifted and Transformer-Based Warm Starts 2026-11-24
📝 13 pages, 11 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

This study investigates model predictive control (MPC) of a multirotor drone (unmanned aerial vehicle, UAV) carrying a cable-suspended payload, comparing conventional shifted initialization with Transformer-based warm starting of a sequential convex programming (SCP) optimizer. The controller uses a coupled mechanical model with an elastic cable and a double-pendulum representation of the suspended load. Its formulation includes move-blocked control inputs, finite-difference sensitivities, constrained quadratic subproblems, and input-feasibility projection. A Transformer trained on expert SCP solutions predicts an initial control sequence, which is subsequently refined by the same optimizer used for the conventional methods. Numerical experiments compare zero, shifted, and Transformer initialization under identical controller settings. For independently solved MPC problems, Transformer initialization reduces computation time relative to zero initialization. In sequential closed-loop simulations, however, shifting the preceding optimized control sequence provides the lowest computation time, while all three methods produce similar tracking behavior. These findings emphasize the importance of comparing learned warm starts with conventional initialization methods that reuse previous MPC solutions. The measured optimization times exceed the control sampling period; consequently, real-time implementation is not demonstrated. Keywords: Model predictive control; sequential convex programming; Transformer; warm start; cable-suspended payload; multirotor drone.

Reliability-aware AI Agent Execution in Collaborative Edge Computing: A World Model Approach 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Collaborative edge computing supports AI agent applications by distributing execution across heterogeneous user-controlled devices with limited resources. This paper studies reliability-aware AI agent execution under node failures and wireless disruptions. These disruptions can make agent execution state unavailable or inconsistent, forcing recovery or recomputation that consumes the remaining deadline budget. Maintaining recoverable execution states can therefore improve request completion before deadlines. We formulate an online AI agent execution management problem that jointly selects resource allocations, state protection actions, and recovery targets to maximize the expected task success rate subject to resource, compatibility, and deadline constraints. We develop a world model that jointly captures evolving agent workflows and recoverable execution state under candidate management actions, accounting for node reliability, network conditions, and resource availability. The model enables the controller to simulate future execution and recovery trajectories under candidate actions and estimate their effects on execution progress and task completion before deadlines. We then employ world model-based model predictive control (WorldMPC) with Monte Carlo rollouts to jointly optimize resource allocation, state protection, and recovery decisions for long-term reliable agent execution under the dynamic environment. We evaluate the proposed approach on the simulation and real-world testbed using real-world and synthetic datasets. Experimental results show that WorldMPC improves task success rate over baselines in both simulation and real-world testbed.

MRCT: An Object-Centric Two-Layer Control Architecture for Heavy-Payload Multi-Robot Transportation 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Cooperative transportation of heavy payloads by heterogeneous mobile manipulators requires coordinated payload motion, force distribution, and compliant physical interaction. This paper presents MRCT, an object-centric two-layer control architecture for multi-robot transportation. At the object level, a model predictive controller (MPC) generates a coordinated contact-force reference for the payload. At the robot level, whole- body impedance control (WBIC) modules on individual robots realise the reference through a confidence-based blending of velocity-tracking and impedance torques. The blending mechanism emphasises tracking when the joint-velocity error is small and increases compliance when tracking degrades because of abrupt reference changes or physical disturbances. Simulation and hardware experiments with a heterogeneous team comprising a single-arm and a dual-arm mobile manipulator transporting a near-rated table payload demonstrate accurate object tracking, regulated contact forces, and stable payload orientation. Parameter sensitivity studies, obstacle-avoidance transportation, and step-load disturbance experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness of MRCT for reliable cooperative transportation under the evaluated conditions

Localization-Uncertainty-Aware UAV Swarm Search for GNSS-Denied Maritime Surveillance 2026-11-24
📝 13 pages
Show abstract

This paper studies UAV swarm search in GNSS-denied maritime environments, where inter-UAV ranging drives cooperative localization and uncertainty propagates along the multi-hop reference chain, affecting connectivity, collision avoidance, and search effectiveness. We propose a control barrier function (CBF) framework using split covariance intersection (SCI) to fuse neighbor ranges and generate covariance-derived uncertainty scales for mapping and control. Pairwise constraints incorporate inter-step relative displacement to establish continuous-time safe-set invariance across discrete control steps under stated containment, feasibility, and synchronization conditions. A hierarchical Voronoi-target strategy with formation keeping supports efficient forward-looking coverage. Sequential local quadratic programs using preceding-UAV commands complete a 14-UAV search in 221.1 s with 0.45 m root-mean-square localization error while satisfying all separation and reference bounds. Comparisons across search regions, swarm sizes, sampled robust CBF, localizability and formation targets, and model predictive control quantify speed-accuracy-margin trade-offs. Tests on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX, a typical UAV onboard computer, verify sub-4 ms mean control cycles and sub-5 ms mean full per-UAV onboard cycles, while Gazebo quadrotor flights verify mission completion and constraint compliance under over-water multipath, beacon-UAV drift, wind turbulence, yaw errors, and communication faults.

Learning Legged MPC with Smooth Neural Surrogates 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Deep learning and model predictive control (MPC) can play complementary roles in legged robotics. However, integrating learned models with online planning remains challenging. When dynamics are learned with neural networks, three key difficulties arise: (1) stiff transitions from contact events may be inherited from the data; (2) additional non-physical local nonsmoothness can occur; and (3) training datasets can induce non-Gaussian model errors due to rapid state changes. We address (1) and (2) by introducing the smooth neural surrogate, a neural network with tunable smoothness designed to provide informative predictions and derivatives for trajectory optimization through contact. To address (3), we train these models using a heavy-tailed likelihood that better matches the empirical error distributions observed in legged-robot dynamics. Together, these design choices substantially improve the reliability, scalability, and generalizability of learned legged MPC. Across zero-shot locomotion tasks of increasing difficulty, smooth neural surrogates with robust learning yield consistent reductions in cumulative cost on simple, well-conditioned behaviors (typically 10-50%), while providing substantially larger gains in regimes where standard neural dynamics often fail outright. In these regimes, smoothing enables reliable execution (from 0/5 to 5/5 success) and produces about 2-50x lower cumulative cost, reflecting orders-of-magnitude absolute improvements in robustness rather than incremental performance gains.

Geometric Control Analysis of an Internally Actuated Flying Wing 2026-11-23
📝 Submitted to IEEE TCST for review
Show abstract

This paper presents a rigorous geometric accessibility and controllability analysis for a fixed-wing Uncrewed Aircraft System (UAS) actuated solely by an internal moving mass, a momentum wheel, and external thrust. This configuration eliminates traditional control surfaces, which in turn reduces the combinatorial set of computational fluid dynamics (CFD) studies necessary for high-fidelity modeling of flight through unsteady flow fields. Using Lie bracket computations and the strong Lie Algebra Rank Condition, it is shown that the full nonlinear dynamics are generically small-time locally accessible, with singular configurations explicitly characterized and shown to be immediately escapable by direct mass actuation. The stronger property of small-time local controllability is shown to hold for the attitude subsystem at equilibrium points, using Sussmann's condition. A key contribution is the explicit treatment of the time-varying inertia tensor via the Huygens-Steiner Theorem, which introduces nontrivial coupling between the control vector fields. The momentum wheel provides direct yaw authority that, combined with inertia variation from the moving mass, generates a full-rank accessibility Lie algebra. A nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (MPC) is derived with consideration of flight envelope constraints and is applied to track different trajectories under unperturbed and perturbed flow field conditions.

DexLatentWorld: 3D Cross-Embodiment Latent Action World Models for Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-23
📝 15 pages, including supplementary material
Show abstract

World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Project materials, including demonstration videos and source code, are available at https://vinaymohan0409.github.io/DexLatentWorld/.

Autonomous UAV Navigation in an Unknown Environment via Multi-Trajectory Model Predictive Control 2026-11-23
Show abstract

A novel model predictive control (MPC) formulation, named multi-trajectory MPC (mt-MPC), is presented and applied to the problem of autonomous navigation of an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) in an unknown environment. The UAV is equipped with a LiDAR sensor, providing only a partial description of the surroundings and resulting in time-varying constraints as the vehicle navigates among the obstacles. The control system layout is hierarchical: the low-level loops stabilize the vehicle's trajectories and track the set-points commanded by the high-level, mt-MPC controller. The latter is required to plan the UAV trajectory trading off safety, i.e. to avoid collisions with the uncertain obstacles, and exploitation, i.e. to reach an assigned target location. To achieve this goal, mt-MPC considers different future state trajectories in the same Finite Horizon Optimal Control Problem (FHOCP), enabling a partial decoupling between constraint satisfaction (safety) and cost function minimization (exploitation). Recursive feasibility and, consequently, persistent obstacle avoidance guarantees are derived under the assumption of a time invariant environment. The performance of the approach is studied in simulation and compared with that of a standard MPC, showing good improvement.

Resource-Aware VLA-MPC Co-Location for Efficient Embodied AI 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Recent advances in embodied artificial intelligence (EAI) have unleashed new potential in many frontiers. Current EAI systems often combine vision-language-action (VLA) inference with high frequency model predictive control (MPC). On mobile GPUs, these workloads are typically serialized, causing control deadline misses. We present Dubot, a framework that automatically schedules VLA inference and MPC on a single mobile GPU. Specifically, Dubot identifies concurrent opportunities and proposes three co-location mechanisms: spatial partitioning, block-level co-location, and intra-block fusion. By leveraging different mechanisms, Dubot enables fine-grained co-location to improve the efficiency of various VLA-MPC operator pairs. Dubot further proposes a co-location optimizer that can determine the optimal co-location schedule under workload dependency, QoS requirements, and hardware-resource constraints. Across representative VLA inference paradigms on NVIDIA Jetson Orin and Thor, Dubot achieves up to 1.4x end-to-end speedup and reduces QoS violations by up to 38%.

In self-adaptive software systems, the role of context is paramount, especially for proactive self-adaptation. Performance Evaluation of Nonlinear Model Predictive and Sliding Mode Controllers on a 4-Cable Spatial CSPR in Simscape 2026-11-22
📝 7 pages, 13 figures, 2 tables. Prepared for the 13th RSI International Conference on Robotics and Mechatronics (ICRoM 2025), Tehran, Iran
Show abstract

This paper offers a comprehensive comparative study between Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) and Sliding Mode Control (SMC) applied to a spatial Cable-Suspended Parallel Robot (CSPR) actuated by four cables. The robot is modeled in two parallel ways: (i) via an analytical dynamic model with included cable elasticity and frictional losses, and (ii) in MATLAB Simscape Multibody, capturing realistic physical effects. Both controllers are implemented under identical reference trajectories, system constraints, and tuning strategies. Our results show that NMPC achieves superior tracking accuracy and stronger disturbance rejection compared to SMC, but at the cost of higher computational demand. Moreover, both controllers were tested on an analytical model of the robot and a Simscape model. The close agreement between the two, for both control schemes, confirms the accuracy of the analytical formulation and its consistency with the physics-based simulation. The study highlights the value of physics-based simulation as a complementary tool for validating advanced controllers under realistic conditions, and demonstrates that NMPC offers superior performance for the studied high-precision, constrained CSPR.

JEPA-Shield: Safe Diffusion Planning with Latent World Models via Conformal Shielding 2026-11-22
📝 Project page: https://www.taekyung.me/jepa-shield
Show abstract

Latent world models enable planning from images, but their predictions do not directly establish whether a robot will satisfy physical constraints. We present JEPA-Shield, which connects a frozen action-conditioned Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world model to task-space safety enforcement. Two downstream models predict relative motion from imagined latent rollouts and decode unsafe geometry from the current image. Split conformal prediction turns end-to-end motion-prediction errors into multi-horizon uncertainty tubes. A backup-based shield checks these tubes against physical constraints and screens both candidate trajectories and final proposals in diffusion-inspired sampling-based model predictive control. Under the stated exchangeability and initial-backup assumptions, we establish probabilistic constraint satisfaction at action-block boundaries for a returned plan executed without replanning. We also introduce VParkBench, with geometric car and tractor-trailer parking tasks and a editable photorealistic Gaussian-splatting environment. In closed-loop evaluation, JEPA-Shield achieves success rates of 84.4%, 82.3%, and 99.0%, respectively, with no observed constraint violations. Ablations show complementary benefits from shielding and uncertainty calibration. The results demonstrate the importance of grounding latent world-model predictions in task-space quantities and explicitly accounting for prediction uncertainty in safety-critical planning.

Teleoperator-Aware and CBF-Based Adaptive Nonlinear MPC for Shared Autonomy in Obstacle Avoidance of Legged Robots 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Safe and effective collaboration between humans and autonomous legged robots is a fundamental challenge in shared autonomy, particularly for teleoperated systems navigating cluttered environments. Shared autonomy for legged robots remains challenging due to uncertain human commands, collision-avoidance requirements, and the need to coordinate high-level planning with dynamically feasible locomotion. This paper presents a teleoperator-aware, control barrier function (CBF)-based, adaptive nonlinear model predictive control (ANMPC) framework for shared autonomy of quadrupedal robots in obstacle-avoidance tasks. The framework employs a prescribed arbitration weight between human and robot actions while modeling human inputs using a noisily rational Boltzmann model, whose parameters are adapted online through a projected gradient descent (PGD) law based on observed joystick commands. CBF constraints are incorporated into a computationally efficient NMPC formulation to promote obstacle avoidance while accounting for predicted human actions. The hierarchical architecture integrates high-level CBF-based ANMPC (10 Hz), mid-level dynamics-aware NMPC (60 Hz) enforcing single rigid body (SRB) dynamics, and low-level nonlinear whole-body control (500 Hz). Human-in-the-loop hardware experiments with ten participants on a Unitree Go2 quadruped demonstrate real-time feasibility and collision-free navigation across 50 trials. Shared autonomy reduced average human control effort by 51% and joystick activity from 88% to 42% of trial time, with comparable obstacle clearance and significantly higher perceived assistance than manual operation. Simulation-based ablation studies demonstrate improved obstacle clearance across operator rationality levels and up to a 66% reduction in median human-action prediction error through online adaptation.

Learned Network Dynamics for Model Predictive Traffic Signal Control under Incidents 2026-11-20
📝 13 pages main manuscript, 26 pages supplementary material (39 pages total)
Show abstract

Model predictive control (MPC) of urban traffic signals relies on a model of how queues respond to signal decisions, which is hard to specify and calibrate in heterogeneous networks, particularly when incidents change capacities and turning shares. Measurements add a second limitation: the controlled lanes at the stop line are often short, so queues extending upstream of them fall outside lane-level measurements. We propose GNN-MPC, a receding-horizon controller that plans with action-conditioned network dynamics learned by graph neural networks. Phase sequences are coordinated across intersections by approximate best responses, with an acceptance test that keeps the predicted cost no higher than that of the initial plan. In microscopic simulation under non-recurrent incidents, GNN-MPC reduces travel time and increases completed trips relative to all tested controllers, on a homogeneous grid and a heterogeneous 21-intersection network. Controlled comparisons on the heterogeneous network distinguish the roles of observation and prediction. With nearly as many vehicles entering the network, GNN-MPC reduces travel time by 13% relative to MaxPressure with the same upstream measurement, and GNN-MPC without the upstream representation does not reproduce its performance. Under the same traffic measurements, planner and objective, replacing the learned dynamics with calibrated store-and-forward predictors increases travel time by 31-34%. Observing hidden demand and predicting its response to control are thus complementary requirements of network-level predictive control.

Acceleration of Sparse and Irregular Workloads through Energy-Efficient Stream Extensions 2026-11-20
📝 Doctoral Thesis; abstract abridged to fit arXiv character limit
Show abstract

The slowdown of Moore's law and Dennard scaling has motivated computer architects to uphold performance scaling through architectural specialization. So far, most efforts have focused on highly regular workloads, which exhibit good data locality, ample reuse, and a data-independent execution flow. As this specialization hits diminishing returns, attention shifts towards sparse and irregular workloads, which are essential to applications like sparse neural networks, multiphysics simulations, and graph analytics. However, they lack the convenient properties of highly regular compute that modern architectures are optimized for, resulting in very low (<10%) functional unit utilization on traditional CPUs and GPUs. The most promising approaches to their general hardware acceleration leverage memory streams, which decouple data movement from execution. In this thesis, we explore energy-efficient hardware stream extensions that significantly accelerate sparse and irregular workloads in general-purpose systems without specializing to specific application domains or compromising on regular-workload performance. We design a flexible, core-coupled stream extension supporting affine and indirect streams as well as intersection and union of sparse tensors. We integrate it into an energy-efficient RISC-V compute cluster, achieving significant performance and energy efficiency benefits on general sparse linear algebra, and further use it to accelerate stencil computations, spiking neural network inference, and model-predictive control. We evaluate it at scale in a 432-core 2.5D RISC-V system fabricated in 12nm FinFET, achieving leading compute utilization and energy efficiency on sparse and irregular workloads while remaining competitive on regular workloads. Finally, we extend chip-level interconnects with end-to-end irregular streams, improving performance, energy efficiency, and bus utilization.

Should Belgians Install an Advanced Energy Management System in Their Electrified Dwelling ? 2026-11-19
📝 Full version including appendices A-C, which are not part of the 11-page proceedings version. Published at ACM BuildSys '26 under CC BY 4.0. 17 pages, 10 figures, 13 tables. Data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18417982
Show abstract

As dwellings electrify and more complex electricity pricing spreads in Belgium, the question arises: What are the potential benefits of installing an advanced energy management systems in an electrified Belgian dwelling ? To answer this question, we simulate 500 representative Belgian dwellings in which fossil systems are replaced by controllable electric technologies: space heating via air-to-air heat pumps, domestic hot water via electric boilers, and private transport via electric vehicles with home chargers. Photovoltaic systems are assigned according to current adoption rates. We evaluate two advanced energy management systems (EMS): model predictive control (MPC) and TreeC, a machine learning method using decision trees to model the EMS strategy. These are compared to a rule-based controller (RBC) that mimics default operation and an MPC with perfect forecasts (MPC P) as an upper bound. Performance for these EMSs is assessed for 2023 under a real contract with hourly prices and a monthly peak tariff. Average annual savings versus RBC are 82.5 EUR, 116.6 EUR, and 177.4 EUR for TreeC, MPC, and MPC P, respectively. All EMSs deliver similar thermal comfort (26-31 Kh). MPC saves more, while TreeC achieves higher EV charge completion (93.3% vs 88.8% for MPC, with a max charge of 95%) and a smoother aggregate load across the 500 dwellings. Savings remain modest relative to EMS purchase (280-680 EUR) and subscription (0-360 EUR/year) costs which is not favourable towards widespread adoption. The data and code to reproduce this study are available at https://github.com/EVERGi/should_belgians_install_advanced_ems_paper.

How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods 2026-11-19
📝 PhD thesis, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, defended 25 February 2026. 178 pages. Promotors: Maarten Messagie and Thierry Coosemans. Chapters 4-5 are based on Knowledge-Based Systems 309 (2025) 112756, doi:10.1016/j.knosys.2024.112756; chapter 6 on Energy and AI 18 (2024) 100448, doi:10.1016/j.egyai.2024.100448; chapter 7 on BuildSys '26, pp. 405-416, doi:10.1145/3744256.3812575
Show abstract

The transition from fossil fuels presents significant challenges in energy management. To ensure a sustainable energy future, it is crucial to reduce energy consumption when possible and to synchronize electricity consumption and storage with the production from sources with low greenhouse gas emissions. This thesis explores a variety of energy management methods used to manage batteries, heat pumps, and other energy-related systems. We study supported reaching to extend a humanoid robot's workspace by bracing against the environment for stabilization using a straightforward yet capable whole-body model predictive control (MPC) approach, exclusively with local sensing and fiducial markers. While typical solutions for whole body humanoid control aim at avoiding or accommodating contact, contact is explicitly required to reach objects placed on surfaces far out of normal reach. CS-MPC: Contraction-Certified Sampling-Based MPC for Nonlinear Systems Under Uncertainty 2026-11-13
Show abstract

We present contraction-certified sampling-based model predictive control (CS-MPC) for tracking and safety of nonlinear robotic systems under parametric uncertainty, disturbances, and safety constraints that are revealed online. CS-MPC uses a contraction-certified tracking feedback inside every sampled rollout, which concentrates the rollouts around the reference, and an execution layer that verifies the applied input using a quantile-tightened discrete-time control barrier function condition. We derive a verification procedure for nonlinear contraction of the feedback-closed dynamics, and we prove a finite-sample stochastic stability bound and a mission-level probabilistic safety guarantee. We validate CS-MPC in simulation on a rover, a quadrotor, and an asteroid landing. When compared with standard model predictive path integral (MPPI) control, CS-MPC attains the same tracking error with 7 to 20 times fewer rollouts for the rover, and a 2 to 4 times lower tracking error for the quadrotor, with no safety violations in 200 Monte Carlo missions.

Vision-language models (VLMs) have shown strong semantic and spatial reasoning capabilities in 3D environments. Recently, connecting VLMs with model-predictive controllers (MPC) has emerged as a paradigm for combining strong semantic reasoning with physics-based planning. However, connecting these two for contact-rich robotic manipulation requires a task representation that bridges the two and captures how physical interactions can be reasoned by VLMs. In this work, we introduce Objectives as Policies (OaP), a framework that connects a pretrained VLM to a shared sampling-based MPC through an editable objective program. Each program specifies a sequence of stages for completing a task, combining running and terminal costs from a cost library with stage completion conditions. Given a natural-language task instruction and scene observation, we build a simulation-based digital twin through real-to-sim, and the VLM generates an objective program that is executed by the MPC in the simulation. The program is iteratively updated by the VLM based on MPC rollouts, before being deployed in the real world. We evaluate our framework in both simulation and the real world on four tabletop manipulation tasks, and show that it outperforms strong baselines.
