A Tutorial on Physics Constrained Machine Learning for Hybrid Modeling, Optimization, and Control 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Physics-constrained machine learning (PCML) integrates domain knowledge, first-principles, and physical constraints into AI/ML models. In this work, we present a step-by-step tutorial of a modular PCML framework, KKT-HardNet, and illustrate how it could be used for hybrid modeling, process simulation, optimization and model predictive control in a single platform. KKT-HardNet combines fast inference capabilities of neural networks with guaranteed satisfaction of algebraic constraints through differential projection. It can handle heterogeneous data and enforce both linear and nonlinear equality and inequality constraints. The modular design supports custom neural-network backbones and user-defined projection mechanisms for PCML. To enable efficient end-to-end training, KKT-HardNet exploits implicit function theorem to backpropagate through the projection layer. This avoids computationally expensive automatic differentiation. This work also introduces an exact reduced-order Fischer-Burmeister reformulation and a primal-dual interior-point method as an alternative projection solve strategy. We also provide a diverse set of benchmarking problems for PCML in engineering applications. KKT-HardNet achieves orders of magnitude better performance in terms of both constraint satisfaction and inference time. The KKT-HardNet code and the benchmark datasets are available at https://github.com/souls-tamu/kkt-hardnet .

Metro-WM: Long-Horizon Latent Planning with Realisable Sub-Goals 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Model-predictive control with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) provides a strong zero-shot goal-reaching planner, but it is only effective over short planning horizons. Hierarchical extensions attempt to bridge this gap by learning a macro planner to predict intermediate latent sub-goals to guide the micro planner. In this work, we demonstrate that unconstrained latent sub-goal prediction is fundamentally flawed. A rigorous evaluation reveals that leading hierarchical macro planners routinely emit physically unrealisable sub-goals. To resolve this, we introduce Metro-WM, a hierarchical framework that issues sub-goals by retrieving genuine states from prior experience rather than generating ungrounded latent vectors. Specifically, Metro-WM constructs a graph whose vertices are observed frames from offline expert demonstrations or random-action trajectories, allowing frames from different episodes to be connected and stitched into routes to the goal. Planning over the full graph also makes the system highly robust to execution errors: if the micro planner drifts off course, Metro-WM instantly finds a new optimal path from the current state. Our experiments show that Metro-WM achieves superior long-horizon success rates of up to 37.33 percentage points over the strongest hierarchical baseline on the same world model while being up to 10.9 times faster, requiring both 13-56 times less offline compute and fewer tuned hyperparameters. Additional analysis reveals that Metro-WM frequently finds shorter paths than the offline demonstrations, outperforms an oracle relying on the query's own demonstration on most benchmarks, and maintains robust performance under extremely sparse dataset conditions.

Robust Offset-Free Lane-Keeping Control Framework for Vision-Only Automated Vehicles 2026-10-23
📝 13 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Achieving accurate, real-time lane-keeping control during high-speed cornering on large-curvature roads while ensuring robust vehicle stability is a critical challenge in autonomous driving. To address this challenge, this study developed a robust offset-free model predictive control (OF-MPC) framework for vision-based autonomous vehicle lane-keeping. The regulation problem in this framework is solved using several algorithms. Numerical simulation results indicated that the interpolation-tube-based constrained iterative linear quadratic regulator (itube-CILQR) algorithm outperformed solvers based on the standard Interior Point Optimizer (IPOPT) and Operator Splitting Quadratic Program (OSQP), achieving a mean computation time of less than 5 ms. The itube-CILQR algorithm was then used for vehicle control in vision-based lane-keeping experiments in a simulation environment to quantify the perceptual uncertainty of a visual perception model (multitask UNet). This quantification was performed using the OF-MPC framework for traffic scenes that were not encountered during model training. Overall, the developed OF-MPC framework and the proposed method for characterizing the perceptual uncertainty of a visual perception system in unseen environments can contribute to the future development of advanced autonomous driving systems.

Real-Time Disturbance-Aware Koopman Model Predictive Control for Quadrotors in Spatially Varying Winds 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Reliable quadrotor flight in disturbed environments requires predictive models that remain accurate as unmodeled aerodynamic effects change during deployment. We introduce a disturbance-aware Koopman Control Family (DA-KCF) framework that preserves an offline-identified nominal model while learning only a disturbance-induced correction online. A reference-dependent KCF is first learned from simulation data and calibrated using limited hardware data. During deployment, the nominal KCF remains fixed while a lightweight residual ensemble learns a low-dimensional disturbance correction from causal state-command-state transitions. The Adaptive DA-KCF model is incorporated into MPC, where disturbance corrections are evaluated along the prediction horizon to optimize position and yaw references sent to the onboard stabilizing controller. Hardware experiments on a nano-quadrotor under uniform wind and a spatially varying wake demonstrate that online adaptation improves multi-step prediction and trajectory tracking as disturbance-induced model mismatch increases, while satisfying real-time control requirements.

Object- and Task-Aware Active Perception for Safe Navigation of Mobile Manipulators in Semi-Static Environments 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Mobile manipulators must navigate safely in cluttered, partially unknown environments across logistics, manufacturing, and service applications. Semi-static changes between robot visits can lead to outdated scene knowledge and increased collision risk. We propose an active perception framework that enables a high degree-of-freedom (DoF) mobile manipulator to inspect uncertain or safety-critical regions with its hand camera during navigation. A probabilistic, object- and task-aware next-best-view (NBV) formulation prioritizes observations near the desired path and around uncertain or potentially changing objects. The perception objective is integrated into hierarchical-task model predictive control (HTMPC), exploiting kinematic redundancy for viewpoint adjustment while preserving the higher priority of base-trajectory tracking. The system unifies navigation, map maintenance, and active perception in a closed loop. Real-world tests show improved map consistency, reduced task-dependent map entropy, and lower navigation risk than passive or non-task-aware baselines. Additional simulation and real-world sequences demonstrate responses to controlled semi-static scene changes.

Certified Neural Approximation of Robust Nonlinear Model Predictive Control 2026-10-21
📝 12-page article plus 4-page supplement, 3 figures
Show abstract

Model predictive control is a high-performance control method, but it incurs comparatively high online computational costs across many control tasks and is challenging to realize in low-level hardware settings. To address this, we present a framework for generating neural network controllers that provably inherit the safety and stability guarantees of a robust model predictive policy. In contrast to approaches based on post-verification or additional online safety filters, our method provides constructive, a priori design conditions that ensure preservation of recursive feasibility and stability before deployment. We treat the approximation error as a bounded input disturbance, which is addressed in the MPC design. This allows the neural network controller to directly inherit robustness from the underlying model predictive controller, which enables the transfer of guarantees to the approximation. The design process is an offline, constructive synthesis pipeline and can be implemented once Lipschitz bounds are specified. We further demonstrate in simulation that the learned controller reproduces the closed-loop behavior of the original controller while reducing online computation.

Integrating Flow Matching and Model Predictive Control for Safe Robot Navigation 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Learning-based path planners based on diffusion and flow matching can generate diverse trajectories from demonstrations but typically lack mechanisms for constraint satisfaction and collision avoidance during deployment. This work proposes a framework that combines flow-matching-based path planning with model predictive path-following control to integrate data-driven path generation with real-time, constraint-aware execution. The flow-matching model captures multimodal path distributions, while the predictive controller adapts motion online to satisfy state and input constraints and avoid obstacles using updated local sensor information. An event-triggered re-planning scheme incorporates predictive control solutions into subsequent path generation, promoting collision-free navigation in environments with previously unseen obstacles. Simulation results for mobile robot navigation demonstrate robust performance across cluttered environments. The framework provides a systematic approach to combining flow matching with predictive control for adaptive, data-driven navigation.

Learning Reachability-Based Safety Representations for Real-Time Robot Navigation in Unknown and Dynamic Environments 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Safe robot navigation in unknown, dynamic environments requires more than geometric collision checking: a robot still far from an obstacle may be unable to avoid it because of its speed, orientation, or the obstacle's motion. Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis captures this in a value function whose sign separates states that admit a safe continuation from those where collision is unavoidable, but computing it online for every newly observed environment is too expensive. This dissertation trains neural implicit representations offline on reachability solutions to give optimization-based controllers this information in real time. Since the value function never exceeds the constraint, it equals a directly computed constraint, such as a signed distance, minus a nonnegative dynamics correction. Learning only this correction keeps represented obstacles outside the predicted safe set despite approximation errors. The first two of five methods use the learned function as a terminal constraint in model predictive control, for static local environments and predicted obstacle motion. The next two use it as a barrier candidate in control barrier function safety filters, for static environments and, through pairwise barriers in relative coordinates, for a varying number of moving obstacles. Simulations and hardware experiments on ground and aerial robots show better collision avoidance than geometric and hand-designed baselines, especially for short horizons and moving obstacles. The fifth method replaces the grid-based offline solver, which limits the first four to low-dimensional models. For control-affine dynamics with polytopic inputs, the HJ Hamiltonian is maximized at a vertex, so a parallelizable tree search over vertex inputs can generate training labels. With a loss from the HJ variational inequality, these labels yield barrier candidates for systems with up to 13 states.

Prescribed Performance Guided Safe Reinforcement Learning for Roundabout Navigation of Autonomous Vehicles 2026-10-20
📝 8 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) face significant challenges in roundabout navigation due to dense traffic interactions and unpredictable agent behaviors, necessitating high levels of adaptability and safety. While the combination of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers a promising approach, existing frameworks suffer from a structural mismatch where pointwise RL decisions fail to align with MPC's trajectory level optimization, and often lack rigorous safety guarantees. This work proposes RLTrajGen-PPMPC-CBF, a hierarchical framework for safe, smooth and comfort roundabout navigation, which regulates the coupling between RL-based planning and MPC-based control. We employ Prescribed Performance Constraints (PPC) to enforce a gradual, stable convergence between reference trajectories and control outputs, ensuring MPC tracking performance remains consistent with intended behavior from RL. Additionally, a Control Barrier Function (CBF) layer provides real-time safety corrections that are fed back into the RL process. Simulation results in the MetaDrive environment demonstrate that our method achieves a 6.25\% increase in average success rate and a 65.87\% reduction in Motion Sickness Dose Value (MSDV) compared to the optimal baseline, meanwhile accelerating training convergence.

Bio-inspired Thrust-Assisted Trotting on a legged-Aerial Robot via Real-Time QP-MPC 2026-10-19
📝 To be published in IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS 2026)
Show abstract

This paper presents the hardware implementation of a quadratic program-based controller on Harpy: thruster-assisted bipedal robot to perform thruster-assisted trotting. This is inspired by wing-assisted locomotion observed in birds such as the chukar partridge, which employ thrust vectoring to manipulate the ground reaction force. The proposed controller formulates the thruster-assisted spring-loaded inverted pendulum dynamics as a condensed QP-based model predictive control problem, enabling simultaneous regulation of ground contact force and active thrust vectoring at 500 Hz. A decoupled control architecture is used, where the robot's sagittal dynamics are controlled using QP-MPC while the frontal dynamics is regulated using a cascade orientation stabilization controller. Two experiments were conducted with different forward reference velocities of 0 m/s and 0.1 m/s. While the instantaneous velocity oscillates, the mean velocities can track their respective reference. Additionally, joint tracking error remains within 1 deg while tracking the desired leg length given by the leg trajectory controller and desired swing trajectory given by Raibert's heuristic. The QP-MPC problem is consistently solved with 0.18ms, which is well within the 2ms computation budget while satisfying the GRF friction cone constraint.

Dynamics-Aligned Representation Learning under Induced Partial Observability 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) has enabled sample-efficient learning of continuous control policies from high-dimensional observations through learned latent dynamics models. These models are typically trained with observation reconstruction objectives, which provide strong supervision but can bias representations toward visual fidelity leading to an undertrained prior and unreliable long-horizon predictions. We propose a stochastic gating mechanism that induces controlled partial observability during training by sampling a spatial binary mask from a Beta--Bernoulli random field and applying it to the raw observation. The gate probability is resampled at each time step, exposing the encoder to conditions ranging from near-total occlusion to near-full visibility. Integration of our method requires minimal architectural changes from existing dynamics models: the gated observation and the binary mask are channel-wise concatenated and passed to the encoder as a single augmented input, with no modifications to the broader training pipeline. We evaluate the proposed approach on a set of pixel-based continuous control tasks selected from the DeepMind Control Suite. Using model predictive control for evaluation, which relies solely on open-loop model rollouts, we show that stochastic gating improves planning performance and data efficiency across tasks. Further analysis demonstrates that performance is robust to the spatial scale of the mask, and a balanced observability during training outperforms configurations biased toward either sparser or denser observations.

Intent-Informed Model Predictive Control for Shared Autonomy in Remote Manipulation Tasks 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Shared autonomy is a control paradigm that combines human cognitive abilities with robot precision to enable effective remote manipulation in complex environments. A key challenge is how to adapt the level of autonomy using information extracted from the human, task, and/or environment. In this paper, we propose a novel arbitration strategy that dynamically adjusts the level of autonomy in the system using the entropy of a belief distribution derived from a multimodal intent inference. Human intent is modeled with a Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN), which updates beliefs over goals given real-time operator gaze data and robot arm motion during teleoperation. We then formulate the blending of human input and autonomous control as a Model Predictive Control (MPC) problem, where the system's autonomous behavior is guided by the belief distribution. This approach enables continuous, gradual transitions across the full spectrum of autonomy levels, from direct teleoperation to full autonomy. To validate our approach, we conducted experiments to compare the performance of our proposed algorithm against two MPC-based shared autonomy methods. Compared to the baselines, our approach achieved reductions of at least 61% in object retrieval time, 44% in trajectory length, and 60% in human control input. Participants reported high perceived control, predictability, intent recognition, and responsiveness, along with low workload.

Safe Driving in Occluded Environments 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Ensuring safe autonomous driving in the presence of occlusions poses a significant challenge in its policy design. While existing model-driven control techniques based on set invariance can handle visible risks, occlusions create latent risks in which safety-critical states are not observable. Data-driven techniques also struggle to handle latent risks because direct mappings from risk-critical objects in sensor inputs to safe actions cannot be learned without visible risk-critical objects. Motivated by these challenges, in this paper, we propose a probabilistic safety certificate for latent risk. Our key technical enabler is the application of probabilistic invariance: It relaxes the strict observability requirements imposed by set-invariance methods that demand the knowledge of risk-critical states. The proposed techniques provide linear action constraints that confine the latent risk probability within tolerance. Such constraints can be integrated into model predictive controllers or embedded in data-driven policies to mitigate latent risks. The proposed method is tested using the CARLA simulator and compared with a few existing techniques. The theoretical and empirical analysis jointly demonstrate that the proposed methods assure long-term safety in real-time control in occluded environments without being overly conservative and with transparency to exposed risks.

Switch-Time-Aware Hybrid Model Predictive Control Using the Hybrid Minimum Principle 2026-10-16
📝 8 pages; 4 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

This article presents a time-aware hybrid model predictive control (MPC) framework that simultaneously optimizes the objective with respect to both continuous control inputs and event switching times. Rather than relying on a fixed time grid, the switching instant is lifted into the decision vector and treated as an explicit optimization variable. The resulting transcription is a smooth nonlinear program whose Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are consistent with the Hybrid Minimum Principle (HMP) event-time stationarity condition, so first-order hybrid sensitivities are recovered implicitly through the KKT system rather than by evaluating saltation matrices. Each MPC step solves a single sparse Quadratic Program (QP), jointly optimizing control inputs and impact timing in real time. On a bouncing mass landing on an oscillating platform, the method attaches in half the time of fixed-grid saltation MPC, reduces cumulative control effort by 30%, and removes the post-impact rebounds, at a 15% increase in per-cycle solve time. It matches the cumulative effort of offline time-aware solvers to within 4% while running at 2.4 ms per cycle against 281 ms for a complementarity-based formulation of the same problem, of which a 10.6x reduction is attributable to the transcription alone at a fixed solver.

Learning Cross-Coupled and Regime Dependent Dynamics for Aerial Manipulation 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Accurate dynamics modeling of aerial manipulators is challenging because platform--manipulator coupling, history-dependent interactions, and changing payload or manipulator configurations produce cross-coupled, temporal, and nonstationary residual dynamics. We propose a structured encoder--decoder framework in which a nonlinear history encoder captures temporal and cross-variable dependencies, while a lightweight linear latent decoder supports efficient deployment-time adaptation. Its linear-in-parameter structure enables closed-form Bayesian updates with consistency-driven covariance inflation and integration with real-time model predictive control (MPC). Experiments on a real aerial-manipulation platform demonstrate improved residual-prediction accuracy, rapid adaptation following dynamics changes, and lower MPC tracking error under changing payload conditions. The results show that combining structured history-dependent representation learning with lightweight online adaptation provides an effective predictive model for aerial manipulation under coupled and nonstationary dynamics.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning-Based UAV Pathfinding for Obstacle Avoidance in Stochastic Environment 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Traditional methods plan feasible paths for multiple agents in the stochastic environment. However, the methods' iterations with the changes in the environment result in computation complexities, especially for the decentralized agents without a centralized planner. Although reinforcement learning provides a plausible solution because of the generalization for different environments, it struggles with enormous agent-environment interactions in training. Here, we propose a novel centralized training with decentralized execution method based on multi-agent reinforcement learning, which is improved based on the idea of model predictive control. In our approach, agents communicate only with the centralized planner to make decentralized decisions online in the stochastic environment. Furthermore, considering the communication constraint with the centralized planner, each agent plans feasible paths through the extended observation, which combines information on neighboring agents based on the distance-weighted mean field approach. Inspired by the rolling optimization approach of model predictive control, we conduct multi-step value convergence in multi-agent reinforcement learning to enhance the training efficiency, which reduces the expensive interactions in convergence. Experiment results in both comparison, ablation, and real-robot studies validate the effectiveness and generalization performance of our method.

ORD-WM: Learning Object-Centric Rigid Dynamics for 3D World Modeling in Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-16
Show abstract

World models provide robots with a predictive mechanism for reasoning about how actions change object states in manipulation tasks. However, existing image-based and dense 3D world models do not explicitly impose object-level rigid motion, leaving rigid-object geometry preservation to be learned implicitly and making multi-step rollouts susceptible to shape drift or object-level motion inconsistency. To address this, we propose ORD-WM, a 3D world model that learns object-centric rigid dynamics for robotic manipulation. ORD-WM learns a vector-neuron variational autoencoder supervised by signed distance fields, which encodes each object point cloud as a geometry-aware latent representation composed of 3D vector features, and uses an action-conditioned dynamics model to predict object-wise SE(3) motion increments. Rather than generating unconstrained next latent states, ORD-WM analytically applies predicted object-wise rigid transformations to the current latent states, separating persistent object geometry from pose changes. Experiments show that ORD-WM enables stable, geometry-preserving multi-step prediction and supports efficient real-time replanning for closed-loop model predictive control in simulated and real-world manipulation scenarios.

Adapting Generalist Vehicle Models for High-Speed MPC Across Terrains 2026-10-16
📝 https://amrl.cs.utexas.edu/optcar/ Selected at CORL 2026
Show abstract

High-speed off-road autonomy requires precise closed-loop control for a target vehicle while remaining robust across changing terrains. Recent forward kinodynamic (FKD) prediction foundation models suggest a promising path, starting from a generalist model and specializing it to the target platform. However, effective specialization remains challenging, as it often requires substantial real-world data, and models adapted to one setting can still overfit to specific terrains or driving regimes. We present OptCar (Optimized Car), a recipe for bridging the gap from generalist to specialist FKD models that preserves cross-terrain generalization while optimizing performance for a specific vehicle. OptCar introduces a transformer FKD architecture that uses FiLM to condition multi-step predictions on a single dynamics context token summarizing recent state-action history. It then specializes the generalist model using limited real-world data and targeted synthetic rollouts from environment-specific system identification. In closed-loop model predictive control (MPC) experiments across three terrains and an out-of-distribution cart-pulling task, the largest gains appear at 6 m/s, the highest speed evaluated and the regime in which slip dominates tracking error. On vegetation + dirt, the most slip-diverse terrain, OptCar reduces 6 m/s trajectory tracking error by roughly 55% relative to AnyCar fine-tuned on real data alone, and remains the most accurate even when an unseen cart payload changes the dynamics. With 5 minutes of real data per terrain, OptCar is competitive on road with a specialist trained on 30 minutes of road data and outperforms it when the terrain changes. Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-15
Show abstract

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a multifingered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world experiment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-specific demonstrations or sim-to-real transfer.

Safe and Fuel-Efficient Motion Planning for Lunar Propulsive Drones 2026-10-15
📝 This work has been submitted to the 2027 IEEE Aerospace Conference
Show abstract

Propulsive drones are a promising new technology for generating high-resolution terrain models of the Moon. In future lunar missions, propulsive drones will complete a series of hops across the lunar surface while capturing images with downward-facing cameras. We present an autonomous motion planning framework that uses a vision-guided optimal control strategy for performing safe, efficient hops between specified waypoints. The drone plans parabolic hops between waypoints generated by a global-level planner; if a nominal waypoint is unsafe or unreachable, the drone then completes a diversion maneuver to safely land. To evaluate the validity of potential landing sites, we develop a custom semantic segmentation model for identifying obstacles from aerial images, and we introduce an approach for rapidly calculating reachable terrain. We then use model predictive control (MPC) to robustly track hop trajectories that guide the drone towards safe landing sites. We test the optimal hop planner in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. Our results demonstrate that our unified motion planning framework yields safe, fuel-efficient hop trajectories which can be tracked rapidly and with low error via MPC.

Inertially Significant Humanoid Teleoperation via Whole Body Model Predictive Control and Online Parameter Estimation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

The effective deployment of humanoid robots in practical tasks of interest will require a capability of sensing and adapting to variations in inertial parameters, as occurs during heavy tool use or when carrying significant payload. Such payloads constitute a significant proportion of the combined payload-robot system, the effects of which cannot be ignored by delegating to feedback control. This paper showcases a general solution to this problem by integrating a whole body model predictive controller (WB-MPC) with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online estimation of fully physically consistent time-varying inertial parameters. These estimates are fed through to the WB-MPC's full rigid body dynamics model, resulting in more accurate feedforward torques for task demands under external load. Additionally, a virtual reality (VR) interface is provided that enables an operator to provide task-specific references without regard to payload while delegating dynamic balance to the WB-MPC. The framework is validated with simulation and hardware experiments on a humanoid system.

CQDC-Warp: GPU-Accelerated Differentiable Quasi-Dynamic Simulation for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Efficient synthesis of contact-rich manipulation trajectories is essential for downstream robot learning. Gradient-based planning offers a promising approach, but jointly optimizing motion and contact decisions remains challenging. Meanwhile, many existing planners run on CPUs with limited throughput. We present CQDC-Warp, a GPU-accelerated differentiable quasi-dynamic simulator for batched model predictive control (MPC). We use it to study contact-guided trajectory optimization, in which intended robot--object contacts are specified before planning and encouraged through soft costs, while the simulated dynamics determine contact geometry and frictional response without a prescribed contact-mode schedule. Across $17{,}408$ trials on the $17$-object Allegro in-hand rotation benchmark, our framework achieves $79.8\%$ success, compared with $73.7\%$ for CFree and $65.2\%$ for IMPACT, and reduces mean trajectory-generation time by factors of $27.0$ and $50.2$, respectively. On a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with $2{,}048$ parallel worlds, forward and backward throughput reach $2.06\times10^5$ and $2.25\times10^5$ world-steps per second, respectively. Further experiments cover manipulation with LEAP and Shadow hands, environmental contact exploitation, bimanual manipulation, whole-body control, and real-world trajectory replay on a Sharpa hand.

MR-MPCC: Model Predictive Contouring Control for Robotic Manipulator Tracking under Moving Reference Frames 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted by The IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems 2026 (IROS 2026)
Show abstract

Maintaining small path-tracking errors under moving reference frames is essential for robotic manipulation tasks with tight execution tolerances, such as machining and surgical automation. Conventional time-indexed controllers, including tracking Model Predictive Control (MPC), rely on a fixed temporal correspondence with the reference trajectory and may therefore accumulate path-relative error under abrupt reference-frame motion or timing mismatch. This paper presents MR-MPCC, a Model Predictive Contouring Control (MPCC) framework for velocity-controlled robotic manipulators with 6-DoF end-effector pose regulation under Moving-Reference disturbances. A hierarchical control architecture is adopted, in which an upper-level MPCC optimizes path progress while penalizing contouring and orientation errors, and a constrained resolved motion rate control (RMRC) layer maps the commanded Cartesian twist to feasible joint velocities under joint-level and safety constraints. MR-MPCC is implemented in a ROS2--MuJoCo pipeline and validated on a UR5e platform under moving-reference disturbances, where the path frame is perturbed and tracked by electromagnetic sensors. Compared with PI control and time-indexed tracking MPC under matched disturbance, MR-MPCC achieves lower geometric path-following error and smaller deviations in the moving path frame, demonstrating improved path-following performance under reference-frame motion.

Plan Before You Trade: Inference-Time Optimization for RL Trading Agents 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning agents for portfolio management are typically trained and deployed as static policies, with no mechanism for policy adjustments using price forecasts at inference time. We propose FinPILOT (Financial Plugin Inference-time Learning for Optimal Trading), a plugin inference-time policy optimization framework inspired by Model Predictive Control (MPC). Under the price-taker assumption, portfolio actions do not affect future market prices, allowing a multi-step price forecaster to provide imagined market trajectories without iterative action-conditioned rollouts. At each decision step, FinPILOT uses the forecaster's predicted price trajectory to construct an allocation-based imagined return objective and optimizes the pretrained policy at inference time before executing one step of the trade. Our framework is compatible with any pretrained policy and adapts the policy using the forecaster's predictions without modifying the original RL training pipeline. Evaluated with five pretrained policies (PPO, SAC, A2C, TD3, DDPG) across DJ30 equities and foreign exchange (FX) over three rolling held-out test years (30 settings in total), FinPILOT improves Total Return in 28 of 30 settings and Sharpe ratio in 27 of 30, with gains spanning both stochastic and deterministic policies. Direct single- and multi-period portfolio optimizers supplied with the same forecasts do not reproduce these aggregate gains under our evaluation protocol. Further, using controlled forecasts at calibrated quality levels, we show that both the magnitude and consistency of FinPILOT's performance increase as forecast error is reduced.

Planning to Learn: Real-Time Robust Adaptive Control on the GPU with Guaranteed Future Learning 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Robust adaptive control is vital for keeping robots safe while actively reducing uncertainty in unfamiliar settings. However, traditional methods are often conservative since they fail to account for guaranteed future parameter learning during planning and struggle to scale to high-dimensional systems. In this work, we present A-SLS, a learning-aware robust adaptive model predictive control (RAMPC) framework based on system-level synthesis. A-SLS forecasts future parameter uncertainty and preserves correlations between process disturbances, parameter error, and estimate updates to construct tight, guaranteed reachable sets that account for future information gain. We also develop a GPU-accelerated A-SLS solver to enable real-time performance. Across nonlinear robotic systems, A-SLS reduces reachable-tube conservativeness, maintains real-time performance, and scales to whole-body control for a quadruped with 61 states, 8 uncertain parameters, and 12 control inputs. We study the closed-loop properties of a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) world model trained on human demonstrations in Gymnasium's LunarLander-v3. The BC policy matches MPC's mean return only under stochastic action selection, and on the same 20 episodes it has one catastrophic episode where MPC has none. Visual world models have shown great potential in learning complex system dynamics. Distributed Motion Planning for Multi-Robot Systems under Topological Constraints 2026-10-07
📝 19 pages, 13 figures
Show abstract

Efficient and distributed coordination of mobile robots is one of the main challenges in multi-robot systems. Topological constraints, often expressed as topological braids, are a popular tool to encode complex coordination patterns between multiple mobile robots, as they offer a compact and abstract representation of the desired qualitative relation between the space-time trajectories of the robots. However, execution of joint motion plans encoded as braid-based topological constraints via distributed controllers is challenging, with existing approaches, generally based on the execution of one braid generator at a time, producing slow and suboptimal trajectories. We propose a distributed controller based on Model Predictive Control (MPC) to efficiently execute braid-based topological specifications. Rather than directly tracking the braid specification, we propose to use winding numbers, which are topological invariants for braids, as a proxy. This has the twofold benefit of converting braids into a continuous function, which can be easily tracked by an MPC controller through an appropriate term in the cost function, and of decoupling the global braid specification into a set of pairwise specifications, which can be tracked distributedly through the solution of only local MPC problems. To maintain global coordination, we propose a consensus-based progress estimation approach, which allows the robots to synchronize their motion toward the desired specification. We validate the proposed approach in simulation and in real-world experiments, where we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach and the improvement over existing approaches in terms of execution speed and control effort.

Ruleless Digital Twins: Toward Declarative Decision-Making Through Standardized Frameworks and Technologies 2026-10-07
📝 47 pages (bibliography included), expanded version of the Spajić and Stolz (2026) DataMod paper
Show abstract

Digital twins (DTs) can be thought of as digital counterparts of physical objects, or more generally, twinning targets (TTs). To enact changes on and optimize for properties of their TTs, DTs use an element of decision-making. Traditionally, many domains, such as home automation, utilize rule-based decision-making models to imperatively define DT actions based on desired TT conditions. With evolving user specifications, rule-based models have become increasingly complex to develop and maintain, particularly in scenarios involving optimizations under dynamically changing systems, such as those affected by weather or dynamic energy pricing. We present an alternative in the form of ruleless digital twins (RDTs) that automatically produce optimal decisions with respect to purely declarative user specifications, similarly to the well-established ruleless approach of model predictive control. They do so through a combination of a semantic knowledge base, logical inference, simulation models, and an autonomic computing architecture, each exclusively based on a widely-used standard or technology. We evaluate our proof of concept through an incubator case study and experiments against a rule-based bang-bang controller in the context of a virtual office room environment. Results show core that RDT functionality performs better than the control in terms of maintaining desired room temperatures and minimizing energy costs under dynamically changing spot pricing. Additionally, due to combinatorial decision tree construction, RDTs require considerable computational power for longer prediction horizons, although their applicability to specific problem domains (and use of shorter horizons) ultimately falls on their users.
