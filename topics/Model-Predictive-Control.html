Model-Predictive Spill-Free Emergency Stopping for Liquid-Manipulating Robots 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Manipulating open liquid containers is challenging because liquids are highly sensitive to vessel accelerations and jerks. Although spill-free liquid manipulation has been widely studied, emergency stopping under unexpected hazards has received little attention, despite the fact that abrupt braking may cause hazardous spills. This letter presents an emergency stop system for robots manipulating liquids in open containers. We formulate emergency stopping as an optimal control problem and solve it in a model predictive control framework to generate time-optimal, spill-free stopping trajectories. The method operates as a plug-and-play safety layer on top of existing slosh-free motion planning methods, enabling immediate reaction to detected hazards while accounting for nonlinear liquid dynamics. We demonstrate, through simulation and on a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot, that the proposed approach achieves fast emergency stopping without spilling.

CineWild: Balancing Art and Robotics for Ethical Wildlife Documentary Filmmaking 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become powerful tools across domains from industry to the arts. In documentary filmmaking, they offer dynamic, otherwise unreachable perspectives, transforming how stories are told. Wildlife documentaries especially benefit, yet UAVs also raise ethical concerns like the risk of disturbing the animals they aim to capture. This paper introduces CineWild, a model predictive control (MPC) formulation for autonomous aerial cinematography that combines robotics, cinematography, and ethics. Building on an MPC solution that jointly controls drone motion and camera focal length, CineWild dynamically adjusts flight paths and camera settings to balance cinematic quality with animal welfare. Key features include adaptive zoom for filming from acoustic and visual safe distances, path-planning that avoids an animal's field of view, and smooth, low-noise maneuvers. CineWild exemplifies interdisciplinary innovation, bridging engineering, visual storytelling, and environmental ethics. We evaluate the formulation in simulation, with ablation studies and a comparison against a cinematographic baseline and a welfare-aware baseline under identical weights, and we demonstrate the perception and camera-actuation pipeline in real-world proof-of-concept trials. We will release the code.

ReLU Networks for Model Predictive Control: Network Complexity and Performance Guarantees 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Recent years have witnessed a resurgence in using ReLU neural networks (NNs) to represent model predictive control (MPC) policies. However, determining the sufficient network complexity to ensure closed-loop guarantees remains a fundamental open problem. This involves a critical precision-complexity trade-off: undersized networks may fail to capture the MPC policy, while oversized ones may outweigh the benefits of ReLU network approximation. In this work, we project the MPC actions onto a tightened constraint set to satisfy the hard constraints, and establish a state-dependent Lipschitz continuity property for the optimal MPC cost function, which enables sharp convergence analysis of the closed-loop system. For the first time, we derive explicit bounds on ReLU network width and depth for approximating MPC policies with closed-loop guarantees. To reduce network complexity for the convergence to user-specified target sets, we propose a non-uniform error framework with a state-aware scaling function to adaptively adjust both the input and output of the ReLU network. Our contributions provide a foundational step toward certifiable ReLU NN-based MPC.

Can I join you? Extended Social Force Based Model with Predictive Control and Barrier Functions for Humanoid Group Repositioning Amidst Obstacles 2026-11-11
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

As social and service robots increasingly share interaction spaces in public, they may form groups while coordinating tasks or interacting with humans. Initial group formation alone is insufficient in dynamic settings, because it can be disrupted when a person or another agent joins, leaves, or passes through the group. Although group dynamics and repositioning have been widely studied in swarm robotics and virtual-agent settings, little attention has been given to socially appropriate repositioning behaviors in groups of humanoid robots. Existing social-force-based approaches provide a useful foundation for group behavior, but they have limited obstacle handling, and can produce unsafe behavior as environmental complexity increases. We propose a novel framework that modulates traditional Social Force, guided by Model Predictive Control (MPC) to plan ahead, bounded by Control Barrier Functions (CBF) to increase safety for robot groups repositioning behaviors. This approach aims to produce safer and socially aware group repositioning behaviors under various group formation disturbance. We evaluate the method in simulation through an ablation study and an online user study. The results show that our proposed method can produce smoother and safer repositioning behaviors in complex environments while preserving social appropriateness.

DynamAuction: A Reinforcement Learning Environment for Repeated Auctions with Dynamic Value 2026-11-10
Show abstract

A buyer who repeatedly participates in auctions for related items often faces a dynamic value problem: what they win today changes what tomorrow's items are worth. Despite the empirical importance of such effects, especially in display advertising, the algorithmic literature on repeated auctions has largely focused on the learning problem, by treating each opportunity as a contextual bandit, while sidestepping the planning problem induced by the dependence of current value on past wins. We introduce DynamAuction, a synthetic environment for single-buyer sequential bidding. The reward from each win depends on the buyer's history of previous wins, summarized through a carry-over state. The environment supports first- and second-price auctions, non-homogeneous Poisson arrivals, delayed feedback, saturation, and recency effects. We also introduce a new class of Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithms. On our benchmark scenario, these MPC policies outperform off-the-shelf deep RL baselines (PPO, SAC, TD3) as well as a tuned shading baseline. We release DynamAuction to support RL research on repeated auctions.

Dynamic Shape Control of Soft Trunk Robots 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Controlling the shape profile of soft trunk robots extends their reach into complex regions of the workspace and enhances their use in practical applications. Recent advances in soft robot design have enabled soft trunk robots to achieve greater flexibility in conforming to complex shapes. Specifically, trunk robots whose geometries are designed as helicoids have been shown to enable extreme shape-changing capabilities. Their compliance makes them highly nonlinear, which in turn makes shape control difficult. On similar tendon-driven trunk robots, model-based controllers based on the theory of adiabatic spectral submanifolds (aSSMs) have achieved high accuracy in closed-loop end-effector tracking. We extend this approach by learning an aSSM model of the full body of a trimmed-helicoid soft trunk robot from data and use this in a model predictive control (MPC) scheme to demonstrate a range of shape control tasks on hardware. On closed-loop dynamic shape regulation, our method outperforms the available linear baselines by a factor of 10. In human-in-the-loop interactive demos, our method completes section-by-section shaping and balances a tennis ball for over 25 seconds, while the linear baselines fall short. A model-free reinforcement learning policy trained for 12 hours on hardware fails on the same balancing task. These results establish aSSM-based MPC as a practical route to full-body shape control on real soft hardware.

OASIS: Optimization-Aware Subgoal Inference with Safety Constraints for Autonomous Mobile Robot Navigation 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Navigation in dynamic environments requires mobile robots to make progress toward their goals while respecting motion limits and avoiding surrounding obstacles. Learned subgoal policies can guide model predictive control beyond its local prediction horizon, but a geometrically suitable subgoal does not necessarily lead to a successfully solved control problem. This paper proposes OASIS, a hierarchical navigation framework that incorporates recent optimization outcomes into reinforcement learning-based subgoal generation. A high-level policy trained using Soft Actor--Critic generates local subgoals, while a nonlinear model predictive controller tracks them subject to motion limits, geometric collision-avoidance constraints, and embedded high-order control barrier function constraints. The outcomes of controller optimizations over each subgoal interval are summarized and provided to the policy at its next decision, without requiring additional optimization calls. The framework is evaluated in five navigation scenarios with static and moving obstacles, including dense interactions and sensing uncertainty. Comparisons with hierarchical and direct-goal control variants show that OASIS achieves the highest aggregate navigation success. Matched comparisons across three training seeds further show a consistent success advantage over the otherwise identical hierarchy without optimization-status input. The selected-policy comparison also shows fewer primary optimization failures with similar mean controller time.

FAME-QP: A GPU-Accelerated Quadratic-Programming Solver for Real-Time Model Predictive Control 2026-11-10
Show abstract

We present FAME-QP, a GPU-accelerated solver for dense quadratic programs arising in linear model predictive control. FAME-QP combines constraint-dependent operator representations with fused single- and two-kernel iterations replayed through CUDA graphs, automatically selecting the execution configuration through device calibration and problem-specific timing. It probes a cached operator bank to select the initial penalty and updates operators lazily when problem matrices change. Numerical benchmarks and simulated humanoid and quadrotor control experiments demonstrate reduced latency relative to competing GPU solvers while maintaining solution accuracy and control performance.

Safe Approximate Nonlinear Model Predictive Control Without Expert Demonstrations 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Repeated online optimization makes nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) computationally demanding for real-time applications. Neural approximations reduce this cost but often require large datasets of expert NMPC solutions. We introduce Sequential Approximate MPC (Seq-AMPC), a label-free recurrent framework that generates complete control sequences along predicted trajectories using parameters shared across the prediction horizon. The policy is trained directly through known dynamics with losses for control cost, constraint satisfaction, and a cost-decrease condition aligned with a feasible-backup safety filter. Building on this filter, we introduce a stage-cost-dependent acceptance tolerance that preserves recursive feasibility, constraint satisfaction, and asymptotic convergence under nominal MPC assumptions, independently of neural approximation error. We further derive Hoeffding-based finite-sample bounds for the expected fraction of applied proposals and closed-loop cost discrepancy relative to NMPC. We evaluate Seq-AMPC on a quadcopter, chain of masses, obstacle-avoidance vehicle, and robotic manipulator, comparing recurrent and feedforward policies under label-free and imitation training. Across four benchmarks, training takes 0.2-1.7 GPU hours per policy without expert control labels. The filtered controller applies 97.2-99.1% of neural proposals and achieves mean closed-loop cost ratios within 0.3% of NMPC on jointly completed episodes. Tests with suboptimal feasible initial plans further demonstrate the benefit of neural replanning.

Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control for Underactuated Upright Physical Assistance 2026-11-09
📝 Accepted for publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), October 2026. 8 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Many rehabilitation robots execute prescribed motions using reactive tracking controllers that do not explicitly incorporate the user's instantaneous human-ground interaction. This paper presents Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control (GRF-MPC), a framework for upright physical assistance. By integrating live GRF and center of pressure (CoP) measurements directly into a human centroidal dynamics model, GRF-MPC formulates a real-time quadratic program (QP) that optimizes assistive forces and moments over a receding horizon. We deployed GRF-MPC on the Robotic Upright Stand Trainer (RobUST) in an underactuated 4-cable configuration and evaluated it against a fully actuated 8-cable Cartesian impedance baseline in nearly 300 trials across six healthy participants. Across tasks spanning impulsive perturbations, lunge trajectories, and voluntary limits of stability, GRF-MPC reduced peak planar center-of-mass (CoM) error by 19.6% during perturbations, lowered trajectory tracking error by 24.1%, required 28.7% less net mechanical work, and permitted a 45.9% greater normalized voluntary excursion area. The underlying QP solves in under 1 ms, comfortably enabling 100 Hz replanning and demonstrating that incorporating live human-ground interaction into predictive control yields stronger, more transparent physical assistance with half the actuators.

A Game-Theoretic MPC Planner for Cooperative and Adversarial Drone Coordination 2026-11-09
📝 7 pages, 4 figures, submitted to European Control Conference (ECC27)
Show abstract

We present and evaluate a game-theoretic model predictive control planner for cooperative goal reaching and adversarial pursuit-evasion with drones. The planner combines 3D prediction, individual objectives, optional shared separation constraints and trajectory-tracking interfaces. A comparison of cooperative game solvers assesses their ability to find feasible trajectories that resolve conflicts. A computational study quantifies how number of drones, prediction horizon and move blocking affect computation time and numerical acceptance. Move blocking holds control inputs constant over groups of prediction steps, reducing the number of independent control variables and lowering computation time. Timing measurements identify the practical limits of real-time planning in the tested configurations. PyBullet simulations demonstrate cooperative goal reaching and pursuit-evasion. A laboratory flight complements the simulations by validating the trajectory-tracking interfaces on physical drones. The planner can serve as a basis for further evaluation and controller development.

Graph-Sparse Distributed Predictive Control for Multi-Robot Motion Planning vianEdge-Consensus ADMM 2026-11-09
Show abstract

This paper presents a distributed model predictive control (MPC) architecture for collision-avoiding motion planning of planar robots with double-integrator dynamics. To reduce coordination demands, a time-varying predicted conflict graph selects relevant pairwise interactions. The associated collision-avoidance constraints are convexified once per MPC update using warm-start trajectories, yielding a fixed convex quadratic program (QP). The resulting QP is then decomposed using an edge-consensus formulation of the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), enabling parallel robot-local optimization and independent closed-form projections onto separating half-spaces. Execution is selectively supervised by a centralized high-order control barrier function (HOCBF) QP when the distributed candidate does not satisfy the direct-execution conditions. Evaluated in C++ simulations involving 8, 16, and 32 robots, the proposed architecture achieves collision-free execution and substantially improves computational scalability while maintaining task-level performance comparable to a centralized complete-graph baseline.

Commercial buildings account for 17\% of U.S. carbon emissions, with roughly half of that from Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC). Data-Driven Precision Control of Soft Helicoid Robots 2026-11-09
📝 Submitted to npj Robotics. 44 pages, supplementary information included
Show abstract

A purely data-driven method grounded in the theory of adiabatic spectral submanifolds (aSSMs) was recently used to model and control nonlinear soft robotic simulators. Here, we extend the methodology to learn aSSM-reduced models from data collected from hardware soft robotic platforms. Specifically, we learn a five dimensional (5D) aSSM-reduced nonlinear ordinary differential equation (ODE) that accurately models the dynamics of a soft-helicoid trunk robot. We then use this ODE in a model predictive control scheme to demonstrate real-time precision control across various challenging closed-loop trajectory-tracking tasks, ranging from 2D to 3D trajectories with static and dynamic constraints. We find that our methodology enhances accuracy and precision across all control tasks compared to available data-driven linear methods and model-free reinforcement learning. While we focus here on a soft-helicoid trunk robot, our methodology relies solely on hardware data and is therefore applicable to a broader class of soft robotic systems.

Floquet-Parameterized Koopman-MPC for Tracking Periodic Orbits 2026-11-08
📝 6 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Designing real-time control for nonlinear systems to track periodic orbits can be challenging, especially for complex and coupled dynamical systems (e.g., tethered kites), where the true model parameters are hard to obtain. To address this, we propose a physics-informed Koopman learning approach that incorporates periodic-orbit stability directly into the learned linear predictor. The approach combines the underlying periodic structure of the dynamics along with periodic state-input trajectory data to learn physically consistent linear predictors (in lifted/observable space). We leverage Floquet theory, which studies the stability of periodic orbits in nonlinear systems, to obtain the periodic stability information of a given orbit via a monodromy matrix (i.e., the state transition matrix over an entire period). Subsequently, we impose open-loop monodromy constraints to learn Floquet-parameterized linear predictors using a least-squares Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) formulation, thereby constraining the search space and aiding in Koopman learning. We then utilize these linear predictors to design a model predictive control (MPC) law for tracking periodic references. Finally, we evaluate the designed framework on a cartpole system under periodic forcing and compare it against a purely data-driven baseline.

BRACE: Belief-guided Residual Adaptive Control for Safe Navigation under Unknown Disturbances 2026-11-07
Show abstract

Disturbances encountered during autonomous navigation generally vary across space, often discontinuously, as obstacles redirect wind and changing terrain alters traction. Tracking controllers typically assume sufficient control authority to mitigate disturbances and maintain safety; the regime where this assumption fails remains underexplored. We present Belief-guided Residual Adaptive Control (BRACE), a hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) framework for reach-avoid navigation under unknown, bounded, spatially varying disturbances. BRACE infers a disturbance belief from model prediction errors and uses that to condition a high-level policy that adapts the reference trajectory. The policy combines a nominal and a belief-guided residual term, trained jointly with a likelihood-ratio advantage reweighting heuristic. We evaluate BRACE in three simulated quadcopter environments with abruptly switching wind fields, and validate it on Crazyflie hardware with fan-generated disturbances. Across these settings, BRACE improves reach-avoid performance over model predictive control and RL baselines.

Sampling-based Model Predictive Control as Sequential Monte Carlo Inference 2026-11-07
📝 8 pages, 5 figures. Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L). Code: https://github.com/CoMMALab/PFMP
Show abstract

Sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) is treated as a family of distinct algorithms: predictive sampling, MPPI, the cross-entropy method, and their biased, colored-noise, and annealed variants all sample control sequences and reweight them by cost. We show a single Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) inference algorithm implements them all, specified by three independent axes: the proposal, the update rule that reduces cost-weighted particles to a nominal plan, and the effective-sample-size threshold for resampling. We evaluate SMC-based planning on MuJoCo non-linear control, open-loop navigation through obstacle fields, 7-DoF motion planning, and non-prehensile push-T, in simulation and on a Franka FR3. Resampling combats particle degeneracy at long horizons. At a 28-knot horizon in our navigation domain it cut the collision rate by 30% over non-resampling baselines. Programmable proposals improve push-T success over perturbative baselines, improving from 1% to 100% in simulation and 10% to 90% on hardware.

Maze Runner: Autonomous Maze Navigation with Line-Based Localization and Control Barrier Functions 2026-11-06
📝 10 pages, 8 figures, 1 table. Code: https://github.com/alvgaona/maze-runner
Show abstract

Maze navigation requires localization from sparse, repetitive geometry, planning through narrow corridors, and control that stays safe when the estimate or the map is wrong. We present Maze Runner, an open autonomy stack in C++ and ROS 2 that addresses these needs end to end on a simulated Husarion ROSbot XL with a 2D LiDAR in Gazebo. Perception extracts wall lines from each scan with RANSAC and total least squares, with per-line uncertainty. The same line front end feeds two estimators: an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that localizes against a line map built from the maze mesh, and an EKF SLAM variant that builds the line map online. Planning runs online on an inflated occupancy grid with A* plus line-of-sight shortcutting, or with a forward-only Hybrid A*. A nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) tracks the plan while discrete-time Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) on world-frame LiDAR points keep the robot clear of walls and unmapped obstacles. The algorithms live in a ROS-free, unit-tested core library, and the whole stack builds and runs from a single reproducible environment. In simulation, the robot drives a 135 m route through the maze with 1.0 cm localization RMSE against the known map and 24 cm in SLAM mode, the planner returns a path in 6 ms, and the NMPC solves in 11.7 ms on average, never exceeding its 80 ms budget.

A Continual Validation, Updating, and Decision-Making Framework for Self-Adaptive Digital Twins: A Case Study in Additive Manufacturing 2026-11-06
Show abstract

Digital Twins support real-time decision-making through predictive models of physical systems, yet their reliability can deteriorate as operating conditions evolve. Maintaining predictive fidelity under such changes is challenging, particularly when model adaptation relies on limited streaming data, and updated models must be evaluated before deployment. Here, we present an online adaptation framework that integrates gradient-based drift detection, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and statistical model validation within model predictive control. Capturability-Aware Footstep Planning in Cluttered Environments: A Recursively Feasible Sequential MPC on Axis-Aligned Safe Corridors 2026-11-06

A fundamental aspect of humanoid footstep planning is to jointly guarantee obstacle avoidance and locomotion stability. This paper presents a capturability-aware sequential Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP)-based humanoid footstep planning in the presence of obstacles. Differentiable Nonlinear Model Predictive Control 2026-11-06
📝 accepted for Neurips 2026
Show abstract

The efficient computation of parametric solution sensitivities is a key challenge in the integration of learning-enhanced methods with nonlinear model predictive control, as their availability is crucial for many learning algorithms. This paper discusses the computation of solution sensitivities of general nonlinear programs using the implicit function theorem and smoothed optimality conditions treated in interior-point methods (IPM). We introduce sequential barrier quadratic programming (SBQP), which generalizes SQP with an IPM quadratic programming (QP) solver by allowing the barrier parameter to approach a prescribed nonnegative target value, recovering classical SQP when this target is zero. Previous works are limited to convex or unconstrained formulations, or lack an implementation for efficient sensitivity evaluation. The publication is accompanied by an efficient open-source implementation within the `acados` framework, providing both forward and adjoint sensitivities for general optimal control problems, achieving speedups exceeding 3x over the state-of-the-art solvers `mpc.pytorch` and `cvxpygen`.

Goodhart in the Planner: Sampling-Based Control Exploits Rewards Learned from Human Preferences 2026-11-06
📝 19 pages, 4 figures. Equal contribution. Code and results will be released
Show abstract

Robots increasingly choose actions by searching against a reward model learned from human preferences, in model-predictive control or best-of-$N$ selection; we measure what this does to the true objective. On HalfCheetah and Hopper with crowd-sourced labels, and on Meta-World drawer-open and plate-slide with synthetic labels and blind labels from the two authors, harder MPPI search cuts true return by up to 76%: with human labels the drawer opens in 0 of 20 runs against 10 of 10 for the cloned policy the planner starts from, while held-out label accuracy stays at 0.75-0.97. On plate-slide human labels are exploited less than synthetic ones, and on window-open search helps. True return roughly follows how far the planner's weights move from its proposals ($\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}$) for both weighting rules we test, and best-of-$N$ with a verifier from synthetic labels falls below a single noisy action. We prove that $χ^2$-regularized MPPI at a temperature set by the scorer's error spread on the proposals is worst-case optimal, that no positive gain can be certified once that spread exceeds the scorer's own, and that an episode's return splits exactly into per-step terms, of which the guarantee covers the selection terms. Calibrated from 100 proposal pairs ranked by true return (an oracle) on start states the evaluation never uses, this rule is the safest fix we test: with each of five independent sets it succeeds in every arm run and keeps on average 86-94% of the cloned policy's return on plate-slide and drawer-open, though it is cautious where search helps. Calibration from a person's comparisons cannot detect a bias the scorer learned from that person; correcting for the pairs people cannot tell apart, or a second person, limits the damage.

FASTFUNNELS: Agile Multi Robot Navigation in Narrow Spaces via Adaptive Dynamic Funnels 2026-11-06
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 1 table
Show abstract

We focus on the problem of high-speed multi-robot navigation in cluttered spaces with tight spatial constraints. A key challenge is balancing safety with agility, as existing methods often trade one for the other (slowing down near a bottleneck to avoid collisions versus failing to swerve in time at high speeds), especially for car-like systems. Our approach belongs to the family of formation control methods, in which Ackermann-steering agents coordinate while navigating tight spaces. However, current formation control techniques rely on rigid, pre-defined shapes and transitions that depend on initial conditions, the environment, and vehicle dynamics, and are impractical to maintain at high speeds. For example, agents passing through a small gap must first form a square, transform into a line using analytical equations, and then return to a square. In contrast, our approach is inspired by a droplet of water that continuously adapts its shape to the space it flows through without slowing down. We propose a hierarchical algorithm in which, at the high level, a reinforcement learning policy produces a dynamic, blob-like elliptical patch that morphs its shape in real time, squeezing, stretching, and orienting itself around obstacles and through tight spaces. At the low level, the robots are controlled via distributed model predictive control (DMPC) to stay inside the patch, which acts as a soft forcing function, while avoiding collisions with each other. We evaluate our approach, FastFunnels, on simulated F1TENTH racing cars across 14 scenarios with N=1 to N=6 robots, averaged over 20 trials each. FastFunnels achieves an 89% completion rate at 7.73 m/s, while all baselines either fail to complete the track or succeed only at very low speeds (0.07-1.79 m/s, about 40% completion rate).

Think Fast, Plan Selectively: Adaptive Deliberation for Efficient Data-Driven MPC 2026-11-06
Show abstract

Data-driven model predictive control (MPC) combines learned world models with online trajectory optimization, achieving strong performance in continuous control. However, the per-step cost of sampling and evaluating hundreds of candidate trajectories restricts deployment to control frequencies well below what real-time robotics demands. Motivated by the dual-process theory of human cognition, which distinguishes between fast, intuitive processing (System 1) and slower, deliberative reasoning (System 2), we ask whether every decision requires the same degree of computational deliberation. We propose Fast-TD-MPC, a lightweight framework that adaptively routes between fast policy execution and test-time planning, reserving costly deliberation for states where it is most needed. Fast-TD-MPC delivers competitive task performance across 103 continuous control tasks while achieving up to ~4x faster inference. Under external disturbances, Fast-TD-MPC selectively falls back to planning, maintaining robustness comparable to the original planner.

Closed-Loop Object-Informed Control for Non-Prehensile Robot Manipulation 2026-11-05
Show abstract

Non-prehensile robot manipulation is challenging due to discontinuous, long-horizon interactions between the robot and the objects it manipulates. Sampling-based model predictive control methods are effective with discontinuous contact but face challenges with finding promising trajectories in long-horizon planning. We propose a closed-loop object-informed (CLOI) method that splits the problem into object-level planning to find long-horizon object poses that lead the object to its goal, and robot-level planning to select robot actions that follow those poses. We use model predictive path integral (MPPI) control to solve the subproblems and couple their solutions through consensus on the object poses using the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). The object plan is revised toward robot-realizable object trajectories, while the robot plan is aligned with the object poses the task requires. In planar pushing tasks with obstacles using an xArm6 manipulator, CLOI increases the success rate by 35% in simulation and 43% on hardware, compared to standard MPPI given the same computational budget.

GUARD: Sensitivity-aware Uncertainty Attribution for Safe Traditional and Learning-based Robot Control 2026-11-05
📝 9 pages, 9 figures, 2 tables. Substantially revised and expanded version with updated title and authors. Extended validation using model predictive control and reinforcement learning-based control
