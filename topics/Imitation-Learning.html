Failure Detection for Surgical Robot Imitation Policies via Flow-Matching World Modeling 2026-11-25
📝 8 pages, 4 figures. Revised following peer review. Accepted for publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)
Show abstract

Imitation learning has shown increasing promise for autonomous robotic surgery, yet safe deployment remains challenging due to the safety-critical nature of surgical tasks and the complexity and variability of surgical environments. Failure detection is therefore an essential safeguard, but its development remains difficult due to the challenges of scarce failure data, highly variable manipulation dynamics, and the need to balance missed detections against disruptive false alarms. To address these challenges, we introduce FoMo-FD (Flow-Matching World Model for Failure Detection), a failure detection method that learns nominal short-horizon visual dynamics with an action-conditioned flow-matching world model. FoMo-FD scores the inverse-transport nonconformity of observed endpoint latents, enabling window-level detection of deviations from learned nominal dynamics without requiring failure demonstrations. Detection thresholds are obtained by conformal calibration on successful policy rollouts, yielding task-specific alarms without assuming future failure types. We evaluate FoMo-FD on four surgically relevant manipulation tasks with twenty failure modes across simulation and real-robot experiments using the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK). In the wrist view, the main model achieves a 95.6% failure detection rate (FDR) at a 2.5% false alarm rate (FAR), exceeding the FDR of the evaluated observation-based, action-based, and vision-language baselines and alternative scores from the same world model. Across four training seeds, mean wrist-view FDR and FAR are 93.4% and 4.7%, respectively. Online replay yields a mean onset-relative detection delay of 0.98 s for wrist-view failures detected offline. Project website: https://janusmaple.github.io/FoMoFD/

ELMO: Emergent Representations In Imitation Learning Via Dynamic Hierarchical Action Chunking 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Robot trajectories naturally decompose into variable-length phases with distinct semantic intents, such as reaching, grasping, and lifting. However, training policies to minimize MSE treats all actions as equal, obscuring their semantic structure. While latent action models attempt to create a structured prediction target by compressing action chunks into latent representations, existing approaches rely on two-stage training and context-blind decoders. Consequently, their representations are context-agnostic motion codes (e.g., "move down") which fail to capture behavioural intent (e.g., "pick up the block"). To overcome these bottlenecks, we introduce the Emergent Latent-action MOdel (ELMO), an end-to-end framework that encodes robot trajectories as grounded semantic codes -- representations that ground task-level behavioural intent in the current environment. ELMO jointly trains a VLA planner and an observation-conditioned decoder over dynamically chunked trajectories, learning a semantically grounded action space without separate pretraining. Across standard benchmarks, ELMO outperforms comparable prior methods by 9.6 percentage points on LIBERO-Plus and by 0.75 in average task length on CALVIN. Furthermore, on Blocks -- our shortcut-free simulation and real-world generalization benchmark -- ELMO reaches a 38.6% success rate while all baselines remain below 5%, demonstrating its ability to capture the semantic structure of demonstrations. Website: https://uoft-isl.github.io/elmo.github.io/

Provably Efficient Concave Utility Reinforcement Learning with Linear Function Approximation 2026-11-25
📝 90 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

Concave utility reinforcement learning (CURL) unifies a diverse range of problems, including standard reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning, and maximum entropy exploration. Unlike standard RL, CURL generally requires estimating high-dimensional state-action occupancy measures. Consequently, existing methods for CURL either sidestep the exploration problem, are restricted to the tabular setting, or lack computationally efficient policy optimization. To address this, we study CURL in finite-horizon episodic bilinear MDPs, where the transition model admits a low-dimensional representation in known feature spaces. We use the bilinear structure to efficiently estimate occupancy measures via low-dimensional feature expectations, and propose a model-based optimistic concave utility policy optimization (OCUPO) framework. We first instantiate OCUPO in the on-policy setting, where each iteration uses independent trajectories to estimate the concave utility policy gradient, and prove an $\widetilde{O}(ε^{-4})$ sample complexity. To improve sample efficiency, we then instantiate OCUPO in the off-policy setting. We propose a novel backward feature regression subroutine that reuses trajectories collected by past policies to estimate the occupancy measure of the current policy. Under suitable technical assumptions on the policy class (satisfied, e.g., by log-linear policies with sufficiently expressive features), we prove that the resulting algorithm achieves an $\widetilde{O}(ε^{-2})$ sample complexity, up to a controllable projection error. We further extend our framework to linear MDPs and show that the resulting off-policy algorithm retains the same sample complexity. In these settings, the dependence on $ε$ matches that for standard RL with linear function approximation. Moreover, our algorithms do not rely on the integral oracle required by prior work.

Learning Scenario Reduction for Two-Stage Robust Optimization with Discrete Uncertainty 2026-11-24
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Two-Stage Robust Optimization (2RO) with discrete uncertainty is challenging, often rendering exact solutions prohibitive. Scenario reduction alleviates this issue by selecting a small, representative subset of scenarios to enable tractable computation. However, existing methods are largely problem-agnostic, operating solely on the uncertainty set without consulting the feasible region or recourse structure. In this paper, we introduce PRISE, a problem-driven sequential lookahead heuristic that constructs reduced scenario sets by evaluating the marginal impact of each scenario. While PRISE yields high-quality scenario subsets, each selection step requires solving multiple subproblems, making it computationally expensive at scale. To address this, we propose NeurPRISE, a neural surrogate model built on a GNN-Transformer backbone that encodes the per-scenario structure via graph convolution and captures cross-scenario interactions through attention. NeurPRISE is trained via imitation learning with a gain-aware ranking objective, which distills marginal gain information from PRISE into a learned scoring function for scenario ranking and selection. Extensive results on three 2RO problems show that NeurPRISE consistently achieves competitive regret relative to comprehensive methods, maintains strong calability with varying numbers of scenarios, and delivers 17-200x speedup over PRISE. NeurPRISE also exhibits strong zero-shot generalization, effectively handling instances with larger problem scales (up to 5x), more scenarios (up to 4x), and distribution shifts.

Oracle-Budgeted Protein Design with Stochastic Search and Iterative Imitation 2026-11-24
📝 Revised manuscript with additional results, incorporating reviewer feedback. Camera-ready version accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Optimizing protein sequences under tight oracle budgets requires methods that can explore a vast combinatorial mutational search space. These methods must also remain robust in the presence of surrogate noise and propose mutations on positions where substitutions are predicted to have favorable effects. We introduce SILO (Self-improvement Imitation Learning Optimization), a trajectory-level approach for oracle-budgeted protein design. SILO combines a hierarchical edit policy with incremental stochastic beam search without replacement (SBS) to explore diverse candidate trajectories. A UCB-based proxy ensemble, complemented by a proxy-derived alanine-scan-inspired fitness score (AFS), guides candidate selection for in silico oracle evaluation. The policy is then updated by next-action cross-entropy imitation on the round's best oracle-labeled trajectories. Across eight in silico protein fitness landscapes and five strong baselines from prior work, SILO achieves competitive performance within the evaluated oracle budget, often demonstrating faster early-stage improvement than other reproduced baselines. Two-sided paired t-tests show positive mean differences in favor of SILO across all eight landscapes for both maximum fitness and top-100 mean fitness, with statistically significant improvements after Holm correction on two and four landscapes, respectively. Under low-labeled data and noisy-proxy conditions, SILO shows competitive, task-dependent performance. Ablations identify SBS as the primary driver of performance, while AFS guidance and iterative imitation provide complementary, task-dependent benefits within the oracle-budgeted optimization loop. Code is available at: https://github.com/grimmlab/SILO.git

Reading Geometric Anchors: Viewpoint Generalization in Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation with RGB-Only Deployment 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Moving the camera changes where objects appear in an image, challenging RGB-based imitation learning. A common strategy pairs synthesized views with the same demonstrated action, but ignores supporting physical correspondence across views. By predicting depth with camera calibrations, 3D policies may handle it yet suffer the issue of varied observed point sets. To address these limitations, we propose ReGeoAnch, a geometry-anchored imitation learning framework for viewpoint generalization. Specifically, we build global anchors in the robot base frame and local anchors in the gripper frame, providing shared physical references across viewpoints. Furthermore, Decoupled Appearance-Guided Geometric Readout uses Point-to-Anchor Aggregation (PAA) to obtain comparable and stable geometry around anchors beyond pointwise correspondence. A controllable and decoupled information flow, Anchor-to-Policy Readout (APR), leverages complementary visual and geometric information and supports viewpoint generalization: view-dependent appearances are queried by view-invariant robot state to select task-relevant anchors, with geometric values alone to form the final readout. We further introduce Cross-View Readout Consistency to align the directions of policy readouts from paired source and synthesized target views. Experiments on 12 manipulation tasks across MetaWorld and ManiSkill3 demonstrate improved viewpoint generalization over baseline policies.

Targeted Modality Dropout for Real-Robot Manipulation Robust to Intermittent Vision Loss 2026-11-24
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 2 tables. Accepted to the 2027 IEEE/SICE International Symposium on System Integration (SII 2027)
Show abstract

Imitation learning policies that integrate multiple sensory modalities are prone to overreliance on a dominant modality, such as vision, during training, which can disrupt policy execution when that modality is lost at inference time. In this paper, we introduce Targeted Modality Dropout (TMD), in which the dependence on each modality is estimated using attention and the most dominant modality is selectively dropped during training. This is combined with entropy regularization over the dependence distribution. We evaluate six tasks on a real bimanual manipulator, simulating intermittent vision loss by jointly masking all vision tokens with 10% probability per inference chunk. Baseline successes decrease from 14/30 under normal conditions to 2/30 under vision loss, whereas TMD maintains an aggregate count of 15/30. TMD also exhibits smaller changes in action commands when chunks generated under vision loss arrive. In contrast, a conventional dropout that selects the dropped modality at random, without the entropy regularization, fails on many tasks even without vision loss.

JaxNaviSim: A GPU-Accelerated, Data-Driven Simulator and Learning Benchmark for Maritime Navigation 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Coordinating vessels through the shared waterways of busy ports grows more challenging as vessel traffic increases. Port authorities need realistic simulations to assess how arrival schedules, anchorage assignments, and routes affect vessel throughput and navigation safety. Existing AIS-based trajectory models predict a vessel's track from its past trajectory in an open-loop fashion. They do not simulate step-by-step responses to other ships or test whether a vessel collides or reaches its goal. To address this problem, we introduce JaxNaviSim, a data-driven simulator and learning benchmark coupling Automatic Identification System (AIS) data with fast, controllable traffic simulation, in which learned models steer vessels through recorded traffic. First, we release a dataset of ~141 million AIS records from over 30K vessels across eight high-density regions of the Singapore Strait, among the world's busiest waterways, with ~6.7 million vessel-hours. Unlike several existing AIS datasets which mostly cover open seas, ours captures close-quarters vessel traffic in the narrow strait. Second, we define a navigation task for each recorded vessel trajectory. A model steers that vessel every 10 s from its recorded state to its anchoring point or region exit, while surrounding ships follow their recorded tracks or are steered by the model. We implement a JAX simulator that runs these tasks in large batches on a GPU. We also incorporate multiple vessel dynamics models and sea surface currents. Finally, we benchmark imitation learning (IL), offline and online RL, and include Predictive Action Refinement (PAR) as a safety baseline that cuts collision rates to 1-3%. The comparison shows that low tracking error does not ensure arrival, and learned policies can reach most destinations yet collide in 20-45% of episodes.

ProMix: Efficient Data Mixture Optimization for Robotic Imitation via Proxy-Reference DRO 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel in open-world manipulation, yet their performance often saturates when training data are naively pooled across heterogeneous robots, tasks, and environments. Distributionally robust data mixture optimization, exemplified by Re-Mix, can recover much of this performance, but its weight search requires training full-scale policies twice on the entire corpus, placing it out of reach for most laboratories. We present ProMix, a compute-efficient instantiation of the proxy-reference DRO recipe for robotic imitation learning: a 15M-parameter proxy learns per-domain sampling weights by minimizing worst-case excess loss against a frozen reference model, and the learned weights transfer, without re-tuning, to VLA backbones up to 7B parameters. We further provide, to our knowledge, the first controlled analysis of the reference model's role in robotic DRO, showing that removing it induces over-pessimism toward noisy, low-quality domains (-3.8% average and -14.3% worst-domain success rate), and that entropy regularization is needed to prevent weight collapse. Evaluated on 9500 real-world episodes from the Open-X Embodiment dataset, ProMix improves the average success rate over uniform mixing from 68.4% to 76.2%, matching Re-Mix within evaluation noise while reducing mixture-optimization cost to a backbone-independent 11 GPU-hours - versus 16 GPU-hours at the 50M comparison scale and 192 GPU-hours at 7B for full-scale searches - with a +17.1% absolute gain on the worst-case domain.

GraSLA: A Lightweight Spatial-Language-Action Model with Grasp Hypotheses as Geometric Priors 2026-11-22
📝 18 pages, 5 figures, 12 tables. Includes supplementary material
Show abstract

This paper focuses on improving the precision and adaptability of language-conditioned manipulation policies learned through imitation learning. Recently, action-based methods have become mainstream for closed-loop control, yet implicit behavior learning from demonstrations limits their generalization beyond training conditions. To address this, explicit geometric priors offer direct guidance for action prediction. In particular, 6-DoF grasp detection generates diverse grasp hypotheses with explicit approach directions for the gripper. Yet raw hypotheses span multiple regions and lack task semantics, requiring joint reasoning with the scene and instruction. RecallFlow: Episodic Memory as the Source Distribution of Generative Robot Policies 2026-11-21
📝 15 pages, 6 figures, 12 tables. Project page: https://xujizhang.github.io/RecallFlow/
Show abstract

Diffusion and flow-matching policies have become a standard choice for visuomotor imitation learning, owing to their ability to model multimodal action distributions. In memory-dependent manipulation, the robot must act on information that is no longer visible. Existing methods feed the observation history to the policy as an additional condition, while action generation still starts from Gaussian noise. However, the policy must then pick out the relevant target from a long and redundant history, leading to spurious correlations and unreliable decisions as the memory load grows. Additionally, the initial noise carries no information about what the robot has seen, leaving generation without guidance toward the remembered target. To address these issues, we propose RecallFlow, a flow-policy framework that builds the source distribution from episodic memory. Recall-Relocalization relates the memory to the current view through a few recall slots, so that the source reflects where the remembered targets are now. Recall Anchors then turns each slot into a Gaussian component of the source, enabling generation to start close to the intended action instead of from noise. The transport network then completes the action from the current observation alone, keeping inference nearly as cheap as a memoryless policy. We further build a suite of memory-dependent tasks grouped by memory load and compare all policies under a matched protocol. Extensive experiments show that RecallFlow achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 80.4% on the public MIKASA-Robo memory benchmark, and outperforms the strongest baseline by 20.3 points on our suite and by 18.8 points on four real-robot tasks.

A context-adaptive policy framework for robust and reactive robotic manipulation via uncertainty-aware imitation learning 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Generating robust and reactive manipulation strategies that can adapt to changing context information is a challenging task in robotics. Over the years, Learning from Demonstration (LfD) has emerged as an intuitive and effective solution for generating reactive policies, particularly by following dynamical-system(DS)-based approaches. However, most state-of-the-art DS-based approaches focus on addressing the robustness limitations, overlooking the modulation of policies in response to the environment. As a result, they tend to be inflexible with respect to parameterization by task-dependent variables. In this work, we build on existing work on policy fusion and uncertainty quantification to propose a context-adaptive policy framework that combines task-parameterized, robust and reactive manipulation. For this, we use LfD to acquire a policy that is conditioned on the robot state and low-dimensional task-dependent parameters reflecting the environment. We combine the learned policy with additional uncertainty-aware policies using a Mixture of Experts (MoE) formulation to improve its out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness and convergence behavior. The approach is evaluated on the LASA handwriting dataset and on a real 7-DoF robot in three scenarios: force-conditioned grasping, manipulation of deformable food items and object-centric grasping.

FlowScope: 3D Scene Flow Pretraining Improves Surgical Robot Policies 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Imitation learning for surgical robots is limited by the cost of collecting demonstrations. Meanwhile, many robot-assisted procedures are recorded as stereo video, yet policies cannot learn from them directly; clinical video carries no action labels. We present FlowScope, which pretrains a surgical policy network on unlabeled clinical video by forecasting scene flow, the metric 3D motion of scene points, with labels computed by off-the-shelf stereo depth and optical flow models. We pretrain the trunk of an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) on a dataset of 1,149 clinical procedures (1.35M clips) and discard the flow head afterward, so the deployed policy is a standard ACT with no added inference cost. On a real surgical robotic system, FlowScope raises needle pickup success from 54% to 90% and first-handover success on needle regrasp from 4% to 52% (full task: 2% to 14%) over training from scratch (n=50 trials each), and retains its gains with a generative flow-matching action head. Ablations indicate that metric 3D motion is the strongest pretraining signal among those tested; kinematics, 2D tracks, depth alone, and scale-free 3D flow, each paired with kinematics, yield smaller or no gains. Representation analyses suggest that scene-flow pretraining encodes metric needle depth more accurately, that kinematics pretraining increases reliance on the robot state, and that needle-depth probes follow the ordering of real-robot success where offline validation error does not.

Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-19
📝 9 pages, 5 figures. Code, videos and results: https://zhanghutony.github.io/ibmarl/. A preliminary version appeared at the PhysCollaBot Workshop at IROS 2026
Show abstract

Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.

Learning Object-Centric Spatial Reasoning for Sequential Manipulation in Cluttered Environments 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted in IROS 2026 Workshop (Compositional and Modular Learning in the Era of Scaling in Robotics)
Show abstract

Sequential manipulation in clutter requires selecting which objects to remove and determining how to manipulate them. Learning both decisions jointly can complicate training and make failures difficult to diagnose. We present Unveiler, a modular framework that separates scene reasoning and object selection from action execution through a discrete object-index interface. Its Spatial Relationship Encoder (SRE) selects the best object to grasp, while a separate Action Decoder generates orientation-discretized push-grasp actions. The SRE is trained in two stages: imitation learning from heuristic demonstrations, followed by search-based policy improvement using simulated object removals. This training procedure requires no real-robot training data and allows the SRE to be paired with a different execution policy. We evaluate Unveiler in PyBullet simulation and on a physical Dofbot-Pro. In real-robot experiments, it retrieves two occluded targets in the prescribed order in 90\% of scenes, compared with 20-30\% for the heuristic, GPT-4o, and the imitation-only variant. In PyBullet, Unveiler achieves the highest task-completion rate across all six clutter-density and occlusion conditions, reaching 90.0\% under full occlusion with 6-9 objects, compared with 67.5\% for the heuristic and 60.0\% for ThinkGrasp. These results demonstrate the potential of modular, object-centric spatial reasoning for sequential manipulation in clutter.

📝 22 pages, 12 figures. Published in IEEE Access
Show abstract

Teleoperation of low-cost manipulators is attracting increasing attention as a practical means of collecting demonstration data for imitation learning. However, most existing low-cost systems rely on unilateral position control without force feedback, while implementing force-feedback bilateral teleoperation is difficult because low-cost manipulators typically have low-resolution encoders and no joint torque sensors. This paper presents a sensorless 4-channel bilateral teleoperation framework that integrates identified nonlinear dynamics compensation with a disturbance-observer-based velocity and external-force estimation scheme. Old Experience, New Lessons: Relabeling Autonomous Robot Rollouts in Interactive Imitation Learning 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Interactive imitation learning enables robots to learn from human interventions during deployment. These interactions naturally produce sparse human corrections and abundant autonomous robot experience without human action labels. Yet existing methods either leave this experience unused for action supervision or imitate historical robot actions, which can reinforce suboptimal behavior. To address this limitation, we propose Set-valued Relabeling of Autonomous experience (SeRA), a framework that learns from autonomous robot experience through set-valued supervision of policy relabels. Later policy checkpoints relabel this experience, and agreement across checkpoints filters the relabels. Since agreement does not guarantee correctness, SeRA constructs desired action sets around accepted relabels. Following Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (SDP), it trains an action-chunking diffusion policy to increase the probability of actions within these sets. A step-masked denoising loss combines accepted relabels with available human corrections in partially supervised chunks. Experiments across five simulated manipulation tasks in online and offline settings show that SeRA improves policy performance over SDP and outperforms baselines that imitate historical robot actions or exact relabels. On a real-robot tomato-picking task, SeRA achieves 80% success, compared with 40% for SDP, using the same human feedback and training budget. These results demonstrate that relabeled autonomous robot experience can complement human corrections to improve policy performance.

Imitation Learning from Human Motion Alone Does Not Guarantee Biomechanically Plausible Gait Kinetics 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Imitation learning (IL) via motion data is increasingly used in robotics and character animation, yet its ability to be applied to human biomechanics to estimate physiologically plausible joint moments without explicit kinetic information remains unclear. In this study, we examined whether motion imitation alone can estimate reasonable biological joint moments in human walking. We compared motion-only IL (MOIL) against a kinetics-aware IL (KAIL) framework, which incorporates additional ground reaction forces (GRFs) and center of pressure (CoP) in imitation rewards. A progressive kinetic reward shaping approach was used to examine the contribution of each kinetic term. Experiments were conducted using walking data from two non-disabled participants at three speeds (0.9, 1.2, and 1.5 m/s). While both MOIL and KAIL achieved comparable kinematic tracking accuracy, KAIL produced kinetics more closely aligned with biomechanical values across participants and walking speeds, reducing the root mean square errors (RMSEs) of vertical GRF and CoP by 40% and 48%, and of hip, knee, and ankle moments by 19%, 18%, and 35%, respectively, relative to MOIL. These findings highlight a fundamental limitation of MOIL approaches, as their failure to accurately estimate human-like gait kinetics may lead to erroneous interpretations of gait biomechanics and adversely affect downstream applications such as wearable robot design and the diagnosis of pathological gait.

Improving While Deployed: Learning Proactive End-to-End Driving from Imperfect Human Takeover Data 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Once deployed on vehicles, end-to-end driving models continuously encounter challenging scenarios, generating valuable human takeover data that capture both model outputs and human behaviors. However, human interventions are often imperfect, as drivers may intervene too late or unnecessarily. Consequently, direct imitation can reinforce late responses or discourage otherwise appropriate maneuvers. To address this challenge, we propose an offline post-training framework that transforms imperfect takeover data into proactive supervision. Diffusion policies have shown strong potential for robotic imitation learning, and recent extensions incorporate additional modalities to improve manipulation performance. However, these modalities often differ not only in information content but also in sensing rates and inference latencies. Existing multimodal diffusion policies typically rely on synchronous fusion or manually designed multi-frequency architectures, which either slow down high-frequency feedback or limit extensibility to new modality combinations. We propose LAG-Fusion, a latency-aware guidance fusion framework for asynchronous multimodal diffusion policy composition. LAG-Fusion allows modality-specific policies to operate at their native inference rates and contribute denoising guidance whenever available. To make asynchronous composition consistent, we derive a reference-frame rebasing rule for diffusion variables under relative action representations, enabling delayed guidance to be aligned before fusion. We instantiate LAG-Fusion in contact-rich manipulation by composing a low-frequency vision policy with a high-frequency force policy. Experiments under heterogeneous modality latencies show that LAG-Fusion improves policy responsiveness and task performance over synchronous fusion and specially designed force-aware baselines.

We introduce CLAW, a fully end-to-end self-supervised framework for learning a world model jointly with continuous latent action representations directly from action-free videos. Our approach leverages adversarial latent regularization to prevent the latent from encoding future observations, so that it captures the structure of the action. The diffusion-based world model then models environment dynamics as a distribution over future observations rather than as a single deterministic rollout. Both components are learned from observation sequences alone without any action annotations. By simultaneously training the Latent Action Model and world model, CLAW learns to reason about how inferred actions induce environment transitions from visual observations alone. We show that the resulting latent action world model supports both imitation learning from observation and goal-directed planning. In imitation learning, latent actions extracted from raw videos enable behavior cloning. For planning, CLAW generates sequences of latent actions and maps them to executable actions to reach desired goals. Extensive experiments across diverse tasks and embodiments demonstrate that CLAW produces semantically meaningful latent action representations, supports effective action transfer, and enables planning and imitation from observation, outperforming existing methods.

Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multiview observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D. MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.

Self-improving embodied agents are increasingly being developed and compared on robot simulation benchmarks. Several recent works expose the benchmark's success criteria to the learning agent, a practice we call Criteria Exposure (CE). These criteria, originally written to verify policies trained by imitation learning, often demand more than the corresponding language instructions, so a score from such a verifier may not faithfully reflect whether the instructed task was completed, especially for robot-control programs written by an agent. As embodied coding agents report ever higher benchmark scores, a natural question arises: when a coding agent is exposed to the benchmark's criteria, does it generate code that better performs the instructed task, or code that simply overfits to the verification criteria? We conduct controlled experiments with a self-improving agent with and without CE, and find that with CE the agent quickly produces code with a higher success rate under the given criteria, but this advantage disappears when the criteria are changed slightly without departing from the original instructions. More importantly, the skills the agent develops under CE transfer poorly across tasks. We further propose instruction-faithful criteria and show that they better reflect the performance of agent-generated programs.

Teleoperation is a key interface for controlling dexterous robotic hands and collecting demonstrations for imitation learning. Its effectiveness largely depends on kinematic retargeting, which maps operator hand motions to feasible and intuitive robot hand motions. Existing methods often require hand-crafted objectives, precise calibration, or global shape matching between human and robot hand spaces, making them sensitive to hand-specific tuning and less reliable across different dexterous hands. Reinforcement learning (RL) promises to unlock capabilities beyond imitation learning for Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models, but its requirement for massive real-world interaction prevents direct deployment on physical robots. Recent work attempts to use learned world models as simulators for policy optimization, yet closed-loop imagined rollouts inevitably suffer from hallucination and long-horizon error accumulation. Such errors not only degrade visual fidelity, but also mislead policy optimization by providing unreliable learning signals. We propose WoVR, a reliable world-model-based RL framework for post-training VLA policies. Instead of assuming a faithful world model, WoVR explicitly regulates how RL interacts with imperfect imagined dynamics. It improves rollout stability through a controllable action-conditioned video world model, reshapes imagined interaction to reduce effective error depth via Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts, and maintains policy--simulator alignment through World Model-Policy co-evolution. Extensive experiments demonstrate that WoVR enables stable long-horizon imagined rollouts and effective policy optimization, achieving superior LIBERO performance and consistent real-world gains across multiple robotic platforms. These results show that world models can serve as practical simulators for RL when hallucination is explicitly controlled.
