From Freehand Demonstrations to Autonomous Robotic Ultrasound: An Anatomy-Anchored Learning Framework 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Ultrasound imaging is highly operator-dependent, motivating robotic systems that reproduce expert scanning skills consistently. These skills involve coordinated interpretation of anatomical landmarks, ultrasound images, and probe-tissue interactions that are difficult to encode in hand-designed control strategies. Imitation learning offers an alternative, but its observation and action representations should reflect the anatomically structured nature of scanning. We propose an anatomy-anchored imitation-learning framework that combines anatomy-guided probe placement with learned ultrasound navigation. The placement module identifies a task-specific anatomical starting point from external visual observations and establishes the initial probe pose as the reference frame for scanning. A flow-based policy then generates scanning motions from anchor-relative probe poses, ultrasound images, probe-mounted camera views, and contact forces, while a termination head predicts task completion. This avoids continuous reliance on third-person vision and reduces dependence on the robot-patient configuration. A handheld acquisition platform enables multimodal demonstrations to be collected during natural freehand scanning without a robot. On 16 unseen participants, task success reaches 96%, 94%, 88%, and 94% for kidney, gallbladder, and liver standard-plane acquisition and radial artery sweeping, respectively. Compared with a fine-tuned pi_0 model, success on the abdominal tasks improves by 10-42 percentage points, with lower time-to-target, peak contact force, and trajectory jerk. Ablations support the complementary roles of anatomical anchoring, egocentric vision, and force feedback. End-to-end trials with automatic placement achieve 90-100% success, and the policies transfer without retraining to a second robot with 83-100% success.

RAPAC-DP: Response-Aligned Pending-Action Compensation for Diffusion Policies under Delayed Execution 2026-10-23
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Cloud-side inference expands the computational resources available to imitation-learning policies, but communication and inference latencies create a mismatch between prediction and execution. We propose RAPAC-DP, a response-aligned pending-action compensation framework for diffusion- and flow-based action generators. By conditioning on actions committed during inference, RAPAC-DP generates a full-horizon action chunk aligned with the scheduled handover time. A lightweight pending-action encoder and LoRA adapters learn this compensation while keeping the pretrained base policy frozen. We construct delay-conditioned training samples from delay-free demonstrations, requiring neither an explicit dynamics model nor additional data collected under delayed execution. In simulation benchmarks at the largest evaluated delays, RAPAC-DP retains 81.4\% of its overall delay-free performance on Kinetix and achieves a mean success rate of 63.3\% across three RoboMimic tasks. Across 450 physical trials on a Franka Panda manipulator, RAPAC-DP increases the high-delay mean success rate from 28.9\% for RTC to 60.0\% across static and conveyor-based pick-and-place tasks.

DiscoDemo: Discovering Efficient and Diverse Robot Demonstrations for Imitation Learning 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Behavior cloning for robotic manipulation is highly sensitive to the coverage of its training data, yet collecting diverse successful demonstrations remains costly. We study reinforcement learning (RL) not as a deployable controller, but as a data generator for downstream visuomotor imitation learning. Our key premise is that multiple successful solution modes cover a broader range of successful trajectories than repeatedly collecting a single narrow solution. We introduce DiscoDemo, which trains a privileged latent-conditioned RL generator with a parallel reverse curriculum and a success-gated diversity objective to discover diverse solutions while preserving task success. The trained generator produces multiple successful closed-loop behaviors, which are safety-filtered and exported as a static dataset for downstream imitation learning. Across four manipulation tasks, DiscoDemo produces broader successful-state coverage and improves downstream visuomotor policies in both simulation and the real world. Averaged across tasks, downstream success increases to 62% from 40% for the strongest baseline in simulation, and to 78% from 39% on the real robot, respectively. These results suggest that successful solution diversity improves imitation learning for a pretrained flow-matching VLA and remains complementary to corrective data collection. Project page: https://davian-robotics.github.io/DiscoDemo

Emergent Neural Automaton Policies: Learning Symbolic Structure from Visuomotor Trajectories 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Scaling robot learning to long-horizon tasks remains a formidable challenge. While end-to-end policies often lack the structural priors needed for effective long-term reasoning, traditional neuro-symbolic methods rely heavily on hand-crafted symbolic priors. We introduce ENAP (Emergent Neural Automaton Policy), a neuro-symbolic imitation learning framework in which a bi-level policy adaptively emerges from visuomotor demonstrations. Specifically, we employ adaptive clustering and an extension of the L* algorithm to learn the task structure from demonstrations as a Mealy state machine, which serves as an interpretable high-level planner capturing latent task modes. This discrete structure then guides a low-level reactive residual network to learn precise continuous control via behavior cloning (BC). By explicitly modeling discrete transitions and continuous residuals, ENAP achieves sample efficiency and interpretability without task-specific labels. Experiments on complex manipulation and long-horizon tasks show that ENAP outperforms state-of-the-art end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) policies by up to 27% in low-data regimes. ENAP can also distill task structure from the latent space of a pre-trained VLA, surpassing it on CALVIN benchmark with 39% fewer parameters (Fig. 1).

HiSTEVE 1.0: Harnessing Human-Interface VLA Agents for Open-World Games 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Building virtual embodied agents that operate in open-ended games like human players is a long-standing goal requiring both long-horizon autonomy and reliable native-interface control. As a procedurally generated open world, Minecraft provides a natural testbed for studying the integration of these capabilities. Existing agents typically provide either native visuomotor control without long-horizon autonomy or high-level planning executed through environment APIs or predefined skills. To bridge this gap, we propose HiSTEVE, a hierarchical VLA agent with a capability-aware agent harness. At the orchestration level, we equip the harness with structured memory for tracking progress and a VLM planner for selecting subtasks within the VLA's capabilities. To reliably determine subtask completion, we further introduce a policy-coupled task completion state detector that conditions on VLA policy context to signal candidate completion. At the control level, a single VLA executes all subtasks with vision-only perception and native keyboard-and-mouse actions. To mitigate interference between heterogeneous GUI and non-GUI interactions, we introduce regime-specific RMSNorm branches and separate mouse-movement token embeddings. We also train the VLA through world knowledge injection, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with environment feedback to improve reliability and recovery. HiSTEVE achieves state-of-the-art atomic-task performance, scoring 85.0% on MineStudio Bench and a 37.4% average success rate on OpenHA, while also completing long-horizon tasks such as crafting an iron pickaxe from scratch like human players.

Revisiting Distribution Matching for State-to-Vision Policy Distillation in Laparoscopic Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

End-to-end control policies trained via Reinforcement Learning (RL) have emerged as an alternative to conventional surgical robotic control approaches, with recent studies exploring state-based policies trained on privileged state observations. However, such approaches rely on precise physical states that are difficult to obtain reliably in surgical environments. Vision-based RL provides an alternative by learning manipulation policies directly from RGB observations, avoiding explicit state estimation and manually engineered perception pipelines while enabling policies to acquire task-relevant visual representations. Nevertheless, obtaining vision-based policies via pure RL methods for surgical manipulation remains challenging. Thus, imitation learning (IL) becomes another solution, yet it requires a large number of expert demonstrations, which are difficult to obtain in minimally invasive surgery. In this work, we propose a framework for state-to-vision policy distillation in laparoscopic robotic manipulation. Specifically, a state-based policy with privileged observations is employed as the teacher, and a vision-based policy learns from RGB observations and robot proprioceptive information, which requires less expert demonstrations than IL and introduces superior performance than RL methods. We conduct extensive experiments on various surgical manipulation tasks in the simulated environment. Experimental results show that, in the context of laparoscopic robotic manipulation, training vision-based policies with general-purpose RL methods is challenging, whereas our proposed state-to-vision policy distillation leads to more consistent and stable performance across tasks.

Tactile-Based Robotic Manipulation: From Mechanics-Driven to Data-Driven Perspectives 2026-10-21
📝 30 pages. Author accepted manuscript. Posted with permission from the Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems, Volume 10; copyright 2027 the author(s), https://www.annualreviews.org. Published version: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-control-032125-030330
Show abstract

Tactile sensing provides robots with direct access to physical interactions at the contact interface, making it a critical modality for robust and dexterous manipulation. Over the past several decades, tactile-based manipulation research has evolved from mechanics-driven approaches, which explicitly interpret tactile signals through contact mechanics and task-specific reasoning, to data-driven approaches that learn tactile representations and manipulation policies directly from data. This paper reviews the development of tactile-based manipulation from these two complementary perspectives. We first introduce the fundamentals of tactile sensing, and then review, from a mechanics-driven perspective, how those signals form manipulation-relevant state representations and subsequently influence the robot action. We then examine the recent advances in data-driven tactile manipulation, covering imitation learning, reinforcement learning, vision-language-action models, and predictive models. Finally, we discuss open challenges and future opportunities toward scalable, generalizable, and robust tactile-enabled robotic systems.

Optimistic Policy Regularization: Elite-Statistic Reward Shaping 2026-10-21
Show abstract

On-policy agents discard trajectories after each update, including rare high-return episodes. We propose Optimistic Policy Regularization (OPR), which turns those episodes into a closed-form reward bonus: we estimate an elite action prior from top-quartile episodes and add a \reviewblue{bounded transform of the log-ratio} of the prior to the policy's action distribution. OPR needs no auxiliary network, value gate, off-policy correction, or adversarial loop. It is a one-line modification of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)'s reward; yet, on 49 Atari games at 10M steps, $5\times$ fewer agent decisions than the 200M-frame baselines, it reaches a 148.4\% median human-normalized score (HNS; 196.9\% interquartile mean): higher scores than published PPO on $36/49$ games ($p{=}0.0014$) and a higher aggregate median than self-imitation learning, which falls well below OPR when frames and backbone are matched. We pair the method with a theory of when it works. The underlying log-ratio has an exact forward-KL interpretation; its pre-advantage placement propagates the signal through Generalized Advantage Estimation; and, under an idealized local update, its first-order effect depends on a prior-advantage alignment whose sign indicates help or harm. Since alignment varies across tasks and runs, we also study an online gating extension. On a ten-game set, it outperforms the backbone by $+17.4$ median HNS on average across seeds. Controlled experiments test temporal propagation and the tradeoffs of state conditioning and gating, supporting OPR as a simple regularizer with an analysis of where to shape.

TerraTransfer++: Demonstration-Free End-to-End Planner 2026-10-21
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks and real-world deployments. Its standard training recipe, however, is expensive across all existing paradigms: imitation learning requires fleet-scale driving logs whose behavior distribution only thinly covers the safety-critical tail; closed-loop reinforcement learning in a photorealistic simulator, while offering diverse behaviors, is bottlenecked by sample-efficiency, per-step cost of rendering, and a forward pass through a large vision backbone. Self-play in vectorized simulators changes the economics: at millions of rollout steps per second, one can obtain a state distribution with natural diversity in collisions, near-misses, and recoveries that no driving log contains, and a highly reactive policy emerges from a basic driving-quality reward. Our approach exploits this asymmetry. We pretrain a single policy by self-play, distill its rollouts into a flow-matching trajectory teacher that also plans, and train lightweight adapters on a frozen pretrained vision backbone to reproduce the latent space of either teacher through the action KL divergence, a batch-relational low-rank structural loss, and, for the planner, a trajectory-matching loss. Every target traces back to the self-play policy, so alignment never supervises against a logged trajectory: a paired dataset of (image, scene-state) frames suffices, with no need for the curated expert demonstrations that imitation pretraining is built on. From this one self-play teacher we obtain two end-to-end students: a closed-loop policy that outperforms every published imitation-based end-to-end method on the photorealistic 3D Gaussian splatting closed-loop benchmark HUGSim, and a planner that reaches 59.4 EPDMS on NAVSIM navhard, above every published method including DriveZero-Scale (57.1), and 95.5 PDMS on navtest, without human demonstrations.

AtomicRL: Atomic Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown promise in robotic manipulation, but imitation learning (IL) alone often struggles to master complex long-horizon skills. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers an effective avenue for further improving VLA policies. However, existing VLA-RL methods typically suffer from delayed and biased credit assignment due to trajectory-level sparse rewards, while imbalanced subtask mastery creates bottlenecks that limit overall performance. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework that leverages atomic task structures for reliable credit assignment and adaptive exploration. AtomicRL introduces Semantic-Aware Policy Optimization (SAPO), which uses semantic-aware gating to constrain TD residuals from failed atomic subtasks from propagating across task boundaries, mitigating credit contamination while preserving intra-subtask credit assignment and value bootstrapping. In addition, Bottleneck-Adaptive Target Exploration (BATE) dynamically coordinates task sampling and local exploration incentives based on the mastery of individual atomic subtasks, directing learning toward the remaining bottlenecks. Finally, we develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that bridges online RL in simulation with real-world deployment, facilitating practical transfer to physical robots. On LIBERO-LONG, AtomicRL achieves absolute performance gains of 3.6% and 4.0% over PPO across two VLA architectures. On CALVIN ABC-D, it improves full-task performance by up to 9.5% over PPO. Combined with the Real2Sim2Real pipeline, AtomicRL further improves real-world task performance by an average of 21.3%. These results demonstrate that AtomicRL effectively improves the learning efficiency and manipulation performance of VLA policies on long-horizon tasks.

Touch sensing is beneficial for solving a wide variety of manipulation tasks. While there exists a wide range of tactile sensors with different properties, exploiting the fusion of multiple heterogeneous tactile sensors to improve manipulation learning remains underexplored. We present Multi-Resolution Tactile Sensing (MiTaS), a representation framework that leverages multiple tactile sensors operating at different temporal resolutions in order to solve complex contact-rich manipulation tasks. DriveMLP: When Training Strategy Outperforms Model Complexity in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-21
📝 Accepted at 2026 Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL)
Show abstract

Learning motion planning policies that remain reliable under closed-loop execution is a central challenge in Autonomous Driving (AD). Imitation Learning (IL) scales well to large AD datasets but suffers from distribution shift, causal confusion, and compounding errors under closed-loop execution. In contrast, Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically requires handcrafted rule-based rewards and massive simulation. We study a simple closed-loop IL framework that uses RL purely as an optimization tool, guided by a trajectory-distance reward derived from logged expert data. We refer to this training strategy as Expert-Guided RL (EGRL). Through a controlled empirical study we find evidence that the training procedure, rather than model size and complexity, accounts for a substantial part of the performance gains. We then introduce DriveMLP, a lightweight MLP policy trained with EGRL, as a simple instantiation of these findings. It achieves a score of 86.51 on the nuPlan Val14 Reactive benchmark, a +3.2 point improvement over the previous best IL planner (Flow Planner), with >15x faster inference. Code is available at: https://github.com/alessandro-canevaro/DriveMLP .

MA-UITD: Offline Multi-Agent Reward Inference for Characterizing Continuous Social Behavior 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Multi-agent inverse reinforcement learning offers a powerful framework not only for imitation learning but also for understanding the neural computations supporting social interactions and decision-making. Inferring rewards from interacting agents is challenging because observed choices reflect both personal preferences and expectations about others. Existing methods often require additional environment interaction, repeated policy optimization as an inner loop, or restrictive assumptions about environment dynamics and action spaces, limiting their applicability to fixed behavioral datasets. We introduce Multi-Agent Unilateral Inverse Temporal Difference Learning (MA-UITD), an offline method for inferring explicit agent-specific rewards from joint demonstrations in continuous-state environments with either discrete or bounded continuous actions. Its core idea is to represent the long-term consequences of each agent's own actions through belief-conditioned successor features that average over other agents' behavior. Shared reward features and agent-specific preference vectors connect these unilateral values to the target reward function, while inverse temporal-difference consistency and noise-contrastive behavioral learning enable inference without known dynamics or an inner policy-optimization loop. Across simulated cooperative, competitive, and general-sum tasks, MA-UITD outperforms baselines in discrete-action settings and infers behaviorally compatible reward profiles in continuous-action settings. When applied to experimentally collected social-foraging trajectories, MA-UITD inferred distinct spatial and interaction-dependent reward structures for leaders and followers, providing a quantitative characterization of latent behavioral objectives that may be relevant for understanding the neural computations underlying social behavior.

PolyUMI: Accessible Visual-Tactile-Audio Data Collection for Object Inference and Manipulation 2026-10-20
📝 9 pages, 10 figures, preprint
Show abstract

Humans typically rely on vision, touch, hearing, and proprioception to perceive contact and adapt their actions during manipulation. Providing robots with comparable responsiveness therefore requires hardware that can retain and use these complementary sensory signals. Most imitation-learning systems, however, observe demonstrations primarily through vision and proprioception, limiting access to contact information that is difficult to infer visually. We present PolyUMI, an open-source platform for scalable visual--tactile--audio demonstration collection and robot deployment. Its lightweight, wireless handheld gripper records synchronized wrist-camera, optical tactile, contact-audio, and proprioceptive observations without requiring a tethered workstation. Together, PolyUMI and VisTA provide an accessible pipeline for collecting multimodal demonstrations and learning policies that perceive physical interaction beyond vision. What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration 2026-10-20
📝 9 pages, 8 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Imitation learning increasingly relies on novice operators, whose demonstrations fall short of experts' in number and inter-trial consistency. Operators act on what they are shown, yet how the rendering of the robot affects their demonstrations, and the policies trained on them, has not been examined. We study this question with PointHand, a teleoperation interface that renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints at its fingertips and joints, the same keypoints a policy can observe. In a simulated mug-in-box task, 18 novices and 4 experts collected 2,200 trials under three renderings: the real shape of the hand, all keypoints emphasized, or the thumb and index finger alone emphasized. The novices shown the thumb and index finger emphasized collected 441 successful demonstrations from 600 trials, against 320 for those shown the real shape. We then trained diffusion policies on these demonstrations, replacing the scene point cloud and proprioceptive state they observe with the hand keypoints and two object keypoints. Within 400 steps these demonstrations went from the weakest of the three novice conditions under the point-cloud policy (46.3%) to the strongest under the keypoint policy (57.3%), a gain of +11.0 percentage points in all five seeds. Those collected with all keypoints emphasized changed by at most +0.7 percentage points, and in no more than three seeds. The value of a demonstration therefore depends on the rendering shown during collection and on what the policy observes together. A shared representation can pay off on both sides, in the number of successful demonstrations and in the success rate of the policy trained on them. Project page: https://omron-sinicx.github.io/point-hand/

Learn the Task, Not the Schedule: Reducing Spurious Cross-Arm Coupling in Bimanual Imitation Learning 2026-10-19
Show abstract

A bimanual demonstration specifies both what the robot should do and one particular schedule for when each arm should act. Standard imitation learning does not distinguish task-required cross-arm constraints from incidental scheduling choices, and may therefore exploit opposite-arm progress as a shortcut cue, creating spurious cross-arm coupling even where relative timing is irrelevant for the current task phase. We introduce Constraint-preserving Temporal Recombination (CTR), a training-time data intervention that preserves recorded arm-local sensorimotor content while retiming the corresponding segments to realize admissible schedules that preserve task-required cross-arm constraints. We use CTR to train Diffusion Policy, Action Chunking with Transformers, and $π_{0.5}$, and evaluate them on four simulated and four real-robot tasks. Across six real-robot bimanual progress-state initializations, CTR raises worst-state success from 56% for the strongest non-CTR schedule mixture to 76%. With $π_{0.5}$ on an Independent task, a 4 s pause transfers 2.56 s of delay to the opposite arm under the Concurrent schedule distribution, but only 0.25 s with CTR. On real robots, CTR violates task constraints in only 0--4% of trials, versus 36--98% for narrow-schedule baselines. Code will be released at https://github.com/MINT-SJTU/CTR.

TRACT: Temporally Routed Action Chunks with Chronological Phase Authority for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-19
📝 6 pages, 3 figures, Accepted for presentation at the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Biomimetics (ROBIO 2026)
Show abstract

Action chunking shortens the effective decision horizon of robot imitation learning by predicting multiple future actions, while conventional phase conditioning describes the current control instant. When a predicted horizon crosses a procedural boundary, assigning the current phase to the entire chunk creates a structural temporal mismatch. We present TRACT, which factorizes phase-structured action chunking into an accepted current phase and a single CURRENT-to-NEXT boundary inside the future horizon. A task-local graph constrains chronological phase authority, and a cumulative boundary distribution monotonically routes future queries through phase-specific query and action paths. For contact execution, a causal response-deficit integrator compares policy intent with ACK-eligible subsequent motion, accumulates arm compensation when directional response is suppressed, and decays after confirmed recovery. Across six real-robot variants with ten trials each, full TRACT achieves 10/10 full-sequence success, 99.00 [88.75, 100.00]% median [min, max] wipe completion, zero observed phase ambiguity, and zero stalls. Under the current complete method package and evaluation setting, the routed representation obtains better observed task results than the flat package (6/10 vs. 3/10 success; 77.08% vs. 8.03% median wipe completion). Chronological authority reduces observed phase ambiguity from 8/10 to 0/10, and response integration reduces stalls from 4/10 to 0/10. The package comparison does not isolate routing from other generator-package differences.

Progress-Guided Preference Generation for Aligning Generative Control Policies 2026-10-19
📝 CoRL 2026 (project site: https://pgpg2026.github.io/)
Show abstract

Generative policies have achieved strong performance in imitation learning tasks, but their success is often constrained by the limited availability of expert demonstrations. Although preference-based fine-tuning can leverage suboptimal rollouts to improve performance, existing approaches heavily rely on expensive human annotations. In this work, we introduce Progress-Guided Preference Generation (PGPG), an unsupervised preference alignment framework that eliminates the need for human-in-the-loop supervision. Our method leverages ordered coverage alignment to measure sequential progress relative to a small set of expert demonstrations, enabling the automatic extraction of pseudo-preference labels from autonomously collected rollouts. Furthermore, we introduce key enhancements to improve the reliability of these progress-guided preferences, enabling effective fine-tuning of pretrained generative policies without requiring manually designed reward functions. Extensive experiments across a suite of challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks demonstrate that PGPG outperforms standard supervised fine-tuning baselines. Moreover, our conceptually simple framework is broadly applicable across multiple classes of generative policies, demonstrating strong versatility when integrated with both diffusion and flow-matching policies.

📝 9 pages, 6 figures, 2 table
Show abstract

Data-driven learning for general-purpose robotic manipulation presents a fundamental trade-off between data-collection cost and adaptability to real-world environments. Imitation learning (IL) requires costly human demonstrations, whereas simulation-based learning is vulnerable to sim-to-real gaps in dynamics and sensor observations. We propose ReDOA (Real-World Distillation via Observation Alignment), a framework that decouples control learning from perception learning to learn image-based policies in the real world. AQUAACT: A Joystick-Level Underwater Teleoperation Dataset and Closed-Loop Benchmark 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Underwater robots support a growing range of work, from inspecting offshore structures and pipelines to surveying reefs and accompanying divers. Most of these tasks, however, are still flown by hand by a pilot, which raises the question of whether they can be carried out autonomously instead. On land, vision-language-action (VLA) models already let robots carry out tasks autonomously from language instructions, which makes them a natural candidate for taking the pilot's place. Underwater, however, there is little data to train such models. We take inspiration from how these tasks are flown today: the pilot watches the camera image and steers with a joystick, whose commands the flight controller turns into motion. We therefore record the pilots and introduce AQUAACT, to our knowledge the first publicly available language-annotated underwater dataset pairing camera images with human joystick commands. It comprises 1.9 hours of real-world and 17.9 hours of simulated demonstrations, both flown by human pilots, together with the vehicle's inertial measurements. Experiments with AQUAACT show that its simulated demonstrations transfer to real data and greatly reduce the real demonstrations a policy needs. A closed-loop benchmark of seven policies in simulation, from imitation-learning baselines to VLA models, each trained with its default recipe, shows that these tasks remain far from solved: no policy completes more than a fifth of the episodes. Pretraining on land data gives large VLA models no advantage over small policies trained from scratch, a gap that land-trained models have yet to close underwater.

Continually Evolving Skill Knowledge in Vision Language Action Model 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models show promising knowledge accumulation ability through pretraining, yet continual learning remains challenging. Existing continual imitation learning (CIL) methods often require additional parameters or modules, limiting scalability for large VLA models. We propose Stellar VLA, a knowledge-driven CIL framework without parameter growth, featuring two variants: T-Stellar for task-centric modeling and TS-Stellar for hierarchical modeling of reusable skills across tasks. Stellar VLA jointly optimizes task representations and a self-evolving knowledge space, with knowledge-posterior-guided expert routing using knowledge relations and Top-K semantic embeddings for task specialization.
