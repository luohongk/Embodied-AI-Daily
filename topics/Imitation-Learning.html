Rollout then Recycle: 3,718 Labelled Real-Robot Episodes from Benchmarking 7 Policies on 12 Tasks 2026-11-02
📝 4 pages, 1 figure, 2 tables. Accepted to the "Oops, I Erred" Workshop at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

We release all 3,718 episodes from the ArmnetBench v0.1 evaluation (arXiv:2607.24481), which contains 600 teleoperated demonstrations and 3,118 rollouts of seven imitation-learning and vision-language-action policies on 12 tabletop tasks, recorded on low-cost SO-101 arms; 73% of the rollouts are failures. Every policy for a task was trained or fine-tuned on the same 50 demonstrations and rolled out on the same cell, so the corpus contains many policies' failures on shared tasks. An operator labelled every rollout successful, suboptimal or failure. Each episode has three camera views, joint states and actions and is available on the Hugging Face Hub in LeRobot v3.0 and a reward-model format. This note documents how the episodes were collected and the limitations that could affect their use.

KlaskTron-IL: A Contact-Rich Adversarial Imitation Learning Benchmark with a Validated Sim-to-Real Bridge 2026-11-02
Show abstract

KlaskTron is an open-hardware physical platform for two-player adversarial reinforcement learning, with a learned actuator model that supports zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of trained policies. Building on this platform, we contribute KlaskTron-IL: a multi-tier imitation learning dataset (80k human demonstration timesteps collected directly on the physical robot, 320k symmetry-augmented timesteps, and synthetic expert trajectories up to 5M steps generated by the zero-shot-validated PPO policy), and diagnostic baselines that expose three characteristic failure modes of standard IL methods under contact-rich adversarial dynamics: BC's data scaling plateau, GAIL's interpretable but weak-gradient discriminator (Spearman $ρ= -1.0$ peg-ball proximity recovery, but a logit span under 0.1), and an asymmetric BC-prior x reward-density interaction. Because the policy interface used throughout this paper is the same one the actuator model bridges to hardware, a policy trained on this benchmark can run unchanged on the physical robot, as we demonstrate zero-shot for the strongest BC baseline, so long as its commands respect the actuator model's $\pm 0.2$ m/s identification range.

Dynamics-Aware Meta-Imitation for Generalization to Unseen Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-02
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Imitation Learning aims to learn skills from extensive observations and demonstrations for robots, so it suffers from data scarcity and environment generalization. The existing methods predominantly focus on imitation from in-domain tasks and consequently struggle with generalization to unseen tasks. To bridge this generalization gap, we propose the \textbf{D}ynamics-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{M}eta-\textbf{I}mitation (DAMI) framework. By integrating meta-learning to construct a shared skill space, DAMI equips agents for rapid adaptation to novel tasks. We introduce the Visual-Motor Trajectory (VMT) module to capture complex spatio-temporal dynamics within the task latent space. Furthermore, we propose the Unpaired Unified Task (U2T) block to fuse unstructured multimodal observations. To coordinate these representations, we integrate a Task-Conditioned Feature Modulation (TCFM) mechanism customized for modulating low-level 3D features. By capturing intrinsic dynamics from a random complete reference demonstration, our framework learns the underlying task logic rather than memorizing static cues, ensuring effective generalization. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art baselines regarding direct inference on seen tasks and adaptation to unseen tasks via few-shot fine-tuning.

How to Peel with a Knife: Aligning Fine-Grained Manipulation with Human Preference 2026-11-02
📝 Project page can be found at https://toruowo.github.io/peel
Show abstract

Many essential manipulation tasks - such as food preparation, surgery, and craftsmanship - remain difficult for autonomous robots. These tasks are characterized not only by contact-rich, force-sensitive dynamics, but also by their "implicit" success criteria: unlike pick-and-place, task quality in these domains is continuous and subjective (e.g. how well a potato is peeled), making quantitative evaluation and reward design difficult. We present a learning framework for such tasks, using peeling with a knife as a representative example. Our approach follows a two-stage pipeline: first, we learn a robust initial policy via force-aware data collection and imitation learning, enabling generalization across object variations; second, we refine the policy through preference-based finetuning using a learned reward model that combines quantitative task metrics with qualitative human feedback, aligning policy behavior with human-evaluated task quality. Using only 50~200 peeling trajectories, our system achieves 100% same-produce success on cucumbers, apples, and potatoes, with performance improving by up to 40% through preference-based finetuning. Remarkably, policies trained on one produce category also transfer zero-shot to selected held-out produce categories, achieving 50% success on zucchini, 90% on pear, and 80% on daikon radish.

ODE Policy: Robot Learning via Action Dynamics, Not Actions 2026-11-01
📝 35 pages, 23 figures
Show abstract

Most robot policies, whether trained by imitation learning (IL) or reinforcement learning (RL), map observations to action values at discrete control steps and leave the continuity of physical motion to be learned implicitly. We propose ODE policy, which learns the dynamics of the action rather than the action itself. A neural ordinary differential equation (ODE) learns the vector field of the action by matching the integrated actions to the targets over short action chunks in IL and single control steps in RL. A lightweight diversity head captures multi-modal behavior by randomizing the direction of the field. At deployment, each action is integrated from the previous one, so the executed trajectory is continuous by construction. We evaluate it as a visuomotor policy, as the action head of vision-language-action (VLA) and world action models (WAMs), and as the actor of model-based RL against 14 baselines on unimanual, bimanual, dexterous, locomotion, and loco-manipulation tasks. In IL, against flow matching, it reaches 5.9$\times$ the success, 5.4$\times$ the generalization, and 3.9$\times$ the mode balance after one epoch, and generates actions 10.8$\times$ faster. It mostly moves more smoothly than human demonstrations. On 14 real-world tasks across single-arm, dual-arm, dexterous, and humanoid robots, it achieves the highest success rate. In RL, it further speeds up SuperMimic for humanoid motion tracking and smooths its actions. Efficient in learning, data, parameters, and execution, ODE policy offers a promising path to scaling robot learning.

ScheduleGen: Demonstration Generation via Skill Scheduling for Multi-Arm Imitation Learning 2026-10-31
📝 22 pages, 9 figures, 5 tables. Project website and videos: https://njawale42.github.io/ScheduleGen/
Show abstract

Multi-arm robot platforms, including bimanual and humanoid robots, have the capability to complete tasks with lower cycle times than single-arm systems, but they are more difficult to safely control due to the presence of multi-arm constraints. The paradigm of training generalist robot policies from massive datasets is a promising approach to address general-purpose multi-arm manipulation, but the acquisition of large multi-arm manipulation datasets remains a bottleneck. Automated demonstration generation can alleviate this burden by synthesizing new demonstrations from a handful of source demonstrations, but existing algorithms ignore multi-arm constraints and as a result often induce unsafe and unsuccessful behaviors. We address this limitation by introducing ScheduleGen, an integrated planning & scheduling algorithm for multi-arm demonstration generation. We evaluate ScheduleGen on 28 simulation task variants, including seven externally authored RoboDojo tasks and settings inaccessible to prior systems; it achieves 79.6% mean data-generation success and trains policies with 65.5% mean success. ScheduleGen policies outperform those trained on a state-of-the-art data generation baseline, and achieve 50-80% success across eight real-world variants through zero-shot sim-to-real and real-to-sim-to-real transfer. Tactile sensing plays an important role in imitation learning for contact-rich manipulation by providing direct observations of physical contact with objects. Recently, several tactile foundation models (TFMs) have been proposed to learn general-purpose tactile representations through pretraining on large-scale tactile datasets. However, how these pretrained tactile representations should be leveraged for end-to-end imitation learning remains insufficiently explored. Learning from an Imitable Teacher: Competition Solution for the MetaFine Challenge 2026-10-31
📝 2026 CoRL MetaFine Open Competition 2nd place solution
Show abstract

The MetaFine Open Competition (RoboFineMani workshop, CoRL 2026) evaluates fine-grained manipulation beyond binary success. Its five simulated tasks, from grasping a bottle by an instructed part to inserting letter-shaped pegs with 4 mm clearance, are scored on perception under lighting and camera changes, instruction understanding, and behavior. This report presents Team Friday's solution, which got second place with an overall score of 0.947, more than twice the $π_0$ baseline. Instead of fine-tuning a large vision-language-action (VLA) model, we train one 19M-parameter policy per task with a separate instruction classifier and focus on the data, all generated in the simulator by a privileged scripted teacher, including its corrections of the student's mistakes. By analyzing failed episodes, we find that a teacher that almost always succeeds can still teach a memoryless student badly: its pauses become stalls, its fixed habits become shortcuts, and it never shows the errors the student makes. Making the teacher \emph{imitable} raised the official score on letter insertion, the hardest task, from 0.44 to 0.82 with 32\% more data and the same architecture, and finally to 0.88. We summarize practical lessons on demonstration data and evaluation, which we hope will help in imitation learning for precise manipulation.

Does Validation Loss Predict Real-Robot Success? Evidence from Four Imitation-Learning Policies on a Low-Cost Arm 2026-10-30
📝 21 pages, 16 figures, 5 tables. Companion to arXiv:2610.37170. Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/fysical/greenball_pool_225_v1
Show abstract

Held-out validation loss is the standard offline signal for choosing checkpoints and comparing models, but its relation to closed-loop success on a real robot is rarely measured. We measure it for ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA and $π_{0.5}$ (action expert only), trained on nested subsets of 12 to 200 demonstrations of a pick-and-place task on an SO-101 arm. We score 82 checkpoints on 25 held-out demonstrations, with each policy's training loss and with the error of the actions it outputs, and compare the scores with 930 bench rollouts. We also score all 700 intermediate checkpoints of 70 training runs, and recover the ball positions of every training, held-out and bench episode from the front camera. Within each policy, hold-out action error correlates with bench success (Spearman $ρ$ from -0.23 to -0.88), but no more strongly than the number of training episodes alone, and not at all among seeds trained on the same data. Across policies the ranking is inverted ($ρ$ = +0.90 at 200 episodes): the policy with the lowest hold-out error succeeded in 3 of 110 rollouts. The most common failure, the arm never leaving its rest pose, ended 96% of rollouts for Diffusion Policy trained on untrimmed demonstrations and 47% for SmolVLA, and cannot occur in a hold-out set made of demonstrations. Among rollouts in which the arm moved, SmolVLA's hold-out action error predicts success well ($ρ$ = -0.81). For the diffusion and flow-matching policies, hold-out loss rises two- to five-fold during training while hold-out action error falls by up to 29%. Held-out metrics are a sound diagnostic of learning but no substitute for evaluation on the robot, and we recommend reporting initiation failure and success among started rollouts as separate bench outcomes.

FOCUS: First-Order Control Using Optimal Transport Supervision for Imitation from Observations 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Physical Understanding for Decision-Making
Show abstract

Imitation learning from observations must recover behavior from state-only demonstrations without known temporal correspondence. Differentiable simulators provide pathwise policy gradients, but differentiating through long, contact-rich trajectories can make optimization unstable. We introduce FOCUS, which combines local optimal-transport alignment with short-horizon differentiable actor-critic learning. FOCUS matches agent rollout windows to multiple sampled expert segments using a soft best-of-$K$ objective that emphasizes locally compatible matches. The resulting transport-based costs provide differentiable imitation rewards, while a learned critic bootstraps value beyond the rollout horizon. FOCUS requires neither temporally aligned trajectories nor expert-aligned initialization or teacher forcing. Across contact-rich locomotion tasks in DFlex, including a musculoskeletal humanoid controlled by 152 muscle activations, FOCUS improves sample efficiency and training stability over adversarial imitation-from-observation methods and prior differentiable imitation baselines.

Decompose and Reorganize: Planning with Primitives and Visuomotor Policies Learned from Demonstrations 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted by T-RO. Website: https://dr-lfd.github.io/DR-LfD-website
Show abstract

Successfully automating dexterous, long-horizon bimanual manipulation requires frameworks capable of both high-level reasoning and fine-grained execution. Traditional task and motion planning (TAMP), while excellent at symbolic planning, is often brittle in contact-rich operations. Simultaneously, imitation learning (IL), while effective in manipulation tasks with visual feedback, is limited by its low capability in spatial generalization and multi-stage operation. To reconcile their complementary strengths and limitations, we propose DR-LfD (Decomposed and Reorganized Skills Learned from Demonstrations), a framework that integrates visuomotor policies into a TAMP-gated decision-making system. Based on contact relationships, DR-LfD decomposes human demonstrations into atomic skills, which are reproduced as visuomotor policies or object-centric primitives. The initiation, termination, and constraints of the visuomotor policies are carefully modeled and implemented in a TAMP-compatible form, enabling reorganization of skills learned from different sources. DR-LfD transforms the learning problem from one requiring exponential demonstration data over possible skill sequences to one whose demonstration burden scales with the number of distinct skill types, with limited data for each skill. Real-world and simulation benchmarks demonstrate data-efficient skill composition under unseen setups and physical constraints. On two DexMimicGen tasks, DR-LfD with 100 demonstrations outperforms diffusion-based imitation policies with 1000.

Cross-Embodiment Motion Planning by Coupling Body-Agnostic World Model with Differentiable Ego-Dynamics 2026-10-30
📝 8 pages
Show abstract

Motion planning in clutter is embodiment-specific: the feasible free space and reachable commands depend on each robot's geometry and dynamics. Existing cross-embodiment policies output normalized commands that disregard these differences, or compensate through per-robot training and distillation. World models can in principle pool cross-embodiment data, yet command-conditioned models bind their latent dynamics to a single action space, while pose-conditioned models omit the mapping from commands to body-specific motion. On flat ground, however, embodiment affects the planner's observations only through the camera ego-motion. This letter proposes Hourglass, an hourglass-shaped architecture that factorizes cross-embodiment motion planning at this interface: a body-agnostic world model and each robot's differentiable ego-dynamics, which map commands to that ego-motion, are coupled only through it. The world model is trained on data pooled across embodiments and camera mounts; each robot's ego-dynamics is a reduced-order template with identified physical parameters. A generalist parameter-conditioned policy is learned once at this junction: each imagined step rolls out the template and then the world model, and imagination training through each robot's template makes the policy body-aware. On seven simulated robots of five locomotion types, the generalist policy outperforms imitation-learning and diffusion-policy baselines on the same world model by 3.8--10.6 percentage points in success rate and matches per-robot specialists without dedicated training, while approaching its parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher within 1.4--2.8 points without an online map. After three minutes of teleoperated driving for identification, the synthetic-data-trained model transfers zero-shot to a real skid-steer robot unseen in training, navigating unseen indoor scenes.

SimRetract: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robotic Assistance for Retractor Take-Over in Open Surgery 2026-10-30
Show abstract

We demonstrate the first zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for a surgeon-robot collaboration task in open surgery: retractor take-over. A robot arm takes a retractor from the surgeon's hand and preserves retraction at handoff, using a policy trained only on simulated demonstrations, without human demonstrations or real-world fine-tuning. Imitation learning from real demonstrations is limited by collection and labeling costs, and by performance degradation when the environment changes. Our framework, SimRetract, generates simulated demonstrations designed for transfer: the policy observes masked instrument images and grasp geometry, learns to recover from off-nominal states, and acts with margin for execution drift. Deployed zero-shot, it grasps the retractor in 90% of 50 surgeon-defined phantom presentations and preserves retraction at handoff in 78%. Results on unseen retractors suggest potential for generalization to instruments with similar grasp geometry. Across ten policy configurations and 1,200 simulated and real trials, we localize the sim-to-real gap to components: in simulation, grasp rates across ablations span 80-96%, while on the real robot removing the instrument mask alone drops performance to 22% grasped and 12% held. Transfer-oriented design thus makes zero-shot sim-to-real transfer feasible for a surgical assistance task without human demonstrations. Code is available here: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/simretract-58D2.

I3L: Iterative Interactive Imitation Learning for Whole-Body Mobile Manipulation 2026-10-29
📝 Project website: https://i-3l.github.io/
Show abstract

Policies trained offline from teleoperated demonstrations can degrade outside their training distribution. This gap is particularly acute in long-horizon whole-body mobile manipulation, where long horizons amplify compounding errors and coordinated arm, torso, and base motion makes exhaustive demonstration coverage impractical. We introduce I3L, an integrated hardware and learning system that combines whole-body correction with iterative policy refinement to address this last-mile reliability gap. Its low-cost, open-source JoyLo+ interface uses policy-tracking leader arms and touch-triggered takeover to enable coordinated corrections across the body parts, while iterative deployment and retraining allow operators to target failures of the updated policy. We evaluate I3L across three robot embodiments and eight simulated and real-world tasks, including four long-horizon whole-body mobile manipulation tasks. Controlled simulation experiments show that four-round HG-DAgger achieves the highest final success on both tasks. Under this protocol, I3L improves autonomous success over the base policies by an average of 68.3 percentage points and achieves at least 90% success on every task, outperforming demonstration-only training by 35.6 percentage points on average with the same number of training trajectories. Averaged across tasks, takeover duration per episode decreases by 72% over four rounds. Together, these results show that I3L helps close the last-mile gap between offline training and reliable deployment and provides practical insights into collecting and using corrections for long-horizon whole-body mobile manipulation. Videos, visualizations, and appendix are at https://i-3l.github.io/.

From Teleoperation to Long-Horizon Dexterity: Evaluating Visuomotor Skills and Offline Proxies for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 Workshop
Show abstract

Imitation learning policies are intended to make robotic manipulation robust to variations in object pose, appearance, and environment that challenge pre-programmed motion. However, real-world performance remains difficult to predict, as conventional optimization signals such as validation loss show no consistent relationship with robot success. To identify the design choices that govern real-world performance, 281 imitation-learning policies spanning seven policy families were evaluated in approximately 1,700 real-robot trials of RJ45 connector grasping and insertion using a five-fingered robotic hand. Controlled ablations showed that observation and action representations dominated conventional optimizer and sampling choices. For point-cloud policies, region-of-interest cropping, representation in the robot-base frame, and normalization within the point encoder each improved success by 70-100 percentage points. For the vision-language-action policy, absolute rather than relative action prediction improved insertion success by up to 70 percentage points. Skill decomposition, multi-view perception, and recovery demonstrations were associated with the strongest-performing systems, although their effects were not independently isolated. Of 67 offline metrics evaluated, only five measures of predicted-motion smoothness and directness reliably correlated with insertion success (r = -0.70 to -0.80), and only within a single policy family. These results identify representation choices as key determinants of real-world imitation-learning performance and show that offline metrics can shortlist policies, but cannot replace real-robot evaluation.

SwapBot: Tool-Centric Robot Manipulation via Interchangeable End-Effectors and Tactile Sensing 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Robot manipulation in everyday environments inherently involves substantial tool usage. Seemingly simple household tasks, such as cooking pan-fried tofu, require forceful cutting, delicate brushing, and precise placement using different tools under contact-rich interaction. Thus, the ability to autonomously swap and use task-appropriate tools is important for tool-centric manipulation. We introduce SwapBot, a modular, tool-centric end effector system featuring low-cost adapters that quickly convert common household tools into robot end effectors. Rather than pursuing a single universal gripper, SwapBot adopts a unified design principle that combines a fixed robot-side coupling interface with low-cost, tool-side adapters, enabling compatibility with diverse tools, including articulated ones, and multiple robot platforms. These results highlight modular, tactile-equipped tools as a practical and extensible pathway toward robust and capable robot manipulation. JEDI: Joint-Embedding Demonstration Compression for In-Context Imitation Learning 2026-10-29
Show abstract

In-context imitation learning (ICIL) adapts robot policies through test-time demonstrations, but long multimodal trajectories constrain how many examples fit in context. We ask what a compressed demonstration should preserve. We propose JEDI, which compresses demonstrations into latent slots trained to predict the query episode's next-stage keyframe in a frozen video-JEPA space. Task and process counterfactuals help the slots preserve execution details, while the predictor selects demonstrations from a bank. Post-training with keypoint tokens enables human-hand conditioning on robots. Experiments on LIBERO and real robots show that JEDI supports many-shot context; LIBERO objective controls show improved success and demonstration following over action-only compression. These findings support query-future prediction as a training objective for preserving execution-relevant information in compressed demonstrations.

RE4: Transformation-aware Imitation of Object Interactions Using Manipulation Modes 2026-10-29
📝 7 pages, appendix
Show abstract

Object interaction tasks have been a focus of advances in imitation learning. End-to-end methods, dominated by generative variants have shown leaps in performance but tend to be black boxes. Recent indications hint at demonstration lookup as the operating process underneath generative policy performance. In this paper, we revisit a few modern imitation learning benchmarks for object interactions, with the aim of composing a simple framework from first principles. We repurpose theories of manipulation modes and apply them to retrieve and transform demonstration datasets, creating a minimal strategy that preserves performance and robustness while remaining interpretable. We identify pose estimation as a key module that governs information about the object. This informs a manipulation mode-aware retrieval of a demonstration, a mode-aware transformation, a replan step that connects to the retrieval point while preserving mode constraints, returning a transformed demonstration for rollout. These compose four key steps of the proposed RE4 framework, evaluated over state-based and image-based benchmarks in simulated Push-T, Robomimic, and MimicGen as well as a real robot experiment. Robustness is measured in an adversarial study that evaluates sparse data regimes, a study with visual distractors, and the addition of an obstructing collision geometry. RE4 outperforms baselines, while notably preserving interpretability. The results overwhelmingly motivate the benefits of identifying simple interpretable building blocks to learn manipulation skills for object interactions.

Smooth Action Chunking Via Movement-Primitive Representations for Imitation Learning 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Imitation learning has become a widely adopted approach for vision-based robot policies, owing to its conceptual simplicity, scalability, and ability to leverage large-scale datasets. As these policies have grown more capable, action chunking has become their standard interface. At each decision step the policy outputs several future actions at once. Backbones from diffusion policies to large vision-language-action models all share it. Whatever backbone produces the chunk, its temporal structure is learned only implicitly. The commanded trajectory is therefore jerky both within a chunk and between successive chunks. On real hardware this appears as visible vibration and as repeated saturation of the follower's velocity limit. We address this issue by incorporating a movement-primitive-based trajectory representation into action chunking. Specifically, a trajectory segment is represented as a linear combination of smooth basis functions, yielding smooth trajectories by construction while leaving the policy architecture, training objective, and inference procedure unchanged. The same representation also yields a closed-form, inference-time correction for the second source of jerk. We condition the predicted coefficients so that the new chunk passes through a via-point at the previous chunk's last commanded action. This correction needs no retraining and, unlike asynchronous Real-Time Chunking, applies to policies queried in one shot. We instantiate it on a one-shot (ACT) and an iterative (DiT) backbone and evaluate in simulation and on a real bimanual ALOHA platform. The representation lowers within-chunk jerk by an order of magnitude on the one-shot backbone and by 2.4 times on the iterative one, with task success statistically non-inferior. The conditioning then removes 73-84% of the boundary discontinuity, at negligible per-step cost.

CuraEval: Evaluating Demonstration Curation on Mixed-Skill Robot Data 2026-10-28
📝 Accepted by the CoRL 2026 Workshop "Oops, I Erred". 10 pages, 2 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Robot imitation learning uses demonstrations from operators of varying skill, but selecting training data remains challenging. Existing methods score demonstrations using smoothness, diversity, or estimated influence on policy performance, but differences in selection and evaluation protocols make it difficult to determine whether they outperform simple baselines. We introduce \textbf{CuraEval}, a framework combining mathematical analysis of demonstration scores with controlled comparisons against fixed size-matched random subsets and full-data training on mixed-skill robomimic data using Diffusion Policy and BC-RNN. We show that the evaluated PSD score depends only on episode length and squared position magnitudes, and produces nearly the same ranking as episode length on \textit{square} ($ρ=0.999$). With Diffusion Policy, selecting the shortest 50\% of demonstrations improves \textit{square} success from 70.7\% under random selection to 76.5\%. Compared with random selection, our adapted geometric-entropy LOO selector reduces success at 25\% retention from 94.7\% to 74.0\% on \textit{can} and achieves 41.7\% on \textit{square}, below the mean success rates of all three independently sampled random subsets (59.8--62.5\%). On the same 3 \textit{square} Diffusion Policy training seeds, length-based selection at 75\% retention improves success over full-data training from 76.7\% to 86.7\% under the original protocol, but lowers it from 80.5\% to 79.5\% after checkpoint reporting, rollout count, and evaluation seed change jointly. No evaluated curated condition significantly outperforms full-data training after Holm correction, although confidence intervals still permit useful gains. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/CuraEval.

Behavior Cloning has demonstrated promising generalization capabilities for robotic manipulation in terrestrial environments.
