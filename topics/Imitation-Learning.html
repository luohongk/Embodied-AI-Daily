Camera equalisation of an 11.5x drop in environmental illuminance: four imitation-learning policies on an SO-101 arm 2026-11-10
📝 10 pages, 2 figures, 8 tables. Data and code: https://huggingface.co/datasets/fysical/so101-lighting-autoexposure
Show abstract

Real-robot benchmarks of learned visuomotor policies often record room illuminance as the lighting condition. We show that, on a typical low-cost rig, room light and image light can be almost unrelated. Four imitation-learning policies (ACT, SmolVLA, Diffusion Policy and pi0.5) were trained on the same 50 demonstrations of a pick-and-place task on an SO-101 arm. Each was run for ten rollouts at about 520 lux and again at about 45 lux, an 11.5x reduction. Success was 20/40 in normal light and 19/40 in low light: a change of -2.5 percentage points (95% CI -23 to +19), with no evidence that the policies differed in sensitivity (interaction p = 0.86). The recorded video shows why: mean image brightness was only 3% lower in low light on the front camera and 19% lower on the wrist camera. Both cameras were set to automatic exposure and white balance, which most likely absorbed the difference; exposure was not locked in this experiment. An accidental repeat of one 10-rollout block reproduced its success count (7/10) but matched the first block at only 6 of 10 placements, consistent with chance. We conclude that lux alone does not describe what a policy sees. Camera settings, and image statistics compared with the training recordings, are the quantities to report and control. Data and code are public.

MAPLE: Latent Multi-Agent Play for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are effective as end-to-end motion planners, but can be brittle when evaluated in closed-loop settings due to being trained under traditional imitation learning framework. Existing closed-loop supervision approaches lack scalability and fail to completely model a reactive environment. We propose MAPLE, a novel framework for reactive, multi-agent rollout of a dynamic driving scenario in the latent space of the VLA model. The ego vehicle and nearby traffic agents are independently controlled over multi-step horizons, while being reactive to other agents in the scene, enabling closed-loop training. MAPLE consists of two training stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on the latent rollouts based on ground-truth trajectories, followed by (2) reinforcement learning with global and agent -specific rewards that encourage safety, progress, and interaction realism. We further propose diversity rewards that encourage the model to generate planning behaviors that may not be present in logged driving data. Notably, our closed-loop training framework is scalable and does not require external simulators, which can be computationally expensive to run and have limited visual fidelity to the real-world. MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art driving performance on Bench2Drive and demonstrates scalable, closed-loop multi-agent play for robust E2E autonomous driving systems.

DeRep: Learning Depth Representations for End-to-End Navigation in Cluttered Environments 2026-11-10
📝 8 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

End-to-end navigation in cluttered environments relies on compact visual representations to support collision avoidance and goal-directed motion. Existing methods typically learn these representations jointly with the policy or through observation reconstruction, which does not explicitly specify what information the representation should preserve. Moreover, they are usually evaluated with a single downstream policy learner, although the benefit of a representation may depend on how the policy is learned. It therefore remains unclear how different representation-learning objectives affect downstream navigation performance and robustness. This work proposes DeRep, a navigation-oriented representation-learning method that combines local bird's-eye-view reachability supervision with depth reconstruction. To compare representation objectives under controlled conditions, we pretrain encoders with a common architecture and freeze them during downstream learning. We evaluate the representations using a linear policy probe, flow-matching imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with PPO. DeRep achieves the lowest linear policy probe prediction error and outperforms reconstruction alone across all six tested PPO conditions. In imitation learning, both reachability-based objectives achieve higher mean success rates than reconstruction alone, with similar mean success rates between the reachability-only and joint objectives. These results demonstrate consistent benefits of incorporating reachability supervision into depth representation pretraining across different downstream learning methods and evaluation conditions. We further deploy the PPO policy with the frozen DeRep encoder in Gazebo and on a physical quadrotor without fine-tuning, demonstrating its capability for zero-shot sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer.

Generative Scenario Rollouts for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are emerging as highly effective planning models for end-to-end autonomous driving systems. However, current works mostly rely on imitation learning from sparse trajectory annotations and underutilize their potential as generative models. We propose Generative Scenario Rollouts (GeRo), a plug-and-play framework for VLA models that jointly performs planning and generation of language-grounded future traffic scenes through an autoregressive rollout strategy. First, a VLA model is trained to encode ego vehicle and agent dynamics into latent tokens under supervision from planning, motion, and language tasks, facilitating text-aligned generation. Next, GeRo performs language-conditioned autoregressive generation. Given multi-view images, a scenario description, and ego-action questions, it generates future latent tokens and textual responses to guide long-horizon rollouts. A rollout-consistency loss stabilizes predictions using ground truth or pseudo-labels, mitigating drift and preserving text-action alignment. This design enables GeRo to perform temporally consistent, language-grounded rollouts that support long-horizon reasoning and multi-agent planning. On Bench2Drive, GeRo improves driving score and success rate by +15.7 and +26.2, respectively. By integrating reinforcement learning with generative rollouts, GeRo achieves state-of-the-art closed-loop and open-loop performance, demonstrating strong zero-shot robustness. These results highlight the promise of generative, language-conditioned reasoning as a foundation for safer and more interpretable end-to-end autonomous driving.

Distill to Think, Foresee to Act: Cognitive-Physical Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Current end-to-end autonomous driving models are fundamentally constrained by the behavioral cloning ceiling of imitation learning. While reinforcement learning offers a path to smarter autonomy, it demands two missing pieces of infrastructure: (1) a cognitive foundation that understands traffic semantics and driving intent, and (2) a foresighted physical environment that can anticipate the consequences of candidate actions. To this end, we propose CoPhy, a CognitivePhysical reinforcement learning framework for autonomous driving. To distill to think, we distill VLM knowledge into the BEV encoder and then discard the VLM entirely, retaining cognitive ability at zero inference cost while releasing the cognitive channel as a pluggable interface for optional human language commands. To foresee to act, we build an auto-regressive BEV world model that explicitly predicts future semantic maps conditioned on candidate actions, serving as an interpretable physical sandbox from which safety metrics are directly derived. Built upon this dual infrastructure, we optimize the driving policy via GRPO with a novel dual-reward mechanism: a physical reward derived from BEV rollouts enforces hard safety constraints, while a cognitive reward from a language-aligned scorer ensures intent compliance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoPhy not only achieves state-of-the-art results on NAVSIM v1 and v2 benchmarks, but also enables safer driving via cognitively informed scene compliance and flexible intent control through user-defined language instructions. Learning Beyond Rigid Manipulation with Hybrid Soft-Rigid Robots 2026-11-09
📝 Revised version with new title, abstract, and experiments
Show abstract

Rigid-soft robot hybrids promise to unite the precision and repeatability of rigid manipulators with the compliance and adaptability of soft structures. Yet, full-fledged rigid-soft manipulators remain largely unexplored, in part because the intelligence required to exploit this combination is still missing. Here, we introduce a hybrid robotic system obtained by the series interconnection of a rigid arm and a soft manipulator, and systematically examine its ability to perform both established and previously inaccessible manipulation tasks. As a result, the system matches or exceeds the performance of rigid robots in classical tasks such as stacking, while enabling qualitatively new behaviors, including grasping objects through narrow openings.

📝 16 pages + 17 pages of supplementary, 9 tables, 7 figures
Show abstract

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods have been the dominant paradigm in continuous control tasks due to their efficacy in function approximation, while interpretable evolutionary methods have lagged largely behind for the opposite reason. In this work, we introduce Symbolic Imitation Learning, a framework for distilling an oracle policy through an offline dataset into a decision list with linear model motion primitives. Learn the Task, Not the Schedule: Reducing Spurious Cross-Arm Coupling in Bimanual Imitation Learning 2026-11-09
Show abstract

A bimanual demonstration specifies both what the robot should do and one particular schedule for when each arm should act. Standard imitation learning does not distinguish task-required cross-arm constraints from incidental scheduling choices, and may therefore exploit opposite-arm progress as a shortcut cue, creating spurious cross-arm coupling even where relative timing is irrelevant for the current task phase. We introduce Constraint-preserving Temporal Recombination (CTR), a training-time data intervention that preserves recorded arm-local sensorimotor content while retiming the corresponding segments to realize admissible schedules that preserve task-required cross-arm constraints. We use CTR to train Diffusion Policy, Action Chunking with Transformers, and $π_{0.5}$, and evaluate them on four simulated and four real-robot tasks. Across six real-robot bimanual progress-state initializations, CTR raises worst-state success from 56% for the strongest non-CTR schedule mixture to 76%. With $π_{0.5}$ on an Independent task, a 4 s pause transfers 2.56 s of delay to the opposite arm under the Concurrent schedule distribution, but only 0.25 s with CTR. On real robots, CTR violates task constraints in only 0--4% of trials, versus 36--98% for narrow-schedule baselines. Code will be released at https://github.com/MINT-SJTU/CTR.

RAIL: Representation-Aligned Imitation Learning for Student-Compatible Teacher Policies 2026-11-09
📝 18 pages, 3 figures. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) from raw sensory inputs such as images or onboard sensors can be challenging due to high-dimensional observations and sparse rewards. A common strategy is to train a teacher policy with access to privileged state information and then distill it into a student policy that acts from raw inputs alone. However, when the teacher relies on information unavailable to the student, exact imitation may be impossible, creating an irreducible imitation gap. Existing approaches typically mitigate this mismatch through careful reward shaping or additional reinforcement learning on the student policy after the teacher has been trained. Instead, we introduce Representation-Aligned Imitation Learning (RAIL), which addresses the mismatch during teacher training itself. RAIL learns a latent representation shared across teacher and student observations using contrastive learning, and trains the teacher policy directly in this space. Because the teacher policy is constrained to operate on this shared space, it learns behaviors that are reproducible from student observations. This substantially reduces the imitation gap while preserving task performance. Across multiple environments, RAIL outperforms strong baselines without reward modification or post-hoc student fine-tuning, and surpasses direct reinforcement learning from raw observations. The learned representations further enable zero-shot transfer to new tasks through teacher-only training. Project site: https://merajmammadov.com/rail/.

PAIR: Proprioception-Aligned Image-Ray Representations for Viewpoint-Robust Manipulation 2026-11-09
Show abstract

While imitation learning has made substantial progress toward generalizable robot manipulation through scaling, viewpoint robustness remains a major bottleneck. Visuomotor policies rely heavily on visual inputs, yet images provide an inherently ambiguous signal under viewpoint change: similar scenes may appear markedly different across viewpoints, while distinct states may share certain visual overlaps. A policy must therefore infer not only the task-relevant state, but also how it is visually projected under a particular camera configuration. This entanglement of viewpoint-dependent appearance and task-relevant state can make learning inefficient and limit generalization to unseen viewpoints. Recent work has begun to address this challenge through camera-ray conditioning and representation learning using geometric cues to induce invariance. However, providing camera information as additional context does not by itself ground visual representations in how the task scene is visually projected, while geometric alignment without camera context can introduce spurious correspondences by ignoring viewpoint-dependent projection. We propose PAIR, a contrastive learning framework that aligns joint image-ray representations using robot proprioception. PAIR encourages observations with similar robot states to remain close in representation space across viewpoints, providing a structured grounding signal for learning viewpoint-robust features. PAIR is a highly adaptable framework: it can be used to train Diffusion Policy (DP) or post-train vision-language-action (VLA) models. In simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks with camera variation, PAIR consistently outperforms the behavior cloning baseline without ray conditioning by 12.7% and 26.7% points, respectively, demonstrating stronger generalization to unseen viewpoints. Project website: PAIRrobot.github.io.

Finite-Sample Probabilistic Safety Certification for AI-Based Grid-Edge Coordination 2026-11-08
Show abstract

Coordinating a large population of flexible grid-edge devices can alleviate the need for time-consuming and capital-intensive network upgrades. Artificial intelligence (AI)-based control methods such as multi-agent reinforcement learning or imitation learning are promising to this coordination due to their real-time decision scalability. However, system operators still need an independent and rigorous way to decide whether a given AI system is safe enough for deployment. This paper develops a \emph{finite-sample} probabilistic safety certification framework for AI-based grid-edge coordination. The central idea is to reduce the complete input--AI--decision--evaluation workflow to a binary unsafe outcome under an operator-defined safety specification, and then use exact binomial inference to certify the corresponding unsafe operation probability. Given a set of held-out calibration scenarios, the framework returns the tightest one-sided upper certificate and an accept/reject deployment criterion that controls the probability of false safety certification. Because the certification is for the calibration distribution that may \emph{deviate} from the future operation, we further combine the nominal certificate with physically interpretable sample-space adversarial attacks, a concept widely used in AI models to investigate their fragility. Case studies on grid-edge flexibility coordination with 1{,}000-agent AI models (independent parameters) verify the safety guarantee and the value of integrating adversarial attacks into a rolling-window training-certification-deployment flow.

HARP-Bench: Benchmarking Robot Manipulation Around Active Humans 2026-11-08
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Recent robot manipulation benchmarks have enabled systematic evaluation of general-purpose robot policies across diverse objects, scenes, instructions, and embodiments. However, most existing benchmarks still assume that the robot is the only active agent and that task semantics are determined primarily by the instruction and object state. This assumption overlooks a common source of real-world variation: humans actively sharing the workspace. In such settings, humans may reveal an implicit goal, complete part of the task, temporarily change object availability, or constrain safe robot motion while working nearby. We introduce HARP-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating robot manipulation policies in workspaces shared with active humans. HARP-Bench treats human activity as part of the manipulation problem, rather than as background scene variation. It organizes human-present manipulation into four conditions: Cueing, Contribution, Interruption, and Co-presence. These conditions are instantiated through everyday manipulation tasks, including paired human-present variants for controlled comparison with standard manipulation. HARP-Bench also provides a demonstration-generation pipeline for dynamic human-present settings. We evaluate VLA models, imitation-learning methods, and heuristic policies on HARP-Bench. Human-present variations challenge current policies, and higher task success does not consistently improve safety. Quantitative failure analysis identifies manipulation and recovery as the dominant remaining challenges. Training with HARP-Bench demonstrations improves human-present performance in simulation. A real-world study on one task family shows improved performance under human contribution, but not interruption, providing initial evidence of the data's utility and identifying limits of transfer. Code is available at https://github.com/UMass-Embodied-AGI/harp.

Wearable-informed generative digital avatars predict task-conditioned post-stroke locomotion 2026-11-07
📝 57 pages, 15 figures
Show abstract

Dynamic prediction of locomotor capacity after stroke could enable more individualized rehabilitation, yet current assessments largely provide static impairment scores and do not indicate whether patients can perform specific tasks such as slope walking or stair climbing. Here, we present a wearable-informed data-physics hybrid generative framework that reconstructs a stroke survivor's locomotor control from wearable inertial sensing and predicts task-conditioned post-stroke locomotion in new environments. From a single 20 m level-ground walking trial recorded by five IMUs, the framework personalizes a physics-based digital avatar using a healthy-motion prior and hybrid imitation learning, generating dynamically feasible, patient-specific movements for inclined walking and stair negotiation. These results suggest that wearable-informed generative digital avatars may augment individualized gait rehabilitation planning and provide a pathway toward dynamically personalized post-stroke motor recovery strategies.

Online Imitation Learning for Stabilizing Vlasov--Poisson Plasmas 2026-11-07
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 43 pages, 9 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

We study the problem of controlling instability in the Vlasov--Poisson plasma systems, a fundamental challenge for nuclear fusion control. Motivated by the partial observability of plasma states in practice, we investigate imitation learning algorithms that learn from a fully-observable expert controller, but operate under macroscopic measurements. We theoretically establish a separation between online and offline imitation learning --- even a small behavior cloning error leads to error compounding that exponentially amplifies instability over time. By way of contrast, the online imitation learning loss can polynomially control the stability of rollout trajectories. We then propose a DAgger-style algorithm that learns the stabilizing controller under partial observability. Simulation results on a 1D Vlasov--Poisson system demonstrate that our algorithm can effectively stabilize plasma instabilities over longer time horizons than behavior cloning.

LAFP: Preserving Latent Action Structure in Latent Policy Learning via Flow Matching 2026-11-07
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Learning high quality latent actions from large-scale unlabeled videos, coupled with limited real-world interaction data for training an action decoder, has emerged as a promising paradigm for scalable latent policy learning. However, existing approaches typically rely on behavior cloning, which tends to collapse inherently multimodal action distributions into unimodal ones, thereby degrading the pretrained latent action structure. While flow matching provides a potential alternative, directly applying it leads to a misalignment between latent actions and physical actions during action decoder training, due to the stochastic nature of the learned policy. To address these, we propose Latent Action Flow Policy (LAFP), which leverages flow matching for latent policy learning and introduces an inference-time interpolation mechanism to mitigate stochasticity-induced misalignment. Experimental results demonstrate that LAFP consistently outperforms prior methods on most downstream imitation learning tasks, achieving an 8.2\% improvement in average success rate while incurring less than 1x additional inference overhead.

Model-Based Imitation Learning For Monocular Vision-Based Navigation 2026-11-07
📝 Under Review
Show abstract

Reliable navigation with a single monocular limited field-of-view camera is challenging because the lack of depth measurements and limited spatial awareness complicate collision checking. Existing approaches to tackle these challenges fall into two categories. Model-based planners can incorporate explicit collision-avoidance guidance which leads to better generalization but are often computationally expensive and require manual tuning of the algorithmic parameters. In contrast, end-to-end approaches trained by imitation learning can be flexible but typically encode safety only implicitly and thus can suffer from high collision rates in novel scenarios. In this paper, we present a middle ground through model-based imitation learning. The model-based part is a learned collision model that can map a sequence of past images and a candidate control sequence to a distribution of worst-case obstacle clearance. This predictive distribution in turn is used to define a collision-risk cost in a trajectory optimization formulation. The imitation component uses human demonstrations to learn scene-dependent balancing of collision risk with other task costs such as goal progress. We also use it to learn the algorithmic parameters (e.g., a warm start) of our optimizer to accelerate the solution process. We show that our approach can demonstrate highly reliable navigation in cluttered environments on robots with different embodiments. We also demonstrate a substantial reduction in collision rate over recent end-to-end and model-based baselines.

Geometric Entropy: When Trajectory Diversity Helps and Hurts in Imitation Learning 2026-11-07
📝 Accepted to IROS 2026. Project page: https://geometric-entropy.github.io/
Show abstract

We study how trajectory-shape diversity in demonstrations affects imitation learning (IL) performance across models, tasks, and data scales. We introduce Geometric Entropy (H_G), a task-agnostic metric that quantifies the intrinsic diversity of transit trajectories after normalizing away extrinsic variation, such as goal pose and workspace scale, via target-frame alignment. Across multiple IL architectures and both simulated and real-robot contact-rich manipulation tasks, we observe a consistent inverted-U relationship between success and H_G: increasing geometric diversity improves robustness in low-diversity regimes but degrades performance once diversity induces strategy ambiguity. Moreover, the optimal entropy shifts toward lower values as task mastery increases through more data, easier tasks, or stronger priors, and for a pretrained vision-language-action model the trend becomes effectively monotonic decreasing. Practically, H_G enables fast pre-training auditing of demonstration datasets and offers a simple guideline for calibrating demonstrations toward the learnable regime.

Natural behavior evolves continuously and often reuses movement structure across contexts, but existing behavioral segmentation methods typically assign a single discrete syllable at each time point or impose restrictive generative assumptions. We introduce Motif-based Continuous Dynamics (MCD), a computational framework that models behavior as time-varying combinations of reusable behavioral motifs. DexDICE: Reconciling Safety and Performance via Dual-Expert Offline Imitation Learning 2026-11-06
📝 NeurIPS 2026. 55 pages, 13 figures. Code: https://github.com/tzs930/dexdice
Show abstract

Ensuring high returns while providing strong safety guarantees in offline imitation learning (IL) is a fundamental challenge: existing approaches either rely on explicitly specified cost functions, which are difficult to design for complex real-world safety constraints, or require expert demonstrations that are both safe and high-performing, which are often too costly to collect. We introduce dual-expert imitation learning, which leverages two complementary data sources: (i) safety-compliant demonstrations that faithfully follow safety guidelines but may be suboptimal, and (ii) performance-oriented demonstrations that achieve high returns but may violate safety guidelines. To address this problem, we propose DexDICE (Dual-expert offline imitation learning via stationary DIstribution Correction Estimation). Instead of conventional distribution constraints, DexDICE combines DICE-style offline learning with safe support constraints, enabling agents to exploit high-return behaviors while remaining within safety-compliant regions. We prove that the ideal solution satisfying this constraint exactly has zero unsafe-region visitation with high probability. We evaluate DexDICE on a re-curated DSRL benchmark and validate it on a real-world mobile-robot navigation task trained directly from human teleoperation demonstrations, where it substantially outperforms baselines and remains robust to data scarcity and quality degradation.

Mitigating Covariate Shift in Behavioral Cloning via Robust Stationary Distribution Correction 2026-11-06
📝 25 pages, 11 figures, NeurIPS 2024, Code: https://github.com/tzs930/drildice
Show abstract

We consider offline imitation learning (IL), which aims to train an agent to imitate from the dataset of expert demonstrations without online interaction with the environment. Behavioral Cloning (BC) has been a simple yet effective approach to offline IL, but it is also well-known to be vulnerable to the covariate shift resulting from the mismatch between the state distributions induced by the learned policy and the expert policy. Moreover, as often occurs in practice, when expert datasets are collected from an arbitrary state distribution instead of a stationary one, these shifts become more pronounced, potentially leading to substantial failures in existing IL methods. Specifically, we focus on covariate shift resulting from arbitrary state data distributions, such as biased data collection or incomplete trajectories, rather than shifts induced by changes in dynamics or noisy expert actions. In this paper, to mitigate the effect of the covariate shifts in BC, we propose DrilDICE, which utilizes a distributionally robust BC objective by employing a stationary distribution correction ratio estimation (DICE) to derive a feasible solution. We evaluate the effectiveness of our method through an extensive set of experiments covering diverse covariate shift scenarios. The results demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach in improving the robustness against the shifts, outperforming existing offline IL methods in such scenarios.

Improving Imitation Learning Efficiency for Manipulation through Geometric Prior Pretraining 2026-11-06
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Applying an imitation learning policy to a new manipulation task usually requires collecting new demonstrations and retraining the model, which makes sample efficiency a practical concern. Pretraining on large-scale robot datasets is effective in this respect, but such datasets are costly to collect and train on, while data augmentation techniques typically require a new round of data generation and retraining for each task. A complementary question is what useful prior can be provided to a policy at negligible cost before any task-specific data are collected. In this study, we construct a geometric visual pretraining dataset in which each scene contains only a plane, an object, and a hand, and trajectories are generated automatically. The scenes contain neither textures nor backgrounds; pretraining primarily exposes the policy to the geometric relationship between the hand and the object. Furthermore, representing the hand as a cube avoids tailoring the dataset to a specific robot morphology. We evaluate this geometric prior using ACT on three simulated robots across five manipulation tasks each, as well as on three real-world robot tasks. Across many of these robot--task combinations, fine-tuning from the geometric prior achieves higher success rates in the early stages of training than training from scratch while using only a small number of task demonstrations. These results suggest that even highly simplified geometric scenes can provide a useful initialization that transfers across robots and to real-world tasks when task data are limited.

MATE: Asynchronous Multi-Rate Diffusion Policy Composition 2026-11-06
Show abstract

Diffusion policies have shown strong potential for robotic imitation learning, and recent extensions incorporate additional modalities to improve manipulation performance. However, these modalities often differ not only in information content but also in sensing rates and inference latencies. Existing multimodal diffusion policies typically rely on synchronous fusion or manually designed multi-frequency architectures, which either slow down high-frequency feedback or limit extensibility to new modality combinations. We propose LAG-Fusion, a latency-aware guidance fusion framework for asynchronous multimodal diffusion policy composition. LAG-Fusion allows modality-specific policies to operate at their native inference rates and contribute denoising guidance whenever available. To make asynchronous composition consistent, we derive a reference-frame rebasing rule for diffusion variables under relative action representations, enabling delayed guidance to be aligned before fusion. CLAW: Continuous Latent Action World Models via Adversarial Regularization 2026-11-05
📝 9 pages, 27 pages of supplementary material
Show abstract

We introduce CLAW, a fully end-to-end self-supervised framework for learning a world model jointly with continuous latent action representations directly from action-free videos. Our approach leverages adversarial latent regularization to prevent the latent from encoding future observations, so that it captures the structure of the action. The diffusion-based world model then models environment dynamics as a distribution over future observations rather than as a single deterministic rollout. Both components are learned from observation sequences alone without any action annotations. By simultaneously training the Latent Action Model and world model, CLAW learns to reason about how inferred actions induce environment transitions from visual observations alone. We show that the resulting latent action world model supports both imitation learning from observation and goal-directed planning. Ultrasound imaging is highly operator-dependent, motivating robotic systems that reproduce expert scanning skills consistently. These skills involve coordinated interpretation of anatomical landmarks, ultrasound images, and probe-tissue interactions that are difficult to encode in hand-designed control strategies. Imitation learning offers an alternative, but its observation and action representations should reflect the anatomically structured nature of scanning. We propose an anatomy-anchored imitation-learning framework that combines anatomy-guided probe placement with learned ultrasound navigation. The placement module identifies a task-specific anatomical starting point from external visual observations and establishes the initial probe pose as the reference frame for scanning. A flow-based policy then generates scanning motions from anchor-relative probe poses, ultrasound images, probe-mounted camera views, and contact forces, while a termination head predicts task completion. This avoids continuous reliance on third-person vision and reduces dependence on the robot-patient configuration. A handheld acquisition platform enables multimodal demonstrations to be collected during natural freehand scanning without a robot. On 16 unseen participants, task success reaches 96%, 94%, 88%, and 94% for kidney, gallbladder, and liver standard-plane acquisition and radial artery sweeping, respectively. Compared with a fine-tuned pi_0 model, success on the abdominal tasks improves by 10-42 percentage points, with lower time-to-target, peak contact force, and trajectory jerk. Ablations support the complementary roles of anatomical anchoring, egocentric vision, and force feedback. End-to-end trials with automatic placement achieve 90-100% success, and the policies transfer without retraining to a second robot with 83-100% success.

Cloud-side inference expands the computational resources available to imitation-learning policies, but communication and inference latencies create a mismatch between prediction and execution. We propose RAPAC-DP, a response-aligned pending-action compensation framework for diffusion- and flow-based action generators. By conditioning on actions committed during inference, RAPAC-DP generates a full-horizon action chunk aligned with the scheduled handover time. A lightweight pending-action encoder and LoRA adapters learn this compensation while keeping the pretrained base policy frozen. We construct delay-conditioned training samples from delay-free demonstrations, requiring neither an explicit dynamics model nor additional data collected under delayed execution. In simulation benchmarks at the largest evaluated delays, RAPAC-DP retains 81.4\% of its overall delay-free performance on Kinetix and achieves a mean success rate of 63.3\% across three RoboMimic tasks. Across 450 physical trials on a Franka Panda manipulator, RAPAC-DP increases the high-delay mean success rate from 28.9\% for RTC to 60.0\% across static and conveyor-based pick-and-place tasks.

DiscoDemo: Discovering Efficient and Diverse Robot Demonstrations for Imitation Learning 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Behavior cloning for robotic manipulation is highly sensitive to the coverage of its training data, yet collecting diverse successful demonstrations remains costly. We study reinforcement learning (RL) not as a deployable controller, but as a data generator for downstream visuomotor imitation learning. Our key premise is that multiple successful solution modes cover a broader range of successful trajectories than repeatedly collecting a single narrow solution. We introduce DiscoDemo, which trains a privileged latent-conditioned RL generator with a parallel reverse curriculum and a success-gated diversity objective to discover diverse solutions while preserving task success. The trained generator produces multiple successful closed-loop behaviors, which are safety-filtered and exported as a static dataset for downstream imitation learning. Across four manipulation tasks, DiscoDemo produces broader successful-state coverage and improves downstream visuomotor policies in both simulation and the real world. Averaged across tasks, downstream success increases to 62% from 40% for the strongest baseline in simulation, and to 78% from 39% on the real robot, respectively. These results suggest that successful solution diversity improves imitation learning for a pretrained flow-matching VLA and remains complementary to corrective data collection. Scaling robot learning to long-horizon tasks remains a formidable challenge. While end-to-end policies often lack the structural priors needed for effective long-term reasoning, traditional neuro-symbolic methods rely heavily on hand-crafted symbolic priors. We introduce ENAP (Emergent Neural Automaton Policy), a neuro-symbolic imitation learning framework in which a bi-level policy adaptively emerges from visuomotor demonstrations. Specifically, we employ adaptive clustering and an extension of the L* algorithm to learn the task structure from demonstrations as a Mealy state machine, which serves as an interpretable high-level planner capturing latent task modes. This discrete structure then guides a low-level reactive residual network to learn precise continuous control via behavior cloning (BC). By explicitly modeling discrete transitions and continuous residuals, ENAP achieves sample efficiency and interpretability without task-specific labels. Experiments on complex manipulation and long-horizon tasks show that ENAP outperforms state-of-the-art end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) policies by up to 27% in low-data regimes. Building virtual embodied agents that operate in open-ended games like human players is a long-standing goal requiring both long-horizon autonomy and reliable native-interface control. As a procedurally generated open world, Minecraft provides a natural testbed for studying the integration of these capabilities. Existing agents typically provide either native visuomotor control without long-horizon autonomy or high-level planning executed through environment APIs or predefined skills. To bridge this gap, we propose HiSTEVE, a hierarchical VLA agent with a capability-aware agent harness. At the orchestration level, we equip the harness with structured memory for tracking progress and a VLM planner for selecting subtasks within the VLA's capabilities. To reliably determine subtask completion, we further introduce a policy-coupled task completion state detector that conditions on VLA policy context to signal candidate completion. At the control level, a single VLA executes all subtasks with vision-only perception and native keyboard-and-mouse actions. To mitigate interference between heterogeneous GUI and non-GUI interactions, we introduce regime-specific RMSNorm branches and separate mouse-movement token embeddings. We also train the VLA through world knowledge injection, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with environment feedback to improve reliability and recovery. HiSTEVE achieves state-of-the-art atomic-task performance, scoring 85.0% on MineStudio Bench and a 37.4% average success rate on OpenHA, while also completing long-horizon tasks such as crafting an iron pickaxe from scratch like human players.

End-to-end control policies trained via Reinforcement Learning (RL) have emerged as an alternative to conventional surgical robotic control approaches, with recent studies exploring state-based policies trained on privileged state observations. However, such approaches rely on precise physical states that are difficult to obtain reliably in surgical environments. Vision-based RL provides an alternative by learning manipulation policies directly from RGB observations, avoiding explicit state estimation and manually engineered perception pipelines while enabling policies to acquire task-relevant visual representations. Nevertheless, obtaining vision-based policies via pure RL methods for surgical manipulation remains challenging. Thus, imitation learning (IL) becomes another solution, yet it requires a large number of expert demonstrations, which are difficult to obtain in minimally invasive surgery. In this work, we propose a framework for state-to-vision policy distillation in laparoscopic robotic manipulation. Specifically, a state-based policy with privileged observations is employed as the teacher, and a vision-based policy learns from RGB observations and robot proprioceptive information, which requires less expert demonstrations than IL and introduces superior performance than RL methods. We conduct extensive experiments on various surgical manipulation tasks in the simulated environment. Experimental results show that, in the context of laparoscopic robotic manipulation, training vision-based policies with general-purpose RL methods is challenging, whereas our proposed state-to-vision policy distillation leads to more consistent and stable performance across tasks.

Tactile sensing provides robots with direct access to physical interactions at the contact interface, making it a critical modality for robust and dexterous manipulation. Over the past several decades, tactile-based manipulation research has evolved from mechanics-driven approaches, which explicitly interpret tactile signals through contact mechanics and task-specific reasoning, to data-driven approaches that learn tactile representations and manipulation policies directly from data. This paper reviews the development of tactile-based manipulation from these two complementary perspectives. We first introduce the fundamentals of tactile sensing, and then review, from a mechanics-driven perspective, how those signals form manipulation-relevant state representations and subsequently influence the robot action. We then examine the recent advances in data-driven tactile manipulation, covering imitation learning, reinforcement learning, vision-language-action models, and predictive models. Finally, we discuss open challenges and future opportunities toward scalable, generalizable, and robust tactile-enabled robotic systems.

Optimistic Policy Regularization: Elite-Statistic Reward Shaping 2026-10-21
Show abstract

On-policy agents discard trajectories after each update, including rare high-return episodes. We propose Optimistic Policy Regularization (OPR), which turns those episodes into a closed-form reward bonus: we estimate an elite action prior from top-quartile episodes and add a \reviewblue{bounded transform of the log-ratio} of the prior to the policy's action distribution. OPR needs no auxiliary network, value gate, off-policy correction, or adversarial loop. It is a one-line modification of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)'s reward; yet, on 49 Atari games at 10M steps, $5\times$ fewer agent decisions than the 200M-frame baselines, it reaches a 148.4\% median human-normalized score (HNS; 196.9\% interquartile mean): higher scores than published PPO on $36/49$ games ($p{=}0.0014$) and a higher aggregate median than self-imitation learning, which falls well below OPR when frames and backbone are matched. We pair the method with a theory of when it works. The underlying log-ratio has an exact forward-KL interpretation; its pre-advantage placement propagates the signal through Generalized Advantage Estimation; and, under an idealized local update, its first-order effect depends on a prior-advantage alignment whose sign indicates help or harm. Since alignment varies across tasks and runs, we also study an online gating extension. On a ten-game set, it outperforms the backbone by $+17.4$ median HNS on average across seeds. Controlled experiments test temporal propagation and the tradeoffs of state conditioning and gating, supporting OPR as a simple regularizer with an analysis of where to shape.
