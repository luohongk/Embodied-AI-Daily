Learning Geometry-Aware Planning Representations for Monocular Flight with Cross-Modal Supervision 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Existing high-speed navigation systems for micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) commonly rely on depth cameras, stereo vision, or LiDAR to obtain explicit geometric information, while monocular cameras offer a lighter and lower-cost alternative. However, monocular flight remains challenging because RGB images offer no direct metric cues and visual appearance varies considerably across environments. We propose a geometry-aware monocular visual planning framework that predicts trajectories from two image frames, their relative pose, and the MAV state. Without explicitly reconstructing depth, the proposed method complements the downstream planning objective with feature-level geometric supervision by transferring the planning-relevant representation learned by a privileged depth expert to the RGB planner through cross-modal distillation. The framework first trains a depth-based geometric expert, then distills its planning features into a monocular branch using paired RGB-D observations, and finally adapts the planner to the downstream trajectory planning objective. This design encourages the policy to learn planning-relevant geometric features, enabling collision avoidance. We further use a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) simulator to render visual observations for data collection, paired RGB-D supervision, and simulation evaluation. The planner trained in simulation is then deployed in real-world environments without real-world fine-tuning, achieving obstacle avoidance at speeds of up to 6.5 m/s.

ZipSplat: Fewer Gaussians, Better Splats 2026-10-19
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs scenes from images in seconds. Pixel-aligned methods keep every Gaussian grounded in an observation, but produce redundant, potentially misaligned Gaussians across views. Token-based methods instead aggregate all views into a fixed set of learned tokens but lack explicit observation support and use a fixed reconstruction budget. We introduce ZipSplat, a method that predicts a compact, observation-grounded scene representation whose size is set at inference. It derives each scene token from an image patch rather than a learned query, and decodes it into freely placed 3D Gaussians. Tokens across views are merged into a compact set whose size is adjustable at inference. However, without geometric supervision, decoded Gaussians can drift from their source patch, and merging can mix tokens from different surfaces. We therefore introduce two geometric losses to ground and shape this representation. A cone loss ties each token's Gaussians to its source patch, and GeoCE aligns token similarity with 3D proximity so that tokens observing the same surface merge together. Building on these observation--geometry associations, we further introduce TokenBA, a token-level bundle adjustment that refines scene tokens and camera poses in a few seconds. ZipSplat requires neither camera poses nor intrinsics, yet outperforms pixel-aligned and token-based baselines on DL3DV and RealEstate10K, even those given ground-truth cameras. It further generalizes zero-shot to Mip-NeRF360 and ScanNet++. Our project page is at https://veichta.com/zipsplat.

GaussTrap: Stealthy Backdoor Attacks on 3D Gaussian Splatting for Targeted Scene Misperception 2026-10-19
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time scene representation and novel view synthesis, gaining traction in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and AR/VR systems. However, the security vulnerabilities of 3DGS remain largely unexplored. In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive study of backdoor threats in 3DGS pipelines. We uncover an inherent security vulnerability in the adaptive density control mechanism of 3DGS, originally designed for detailed reconstruction, which enables attackers to induce localized gaussian proliferation, effectively embedding malicious backdoors into the scene geometry while remaining invisible from other viewpoints. To expose this threat, we propose GaussTrap, a novel stealthy poisoning framework tailored for 3DGS. GaussTrap operates in three stages (adversarial injection, geometric stabilization, and global fidelity restoration) to embed imperceptible yet adversarial Gaussian components into the reconstruction. It ensures consistent poisoning effects at specific viewpoints while preserving photorealism in benign views, thereby achieving high attack efficacy with low detectability. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks demonstrate that GaussTrap reliably induces targeted visual corruption under trigger conditions while maintaining high rendering fidelity elsewhere, highlighting a critical security blind spot in modern 3D rendering systems. The code is available at https://github.com/acang425/GaussTrap.

SplatXR: A Toolkit for Multi-Layered Extended Reality via On-Headset 3D Gaussian Splatting 2026-10-19
📝 UIST 2026, Project page: https://splatxr.github.io
Show abstract

Recent advances in Gaussian splatting, 3D foundation models, and video-passthrough mixed reality offer unprecedented fidelity for capturing and blending physical and virtual worlds. However, established XR methods rely on polygon-based pipelines that treat physical and digital environments as separate domains, making seamless integration across recent novel representations difficult. Because splats lack explicit surface geometry, they impede standard graphics techniques necessary for cross-reality interactions, such as shadow or collision handling. Furthermore, while 3D foundation models can reconstruct physical objects on-the-fly into digital splats, current XR systems fall short of leveraging this capability for user experiences that transition seamlessly between physicality and virtuality. To address this, we organize XR as a multi-layered reality of interacting primitives, letting pixels and objects move across physical and digital boundaries. We contribute SplatXR, a Unity toolkit that operationalizes this structure as building blocks for interactive, immersive apps leveraging 3D Gaussian splatting: a standalone renderer, splat-to-mesh reconstruction, splat physics, splat blending, and offloaded splat object reconstruction via on-headset capture. SplatXR unites vision and graphics techniques to make splat-based XR development broadly available, and we validate it through demonstrative applications and a small developer study.

Rethinking Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Splatting: A Few-Shot Learning Perspective on Overfitting Prevention 2026-10-19
📝 5 pages, 5 figures. First-place solution in the sparse-view track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 Volumetric Video Challenge
Show abstract

Sparse-view novel view synthesis (NVS) is essential for AR/VR and real-time communication applications, yet remains challenging when reconstructing 3D scenes from limited viewpoints. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels in dense-view scenarios, it suffers from overfitting and geometry degradation under sparse inputs. Current approaches often tackle these problems from a local perspective, addressing either data quality or training regularization individually, lacking a unified and interpretable framework for systematic improvement. We introduce a new perspective that reframes sparse-view 3DGS from a few-shot learning viewpoint, treating each Gaussian primitive as a neuron and leveraging deep learning regularization techniques. Under this unified framework, our approach generates more effective supervision from scarce observations through two strategies: (1) data-related supervision enrichment via optical flow-guided pseudo-view synthesis and joint camera-scene optimization; and (2) training-time regularization via motion-aware dropout and early stopping, which suppress overfitting while preserving dynamic-scene stability. Extensive experiments on the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 volumetric video challenge sparse-view dataset demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art optimization-based and feed-forward baselines. Our system won first place in the sparse-view track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 challenge.

RxGS: Relightable Gaussian Stylization via BRDF Decomposition 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Reference-based 3D scene stylization transfers the appearance of a reference image onto a reconstructed 3D scene while preserving scene geometry. Recent methods perform this transfer by optimizing the appearance representation of neural scene models including Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). These methods optimize an RGB appearance representation, in which a single per-point colour combines the effects of material reflectance, environment illumination, together with the shading it produces, and the viewing direction. When stylization modifies this combined colour, the illumination effects present during stylization are baked into the stored appearance, so the stylized scene cannot be rendered under different illumination. To address this limitation, we present RxGS, a relightable 3D scene stylization framework built on a Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF)-aware 3D Gaussian representation. Rather than modifying a per-point RGB colour, RxGS optimizes only the diffuse albedo field recovered by inverse rendering, while leaving roughness, metallicity, surface normals, and the estimated environment lighting unchanged. As a result, the stylized scene keeps all the components a physically-based renderer needs other than the edited albedo, and can therefore still be relit. RxGS optimizes the albedo field using a nearest-neighbour feature matching loss together with a spatial smoothness prior over Gaussian centres. We evaluate the method using a Geometry Consistency Cost (GCC) based on optical-flow warping error. RxGS achieves the lowest GCC on all evaluated NeRF-Synthetic scenes and remains competitive on real-captured scenes, while additionally supporting relightable stylization, which existing reference-based 3D scene stylization methods do not provide.

MAGS-SLAM: Monocular Multi-Agent Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Geometrically and Photometrically Consistent Reconstruction 2026-10-19

Collaborative photorealistic 3D reconstruction from multiple agents enables rapid large-scale scene capture for virtual production and cooperative multi-robot exploration. While recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM algorithms can generate high-fidelity real-time mapping, most of the existing multi-agent Gaussian SLAM methods still rely on RGB-D sensors to obtain metric depth and simplify cross-agent alignment, limiting their deployment on low-cost or power-constrained robotic platforms, especially given the wider availability of RGB cameras. To address this challenge, we propose MAGS-SLAM, the first RGB-only multi-agent 3DGS SLAM framework for collaborative scene reconstruction. MCSplat: Multi-View Photometric and Geometric Consistent Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting for Driving Scenes 2026-10-18
📝 24 pages, 14 figures, 6 tables; Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Driving-scene reconstruction via feed-forward Gaussian splatting from sparse multi-view images remains challenging due to two factors. First, cross-camera photometric inconsistency, caused by variations in exposure, ISP, and illumination, can be incorrectly absorbed into Gaussian geometry. Second, unreliable local geometric supervision arises from normal-based constraints near depth discontinuities. To address these issues, we present \textbf{MCSplat}, a multi-view consistent feed-forward Gaussian splatting framework for driving scenes. MCSplat constructs a canonical appearance space from depth-guided multi-camera correspondences to decouple geometry learning from camera-specific appearance. A multi-scale Bilateral Grid Transformer restores raw camera appearance from canonical renderings, enabling photometric supervision without contaminating Gaussian geometry. To improve local geometric reliability, we estimate Fisher-information-based uncertainty from photometric sensitivity and use it to adaptively weight surface-oriented self-supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved reconstruction quality and generalization across driving datasets. Geometry-oriented evaluations further reveal enhanced cross-view depth consistency and local surface-normal agreement, while qualitative results show fewer geometric artifacts and more coherent structures across overlapping views.

In this letter, we present GOFusion, a real-time visual mapping system for autonomous robot navigation that anchors Gaussian primitives to local occupancy maps, providing both spatial awareness for path planning and high-fidelity appearance that enables information-driven robot navigation. GOFusion builds 3D Gaussian maps from pose estimates and local occupancy maps obtained via visual-inertial SLAM and enables pose corrections to maintain both geometric and visual consistency. Voronoi-guided Bilateral 2D Gaussian Splatting for Arbitrary-Scale Hyperspectral Image Super-Resolution 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Most existing hyperspectral image super-resolution methods require modifications for different scales, limiting their flexibility in arbitrary-scale reconstruction. 2D Gaussian splatting provides a continuous representation that is compatible with arbitrary-scale super-resolution. Existing methods often rely on rasterization strategies, which may limit flexible spatial modeling. Extending them to hyperspectral image super-resolution remains challenging, as the task requires adaptive spatial reconstruction while preserving spectral fidelity. This paper proposes GaussianHSI, a Gaussian-Splatting-based framework for arbitrary-scale hyperspectral image super-resolution. We develop a Voronoi-Guided Bilateral 2D Gaussian Splatting for spatial reconstruction. After predicting a set of Gaussian functions to represent the input, it associates each target pixel with relevant Gaussian functions through Voronoi-guided selection. The target pixel is then reconstructed by aggregating the selected Gaussian functions with reference-aware bilateral weighting, which considers both geometric relevance and consistency with low-resolution features. We further introduce a Spectral Detail Enhancement module to improve spectral reconstruction. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of GaussianHSI over state-of-the-art methods for arbitrary-scale hyperspectral image super-resolution.

OUGS: Active View Selection via Object-aware Uncertainty Estimation in 3DGS 2026-10-18
📝 12 pages. Updated author manuscript corresponding to the Eurographics 2026 / Computer Graphics Forum publication
Show abstract

Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have achieved state-of-the-art results for novel view synthesis. However, efficiently capturing high-fidelity reconstructions of specific objects within complex scenes remains a significant challenge. A key limitation of existing active reconstruction methods is their reliance on scene-level uncertainty metrics, which are often biased by irrelevant background clutter and lead to inefficient view selection for object-centric tasks. We present OUGS, a novel framework that addresses this challenge with a more principled, physically-grounded uncertainty formulation for 3DGS. Our core innovation is to derive uncertainty directly from the explicit physical parameters of the 3D Gaussian primitives (e.g., position, scale, rotation). By propagating the covariance of these parameters through the rendering Jacobian, we establish a highly interpretable uncertainty model. This foundation allows us to then seamlessly integrate semantic segmentation masks to produce a targeted, object-aware uncertainty score that effectively disentangles the object from its environment. This allows for a more effective active view selection strategy that prioritizes views critical to improving object fidelity. Experimental evaluations on public datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the efficiency of the 3DGS reconstruction process and achieves higher quality for targeted objects compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, while also serving as a robust uncertainty estimator for the global scene.

Localization and Neural Splatting on the Edge: An FPGA Scene Memory for Robots 2026-10-18
📝 8 pages
Show abstract

Resource-constrained robots and autonomous vehicles need scene memories that localize a camera and render nearby views without a desktop GPU. Scene-coordinate regression (SCR) and 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) fit because they avoid an SfM stage, but ACEsplat-style pipelines are GPU-centered, and moving them to an edge FPGA is hard because SCR is pose-sensitive and 3DGS rendering is irregular and capacity-sensitive. We present LENS, an edge scene-memory acceleration system on the AMD KR260 that brings an ACEsplat-style workload to a low-power FPGA/ARM platform. Rather than proposing a new scene model, LENS co-designs the two dominant kernels with the board data path: an INT16 SCR accelerator that preserves pose accuracy, and a bit-exact fixed-point ordered 3DGS renderer that rewrites alpha transmittance as a log-sum-exp recurrence and removes provably useless streamed Gaussians. All LENS latency, power, and quality results are board-measured. The SCR engine preserves DSAC* localization accuracy at 25.42 ms, the on-board renderer reaches 34.11 dB on Cambridge against ACEsplat's 33.20, novel-view synthesis on RealEstate10K reaches 27.84 dB against 26.11, and all ten Wayspots scenes reach the ACEsplat rendering target. LENS wins performance per watt over a Jetson Orin NX on every deployment task, by 1.9x to 5.7x at about a quarter of the power, and is faster in absolute latency on localization and Wayspots rendering. On-device training reaches ACEsplat-level quality but not the GPU's time or energy, marking the remaining boundary for a future training accelerator.

FineSplat: Fine Patches, Local Groups, Detailed Splats 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) promises instant scene reconstruction with high-quality novel-view synthesis. Yet, fine-grained geometry and appearance remain difficult to recover, motivating us to revisit how feed-forward models represent, aggregate, and refine detailed visual information. We introduce FineSplat, a feed-forward 3DGS framework built around fine-grained image patches. Since global multi-view attention becomes prohibitively expensive at such high token resolutions, we develop a dual-grouping strategy that combines two complementary forms of locality: view-wise grouping enables efficient interaction among neighboring views, while 3D spatial grouping reconnects geometrically related tokens that may never have interacted under view-wise grouping by lifting them into the 3D scene space. We further leverage reconstruction error as direct feedback, providing an explicit rendering-based signal to recover fine-grained structures and appearance, thereby overcoming the limitations of one-pass feed-forward prediction. Finally, a compact Gaussian generation scheme preserves the benefits of fine-grained features without unnecessarily increasing the number of primitives. Together, these designs enable FineSplat to recover richer geometry and appearance while remaining efficient and compact, achieving state-of-the-art results by a substantial margin, under the same or smaller Gaussian budgets than existing methods.

SwitchSplat: Learning Discrete Object States from Casually Captured Scenes 2026-10-17
📝 SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 (Conference Papers). 19 pages, 15 figures, 8 tables. Project page: https://switchsplat.github.io
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting has made high-fidelity 3D reconstruction fast and accessible, yet existing representations assume a static world. This assumption breaks down in casual captures, where a user naturally interacts with the environment -- opening doors, toggling switches, or moving objects -- introducing discrete state changes that no current framework can model. Time-conditioned dynamic methods such as 4DGS conflate when a state was observed with what state the scene is in, limiting them to replaying seen sequences rather than composing arbitrary object configurations. We present SwitchSplat, the first 3DGS representation to address this gap via a base+slot decomposition: a shared static base captures the invariant scene, while each slot independently holds the two binary states (on/off) of one interactable object. Because the assignment of Gaussians to slots is fully differentiable, the model discovers the geometry of each state directly from video, without manual annotation. At inference, the user can freely combine slot states and render the resulting scene from any viewpoint. We validate SwitchSplat on a new casual-capture dataset and show that it outperforms static and dynamic baselines in novel-view synthesis quality, while uniquely supporting rendering of state combinations never seen during capture. Directionally Factorized Illumination with Spherical Harmonic Rotation 2026-10-17
📝 Submitted to ICRA
Show abstract

Many real-world capture scenarios involve an active light source rigidly attached to the camera: camera flashes for dark-environment imaging, underwater vehicle lights, and endoscopic/laparoscopic medical procedures. As the camera moves, the co-mounted light moves with it, causing the illumination direction at each surface point to change across views. Neural rendering methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) encode this pose-dependent illumination as surface coloration, producing appearance inconsistencies and making it harder to recover the underlying scene. We present \textbf{DaFISH}, which can be plugged into any novel-view synthesis (NVS) pipeline such as NeRF. It factorizes per-point emitted color into surface albedo and a spherical harmonic (SH)-represented illumination factor. During training, the SH illumination is evaluated directly, enabling stable joint optimization of albedo, lighting, and geometry. \textbf{DaFISH} provides analytical generalization of illumination to arbitrary camera poses without retraining. Building on the closed-form SH-to-irradiance conversion, the training pipeline is fully differentiable from SH evaluation through volume rendering. Averaged over the held-out frames of the five scenes, \textbf{DaFISH} attains the best mean PSNR (38.91\,dB), SSIM (0.9613), and LPIPS (0.0582) among all compared methods. It improves over the strongest splatting baseline as well as the closest NeRF-based baseline, while recovering the scene consistently across views compared to prior methods.

RoMo-GS: Efficient Online 3D Gaussian Splatting for Robot Mapping via Exposure-Integrated Motion Blur 2026-10-17
Show abstract

Online 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is emerging as a practical way to build a representation for robotics applications. However, deploying it on real-world platforms exposes multiple challenges. Rapid maneuvers of a robot induce motion-blurred images, which corrupt the reconstructed geometry and cause camera pose drift. Furthermore, the number of Gaussians grows without bound during continuous online mapping. In this study, we present RoMo-GS, an online 3DGS system designed to address these challenges. Instead of averaging N discrete sub-frames as in prior motion blur-aware 3DGS, we analytically integrate each projected Gaussian over the exposure interval, yielding an O(1) complexity per Gaussian. Building on this, we bound the Gaussians' growth with a Hessian-based pruning score, while jointly refining pose, velocity, and Gaussians with Adam and an online LM step over the Gaussians. On multiple public datasets and a self-collected dataset, RoMo-GS matches or exceeds offline deblurring 3DGS methods in rendering quality while running 15--150x faster, and operates online at competitive quality with about 20% fewer Gaussians than the online 3DGS baseline.

O-SCD++: Replay-Free Online Scene Change Detection via Recursive Evidence Fusion 2026-10-17
📝 Project Page:https://chumsy0725.github.io/O-SCD/
Show abstract

Online scene change detection requires an agent to detect what has changed in a previously mapped environment while it is still on site, observing from unconstrained viewpoints and without access to future observations. This causal setting is strictly harder than its offline counterpart, and online methods have consistently trailed offline accuracy. O-SCD closed that gap by treating change as a persistent property of a 3D Gaussian splatting map of the reference scene, surpassing the strongest offline methods in change detection accuracy while operating at over 10 frames per second. O-SCD optimizes a change field over the Gaussians of the reference map, refitting it to all past cue maps at every frame, a step that consumes more than half of the per-frame budget and forces accuracy to be traded against speed. We present a replay-free formulation that eliminates this trade-off. Because the reference map stays fixed, rendering the change field is a linear operation. Each new observation can therefore be carried onto the map's Gaussians directly, by a single backward pass of the renderer, which reduces multi-view fusion to an exact two-scalar recursion per Gaussian. Past observations are never stored or revisited. The self-supervised objective that O-SCD minimizes by descent also determines the decision threshold exactly, by sorting, yielding a label-free per-frame cutoff whose margin provides a selective-prediction signal. On PASLCD, our method improves mean intersection over union from 0.489 to 0.575 and throughput from 11.8 to 25.6 frames per second, even exceeding O-SCD's offline result while remaining strictly causal.

HumanSkill-4D: Training-Free Language Querying of Animatable Gaussian Avatars in Space and Time 2026-10-17
📝 29 pages, 14 figures, 16 tables. Project page: https://jjunsss.github.io/HumanSkill-4D/
Show abstract

We introduce avatar referring segmentation, which selects regions of animatable Gaussian avatars from language queries. A query can also specify a pose or motion to select matching intervals. This lets users interact with avatars for local editing and motion inspection. However, making these selections with existing language Gaussian splatting methods poses two challenges: adjacent parts and garments are hard to separate and may be referred to indirectly (region ambiguity), while fine-grained motions can look alike in RGB (motion ambiguity). We address these challenges with HumanSkill-4D, a training-free framework that shares a multimodal large language model (MLLM) and geometric operators across avatars. The MLLM uses category names and descriptions to select categories by appearance, relation, or function. To find matching intervals, it composes operators into programs that check query conditions on driving joint trajectories stored in a motion memory. These category choices and operator outputs make selections traceable, while composing or adding operators supports new motion conditions. To evaluate this task, we introduce three human-annotated benchmarks and show that HumanSkill-4D outperforms the baselines in part segmentation and 3D and 4D referring segmentation, with compact memories and only minutes of setup per avatar.

DIGS: Distribution-Informed Gaussian Splatting for Training-Free Open-Vocabulary 3D Segmentation 2026-10-17
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Open-vocabulary 3D Gaussian segmentation typically binds semantic features to the per-Gaussian state via per-scene gradient training (1-4 h) or the more recent closed-form distillation. Both commit each primitive to a single feature; therefore, boundary Gaussians spanning multiple 2D instances are forced into a hard decision that degrades retrieval accuracy and yields unstable mask boundaries at render time. We present DIGS, a training-free framework with two stages, class-agnostic mapping and multi-modal encoding, that decouples instance discovery from linguistic naming. At the Gaussian level, our distribution-informed representation maintains an explicit top-$M$ label distribution per primitive, updated by a tempered Dirichlet rule; preservation of multi-peaked label distributions at boundary primitives allows a late-argmax renderer to recover clean instance boundaries. A bidirectional state machine aggregates these distributions into a persistent class-agnostic instance ontology. The instance-level encoding stage then fuses the CLIP visual embedding of each instance's isolated 3D footprint with the CLIP-text embedding of an LLM-generated attribute description, disambiguating visually similar instances. On a single A100, DIGS completes mapping and instance encoding on LERF-OVS in 1.0-3.2 min per scene, depending on whether LLM fusion is enabled, and serves native 3D queries in 5.4 ms. It reaches 71.95% mean mIoU on LERF-OVS, outperforming both the training-free SFS and training-based LaGa baselines. DIGS also achieves the highest mean mIoU on LERF-Mask and 3D-OVS and surpasses Gaga and ObjectGS on Replica under their respective evaluation protocols. Code is available at https://github.com/ZephyrZos/DIGS.

X-Splat: Unified Gaussian Splatting for Joint RGB and X-ray 3D Reconstruction 2026-10-16

Show abstract

Jointly reconstructing RGB and X-ray provides new insights by connecting external appearance with internal structure, enabling a more complete understanding of real-world objects than single modality.
