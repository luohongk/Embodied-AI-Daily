GSVC: Efficient Video Representation and Compression Through 2D Gaussian Splatting 2026-10-28
📝 23 pages, 15 figures, 1 table. Extended version of our NOSSDAV 2025 paper
Show abstract

In this work, we explore 2D Gaussian splat as a new primitive for representing and compressing videos, showing its promise as an alternative to traditional signal-based primitives. We introduce GSVC, a new Gaussian splatting-based video codec that incorporates common techniques used in classical video codecs to exploit temporal redundancy between nearby frames. In GSVC, each frame is represented by a set of overlapping Gaussian splats, which, when rendered together, approximate the original frame. Gaussian splats for the first frame and subsequent frames following scene changes (called anchor frames) are learned from scratch, while the remaining frames (called propagation frames) are initialized from the Gaussian splats of their previous frames and then fine-tuned. Scene changes are detected through loss differences during the training process. To capture large motion or newly appeared objects, we randomly add Gaussian splats to propagation frames before fine-tuning. GSVC supports a knob for rate-distortion trade-offs through controlling the number of Gaussian splats for each frame. Gaussian splats with low contributions to the frame quality can be removed during training. GSVC supports two-pass encoding by propagating the Gaussian splats from a future propagation frame back to an anchor frame, further improving the video quality for challenging video scenes. To achieve efficient compression, GSVC uses a residual-based Gaussian splats quantization framework that encodes only changes to the Gaussian parameters between frames. Experimental results show that GSVC achieves rate-distortion performance comparable to state-of-the-art video codec implementations for AV1 and VVC. GSVC inherits the advantages of Gaussian splatting in rendering, allowing it to achieve the rendering speed of 1500 fps for a $1920\times 1080$ video. Project page: https://longanwang-cs.github.io/gsvc/.

BudSplat: Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting under a Rendering Budget 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting has recently emerged as a promising alternative to per-scene optimization, enabling fast novel view synthesis by predicting Gaussian primitives directly from input images. However, it quickly faces a bottleneck of rendering efficiency while scaling from sparse to dense multi-view inputs, which offer more geometric constraints but produce a rapidly growing set of candidate Gaussians. Existing solutions cannot reliably distinguish redundant primitives, leading to many redundant primitives from repeatedly observed regions and increased rendering cost without much gain in visual quality. We propose BudSplat, a compact feed-forward 3DGS framework that learns to keep the most valuable Gaussians under a limited primitive count. BudSplat has two novel designs. The first is a detail-aware importance score that is extracted from multi-level visual tokens, providing an explicit cue for regions where pruning is likely to harm perceptual quality. The score can be injected into voxel fusion to preserve high-value local contributors during multi-view aggregation. The second is quality-aware Gaussian pruning that selects a compact set of primitives by jointly considering rendering contribution and detail importance. The quality-aware pruning enables local quota redistribution that helps avoid over-allocating primitives to large redundant regions. Experiments on RE10K, DL3DV, and ACID show that BudSplat achieves a favorable trade-off among rendering quality and efficiency.

BitC-3DGS: High-Capacity 3D Gaussian Splatting Watermarking via Bit Compression 2026-10-28

High-capacity watermarking is necessary for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets to embed rich information (\eg, ownership, provenance, and authentication codes), enabling reliable identification and provenance tracking in large-scale 3D asset pipelines. Existing bit-to-token watermarking methods based on a pre-trained text encoder are limited to 77-bit messages due to CLIP's fixed 77-token context length, as tokens beyond this limit are unsupported by learned positional embeddings. To address this limitation, we introduce BitC-3DGS, a bit-compression framework that encodes multiple message bits per token. It employs a bit-compressed tokenization scheme that encodes multiple bits within the same chunk into a single semantic token. To enable recovery of the compressed information, it further introduces a dual-branch architecture for joint chunk decompression and bit decoding, along with a hard-message sampling strategy to improve combinatorial coverage during decoder training. Extensive experiments on the Blender and LLFF datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of BitC-3DGS for high-capacity watermarking, achieving high message recovery accuracy and rendering fidelity. For example, it supports 128-bit message capacity with recovery accuracy comparable to that of 64-bit messages in recent state-of-the-art methods.

Robust, Estimator-Agnostic Dynamic 3DGS Compression 2026-10-27
📝 Submitted to IEEE ICASSP 2027. This version adds an appendix; 16 pages, 15 figures
Show abstract

Dynamic 3D Gaussian splats (3DGS) model time-varying scenes using a separate Gaussian set per frame. While neighboring video frames are highly correlated due to smooth motion, Gaussian representations retain this correlation to varying degrees, depending on whether the estimator tracks them across time. Some 3DGS compression methods integrate the estimation to exploit temporal redundancy; here, we focus on robust compression regardless of the estimator. We concatenate groups of frames into one Gaussian set, augment each Gaussian with a frame index, and pass it to a static (i.e., non-temporal) 3DGS codec, converting temporal redundancy into spatial redundancy. Concatenated sets are spatially partitioned to limit memory. Our technique requires neither a motion model nor knowledge of the training method. Averaged over six N3DV sequences, all six static codecs achieve gains on tracked sets (-42.0% to -71.8% BD-rate) over per-frame coding. On untracked sets, all codecs except HGSC, which appears incompatible with our technique, remain competitive with per-frame coding (-3.5% to +5.0%). We further replace D-FCGS's I-frame coding with our technique while retaining its P-frame coding, yielding an overall BD-rate of -46.2%. We propose to visualize "trackedness" using an inter-frame similarity metric. The project is available at https://wcjj1236.github.io/d3dgs-benchmark.

GOFish: Full Field-of-View Gaussian Splatting and Mesh Extraction 2026-10-27
📝 Project Page: https://3dgofish.github.io/
Show abstract

Robots, autonomous vehicles, and consumer 360° cameras use fisheye lenses for wide fields of view (FoVs). Simulation-ready digital twins require photorealistic rendering across the full FoV and an aligned collision mesh. However, prior fisheye Gaussian Splatting methods only rasterize up to 180° and do not produce meshes, while Gaussian surface reconstruction methods assume pinhole training images. We present GOFish, the first 3D Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction method to train directly on fisheye images, rasterize FoVs beyond 180°, and extract an aligned mesh from the same scene. Together, the Gaussian scene and mesh form a LiDAR-free, simulation-ready digital twin that renders in real time, ray traces the full FoV, and provides collision geometry.

CTBakeGS: training-free baking of photorealistic CT volume renderings into 3D Gaussian splats 2026-10-27
📝 Code and project page: https://github.com/BocchiSlice/CTBakeGS
Show abstract

Photorealistic volume rendering of computed tomography (CT) may convey anatomy intuitively, but it needs the CT data, dedicated software and a powerful graphics processor. Three-dimensional Gaussian splatting (3DGS) displays photorealistic scenes in real time on less powerful devices, but existing medical approaches train each scene for minutes to hours. We aimed to convert a CT volume rendering directly into a 3DGS model without training. Splats were placed on an adaptive octree of millimetre-sized cells. Their colour and opacity were computed on the graphics processor with the renderer's lighting, cell opacity was converted analytically into splat opacity, and faint splats were retained stochastically above the 1/255 opacity floor of standard 3DGS rasterisers. Six public CT scans (1.5-mm voxels) were converted with three transfer functions and compared with converged references by peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity and a learned perceptual metric. Two-million-splat models reached a PSNR of 32.6 +/- 0.9 dB for bone, 30.1 +/- 1.0 dB for contrast-enhanced vessels and 32.3 +/- 1.5 dB for skin and bone. A conversion pass (usually two or three per model) took 4-30 s on a current laptop and 9-60 s on a desktop with a 4-GB graphics card. On the laptop, the models rendered at 68 frames per second at 800 x 800 pixels. Training-free conversion produced real-time 3DGS models of photorealistic CT renderings in about a minute or less per conversion pass on ordinary computers, with fidelity that we consider sufficient for interactive viewing.

The growing need for high-quality 3D content remains among the most prevalent challenges in virtual reality (VR). 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has established itself as an efficient representation for real-time, high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction. However, scaling 3DGS to large and unbounded scenes such as city blocks remains difficult. Existing divide-and-conquer methods alleviate memory pressure by partitioning the scene into blocks and training on multiple, non-communicating GPUs, but introduce new bottlenecks: (i) partitions suffer from severe load imbalance since uniform or heuristic splits do not reflect actual computational demands, and (ii) coarse-to-fine pipelines fail to exploit the coarse stage efficiently, often reloading the entire model and incurring high overhead. It also substantially reduces end-to-end and post-coarse training time under matched GPU budgets while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. Inserting virtual objects into scenes reconstructed via 2D or 3D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS/3DGS) is a key capability for content creation, AR, and synthetic data generation. However, realistic insertion requires the object to be seamlessly lit by the scene and, conversely, to cast physically plausible shadows onto it. Existing approaches either harmonize appearance in image space, sacrificing multi-view consistency, or approximate scene illumination with an environment map at the insertion point, which cannot represent near-field area lights such as windows and lamps that dominate indoor lighting. We study 3D visual grounding in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), where a referring expression should identify a target object as both a rendered 2D mask and a set of explicit 3D Gaussians. Prior methods typically extract 3D targets by applying heuristic thresholding or top-ratio filtering to dense primitive-level relevance scores. However, these scores often vary substantially across scenes and prompts, making such heuristics sensitive and leading to unstable targeting. ClusterSplat addresses this by reframing 3D target selection as cluster-level selection over scene-adaptive candidates. The method first learns instance-aware Gaussian features, forms feature-aware seed voxels, and merges neighboring seed voxels when a description-length criterion decreases, producing a scene-specific set of cluster candidates. Given a referring expression, a query-conditioned cluster scorer ranks the cluster candidates with a lightweight MLP, and the selected cluster directly defines the explicit 3D target while its cluster score is rendered for the 2D mask. On ScanRefer, ClusterSplat achieves the best rendered 2D segmentation and explicit 3D target metrics among the compared 3DGS baselines. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has rapidly become the preferred representation for high-fidelity novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. CleanGS: Pareto-Guided Post-Hoc Cleanup of 3D Gaussian Splatting Scenes 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted to ICVGIP 2026
Show abstract

A pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction can reproduce its capture views well while retaining off-object structure that becomes visible under reframing. We present CleanGS, a post-hoc cleanup method that removes such structure without retraining the underlying representation. Given calibrated views and binary target masks, CleanGS scores candidate three-view subsets using mask reliability, camera baseline, viewing-direction diversity, and complementary sparse 3D support. Rather than collapsing these objectives a priori, it computes the exact Pareto front over subsets of the reduced candidate pool and selects a balanced on-dominated subset using weighted Chebyshev scalarization. The selected views then drive rasterization-aware two-of-three voting to retain a subset of the original Gaussians. We evaluate the exported asset directly and introduce a fixed-pose widened-field protocol that exposes structure outside the original framing. Across 18 scenes, CleanGS achieves 19.03 dB standard and 17.83 dB widened-field PSNR, compared with 16.44/16.29 dB for FlashSplat and 15.28/15.10 dB for GaussianCut with the same three masks. CleanGS leads five of six photometric metrics and remains within 0.32 dB of the uncleaned reconstruction in standard PSNR. Its filtering-and-export stage takes a median of 20 seconds and 1.9 GB peak memory, compared with 3.1 minutes and 41.6 GB for FlashSplat and 4.2 minutes and 34.9 GB for GaussianCut. Code is available at https://github.com/smlab-niser/clean-gs/.

SG-Splat: Stable Geometry Learning under Pose Uncertainty for Pose-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting 2026-10-26
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a promising representation for real-time novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. For practical deployment, recent studies have explored pose-free feed-forward 3DGS, which reconstructs Gaussian representations directly from unposed input images. However, these methods remain sensitive to residual pose errors, which perturb canonical-space Gaussian transformation, introducing noisy supervision for geometry and attribute learning. To address this problem, we present SG-Splat, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that improves robustness to pose uncertainty and stabilizes the learning of Gaussian primitives. SG-Splat decouples geometry estimation and Gaussian attribute prediction into two dedicated branches, reducing error coupling between pose-sensitive geometry and Gaussian attributes. We further introduce a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline, consisting of Flow-Guided Pose Refinement (FGPR) and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction (RGC), to improve geometric alignment before Gaussian attribute reconstruction. Specifically, FGPR refines camera poses with flow-based correlation features and differentiable bundle adjustment. RGC then corrects initial point maps using depth residuals derived from FGPR, producing more reliable Gaussian centers. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time scene representation and novel view synthesis, gaining traction in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and AR/VR systems. However, the security vulnerabilities of 3DGS remain largely unexplored. In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive study of backdoor threats in 3DGS pipelines. We uncover an inherent security vulnerability in the adaptive density control mechanism of 3DGS, originally designed for detailed reconstruction, which enables attackers to induce localized gaussian proliferation, effectively embedding malicious backdoors into the scene geometry while remaining invisible from other viewpoints. To expose this threat, we propose GaussTrap, a novel stealthy poisoning framework tailored for 3DGS. GaussTrap operates in three stages (adversarial injection, geometric stabilization, and global fidelity restoration) to embed imperceptible yet adversarial Gaussian components into the reconstruction. It ensures consistent poisoning effects at specific viewpoints while preserving photorealism in benign views, thereby achieving high attack efficacy with low detectability. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks demonstrate that GaussTrap reliably induces targeted visual corruption under trigger conditions while maintaining high rendering fidelity elsewhere, highlighting a critical security blind spot in modern 3D rendering systems. Recent advances in Gaussian splatting, 3D foundation models, and video-passthrough mixed reality offer unprecedented fidelity for capturing and blending physical and virtual worlds. However, established XR methods rely on polygon-based pipelines that treat physical and digital environments as separate domains, making seamless integration across recent novel representations difficult. Because splats lack explicit surface geometry, they impede standard graphics techniques necessary for cross-reality interactions, such as shadow or collision handling. Furthermore, while 3D foundation models can reconstruct physical objects on-the-fly into digital splats, current XR systems fall short of leveraging this capability for user experiences that transition seamlessly between physicality and virtuality. SplatXR unites vision and graphics techniques to make splat-based XR development broadly available, and we validate it through demonstrative applications and a small developer study.

Sparse-view novel view synthesis (NVS) is essential for AR/VR and real-time communication applications, yet remains challenging when reconstructing 3D scenes from limited viewpoints. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels in dense-view scenarios, it suffers from overfitting and geometry degradation under sparse inputs. RxGS: Relightable Gaussian Stylization via BRDF Decomposition 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Reference-based 3D scene stylization transfers the appearance of a reference image onto a reconstructed 3D scene while preserving scene geometry. Recent methods perform this transfer by optimizing the appearance representation of neural scene models including Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). These methods optimize an RGB appearance representation, in which a single per-point colour combines the effects of material reflectance, environment illumination, together with the shading it produces, and the viewing direction. When stylization modifies this combined colour, the illumination effects present during stylization are baked into the stored appearance, so the stylized scene cannot be rendered under different illumination. To address this limitation, we present RxGS, a relightable 3D scene stylization framework built on a Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF)-aware 3D Gaussian representation. Rather than modifying a per-point RGB colour, RxGS optimizes only the diffuse albedo field recovered by inverse rendering, while leaving roughness, metallicity, surface normals, and the estimated environment lighting unchanged. As a result, the stylized scene keeps all the components a physically-based renderer needs other than the edited albedo, and can therefore still be relit. RxGS optimizes the albedo field using a nearest-neighbour feature matching loss together with a spatial smoothness prior over Gaussian centres. We evaluate the method using a Geometry Consistency Cost (GCC) based on optical-flow warping error. RxGS achieves the lowest GCC on all evaluated NeRF-Synthetic scenes and remains competitive on real-captured scenes, while additionally supporting relightable stylization, which existing reference-based 3D scene stylization methods do not provide.

Driving-scene reconstruction via feed-forward Gaussian splatting from sparse multi-view images remains challenging due to two factors. First, cross-camera photometric inconsistency, caused by variations in exposure, ISP, and illumination, can be incorrectly absorbed into Gaussian geometry. Second, unreliable local geometric supervision arises from normal-based constraints near depth discontinuities. To address these issues, we present \textbf{MCSplat}, a multi-view consistent feed-forward Gaussian splatting framework for driving scenes. MCSplat constructs a canonical appearance space from depth-guided multi-camera correspondences to decouple geometry learning from camera-specific appearance. A multi-scale Bilateral Grid Transformer restores raw camera appearance from canonical renderings, enabling photometric supervision without contaminating Gaussian geometry. To improve local geometric reliability, we estimate Fisher-information-based uncertainty from photometric sensitivity and use it to adaptively weight surface-oriented self-supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved reconstruction quality and generalization across driving datasets. Geometry-oriented evaluations further reveal enhanced cross-view depth consistency and local surface-normal agreement, while qualitative results show fewer geometric artifacts and more coherent structures across overlapping views.

Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have achieved state-of-the-art results for novel view synthesis. However, efficiently capturing high-fidelity reconstructions of specific objects within complex scenes remains a significant challenge. A key limitation of existing active reconstruction methods is their reliance on scene-level uncertainty metrics, which are often biased by irrelevant background clutter and lead to inefficient view selection for object-centric tasks. We present OUGS, a novel framework that addresses this challenge with a more principled, physically-grounded uncertainty formulation for 3DGS. Our core innovation is to derive uncertainty directly from the explicit physical parameters of the 3D Gaussian primitives (e.g., position, scale, rotation). This foundation allows us to then seamlessly integrate semantic segmentation masks to produce a targeted, object-aware uncertainty score that effectively disentangles the object from its environment. FineSplat: Fine Patches, Local Groups, Detailed Splats 2026-10-18

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) promises instant scene reconstruction with high-quality novel-view synthesis. Yet, fine-grained geometry and appearance remain difficult to recover, motivating us to revisit how feed-forward models represent, aggregate, and refine detailed visual information. We introduce FineSplat, a feed-forward 3DGS framework built around fine-grained image patches. Since global multi-view attention becomes prohibitively expensive at such high token resolutions, we develop a dual-grouping strategy that combines two complementary forms of locality: view-wise grouping enables efficient interaction among neighboring views, while 3D spatial grouping reconnects geometrically related tokens that may never have interacted under view-wise grouping by lifting them into the 3D scene space. We further leverage reconstruction error as direct feedback, providing an explicit rendering-based signal to recover fine-grained structures and appearance, thereby overcoming the limitations of one-pass feed-forward prediction. Finally, a compact Gaussian generation scheme preserves the benefits of fine-grained features without unnecessarily increasing the number of primitives. Together, these designs enable FineSplat to recover richer geometry and appearance while remaining efficient and compact, achieving state-of-the-art results by a substantial margin, under the same or smaller Gaussian budgets than existing methods.

Directionally Factorized Illumination with Spherical Harmonic Rotation 2026-10-17

Show abstract

Many real-world capture scenarios involve an active light source rigidly attached to the camera: camera flashes for dark-environment imaging, underwater vehicle lights, and endoscopic/laparoscopic medical procedures. As the camera moves, the co-mounted light moves with it, causing the illumination direction at each surface point to change across views. Neural rendering methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) encode this pose-dependent illumination as surface coloration, producing appearance inconsistencies and making it harder to recover the underlying scene. We present \textbf{DaFISH}, which can be plugged into any novel-view synthesis (NVS) pipeline such as NeRF. It factorizes per-point emitted color into surface albedo and a spherical harmonic (SH)-represented illumination factor. During training, the SH illumination is evaluated directly, enabling stable joint optimization of albedo, lighting, and geometry. \textbf{DaFISH} provides analytical generalization of illumination to arbitrary camera poses without retraining. Building on the closed-form SH-to-irradiance conversion, the training pipeline is fully differentiable from SH evaluation through volume rendering. Averaged over the held-out frames of the five scenes, \textbf{DaFISH} attains the best mean PSNR (38.91\,dB), SSIM (0.9613), and LPIPS (0.0582) among all compared methods. It improves over the strongest splatting baseline as well as the closest NeRF-based baseline, while recovering the scene consistently across views compared to prior methods.
