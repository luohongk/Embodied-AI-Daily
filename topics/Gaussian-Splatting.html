VeilGS: Solving Darkness as a Veil of 2D Basis Maps over Retinex 3DGS 2026-11-20
📝 Project page: https://errorlogic1211.github.io/VeilGS/
Show abstract

In a dark capture, the darkness is not uniform. How much light reaches a surface depends on where it lies relative to the few light sources, and the camera compresses what remains with a tone curve. Most low-light 3D reconstruction methods either apply one curve to every pixel of a view, so a dark object beside a lamp and a bright one in an unlit corner receive the same correction, or store the darkness in the 3D representation, where it can absorb scene detail. We present VeilGS, a 3D Gaussian Splatting method for low-light novel view synthesis that treats the degradation as a veil drawn over a clear scene. Each Gaussian carries an albedo and a shading latent instead of spherical harmonics, separating surface colour from illumination. Each view is darkened by a spatially varying gain followed by a global tone curve, and rendering without this veil gives the restored scene. A small network predicts a coarse basis for the gain from the observation, and its coefficients are solved for each view rather than learned. Training through this solve discourages the Gaussians from absorbing what the veil can explain. VeilGS needs no paired normal-light supervision and stores no per-view parameters. On Gaussian-DK, its restored renders score best on no-reference NIQE and CLIP-IQA and are rated highest in a blind user study with 55 participants. On LOM, it achieves results competitive with the state of the art.

PennyGS: Full Resolution 3DGS Training in Minutes on Consumer GPUs 2026-11-20
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting is increasingly fed by high-resolution captures from commodity devices, yet training still happens at reduced resolution with research-grade GPUs. We present PennyGS, a method to achieve full-resolution (4K) training of 3DGS on consumer GPUs by improving the efficiency of every stage of a training step. Improvements include a leaner splat with flat RGB color and a directional cone opacity, fused projection and key emission, forward rasterization optimizations with strided pixel sampling, an approximate tile SSIM loss, a warp-per-splat backward rasterization, and a fused backward projection and sparse Adam step. At the standard 30k-step budget, PennyGS is the fastest of 10 methods on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks&Temples 4K on all four GPUs at competitive quality, at least 3.9x faster than the quickest of the others, while peaking below 2 GB of GPU memory. On an RTX 3060, a full-resolution Mip-NeRF 360 scene trains in 6m 25s, faster than any other method trains it on an RTX 4090.

GSPIM: A Temporal-Locality-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting Accelerator with Polymorphic PIM and Multi-Frame Rendering Dataflow 2026-11-20
📝 17 pages, 23 figures, Accepted by HPCA 2027. Code repo: https://github.com/MAdrid1011/GSPIM
Show abstract

4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) enables high-quality real-time novel-view synthesis for dynamic scenes, supporting free-viewpoint video, mobile XR, and autonomous systems at the edge. Methods using explicit 4D distributions, keyframe interpolation, or anchor binding share two GPU data-movement bottlenecks. Temporal filtering follows model record loading, wasting LPDDR5 bandwidth on inactive data. Frame-isolated execution also overlooks highly overlapping active sets and depth relationships across adjacent frames, reloading shared attributes when working sets exceed on-chip cache capacity. To address these bottlenecks, we present GSPIM, a temporal-locality-aware 4DGS accelerator for PIM-enabled edge devices. Polymorphic PIM adapts a shared near-memory datapath to representation-specific field layouts and selection procedures. It filters and compacts valid data into contiguous inputs before the GPU accesses complete attributes, reducing ineffective transfers. Multi-Frame Rendering Dataflow reuses active attributes and stable depth relationships within a temporal window, reducing repeated work across frames. Hybrid Dataflow Scheduling overlaps near-memory data preparation with GPU rendering to reduce waiting. Evaluation combines a GPU-PIM model calibrated to Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB with a 1z DRAM physical implementation of the added circuits. Across three 4DGS methods and six dynamic scenes, GSPIM achieves a 5.36x geometric-mean speedup and a 4.99x geometric-mean energy-efficiency improvement over a fully optimized frame-by-frame GPU baseline. The maximum scene-level PSNR difference is 0.006 dB, and the added physical footprint is 18.7172 square millimeters across 32 LPDDR5 dies.

CryoGaussian: Heterogeneous Cryo-EM Reconstruction Made Explicit with Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-19
📝 59 pages, 25 figures, 15 tables
Show abstract

Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) determines macromolecular structures from noisy particle images. However, rare, transient compositional states and subtle, continuous conformational variation are difficult to resolve. We present CryoGaussian, a Gaussian splatting method for heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction that fits an explicit Gaussian density representation directly to the particle images through a differentiable rasterizer rendering orthographic line integrals modulated by the contrast transfer function. The Gaussians are initialized randomly, with their number derived from the estimated spatial support of the molecular density and their minimum size bounded by the Nyquist frequency of the particle images. To capture the compositional and conformational variation across particle images, we learn particle-image and per-Gaussian latents that jointly modulate the base position, amplitude, and scale of each Gaussian. Each structural state is then reconstructed by fitting the Gaussians conditioned on its inferred latent. On CryoBench, CryoGaussian achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy for both compositional and conformational heterogeneity. On experimental data, it resolves a rare compositional state of the 50S ribosomal subunit (class C4, 1.3% of particles) with rRNA helix 68 docked in the absence of the central protuberance, and continuous conformational motion of the SF3b and helicase subcomplexes of the pre-catalytic spliceosome. We further optimize the rasterizer for additive cryo-EM projection, reducing training time by 12.8-28.0$\times$.

PaveSplat: Resolution-Robust Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting with Residually Corrected Surface-Aligned Covariances 2026-11-19
📝 Accepted to ACCV 2026. 16 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables. Project page: https://janghunn.github.io/pavesplat/ ; code: https://github.com/janghunn/pavesplat
Show abstract

We present PaveSplat, a feed-forward Gaussian splatting method that adds a learned residual to the analytic surface-aligned covariance, keeping surfels paving the visible surface across rendering resolutions. Pixel-aligned feed-forward Gaussian splatting predicts one primitive per pixel from a few images in a single pass, and its rendering quality depends on how well these primitives cover the surface. Recent surfel-based methods compute the surface-aligned covariance analytically from predicted depth. However, this covariance has no way to correct the surfel orientation where the depth-derived frame is ill-posed. The surfels then cover the surface loosely; at the training resolution each surfel's one-pixel minimum footprint covers the gaps, but they show up as holes when the same prediction is re-rendered at a higher resolution. To address this, we keep the analytic covariance as an initialization, with its axes aligned to the surface tangents, and add an identity-initialized unit-quaternion residual that reorients a surfel only where this improves rendering, turning surfels that the analytic frame mis-aims back onto the surface. On RealEstate10K, PaveSplat achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS among single-Gaussian-per-pixel methods at every scale (1x-8x). The native-resolution gain is small; the benefit appears when the same prediction is rendered at higher display resolutions without re-running the network. On zero-shot DL3DV, it leads PSNR within this group at every scale and ranks first or second on SSIM/LPIPS. A coverage-hole analysis shows fewer uncovered pixels than both baselines under high-resolution re-rendering.

Multi-View Transformer with Geometry-Guided Multi-Modal ControlNet for 3D Virtual Try-On 2026-11-19
📝 20 pages, 2 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Editing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) capture of a person so that they wear a new garment requires every rendered view to agree on the same garment details. Existing 3D virtual try-on (VTON) pipelines, however, edit training views largely independently and fuse them with hand-designed heuristics such as manually chosen reference views and fixed attention-blending weights. The resulting inconsistencies are baked into the 3D representation and surface as texture drift, blurred prints, and implausible geometry in novel views. We present GS-VTON-MVT, a three-stage framework that combines implicit and explicit 3D reasoning for consistent 3DGS try-on. (i) A multi-view inpainting transformer jointly edits a set of training views with full cross-view self-attention, so that cross-view correspondence is learned rather than imposed. (ii) A geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet conditions the diffusion editor on the garment image together with depth and surface normals rendered from the current 3DGS state, supplying explicit structure where visual evidence is weakest. (iii) A pose-adaptive persona-aware attention replaces the fixed blending weight between current-view and reference-view features with weights derived from camera-pose proximity. On the 3D-VTONBench benchmark, GS-VTON-MVT improves FID, KID, LPIPS-based cross-view consistency, and user preference over general-purpose 3D editors and prior 3D try-on methods.

AGSR: Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution for Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-19
📝 Accepted at the 2026 IEEE International Workshop on Multimedia Signal Processing (MMSP 2026). Recipient of the Best Paper Award
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time novel view synthesis, but Gaussian rasterization attenuates fine-scale scene structure through the projected covariance of each primitive. We show that contracting these projected covariances at inference time can mitigate the effective bandwidth limitation induced by finite Gaussian footprints, motivating a multi-aperture rendering framework for super-resolution. Based on this insight, we propose AGSR (Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution), a lightweight inference-time framework that progressively fuses multi-aperture renderings using uncertainty-guided feature aggregation and geometric conditioning from transmittance and depth cues. AGSR operates as a plug-in enhancement for pre-trained 3DGS models. Experiments on the Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark show that AGSR outperforms lightweight image-space super-resolution baselines, with gains increasing as the underlying Gaussian representation deteriorates. The results provide evidence that covariance contraction exposes bandwidth-sensitive structural cues attenuated by native Gaussian rasterization. AGSR achieves these improvements while maintaining real-time performance with up to 139k parameters.

GeoGSplat: Geometry-Grounded Non-Pixel-Aligned Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Feed-forward Gaussian Splatting enables efficient novel view synthesis, but pixel-aligned prediction makes the primitive count grow with image resolution and view count, leading to substantial redundancy and increased computational cost. Non-pixel-aligned methods address this limitation by predicting a fixed number of Gaussians. Without explicit ray and depth constraints, these methods yield misplaced Gaussian centers, causing rendering artifacts under large viewpoint changes. To address this issue, we introduce GeoGSplat, a geometry-grounded feed-forward model that explicitly incorporates geometric priors into the non-pixel-aligned framework. A dual-branch encoder constructs Anchors and Triplane to capture coarse 3D structure and fine-grained scene features, respectively. Subsequently, a hierarchical decoder progressively decodes dense 3D points and Gaussian primitives, enabling 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis. GeoGSplat surpasses both state-of-the-art pixel-aligned and non-pixel-aligned Gaussian Splatting methods on DTU and NRGBD in terms of novel view synthesis and reconstruction. Multi-View Relighting with Mergeable Radiance Fields 2026-11-19
Show abstract

We propose MergeableRF, a method for radiance field relighting through model parameter merging. Given multi-view images of a scene under known basis lights, our goal is to relight the scene by directly combining the parameters of radiance fields trained under these lights. This is motivated by the linearity of light transport: the image under a mixture of lights is the same weighted combination of the images under each light. However, combining the parameters of independently trained radiance fields does not produce the corresponding combination of images. To address this, we represent a scene with shared geometry and a separate appearance network for each basis light. We start these networks from the same initialization and introduce merge-aware training, which teaches their parameter combinations to reproduce the corresponding combinations of images. Given a target HDR environment map, we compute its basis-light coefficients and use them to merge the appearance networks into a single relit model. Our method supports both Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), without material estimation, additional relighting networks, or target-specific optimization. Unlike image-space blending, it requires only one rendering pass per viewpoint. Experiments on synthetic Neural Gaffer objects and real OLATverse captures demonstrate relighting quality competitive with methods trained on the same multi-light observations.

SplatQ: Interaction-Aware Pruning of 3D Gaussian Splatting via Quadratic Selection 2026-11-19
📝 Under review at ICLR 2027 (main paper 9 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables; appendix included)
Show abstract

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pruning methods primarily rely on global Top-K selection based on independent importance scores, lacking regional budget control and ignoring joint deletion effects. Consequently, they cannot ensure balanced reconstruction quality across a scene. We propose SplatQ, a combinatorial pruning algorithm that coordinates regional budget allocation and joint Gaussian selection under a fixed budget. SplatQ partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, decomposing global selection into subproblems of a specific size. It then smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent excessive budget concentration. Finally, it constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects using the responses of representative pixel colors to changes in Gaussian opacity. Individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties are incorporated into a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) objective, solved using simulated annealing (SA) or a coherent Ising machine (CIM) to jointly select Gaussians. Experiments on 5 benchmarks demonstrate that SplatQ outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods in aggregate reconstruction quality, spatial quality balance, and rendering speed. Ablations confirm that budget smoothing improves local detail reconstruction, while interaction modeling improves Gaussian selection quality under a fixed budget.

VideoRNG: Relightable Neural Gaussians from Generative Video Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Generating realistic 3D assets remains constrained by classical representations: surface meshes and compact physically based materials struggle to express objects whose appearance arises from fine geometry or complex light transport, while existing Gaussian-splatting generators typically bake fixed illumination into appearance. We introduce VideoRNG, a two-stage method that generates complete, relightable 3D assets from a text prompt or a single image without prescribing a mesh or analytic material model. A video generator fine-tuned from a pretrained video diffusion backbone first produces a structured RGBA turntable sequence under a known pose, camera, and illumination. A feed-forward large reconstruction model initialized from a pretrained video diffusion backbone then maps the video-VAE latent frames of selected turntable frames directly to millions of anisotropic Gaussians with learned neural appearance features. To preserve high-frequency appearance while learning geometry, we introduce depth-anchored appearance training, and we make million-Gaussian reconstruction practical through foreground token selection and cost-aware distributed sampling. VideoRNG generates complex assets such as fur, feathers, and spider webs that can be interactively rendered, relit, and integrated into scenes.

HDR-GS: Efficient High Dynamic Range Novel View Synthesis at 1000x Speed via Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-18
📝 NeurIPS 2024; The first 3D Gaussian Splatting-based method for HDR imaging
Show abstract

High dynamic range (HDR) novel view synthesis (NVS) aims to create photorealistic images from novel viewpoints using HDR imaging techniques. The rendered HDR images capture a wider range of brightness levels containing more details of the scene than normal low dynamic range (LDR) images. Existing HDR NVS methods are mainly based on NeRF. They suffer from long training time and slow inference speed. In this paper, we propose a new framework, High Dynamic Range Gaussian Splatting (HDR-GS), which can efficiently render novel HDR views and reconstruct LDR images with a user input exposure time. Specifically, we design a Dual Dynamic Range (DDR) Gaussian point cloud model that uses spherical harmonics to fit HDR color and employs an MLP-based tone-mapper to render LDR color. The HDR and LDR colors are then fed into two Parallel Differentiable Rasterization (PDR) processes to reconstruct HDR and LDR views. To establish the data foundation for the research of 3D Gaussian splatting-based methods in HDR NVS, we recalibrate the camera parameters and compute the initial positions for Gaussian point clouds. Experiments demonstrate that our HDR-GS surpasses the state-of-the-art NeRF-based method by 3.84 and 1.91 dB on LDR and HDR NVS while enjoying 1000x inference speed and only requiring 6.3% training time. Code and recalibrated data will be publicly available at https://github.com/caiyuanhao1998/HDR-GS . Our project page at https://caiyuanhao1998.github.io/project/HDR-GS/ shows more video and interactive results. An online Gradio demo is also available on HuggingFace at https://huggingface.co/spaces/CaiYuanhao/HDR-GS

RPC-GS: Gaussian Splatting with native RPC Rendering for Satellite Imagery 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

We present RPC-GS, the first Gaussian Splatting framework for satellite imagery that operates natively with Rational Polynomial Camera (RPC) models. The RPC model is the de facto standard for representing the complex imaging geometry of modern pushbroom satellite sensors. To simplify rendering, prior satellite Gaussian Splatting methods replace the RPC model with perspective or affine camera approximations, leading to geometric errors during reconstruction. RPC-GS avoids these approximations by projecting Gaussian means and covariances directly through the RPC model during the splatting process. We embed the RPC model in a chain of carefully selected geo-coordinate transformations representing a mapping from splatting-suitable scene coordinates to image coordinates. To map the Gaussian covariance matrices, we derive a numerically robust Jacobian-based covariance projection for the (partially nonlinear) coordinate transformations. Since RPCs lack an explicit notion of camera depth, we integrate a metric ray-based depth formulation. We benchmark RPC, perspective, and affine camera models in a unified framework, with our native RPC renderer achieving the lowest reconstruction error on leading satellite benchmark datasets, improving mean altitude error over perspective and affine approximations by 34.3% and 13.5% on DFC2019, and by 5.0% and 1.2% on IARPA2016. Our implementation is publicly available at https://github.com/wagnva/RPC-GS.

Facet-SLAM: Real-Time Triangle Splatting SLAM 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Dense SLAM systems based on 3D Gaussian splatting build maps that render photorealistically, but their surface geometry is poor, and obtaining a mesh requires extraction or fusion stages. Triangle splatting avoids this with surface primitives whose vertices can be triangulated directly into a connected mesh, yet SLAM with this representation has so far relied on photometric tracking, which is too slow for localization at sensor rate. We present Facet-SLAM, the first real-time triangle splatting SLAM system for RGB-D cameras. Fast geometric registration is the natural alternative, but it is not sufficient alone, since the mapper's optimization displaces triangles from the observed surface and makes them unreliable registration targets. We therefore separate the two roles: the tracker sets each triangle's centroid from fused depth and registers frames to these centroids by GICP, while the mapper is restricted to non-translating updates via a change of variables. Our system achieves competitive tracking accuracy at around 100 FPS on a desktop GPU and runs at sensor rate on a Jetson AGX Orin. Compared with the photometric approach, it is over 20 times faster, matches rendering quality, and improves surface accuracy, reducing the chamfer distance of the Delaunay mesh on Replica from 1.55 to 0.86 cm. We will release the implementation at https://github.com/edward62740/Facet-SLAM.

Lifting 3D Gaussians: A Wavelet Multiresolution Representation for Scalable Gaussian Splatting Streaming 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted by MMSys'27; Project page: https://yuang-ian.github.io/liftgs/
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a scene as an unstructured set of primitives optimized at a single scale, offering no native support for progressive delivery to heterogeneous devices. Existing scalable approaches organize these primitives into independent quality layers or spatial hierarchies. However, maintaining separate splat representations across scales introduces redundancy, while independent layers have limited ability to correct coarse geometry as finer detail arrives. We present LiftGS, a multiresolution representation that organizes a scene into a coarse Gaussian set and a tree of refinements. Each refinement replaces one coarse Gaussian with two finer children whose attributes are learned relative to their parent. Inspired by wavelet lifting, we encode these changes using a shared detail vector and per-attribute factors that allow the two children to differ asymmetrically from the parent. A client first receives the coarse set, then reconstructs and substitutes children as refinement data arrives. This construction reuses previously transmitted attributes to encode finer detail and removes replaced parents from the active rendering set, reducing both refinement payload and rendering memory. Extensive experiments on four benchmark datasets show LiftGS cuts splat redundancy from over 65% to under 25%, reduces transmission payload and GPU memory by up to 2.4x and 5.5x over state-of-the-art scalable-3DGS baselines, achieves smooth quality transitions in simulated progressive delivery, and compresses a further 8.7x with standard coding at negligible quality loss. Improving While Deployed: Learning Proactive End-to-End Driving from Imperfect Human Takeover Data 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Once deployed on vehicles, end-to-end driving models continuously encounter challenging scenarios, generating valuable human takeover data that capture both model outputs and human behaviors. However, human interventions are often imperfect, as drivers may intervene too late or unnecessarily. Consequently, direct imitation can reinforce late responses or discourage otherwise appropriate maneuvers. To address this challenge, we propose an offline post-training framework that transforms imperfect takeover data into proactive supervision. We first construct diverse trajectory candidates by augmenting the plans of the deployed model and shifting recorded human corrective maneuvers earlier in time. We then train a scene-conditioned trajectory scorer to rank these candidates using expert demonstrations and unpaired preference signals from takeover logs. Using this scorer, we selectively replace pre-takeover training targets with higher-scoring candidates while retaining the original targets in normal driving scenarios.
