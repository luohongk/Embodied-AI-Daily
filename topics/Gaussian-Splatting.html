Lost on Campus: Evaluating Embodied Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in the Wild 2026-11-24
📝 NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://dyfcalid.github.io/Lost-on-Campus/
Show abstract

Embodied Spatial Reasoning (ESR) is central to deploying Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in real-world embodied tasks, yet remains inadequately evaluated. Existing benchmarks primarily assess spatial reasoning in static images or synthetic indoor environments, where semantic shortcuts often suffice, making it difficult to faithfully evaluate spatial reasoning capabilities in the wild. In light of this, we present Lost on Campus, a benchmark for evaluating ESR in large-scale real-world outdoor 3D environments reconstructed by 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our benchmark introduces a unified reasoning-action evaluation framework that seamlessly integrates diagnostic QA for isolated reasoning with closed-loop interactive navigation for active reasoning under multimodal instructions. To enable fine-grained diagnosis, we evaluate six capability aspects of ESR: action grounding, spatial foresight, metric awareness, goal-directed planning, ego-allocentric localization, and spatio-temporal consistency. Extensive experiments reveal that: (i) Compared to indoor settings, real-world outdoor environments impose substantially higher demands on spatial reasoning, where existing models exhibit poor performance; (ii) VLMs still struggle with fine-grained visual alignment, with failures in spatial foresight and ego-allocentric localization emerging as primary bottlenecks; (iii) Improving multimodal interaction capabilities and long-term spatial reasoning is crucial for advancing embodied intelligence. These findings highlight that faithful evaluation of ESR demands benchmarks tightly coupling perception, reasoning and action within realistic and continuous environments.

When Variance Is Not an Error Map: Calibrated Uncertainty for Radiative Gaussian Splatting in Sparse-View CT 2026-11-24
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Radiative Gaussian Splatting enables fast and high-quality reconstruction in sparse-view CT, but reliable localization of reconstruction errors within the imaged anatomy remains challenging. It is unclear whether current uncertainty maps can reliably indicate where the reconstruction is wrong. We investigate when uncertainty can serve as a spatial error map and present an error-localization system that predicts voxel-wise reconstruction error by combining predictive uncertainty with complementary reconstruction-derived information. Under fixed Gaussian geometry and independent rectified-Gaussian density variables, an exact closed-form predictive moment is derived in this work to enable direct computation of voxel-wise variance without repeated reconstruction sampling. The resulting uncertainty maps were evaluated over both the whole reconstruction volume and the object foreground. Experiments show that strong whole-volume uncertainty-error correspondence can conceal poor error ranking within the object foreground area. This discrepancy recurs across three tested uncertainty constructions on a 15-scene benchmark. Guided by this analysis, our system combines closed-form predictive dispersion with image, geometry, cross-method discrepancy, and projection-residual cues through a learned error predictor. The resulting system substantially improves voxel-wise error localization within the anatomy, providing a spatially resolved approach to reconstruction reliability assessment in sparse-view CT.

First-Hit Photoconsistency for Neural Surface Reconstruction 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Recent neural implicit and Gaussian splatting approaches have achieved impressive results in multi-view surface reconstruction. However, jointly optimizing geometry and appearance introduces ambiguities. A parametric appearance model can compensate for geometric errors, reproducing the input images accurately without an accurate surface. We demonstrate accurate neural implicit surface reconstruction using only multi-view patch photoconsistency with geometry-based visibility, without volumetric rendering or parametric appearance models. For each reference ray, sphere tracing computes the first surface intersection. Its position and normal define a local patch whose photoconsistency is evaluated across visible neighboring views using colors sampled directly from the input images. For efficient sphere tracing, we use a 1-Lipschitz multi-layer perceptron. We use a single photoconsistency loss, without foreground masks or auxiliary geometric regularization. Experiments show that our method achieves the lowest mean Chamfer distance on BlendedMVS (BMVS) among compared methods and matches the geometric accuracy of leading reconstruction methods on DTU.

FastBridge: Closing the Model-Based Realization Gap in Safety Filters on 3D Gaussian Splatting for Fast Quadrotor Flight 2026-11-24
📝 preprint, 8 pages, 4 figures
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Fast quadrotor flight requires safe obstacle avoidance under tight onboard compute limits. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides a continuous, geometry-aware scene representation for perception-driven navigation, existing 3DGS safety filters use reduced-order models such as single- and double-integrators that ignore actuator limits and assume commanded accelerations are realized instantaneously. Building on an analytic collision cone barrier for 3DGS, we introduce a nonlinear, actuator-aware safety filter enforced through the full quadrotor dynamics. We derive a high-relative-degree collision cone exponential CBF (ECBF) and a backup CBF whose forward-simulated backup policy provides a certified admissible fallback whenever the QP is infeasible under input constraints. Compared with a state-of-the-art 3DGS safety filter, our approach reduces trajectory jerk by 47% and runs 2.25 times faster. We demonstrate the ECBF in simulation and validate the backup CBF on hardware for real-time navigation in cluttered, perception-derived environments.

Beyond Primitive Bloat: High-Fidelity Gaussian Splatting via Coordinate-Convolution Neural Network 2026-11-24
📝 Project Page: https://abhi1kumar.github.io/qonvolution/
Show abstract

Gaussian Splatting (GS) has revolutionized Novel View Synthesis (NVS) with fast training and real-time rendering, yet pushing its fidelity to arbitrary high levels remains an open challenge. We theoretically explain this barrier by proving that discrete Gaussian representations require an exponentially increasing number of primitives to raise training PSNR, which inevitably triggers optimization issues and validation overfitting. To bypass this scaling limit without primitive bloat, we formulate detail recovery as a continuous neural field over coarse renderings and prove that monotonically reducing approximation risk to zero requires jointly leveraging low-fidelity signals, local neighborhood, and coordinate queries. Guided by this result, we augment splatted renderings with an existing Coordinate-Convolution Neural Network (CCNN) that jointly convolves rendered low-fidelity signals with 2D pixel coordinate queries to synthesize residuals. Integrating CCNN into five diverse Gaussian Splatting baselines establishes new state-of-the-art fidelity across NVS benchmarks while preserving real-time rendering speeds with minimal training and storage overhead. Beyond NVS, they also consistently boost performance of 1D/2D regression and 2D super-resolution tasks.

AnyPrune: Optimization-Free Pruning for Any Feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting Pipeline 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pipelines reconstruct a scene from images in a single forward pass, representing it as real-time-renderable 3D Gaussians. However, the resulting representations contain far more Gaussians than are needed to render the scene faithfully, especially when there are many input views. This drastically increases memory and storage requirements. Existing pruning methods either require costly re-optimization, which defeats the purpose of a single forward pass, or rely on simple strategies that do not exploit all the available information. We present AnyPrune, a transformer-based, optimization-free pruning module designed to work on top of any feedforward 3DGS pipeline. To remove unnecessary Gaussians, AnyPrune learns and exploits pipeline-specific redundancy patterns through a short single-GPU training on the frozen target feedforward pipeline. AnyPrune achieves better rendering quality than existing optimization-free pruning methods in most settings and generalizes to unseen datasets, as shown through extensive experiments across multiple feedforward pipelines, datasets, and input view counts. It prunes a 1.6M-Gaussian field in about $5\,\mathrm{s}$ on a consumer GPU, faster than every method that merges Gaussians or re-optimizes the field. It can also be combined with the pruning built into feedforward pipelines, pruning further at moderate ratios while preserving or improving rendering quality. Finally, it can even suggest a scene-specific pruning ratio that retains most of the quality.

Seeing to Drive on a Single Board: Attention-Free Depth for Onboard Robot Navigation 2026-11-24
📝 13 pages, 14 figures, 5 tables. Code, models and data: https://github.com/itaim18/DANIEL
Show abstract

A small ground robot can carry one camera and a few watts of compute, but the depth foundation models that could turn that camera into a range sensor are vision transformers sized for GPUs. We bring one to a Raspberry Pi 5 with the Hailo-8L, an accelerator that runs every layer in integer arithmetic, attention included. Quantised for it, Depth Anything V2 loses large parts of the scene, and we measure why: at 449 tokens, 91% of softmax weights fall below one int8 step while carrying 17-33% of the attention mass. Our answer is to distil, not quantise. An attention-free ResNet-34 U-Net student of Depth Anything V2-Large (24.5M parameters) runs at 27.1 FPS on the chip, its int8 output matches its float twin (Pearson 0.99993), and on held-out scenes of its simulated yard it is the most accurate depth network on the chip (AbsRel 0.269 against 0.354-0.530). On 1,000 stereo-verified points of a real outdoor drive it is not resolvably different from the vendor's Depth Anything V2-Small build, while running faster (27.1 vs 21.6 FPS). Ranging obstacles by where they meet the floor needs only the camera height, so mapping, hybrid-A* planning and SLAM run on the Pi's own CPU. In real-time hardware-in-the-loop tests in a Gaussian-splat reconstruction of a real yard, the stack reaches the goal without contact in 7.9$\pm$1.1 of 10 scenes, against 9.2$\pm$0.4 with exact obstacles, and in 7.7$\pm$0.7 on its own pose with simulated wheel odometry (ten seeds each; 8.2$\pm$0.4 with the real Pi and Hailo-8L in the loop, five seeds). The source code, pre-trained models and data are available at https://github.com/itaim18/DANIEL.

OP2GS: Feature-Free Gaussian Splatting with Dual Opacity for 3D Instance Segmentation 2026-11-24
📝 Under review
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides an explicit and efficient scene representation, but its primitives do not naturally expose object-level identity. Existing methods either learn contrastive per-Gaussian features that need post-hoc clustering, or supervise identity encodings with view-consistent 2D masks. We propose OP2GS, a lightweight object-aware Gaussian representation that augments each primitive with an explicit instance label and a dedicated instance opacity $σ^{*}$, which serves as an instance occupancy field for object-mask rendering. This dual-opacity formulation decouples visual appearance from instance occupancy, so that $σ^{*}$ can be learned and the instance labels can be corrected without touching the appearance parameters $σ$ and $c$. We learn $σ^{*}$ with an instance occupancy loss that follows the standard transmittance-based visibility of 3DGS. A discrete regrouping step then corrects the instance labels using multi-view mask consensus. Since no high-dimensional features are required, OP2GS can be plugged into existing 3DGS training pipelines and applied unchanged to frozen 3DGS assets. On the ScanNet++ validation set with the official class-agnostic protocol, OP2GS improves over OpenSplat3D, the strongest comparable baseline, with both SAM and EntitySeg masks; it is also best on Messy Rooms and, under the Gaga protocol, on Replica, while storing only one scalar and one integer per Gaussian. Code will be released.

Completion-Aware Compression for Progressive 3D Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-24
📝 23 pages, 6 main-paper figures and 4 supplementary figures
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables high-quality novel-view rendering, but its large scene representations are costly to transmit. Pruning and compression reduce file sizes, yet progressive delivery also requires usable reconstructions before the full scene arrives. Existing progressive representations provide partial scenes, but level-based updates are limited by predefined coding boundaries. This paper presents a progressive 3DGS codec that balances attribute fidelity with the image benefit of making Gaussians available earlier. Its single compact stream supports partial 3D reconstruction and adds newly decoded Gaussians as more bytes arrive. The stream supports fine-grained updates at arbitrary received-byte budgets, without separate encodings or later attribute refinement. At matched complete-file sizes and received bytes, completion-aware allocation improves 1/8 PSNR over attribute-only allocation by 0.48-1.43 dB. The proposed codec compresses original 3DGS representations by 62-88 times while retaining near-reference Full PSNR. Under the evaluated 50-Mbps delivery model, partial reconstruction becomes available in approximately 0.2 s.

Panoramic cameras capture nearly the entire spherical field of view in a single frame. ST-SfM: Spatio-Temporal Structure-from-Motion for Initialising 4D Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-24
📝 9 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Dynamic Gaussian splatting is usually initialised either with random points or with a structure-from-motion (SfM) cloud of the first frame. Neither says anything about what moves, where and when. We introduce ST-SfM, a sparse spatio-temporal SfM for synchronised, calibrated multi-camera video that splits the scene into two branches. Static structure is detected once per camera on a temporal-median image, gated for temporal stability and matched along epi-polar lines. Motion is detected as space-time interest points (STIPs) in the video volume, described with HOG3D and matched only between candidates that agree both epipolarly and in time. Union-find tracks that refuse inconsistent merges are triangulated with RANSAC over all view pairs, giving static 3D points and dynamic 4D points (x, y, z, t) with sub-frame times. We use ST-SfM derived sparse 4D cloud to initialise 4D Gaussian splatting. In comparison with random initialisation on N3DV scenes we get improvements in PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS.

SplatTok: Data-Driven Compression of 3D Gaussian Splats with Scene-Level Tokens 2026-11-24
Show abstract

3D Gaussian splats enable real-time rendering but require substantial storage. Existing compression methods either encode the primitives of a given splat or optimize a compact representation independently for each scene. Yet a Gaussian splat is only one realization of a scene: different arrangements of primitives can produce nearly identical rendered views. We exploit this freedom on the decoding side with SplatTok, a learned scene-resynthesis codec. Trained across thousands of real-world scenes, a transformer encodes an existing splat into compact, spatially anchored latent tokens, and a shared decoder synthesizes a new set of standard Gaussian primitives without preserving correspondence to the input. The representation is learned through rendering supervision rather than primitive reconstruction. At compression time, a quantization-aware latent search refines only the scene code, keeping the networks frozen, and closes most of the encoder's remaining error. On standard benchmarks, using only renders of the input splat for the latent search, without the original training images, SplatTok matches the PSNR of the feed-forward codec FCGS with 4 to 6.5 times smaller files, compressing input splats about 75 to 110 times. With the training images, it exceeds the PSNR of the per-scene method HAC++ with 13 to 41% smaller files. A single model trained on DL3DV generalizes to unseen scenes with millions of Gaussians and supports multiple target rates without retraining.

CryoGaussian: Heterogeneous Cryo-EM Reconstruction Made Explicit with Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-24
📝 59 pages, 25 figures, 15 tables
Show abstract

Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) determines macromolecular structures from noisy particle images. However, rare, transient compositional states and subtle, continuous conformational variation are difficult to resolve. We present CryoGaussian, a Gaussian splatting method for heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction that fits an explicit Gaussian density representation directly to the particle images through a differentiable rasterizer rendering orthographic line integrals modulated by the contrast transfer function. The Gaussians are initialized randomly, with their number derived from the estimated spatial support of the molecular density and their minimum size bounded by the Nyquist frequency of the particle images. To capture the compositional and conformational variation across particle images, we learn particle-image and per-Gaussian latents that jointly modulate the base position, amplitude, and scale of each Gaussian. Each structural state is then reconstructed by fitting the Gaussians conditioned on its inferred latent. On CryoBench, CryoGaussian achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy for both compositional and conformational heterogeneity. We further optimize the rasterizer for additive cryo-EM projection, reducing training time by 12.8-28.0$\times$. Reconstructed density maps are available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/ZiwenWang28/CryoGaussian.

UniView: Large-Baseline View Synthesis via Video Diffusion Models 2026-11-24
📝 Project Homepage: https://zhouhyocean.github.io/uniworld-view/ Code: https://github.com/PKU-YuanGroup/UniWorld-View
Show abstract

Novel view synthesis from casually captured monocular images and videos offers a practical route to immersive content creation without dedicated multi-view capture. However, the limited viewpoint coverage of such inputs leaves scene geometry insufficiently constrained, restricting the ability of reconstruction-based methods, such as neural radiance fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), to render views far from the observed viewpoints. Generative approaches leverage learned visual priors to synthesize unobserved content, but maintaining geometric consistency and precise camera control remains challenging under large viewpoint changes. We present UniView, a unified framework for large-baseline novel view synthesis and scene generation from monocular images and videos. Central to our approach are an occlusion-aware point cloud renderer and a dual-branch conditioning mechanism. The renderer uses triple reprojection to identify and filter unreliable projections under large viewpoint changes. The conditioning mechanism integrates the resulting geometric guidance with source-view appearance within the video diffusion model, enabling precise camera control while preserving visual details and synthesizing unobserved content. To extend view synthesis to scene generation, we introduce a two-stage generation strategy that decouples viewpoint changes from temporal evolution. This strategy produces dense, synchronized multi-view videos that provide additional supervision for dynamic Gaussian Splatting, enabling more complete 4D generation from monocular observations. Extensive evaluations across static and dynamic scenes demonstrate the effectiveness of UniView in novel view synthesis and scene generation, combining precise camera control with geometric consistency and visual fidelity.

View Management with Gaussian Placement and Opacity Regularization for Online 3DGS 2026-11-24
📝 17 pages, 5 figures, including supplementary material
Show abstract

The realistic rendering of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has drawn interest from real-time applications that require maps to be built online. This led to coupling 3DGS with SLAM, providing camera poses and initial Gaussians for the map. However, the map is still optimized with designs inherited from conventional SLAM, which train only on keyframes selected for localization and regularize geometry with uncertain frontend depth. These designs overlook the model complexity of a 3DGS map: with millions of parameters but few views of each region, the map overfits to sparse inputs. Resolving this is challenging, as every view and Gaussian added to the map competes for the same real-time budget. We close this gap from both sides. To add views while keyframes preserve the structure of the map, we progressively admit non-keyframe images as mapping updates complete and allocate the limited updates across different groups of frames according to the supervision each group provides. To control the model complexity of the map, edge-guided initialization places new Gaussians where image detail requires them. To complement the uncertain frontend depth, Self-Referenced Opacity Regularization (SOR) uses accumulated rendering evidence, at no extra rendering cost, to suppress the opacity of Gaussians that persistently contribute in front of a map-derived surface reference. Together, these designs improve both the appearance and the geometry of online maps within the real-time budget.

NIRJALA: Draining the Sea out of Gaussian Splatting with Three-Path Optics and Reversible Physics 2026-11-24
📝 Withdrawn by the authors due to unresolved data provenance and authorization issues affecting the experimental results, tables, and figures in Sections 4--5 (Tables 1--6 and Figures 4--9). The manuscript was submitted prematurely without complete verification
Show abstract

Water does not just tint an underwater photograph; it rewrites it. Each pixel mixes attenuated scene radiance, backscattered light, and scene light scattered forward on its way to the camera, and many combinations of scene and water explain the same pixels. Most Gaussian-splatting methods for underwater scenes model only the first two effects and remove the water by dividing by a transmission that decays exponentially with range, so their inversion gain grows without bound. We present NIRJALA (Sanskrit for "without water"), a 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that recovers water-free appearance and geometry together. TRIVENI, a three-path image formation model, adds a range-saturating forward-scattering term to direct transmission and backscatter; we prove that this keeps the inversion gain bounded at every range and bound how errors in the estimated backscatter and attenuation propagate into the recovered colours. PRATIBIMBA, a reversible physics anchor, ties a flexible neural water model to a ten-parameter physical model in both directions, so scene content cannot hide inside the water. SAMARASA collects the physics-informed objectives, including a range-binned colour prior. To score water removal against ground truth, we built SAJALA ("with water"), a 30-scene suite path-traced in Blender Cycles with water-free images, depth, and normals under controlled water colour and turbidity. On SAJALA, NIRJALA raises PSNR from 24.34 to 24.76 dB and lowers depth AbsRel from 0.109 to 0.091 against the strongest baseline; on real SeaThru-NeRF scenes it lowers LPIPS from 0.194 to 0.175 while rendering at 44 FPS. We also discuss where appearance metrics and geometry disagree underwater, and the limits of our evaluation.

HiGS: A Hierarchical Architecture for Fast Differentiable 3D Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-24
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) makes one screen tile the cell of partitioning, the cell of rasterization, and the unit of parallel work. Partitioning favors large tiles and rasterization small ones, and with one unit per tile the densest tiles set the frame time. We present Hierarchically Tiled Gaussian Splatting (HiGS), a unified architecture for rendering and reconstruction that gives each role its own granularity: Gaussians are partitioned over coarse macro-tiles, rasterized over the fine render tiles within them, and processed in bounded depth-sorted batches. A dense region issues more batches instead of one longer unit, so frame time follows the work in a view rather than its densest tile. The same batches carry reconstruction: compositing supplies the transmittance and color needed to differentiate each one independently, leaving optimizer memory traffic as the dominant training cost. We fuse the optimizer into the backward pass, halve the parameters and moments it stores and updates directly, and defer appearance updates outside the view. HiGS renders scenes faster than every published method we evaluate, by the widest margin on views with uneven load, and reconstructs them faster and in less memory, at equal image quality and with exact front-to-back alpha compositing.

GS-Pool: Object-Level Change Detection in 3D Gaussian Splatting 2026-11-23
📝 20 pages, 11 figures, 9 tables, including supplementary material. Project page: https://boazkg93.github.io/gs-pool/
Show abstract

Many physical spaces require photographic monitoring of changes to their structure over time. When each visit is reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), a direct comparison of the two reconstructions cannot answer this because training is stochastic and two reconstructions of an unchanged space never coincide. To address this, we propose GS-Pool, which takes two independently reconstructed Gaussian scans of the same space and returns the changed objects in each, together with their change masks. Our method begins by extracting SAM2 masks from the photographs of each visit. These are then lifted onto the Gaussians that render them and merged into an object pool, a collection of 3D objects, so every decision is taken once per object in 3D. We introduce a novel photographic carrier approach, which co-opts the existing 3DGS mechanism. It does so by applying the photographs of the other visit and back-propagating them to the Gaussians that rendered each pixel. We combine this with GS-Diff's geometry and colour terms and our distilled DINOv3 features. This evidence is compared with objects identified as unchanged in both visits to set a change threshold for each scene. On the PASLCD benchmark, GS-Pool achieves mIoU/F1 scores of 0.75/0.85 against 0.64/0.76 for GS-Diff, the strongest offline method for detecting change in 3DGS. Finally, we return each changed object as a set of Gaussians, which can be inspected in 3D.

Enhancing Sparse-View Gaussian Splatting with Feed-Forward Priors via Spectral Fusion and Distillation 2026-11-23
📝 15 pages. Project page: https://linwk20.github.io/spectral-fusion-gs/
Show abstract

Gaussian splatting from sparse training views renders high-quality images near training views, but reconstruction quality degrades at distant regions, which are undersampled by the training set. Feed-forward novel view synthesis (NVS) models use strong learned priors to resample the scene from substantially different viewpoints, but they take more than ten times as long to render and produce high-frequency artifacts on out-of-domain scenes. We propose a plug-and-play method that enhances an existing sparse-view Gaussian model with a pretrained feed-forward NVS model. We observe that the two are complementary in the frequency domain: the prior is more accurate at low frequencies, and the Gaussians at high frequencies. The reliability of the prior relative to the Gaussians is inversely proportional to the image frequency, so the optimal fusion weight is a first-order low-pass filter with one parameter that can be fitted. Our key idea is then to intelligently fuse the reliable part of each of the two methods. To remove the slow feed-forward model at render time, we use these fused images as teachers and distill them back into Gaussians, balanced with supervision from input views. Our method improves the quality of various sparse-view Gaussian methods without sacrificing rendering speed, and our analysis works on different prior models. WildAnySplat: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting in the Wild 2026-11-23
📝 Accepted to SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 (Conference Papers). Project page: https://wildanysplat.github.io/ ; Supplementary material: https://wildanysplat.github.io/wildanysplat_supp.pdf
Show abstract

We present WildAnySplat, a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that enables robust novel-view synthesis from in-the-wild multi-view images. Unlike controlled captures, real-world images often contain multi-view inconsistencies caused by transient occluders, illumination changes, and camera ISP, which degrade the quality of existing feed-forward reconstruction methods. To address this challenge, we introduce a student-teacher framework that learns transient-robust and appearance-consistent 3D representations. A teacher network operates on clean images, while a student processes the corresponding in-the-wild inputs. The teacher provides clean supervision to guide the student toward a consistent canonical representation. Since paired clean and in-the-wild data are scarce, we use a hybrid data strategy, combining real paired captures, synthetic augmentations, and pseudo-clean labels generated from optimization-based reconstructions, bridging the domain gap between synthetic and real-world data. We adopt a transformer architecture with dedicated DPT heads for camera pose, depth, and Gaussian prediction.
