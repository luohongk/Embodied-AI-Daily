DexLatentWorld: 3D Cross-Embodiment Latent Action World Models for Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-23
📝 15 pages, including supplementary material
Show abstract

World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Project materials, including demonstration videos and source code, are available at https://vinaymohan0409.github.io/DexLatentWorld/.

World Models as Native Latent Action Models 2026-11-23
📝 19 pages, 7 figures, 6 tables. Project page: https://pawteam-eai.github.io/native-lam/
Show abstract

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

Can Multimodal Fusion MakeSense for Embodied Vision in Degraded Sensing? 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Embodied vision systems rely on complementary sensors to perceive and act as conditions change. Even models that accept arbitrary sensor subsets can exhibit negative fusion: adding a sensor increases prediction error. We propose MakeSense, a multimodal fusion framework that preserves sensor-specific evidence in private streams and combines information through shared bottleneck tokens. Training pairs full and subset views of the same degraded observations, penalizing higher error with more sensors. Where geometry permits, the distance readout uses range measurements alone, while the rest of the prediction draws on all available inputs. Paired tests across five datasets reveal helpful and harmful sensor additions. MakeSense reduces negative fusion on MM-Fi. With lightweight adapters and per-dataset training, the same design improves UAV localization on MMAUD, cooperative 3D detection on V2X-Real, few-shot pose transfer to RELI11D, and command prediction on Humanoid-Everyday, where a world model trained on the frozen core's outputs forecasts future fused states more accurately than a linear baseline at longer tested horizons. MakeSense supports embodied sensing across human activity, aerial systems, robotics, and autonomous driving.

LiveAudio: From Bidirectional to Autoregressive Diffusion for Real-Time Streaming Video-to-Audio Generation 2026-11-23
📝 23 pages, 12 tables, 14 figures
Show abstract

Video-to-audio generation synthesizes sound that matches a video in content and timing, with applications ranging from film post-production to live streaming, games and interactive world models. Interactive applications receive video continuously and need the sound to follow it in real time, yet most existing video-to-audio models are bidirectional and must observe an entire clip before producing any sound. We propose LiveAudio, a streaming video-to-audio framework that generates high-quality and synchronized audio in real time while the video is playing. In contrast to prior streaming models trained from scratch, LiveAudio converts a bidirectional model into a streaming generator so as to build on its audio quality and synchronization. LiveAudio generates audio block by block, and each block depends only on the video that has already been played. The streaming generator is obtained from the bidirectional model through progressive causal fine-tuning and distillation, so that each block needs only a few sampling steps. Experiments on VGGSound show that LiveAudio outperforms existing streaming models in audio quality and synchronization, and surpasses the bidirectional model it starts from in synchronization. On a single A100 GPU, it runs in real time with a real-time factor of 0.79 and a time to first frame of 143 ms. Audio samples are available at https://streamaudio.github.io.

AI for Games in the Foundation Model Era 2026-11-23
📝 130 pages, 27 figures, 21 tables. Project page: https://eurekaleo.github.io/awesome-ai-for-games

Show abstract

Foundation models, alongside advances in learned game-world models, are reshaping AI across the game lifecycle. Beyond playing games, recent systems model players and game dynamics, support design and development, adapt player-facing experiences at runtime, and evaluate resulting artifacts. Yet these directions have evolved largely separately, obscuring which capabilities transfer across settings and which remain tied to particular games, engines, interfaces, or player populations. We organize the literature into six roles according to the immediate use of AI output: playing and acting; modeling players and games; designing games; building and maintaining games; generating and adapting at runtime; and testing and evaluating games. For each role, we examine what structure is supplied by the game or workflow, what AI learns or produces, which capabilities and artifacts transfer across settings and roles, and what evidence supports the claims. We identify cross-role connections: trajectories train world models, learned environments provide experience for agents, design specifications drive executable implementations, and play or testing feedback guides revision. Evaluation is most standardized for bounded game playing and selected learned environments, while persistent state in learned worlds, repeated software revision, validated player modeling, sustained runtime adaptation, and representative automated testing remain less established. The central challenge is to reuse or transfer outputs and capabilities across roles while re-establishing evidence for effectiveness in the game-specific contexts where they are used.

Zhengrong: World Models with Pole-Based Action-Response Factorization for Direct Visual Planning 2026-11-23
📝 16 pages, 4 figures, 11 tables. Technical report, Beijing Freedo Technology Co., Ltd
Show abstract

Direct visual world models predict the effects of candidate action sequences without recursive latent rollout, yet they leave the temporal structure of action effects unspecified. We introduce Zhengrong (Zhengrong-PoleWM), a world model that decomposes a latent trajectory into an initial-condition anchor, a free response, and delayed responses to action innovations. Nonlinear networks map visual history and causal action prefixes to response coefficients. A shared bank of stable conjugate poles determines how each response evolves with elapsed time. This factorization preserves nonlinear interactions across actions, anchors predictions exactly at the initial state, prevents future actions from affecting earlier predictions, and yields bounded responses to finite control plans. We derive an exact re-anchoring identity that identifies the additional basis direction $Ψ_κ(s)$ needed to represent shifted trajectory tails. Across four offline image-goal control benchmarks (Cube, TwoRoom, PushT, and Reacher), Zhengrong-PoleWM achieves $90.83\% \pm 0.47\%$ macro-average success over two training seeds at epoch 20, exceeding the strongest baseline (QQWorld) by 6.08 percentage points. Selecting checkpoints by validation loss yields $91.21\% \pm 1.00\%$. In a controlled first-epoch Cube study without restart regularization, neural and pole response heads outperform a generic direct predictor by 20.67 and 23.34 percentage points, respectively. On an NVIDIA A100 GPU, median latency for a full 30-iteration cross-entropy method search ranges from 74 to 87 ms across the four tasks. The mean of these medians is 77.5 ms, a 15.8% latency reduction relative to Fast-LeWM and a $7.1\times$ speedup over autoregressive LeWM.

ActField: Learning Action Effects as Continuous-Time Latent Dynamics 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Learning how actions change visual states is essential for prediction and decision-making in embodied tasks. Existing action-conditioned JEPA world models typically predict future representations at discrete observation intervals, leaving the temporal evolution of action effects implicit. We introduce ActField, a continuous-time JEPA world model that explicitly models latent state evolution through an action-conditioned vector field integrated over physical time. ActField separates action-independent evolution from action-induced changes, with the latter modulated by the latent context. A reference-conditioned representation and multi-step dynamics loss further support accurate long-horizon prediction. Together, these components provide a structured and temporally continuous formulation for predicting action-conditioned visual dynamics. Experiments across LIBERO, DROID, and BEHAVIOR demonstrate improved visual prediction fidelity and more accurate action-induced motion prediction over existing world models. Further evaluations on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate the utility of ActField in improving downstream policy planning through VLM-guided action selection.

LiteNWM: Efficient Latent World Models for Onboard Visual Navigation in the Wild 2026-11-23
📝 12 pages, 11 figures, including appendix
Show abstract

Direct visual navigation policies generate trajectories efficiently but do not explicitly evaluate their future consequences. Generative navigation world models provide this foresight through visual rollouts, which are costly when evaluating multiple candidates. We present LiteNWM, a latent navigation world model that shares visual encoding across candidates and jointly predicts their action-conditioned future representations at multiple horizons, while a learned scorer uses these predictions to select trajectories. In offline evaluations on RECON, SCAND, and SACSoN, LiteNWM reduces macro-averaged trajectory error by 17.56% relative to NoMaD+NWM-XL and achieves a 128.00-fold end-to-end speedup on an RTX 5090. The same evaluator transfers from NoMaD to MBRA without proposer-specific retraining, reducing MBRA's macro-averaged trajectory error by 16.2%. In real-robot experiments in unseen indoor and outdoor environments, LiteNWM improves navigation success from 43.3% to 83.3% relative to NoMaD. These results demonstrate that LiteNWM can be deployed for future-aware planning and closed-loop navigation on a physical robot.

CivWorldBench: Evaluating Language World Models in Structured Multi-Agent Sandbox Worlds 2026-11-23
Show abstract

The growing use of language models for forecasting motivates a broader evaluation of their world-model capabilities. Such evaluation must go beyond predictive accuracy to assess hidden-state inference and intervention prediction. Real-world forecasting benchmarks offer limited access to hidden-state ground truth and controlled experiments. We introduce CivWorldBench, a benchmark built on a multi-agent strategy game engine with recorded internal states, controlled observations, and paired intervention rollouts. Its 1,900 decision points from 475 worlds support three task levels: association forecasting, belief inference, and intervention prediction. Reference predictors support attributable evaluation of capability gaps, with an expert harness and empirical ceiling estimates contextualizing scores. Stored trajectories make intermediate predictions verifiable, while agent-play experiments test whether world-model capabilities support decision-making. The main panel comprises eleven frontier language models. Composite benchmark scores correlate with real-world forecasting leaderboards on matched model panels, more closely than with a general-capability index, supporting its use as a proxy for real-world forecasting capability. Strong association forecasts do not consistently extend to belief inference or intervention prediction, and effect magnitudes remain poorly calibrated despite informative direction predictions. Process verification reveals errors missed by final-answer evaluation, and structured reasoning improves intervention prediction for the strongest model in the process study. Agent-play experiments identify useful assisted plans and motivate evaluating decision-relevant capabilities beyond association forecasting. These findings identify hidden-state inference, intermediate-state prediction, and effect calibration as priorities for world-model training.

Write, Evolve, Read: Streaming Cameras into a Persistent World State for 4D Occupancy Forecasting 2026-11-23
Show abstract

4D occupancy forecasting predicts the 3D semantic occupancy of a driving scene seconds ahead. Over a few seconds, the scene changes little while the vehicle moves, so most of the space ahead has already been seen. Sorting the future into unchanged, changed, and unseen space, we find that current camera-based world models fall short on the unchanged part: they fail to hold on to what they have already seen, rather than to forecast what will change. We therefore make what the model has seen its state. PersistWorld streams camera frames into a persistent 3D world state anchored in the scene, writing each frame once instead of rebuilding the state from past frames. The state is learned end to end with the forecast, and an evolver predicts it at any queried time along the ego trajectory. On Occ3D-nuScenes, PersistWorld raises the IoU of the best camera-based world model by 19 points at 1-3 s and by 18 to 24 points up to 8 s, while running more than twice as fast. VeriTouch: Verified Visuo-Tactile World Models that Improve Robot Policy Learning 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Scaling visuo-tactile policy learning is constrained by the high cost of real-world data collection and the difficulty of building realistic simulations. Generative models offer a scalable source of training data, yet useful demonstrations require plausible tactile feedback, temporal alignment with visual contact, and accurate actions. To this end, we present VeriTouch, a framework for generating and verifying synthetic visuo-tactile trajectories for policy learning. Conditioned on initial images and task instructions, our world model, VeriGen, jointly generates high-fidelity dual-view videos and realistic tactile sequences over complete manipulation trajectories. WM-VS: Progress-Aligned World Models for Closed-Loop Visual Servoing 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Closed-loop visual servoing requires predictions that indicate whether an action reduces task error, not only whether the action is plausible. We call this gap the prediction-control mismatch and introduce WM-VS, a target-centric progress-aligned world-model framework for closed-loop visual servoing. Offline target-region DINOv2 correspondences define a signed four-dimensional servo coordinate for translation, scale, and in-plane rotation. Stage 1 aligns action-conditioned latent transitions with this coordinate; Stage 2 freezes the world model and trains a reactive joint-velocity policy with action imitation, consequence supervision, and short imagined rollouts that favor error contraction. Deployment is RGB-only and reactive, without online trajectory optimization. On a real 7-DoF eye-to-hand system, WM-VS reaches a corner RMSE no larger than 10 percent of its initial value in 30/30 trials and retains this criterion at the final valid frame in 25/30 (83.33 percent). Removing future-error alignment reduces retention to 26.67 percent. The learned progress signal agrees with an external AprilTag corner error not used for training or control (mean Spearman rho = 0.8778). Without retraining, two unseen 3D targets achieve translation-error reductions of 86.48 percent and 90.27 percent and rotation-error reductions of 70.01 percent and 65.70 percent. These results link progress-aligned action consequences to repeated closed-loop correction and transfer. Code and data will be released as open source.

Seeing Together, Acting Apart: Shared Environmental Understanding for Multi-Robot Navigation 2026-11-23
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Multi-robot embodied systems are typically designed to collaborate by coordinating actions, sharing trajectories, or learning joint policies. We ask a complementary question: can collaboration emerge before decision-making, by first building a shared understanding of the environment? We introduce Shared Environmental Understanding, a policy-agnostic representation that aggregates partial egocentric observations from multiple robots into a shared latent world model. This shifts collaboration from the policy space to the representation space, enabling decentralized decision-making with shared environmental context. To this end, we propose Shared Environment Encoding and Retrieval (SEER), a framework that organizes multi-robot observations into a shared memory, retrieves relevant context via spatio-temporal similarity, and encodes it using a predictive latent world model. Experimental results demonstrate that SEER captures action-relevant environmental structure and consistently improves navigation performance across both specialized VLN agents and general vision-language model agents, while preserving single-agent capabilities.

AxE: Benchmarking Agent-Environment Generalization of Video World Models via Grounded Events 2026-11-23
📝 Project page: https://AxEBench.github.io
Show abstract

Edge cases are not the long tail; they are the norm. An embodied system deployed across changing environments, bodies, objects, and tasks will routinely encounter combinations that were absent from its training data. General-purpose physical intelligence therefore requires world models that reuse prior knowledge, predict consequences, and adapt with little or no additional training. Yet current evaluations of conditional video generation emphasize visual quality, prompt alignment, or isolated physical plausibility, leaving systematic generalization difficult to measure. We introduce AxE Bench, a benchmark that organizes condition changes along eight factors of the acting agent and of its environment, and pairs every original condition with controlled variants that change one side or both. WBench-plus applies the same structure to an existing multi-turn benchmark, pairing each WBench scenario with variants on the environment side, through its world setting or camera behavior, or on the agent side, through its interaction instructions. Additionally, to score generated futures, we develop an evaluation harness that combines vision-language reasoning with targeted computer-vision evidence, converting each video into temporally localized events and comparing generated events with reference events in both time and semantics. We summarize each model with the AxE Score, event Fidelity weighted by its Stability across original-variant pairs. Across twenty models, generated events match reference events far more often in timing than in content: no model exceeds a Fidelity of 13.01, the highest Stability is 0.55, and the highest AxE Score is 7.10. Together, these components turn world-model generalization from an informal aspiration into a falsifiable evaluation program for real and simulated interactions. How Latent Transition Geometry Matters for Planning in World Models 2026-11-23
Show abstract

World models support planning by predicting how the environment evolves under candidate actions. However, one-step predictive accuracy alone does not characterize multi-step planning utility, which depends on distinguishing candidate futures. This raises a natural question: How does latent transition geometry shape planning? We investigate this through an action-conditioned transition-field view, using curvature regularization as a controlled intervention. Across four continuous-control tasks, appropriately tuned regularization improves planning, while excessive regularization can be detrimental. By decomposing the transition field into affine and non-affine components, we find that regularization reduces action sensitivity mainly through the non-affine component. To assess its role in planning, we directly manipulate the non-affine action response in frozen models and show that it affects candidate selection and closed-loop success. Further analysis shows that curvature generally brings predicted futures closer together, while preserving more of the differences along the goal direction. This helps explain why the planning effects vary across tasks. We observe broadly consistent planning trends across five sampling- and gradient-based solvers on LeWM and similar rollout-geometry trends on DINO-WM. Qualitative attention visualizations reveal accompanying shifts toward task-relevant visual structure. Together, these findings clarify how latent transition geometry influences multi-step planning.

SWAM: State-aware World Action Model for Multi-Agent 3D Environments 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Interactive world models generate virtual environments autoregressively from player actions and visual history. However, visual history alone cannot reliably capture the evolving states of independently acting non-player characters (NPCs) in real games, particularly when they are outside the camera view. Recent multi-agent world models address this challenge through joint multi-view generation or persistent shared state, but often rely on external observations, like maps or scene geometry, or predefined state transitions. We present SWAM, a State-aware World Action Model that separates agent-state evolution from autoregressive visual generation in interactive 3D environments. A lightweight state model propagates character dynamics from actions without consuming generated frames, combining motion priors with learned scene-dependent corrections. The predicted states then guide an autoregressive diffusion transformer alongside visual history through a novel conditioning scheme. Rather than requiring an explicit scene geometry map or visibility tests, the conditioning indicates where characters may appear, allowing the diffusion model to learn their appearance and occlusion. This design enables NPCs to evolve independently of visual generation while maintaining consistent observations across visibility changes, interactions, and respawns. Evaluations in a custom Godot-based first-person shooter game demonstrate improvements in visual fidelity, NPC consistency, and long-horizon stability over existing approaches.

MOVER: Planning Beyond Motion 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Residual world models forecast a driving scene through its temporal change, so feature components that stay constant over time vanish from the residual pathway. We ask whether evidence with weak temporal change, such as a waiting pedestrian or a stopped vehicle, can be used more effectively for planning, and introduce MOVER, a complementary event-routing mechanism. A training-time teacher measures how each spatially grounded event query responds when a past frame is removed from shared history and contrasts that sensitivity with the event's pooled temporal residual, prioritizing events that depend on history but receive weak residual evidence. A student predicts the resulting priorities from unperturbed history, so the teacher adds no inference cost. Under fixed total routing mass, one distribution scales a low-rank future bird's-eye-view update and biases trajectory-conditioned event reading. On nuScenes, MOVER reduces ResWorld's UniAD-style average L2 from 0.65 to 0.55 m and its collision rate from 0.23% to 0.12% without direct planner ego status (0.50 m and 0.09% with it), adding 4.1 ms per frame. Matched controls show that the complementary teacher outperforms either signal alone and that the allocation acts mainly during training. Adapted to BridgeDrive-LEAD, MOVER reaches a 96.52 +/- 0.65 driving score over three seeds on the 220-route Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, exceeding matched controls in each paired seed by a margin within seed variation.

One Latent World for Heterogeneous LiDARs: A Scan Pattern Agnostic 4D World Model via Direct Ray Decoding 2026-11-23
📝 34 pages, 29 figures, 28 tables
Show abstract

LiDAR world models generate and predict metric 3D observations in the form of LiDAR scans. They typically synthesize scans following the scan pattern of their training sensor or render them from an intermediate 3D surface or field. However, following one scan pattern limits transfer to LiDARs with other patterns, and rendering from intermediate geometry can cause incorrect depths and false returns. In this paper, we propose a scan pattern agnostic 4D LiDAR world model that decouples the scene from sensor observation, so one spatial latent can be observed by heterogeneous LiDARs at the same mounting position without regeneration. On KITTI-360 and nuScenes, our model generates realistic scenes and reduces the registration error between predicted frames by about 70% relative to the best compared world model. Its decoder yields 19 to 28% lower depth error than the rendering used by other world models, and generated scans benefit several downstream tasks. Our code will be open-sourced. EnvTrap: Revealing the Environment-Only Attack Surface in Embodied AI via Consequence-Blind Action Execution 2026-11-23
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 15 tables, 5 figures
Show abstract

As AI models evolve from text and vision to physical agents, embodied AI faces a fundamentally different attack surface. Prior attacks on embodied systems have used crafted instructions, adversarial visual inputs, and training-time policy modifications; we study environment-only object rearrangements with unchanged models and instructions. We reveal an attack surface in safety-critical spatial relations among existing objects. An adversary who rearranges objects, without modifying instructions or accessing models, can cause hazardous consequences. In this work, we propose EnvTrap, a diagnostic pipeline that constructs paired safe, trap, and null-trap (benign but misleading) embodied scenarios. Vector-Quantized World Model: Latent Feedback Motion Planning for Robot Manipulation 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Visual world models have shown strong potential for robot planning, yet long-horizon manipulation remains challenging due to compounding prediction errors, large search complexity, and latent plans that the low-level controller cannot reliably reach. In this work, we introduce Vector-Quantized World Model (VQ-WM), a latent feedback motion planning framework that integrates discrete long-horizon search with continuous visual feedback control: a discrete latent predictor models transitions between vector-quantized latent states, allowing the planner to look far ahead and find a shortest path to the goal through discrete search and a continuous learned controller sequentially reaches the planned states through visual feedback, correcting deviations to mitigate compounding errors. Whereas most vector quantization uses Euclidean distance, we use a novel estimator of the first-passage-time as a quasi-metric, which aligns the planning abstraction with the controller's capabilities. Our solution also outperforms strongest world-models by 13.3--85.3 percentage points on simulation benchmarks spanning manipulation and locomotion, demonstrating consistent progress and new capabilities.
