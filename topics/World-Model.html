ScaleWorld: High-Resolution World Generation through Shared Prediction and Refinement 2026-11-19
Show abstract

High-resolution generation enables video world models to render fine-grained scene structures and textures, but directly generating high-resolution video incurs substantial computational and memory costs. We present ScaleWorld, a causal video model that unifies low-resolution world prediction and high-resolution refinement within a single shared backbone. By refining its own predictions, ScaleWorld produces high-resolution world videos without requiring a separate refinement model. For each video chunk, the shared backbone first completes a low-resolution prediction and then performs one-step high-resolution refinement conditioned on that prediction. To support efficient streaming generation, we maintain separate causal states with a long low-resolution history for prediction and a short high-resolution history for refinement. To address task interference during distillation, we separate specialist optimization from capability consolidation and introduce task-asymmetric on-policy distillation (TA-OPD). This procedure transfers prediction and refinement behaviors from specialized teachers into a shared student through local velocity matching under their respective sampling and history policies. {Extensive experiments demonstrate that ScaleWorld achieves high-quality world generation with improved computational and memory efficiency.

📝 NeurIPS 2026, Project Page: https://fogradio.github.io/plan4d-project
Show abstract

Most world models for future prediction prioritize aestetic quality of generated videos, but offer only weak physical grounding and limited geometric consistency over time, due to the lack of explicit 4D modeling. In this paper, we introduce plan4d, a possible next milestone towards building a generative plannable 4D world model that predicts future RGB videos together with dense 4D geometry, including depth, cameras, point clouds, and scene flow from an observed scene context and text-specified actions. Since training data with paired actions and dense 4D annotations is inherently scarce, plan4d leverages the complementary priors of foundational video generators and 3D reconstructors. Specifically, we propose a model-stitching architecture that couples Depth Anything 3 with LongCat-Video and augments it with an auxiliary DPT head for scene-flow prediction. The resulting model allows us to plan and generate complex, long-horizon, and fine-grained actions in a dynamic 3D scenes, while substantially improving both visual fidelity and geometric accuracy of the generated future videos. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that plan4d achieves state-of-the-art performance in generative 4D modeling of environmental reactions. Forecasting Patient Trajectories from the Electronic Health Records with Action-Conditioned World Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

We introduce a predictive world model for electronic health records (EHR) that represents patient histories as action conditioned latent trajectories. Rather than modeling clinical events solely through autoregressive token prediction, our framework explicitly separates patient states from observed clinical actions and learns their evolution through a state and action conditioned transition operator. At its core is a state dependent linearization predictor that propagates local changes in latent patient state, enabling time resolved prediction, multi step rollout, and action conditioned simulation. Empirically, we show that generated latent trajectories preserve downstream predictive signal, temporal structure, and subgroup level statistical relationships present in encoder derived representations. We further decode free running rollouts into the original clinical feature space and evaluate future clinical measurements against observed values using task matched forecasting baselines, demonstrating that the learned dynamics retain predictive information beyond latent space agreement. Across binary classification and time to event tasks, EHR WM achieves competitive, though task dependent, performance relative to substantially larger EHR foundation models while using a 30x smaller parameter footprint. Finally, perturbing observed action sequences produces corresponding changes in generated trajectories, demonstrating the action sensitivity of the learned dynamics. Together, these results support world modeling as a complementary framework for learning explicit, time indexed representations of patient evolution from longitudinal EHR.

Monte Carlo Pass Search for Setpieces 2026-11-19
📝 Hudl Performance Insights 2026
Show abstract

Set pieces produce about a third of non-penalty expected goals (xG), yet they are usually evaluated by what happened next. We present Monte Carlo Pass Search (MCPS) for set pieces, which treats a corner or a lofted free kick as a distribution over plausible executions and plausible futures instead of a single realised event. From the pre-kick state, MCPS proposes counterfactual deliveries and counterfactual set-ups (where the attackers stand at the kick), plays each out many times in a world model (3D ball physics, a generative model of all 22 players, the keeper's come-or-stay decision and a Player-to-Touch model) and scores every future with a value model. On 249 deliveries from 30 Serie A 2025/26 matches, with every model applied only to matches it never saw, the best available delivery is worth 0.0085 xG more than the one kicked (+28 %), mainly because it reaches an attacker before the keeper. Searching where the attackers stand as well adds +0.0027 xG with the observed ball and +0.0033 on top of the best ball; designing their runs too adds +0.0007 more. We show how to keep such a search honest, and estimate how much more data each component needs before a club could rely on it.

FEA solver integration of a composite World Model with a Spiking Neural Network memory Block for computationally efficient modelling of defects and variabilities 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Each fibre-reinforced composite structure has a unique material architecture. The manufacturing methods used and the presence of defects lead to local variations which require material-scale models that can resolve the response in real time at every location in the structure. Concurrent multi-scale schemes provide this capability, but at a prohibitive computational cost for large structures as the number of integration points and time increments scales with size. Surrogates have been studied extensively as replacements for material-scale models to mitigate these computational limitations. A surrogate must represent an arbitrary local architecture including defects, track the irreversible material state over an unknown number of increments, remain stable when driven recursively by its own predictions, and fit within the memory available at every integration point. Here, a Composites World Model based on a Multi-Modal Spiking Transformer architecture is designed to meet these requirements. The proposed architecture combines a material encoder, a Spiking Neural Network memory block that tracks the long-term internal state of the material, and transformer blocks that learn the short-term applied strain state. The system is trained in three stages: material architecture encoding, base training on short time windows, and recursive fine-tuning with scheduled sampling to suppress cumulative error drift. The trained model is disassembled at inference and coupled to a non-linear FE solver, enabling structural scale simulation of composites containing embedded defects. Benchmarks against experimental results for IM7/8552 laminates under tension, compression and transverse shear, for pristine and wrinkled composites, show that the SNN memory block successfully tracks the material internal state and results in prediction errors between 0.4% and 4.8%.

World Models Favor Learned Priors over Visual Evidence 2026-11-19
Show abstract

World models are increasingly used to simulate physical interactions, but training a separate model for each environment limits scalability. Cross-environment prediction requires identifying the current physical rule, which appearance alone may not reveal. We therefore study visual calibration: using object motion and interactions in a short video as direct evidence to infer the current rule and predict motion under that rule from a different initial state. However, differences in data availability across physical conditions can yield a long-tailed training distribution, biasing models toward common rules even when evidence supports rare ones. To study this challenge, we introduce TailWorld, which evaluates the transfer of demonstrated rules across initial states while independently controlling training imbalance and calibration informativeness. The benchmark compares predictions with two reference futures from the same prediction initial state, one following the demonstrated rule and the other a common rule, to measure prediction bias toward common dynamics. We further propose Rule Inference for Transfer (RIFT), which learns how different rules affect object motion, infers the current rule from calibration interactions, and applies it to prediction from a new initial state. RIFT explicitly controls the influence of training frequency on rule inference, allowing sufficiently informative observational evidence to support rare rules. Experiments on TailWorld show that RIFT improves prediction accuracy and physical consistency across varying training imbalances and distribution shifts, with gains in predicting rare dynamics.

SpecWM: Turning Verification into Refinement for Streaming World Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Recently, video world models have advanced interactive generation, with large models offering higher quality and smaller models enabling faster inference. Existing speculative methods combine their strengths through fast draft generation and selective target regeneration guided by rewards on decoded frames. However, verification covers only the external scorer's evaluation dimensions and adds decoding and scoring costs. Moreover, these scores provide no correction, requiring costly target regeneration for rejected drafts. To address these limitations, we propose Speculative World Model (SpecWM), a unified, training-free inference framework for streaming world models. It verifies draft chunks using the target's latent velocity field. Verification also yields a reusable correction; two thresholds select Accept, Refine, or Reject. Specifically, one-step refinement reuses it to improve perceptual quality, while three-step refinement strengthens camera following and trajectory consistency. These findings motivate transition-aware refinement. On WBench Navigation, SpecWM improves visual quality at 1.63$\times$ speedup over the target and raises NavScore from the draft's 75.99 to 85.63, achieving the best quality-speed-control trade-off. Additionally, our LongSpecBench shows sustained quality and control over long, multi-turn rollouts, while experiments on Hy-Nomas validate SpecWM across backbones and denoising budgets.

Can World Models Predict Rice Growth? Toward Agricultural World Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

World models can imagine how a physical scene evolves over the next few seconds, but growers need to know how a crop will develop over weeks to months: will this rice plant have headed a month from now, and how will it look at harvest? We introduce agricultural world modeling, the task of predicting how the same plant will look at a requested horizon of days to months, from one photo of it, its cultivar, and its camera view. In preliminary analysis, even state-of-the-art image generators get this wrong: they often grow the plant too little or give it the color and form of the wrong growth stage. We argue that they do not work out the plant's developmental state, which the photo reveals only indirectly. We therefore propose AgroWM, which reasons about development before it draws growth. Its reasoning module, PhenoClock, works out the current growth stage, predicts the future one, and writes a Growth Script with numeric targets for height, plant area, and color; its generation module, the Rooted Renderer, then edits the photo to follow this script, adding new organs and colors to the photographed plant. Because pixel scores can prefer an unchanged copy of the photo, we instead check each forecast's traits and developmental timing against the plant's own later photos. On rice, 54.6% of the forecasts of AgroWM are matched to the target stage (Stage R@1), against 34.8% for GPT Image 2.5, 24.0% for Qwen Image 2.1, and 47.4% for the strongest domain-specific baseline, the same backbone fine-tuned without Growth Scripts; 9 to 20 weeks ahead, it still reaches 46.4%, against 35.7% for that baseline. On maize, a crop with a different body plan and stage scale, the same design again scores best on all four measures.

Homeostatic Continual Learning 2026-11-19

I formulate a Continual Learning problem for an autonomous agent that imitates the one animals face. The agent has intents to satisfy. It experiences contexts where different combinations of features are active and impact intent outcomes. I propose a method named ``Homeostatic Continual Learning'' that imitates the one animals use. The agent learns incrementally environment data and features relevant for their intents from the impact of contexts, gradually completes its model and policy and performs well in more and more contexts. The core of the method is to find outliers in the environment data when the agent experiences an outlier in its intent outcome after the context changes. This method enables the agent to learn continuously in changing contexts without catastrophic forgetting. When pursuing many intents concurrently, the agent may build a transformer-like world model with the method where it factorizes the objects in the world into features, abstract objects into comparable instances of concepts and map concepts to intents through features. The world model is composed of semantic nodes and explainable by nature. I discuss the works needed to render the method practical, the connections to many fields in Artificial Intelligence and the broader implications of the method.

Show abstract

World model has emerged as a powerful paradigm in artificial intelligence, enabling agents to represent their environments, predict future states, and evaluate possible actions before acting. However, existing world models have largely been developed for visual or physical domains such as computer vision, robotics, gaming, and autonomous driving, which are governed by relatively stable dynamics. These formulations are not directly applicable to business practice, where the relevant environment is semantic, organizational, and market driven. Outcomes depend on context-sensitive factors such as customer behavior, pricing, competition, and regulation. This paper introduces the concept and architecture of a Business World Model, a world model specialized for business and organizational environments. We propose a business-semantics-centric formulation in which states, dynamics, and feasible actions are linked to business entities, their attributes, and their relationships. Within this framework, intelligent agents can simulate alternative action sequences, estimate their effects on future business outcomes, and evaluate trade-offs under uncertainty and business constraints. We present a reference design and open-source implementation in which LLM agents interpret natural-language business goals and explain results, while a deterministic, metadata-driven simulation engine integrating machine learning models, business rules, and action spaces performs all calculations. A churn case study of a digital subscription business demonstrates that the system recommends options meeting the churn-reduction target within the revenue constraint. This work establishes a conceptual foundation for autonomous business systems capable of moving from instruction-based execution toward goal-driven planning, optimization, and execution.

Learning What Actions Change: Action Effect Matching for Dynamics-Faithful World Models 2026-11-19
📝 27 pages, preprint
Show abstract

World models aim to predict environment dynamics under actions, enabling the prediction of action consequences without executing them in the real world. Yet despite producing increasingly realistic videos, current world models often fail to capture how small action changes affect future observations. This limits their use in tasks such as robotic policy evaluation and model-based planning, where nearly identical actions can lead to qualitatively different outcomes. To address this, we propose AFFECT, an auxiliary training objective for learning the distribution of visual changes induced by action perturbations. Specifically, we inject temporally correlated noise into the actions of a policy trained on task-agnostic human teleoperation. We then train the world model so that its generated videos respond to this noise as the recorded videos do, without requiring repeated executions from the same initial state. Across OGBench simulation and real-world DROID and ALOHA-2 setups, AFFECT makes world-model rollouts more faithful to real outcomes. On DROID, the Pearson correlation between policy success rates in the world model and on the real robot rises from 0.47 to 0.79, alongside up to 23% lower FVD. These gains carry over to planning, where selecting actions with AFFECT-trained world models improves task success by up to 8.3%.

Point Dynamics as Proxy for Robotic World Models 2026-11-19
📝 24 pages, 7 figures, 11 tables. Project page: https://marsaki.github.io/point-dynamics-proxy/
Show abstract

Robotic world models predict future video frames from the current observation and a sequence of robot actions. While recent models follow the given actions closely, manipulated objects often collapse or move inconsistently with the action. We attribute this to the conditioning: an action, or a rendering of the robot, specifies where the robot goes but not how the scene responds, leaving a single video model to infer the interaction dynamics implicitly while also rendering appearance. We therefore separate dynamics prediction from visual rendering, with 3D point dynamics as the proxy between them. Specifically, we lift the first frame into 3D points, simulate how they respond to the robot's motion with a learned point dynamics model, and render the simulated points back into pixel-aligned videos that any video model can read. In controlled experiments on the DROID dataset with two video renderers of different architectures, this point dynamics proxy reduces object motion error by 8.6% and 6.2% without degrading image quality, and by 8.1% on a real bimanual robot. Moreover, the video follows the simulation: more accurate dynamics yield more accurate object motion, and editing the points steers the video. This suggests that advances in dynamics modeling can translate into more faithful robotic world models.

Latent world models predict the outcomes of action sequences, but comparing candidate plans through recursive rollout remains expensive at test time. We introduce Fast-JEPA, a latent world model planning framework that advances the success-latency Pareto frontier of predictive search. Fast-JEPA reorganizes predictive computation to support effective action-sequence search at low planning cost. It constructs a goal-directed plan to guide search and directly predicts the outcomes of complete candidates for efficient comparison, avoiding recursive state rollout during refinement. World-delta: Continually Integrating Observations for Incremental 3D Scene Generation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Existing 3D generation methods synthesize visually compelling scenes from fixed inputs, yet struggle to continually expand and revise a 3D world as new observations arrive. Iterative approaches often fuse real observations and generated content into a single representation, treating generated content as established structure rather than revisable predictions. Consequently, early generation errors can persist and accumulate despite new evidence. We propose World-delta, an incremental 3D scene generation framework combining uncertainty-aware persistent world modeling with delta-guided diffusion for continual scene expansion and correction. Our framework comprises three components: 1) Visual Evidence Encoding integrates geometric and semantic cues from incoming images into unified world tokens. 2) Observation-Calibrated World Modeling maintains a persistent latent world state that distinguishes observation-supported content from provisional completion hypotheses. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of World-delta in incremental 3D scene generation, observation-driven correction, scene consistency, and update efficiency over successive scene updates.

GameWM: Toward Next-Generation Video Games with Interaction-Centric World Models 2026-11-19

Show abstract

For more than half a century, video games have pursued better graphics, more natural interaction, and more engaging gameplay. Generative video models (VMs) now bear on all three. Neural networks already draw most of the pixels in shipped games, and recent world models let a lightweight game engine keep the world state while a VM turns the engine's coarse proxy video into final frames. What One Sample Cannot See: Why Fewer Denoising Steps Can Score Higher on Physics Benchmarks 2026-11-19
Show abstract

When a storm can head north or south, a forecast down the middle may score better under squared error than either plausible path. The same paradox appears when video generators are tested as world models: fewer denoising steps can raise physics scores even as videos blur or stop moving early. Why? Physics-IQ compares each generated video with one recording, although a scene can have multiple valid futures. An exact decomposition shows its scores can reward reduced variation among generated futures, even when their distribution becomes less accurate. We introduce Kernel Physics-IQ to remove this incentive in expectation, needing only two independent samples per condition. Two-step sampling lowers Kernel Physics-IQ in all five image-to-video models. For CogVideoX, the official score rises by 0.055 while ours falls by 0.131. Changing the sampling schedule reveals further differences official scores miss. In controlled physical systems with known outcome probabilities, our metric favors the true distribution over collapsed predictions. In short, a world model should be judged not only by how closely one video matches what happened, but by how faithfully its predictions represent what could have happened.

Physical Laws without Physics Loss? Probing Learned Dynamics in Video World Models through Forced Response 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Do video world models learn physical laws without a physics loss, or merely imitate motion? Overall motion and appearance scores cannot settle it. We investigate this through nonlinear forced response: an excitation shakes the support of a suspended object. We introduce dual visual-numeric conditioning and adapt six image-to-video backbones to predict this motion using only the flow-matching loss. Wan predicts motion accurately and generalizes to unseen excitations, objects, poses, and scenes. Counterfactual tests suggest capabilities beyond replaying training videos. But what laws have they learned? We design three tiers of tests using Taylor expansions of the dynamics and geometry. All six reproduce the linear response, including resonance; five capture higher-order dynamics, including nonlinear softening. Remarkably, softening is barely present in training: only 0.74% of the swing's squared displacement.
