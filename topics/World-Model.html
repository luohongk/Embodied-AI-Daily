Persistent Planar Memory for Video World Models 2026-10-16
📝 20 pages, 9 figures. Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Video world models that generate and explore environments at scale need a persistent 3D memory of what they have produced. Plane primitives offer a natural candidate: a compact set of oriented planes captures the dominant structural elements (e.g., walls, floors, facades) of indoor and outdoor scenes, while requiring only 7 parameters per primitive. We present PPMem, a video world model that adopts plane primitives as its persistent 3D memory. PPMem augments a video diffusion transformer with a planar prediction head and a Depth Token Fusion (DTF) module: each autoregressive chunk jointly produces RGB video and planar geometry, which merges into a persistent plane memory that conditions subsequent chunks via rendered depth. The resulting memory is three orders of magnitude more compact than per-pixel point clouds (~100K primitives, 5MB for a full scene), geometrically explicit (metric depth, normals, exportable surfaces), cross-view consistent (jointly predicted by the shared backbone), and adaptive (refined by the merging procedure as generation proceeds). On DL3DV and RealEstate10K, PPMem outperforms prior 3D-grounded world models in video quality while jointly producing metric-scale depth, surface normals, and exportable planar geometry, sustaining coherence across hundreds of frames under large viewpoint changes. Project page: https://hyzcluster.github.io/ppmem.

Beyond Large Language Models: A Mental World Model Framework for Mental Modeling, Simulation, Evolution, and Interpretability 2026-10-16
📝 12 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Understanding and simulating how subjective cognition and mental states emerge, evolve, and shape behavior is essential for advancing artificial intelligence toward deeper social cognition and general intelligence. Existing large language models (LLMs) and Theory of Mind (ToM) methods primarily infer mental states from linguistic information, lacking unified mechanisms for subjective world construction, cognitive-psychological interactions, dynamic evolution, and historical dependencies. This paper introduces the Mental World Model (MWM) and its implementation, the Multi-Branch Mental Evolution Architecture (MMEA), which explicitly constructs subjective worlds, models interactions between cognition and mental states, and simulates continuous mental evolution shaped by historical experiences. On DynToM and RecToM, MMEA achieves 96.48% and 94.00% accuracy, respectively, outperforming leading LLMs and specialized ToM methods. Additional experiments demonstrate improvements of 28.7, 39.3, and 16.6 percentage points in mental state and behavior prediction, long-sequence prediction, and implicit mental state inference, respectively. The results demonstrate that MMEA provides an interpretable framework for continuous mental modeling, enabling AI to move beyond surface-level semantic representations to infer individuals' underlying mental states from linguistic information and dynamically simulate their internal cognitive processes and evolution over time. MWM also provides a promising foundation for causal reasoning, personalized AI, sustained human-AI interaction, and future advances toward artificial general intelligence (AGI).

Beyond Action Prediction: Learning Search-Free Planning in Latent Space 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Latent world models learn action-conditioned state transitions in a compact representation space, providing a predictive model for visual planning and control. However, planning with these models typically requires costly test-time optimization over action sequences. We propose to replace this online search with a goal-conditioned inverse model that directly predicts actions from current and goal observations. A key challenge is that action prediction alone neither ensures that goal representations encode action-relevant information nor that predicted actions are consistent with the learned latent prediction model. We introduce Hindsight Inverse JEPA Planning (HIJP), a search-free framework that addresses both aspects during training. HIJP relabels future observations along offline trajectories as goals, providing action supervision across multiple temporal horizons. By retaining gradients through the goal representations, this supervision directly shapes the visual representation. HIJP further feeds predicted actions into the latent predictor model and aligns the resulting next-state representations with encoded successor observations, enforcing action-transition consistency. At inference time, the inverse dynamics model and latent predictor models are applied alternately to generate multi-step action sequences without online search. Across four visual control tasks, HIJP achieves 86.8% overall success and a 1.9-16.5x planning speedup over search-based planning.

OrbiSim: World Models as Differentiable Physics Engines for Embodied Intelligence 2026-10-16
📝 Project page: https://jjleejj85.github.io/projects/orbisim
Show abstract

We present OrbiSim, a novel robotic simulation paradigm that redefines world models as a fully differentiable physics engine for embodied intelligence. Unlike prior world models that focus on unconstrained imagination in latent or visual domains, OrbiSim establishes a unified, physically-grounded pathway that bridges structured scene assets, neural dynamics, and downstream reinforcement learning. By enabling end-to-end differentiability throughout the entire simulation loop -- spanning from explicit state transitions to visual observation generation -- OrbiSim supports tasks traditionally intractable for classical simulators, such as differentiable contact modeling, gradient-based policy optimization under sparse rewards, and intuitive physical inference. Empirical results demonstrate that OrbiSim significantly outperforms state-of-the-art world models in both predictive fidelity and control performance. Furthermore, its consistent responsiveness to asset configurations and physical parameters suggests its potential as a differentiable tool for enhancing robot simulation and policy training.

Execution Before Memory: Diagnosing Memory Failures in Video World Models 2026-10-16
📝 27 pages, 7 figures. Project page: https://hku-mmlab.github.io/MIST/ Code: https://github.com/HKU-MMLab/MIST
Show abstract

A memory test for a video world model should say whether the scene was set, whether the asked change was carried out, and whether the last frame shows the result. Current tests fold these into one number, so a low score is easy to read as forgetting. When the change is not carried out, the hidden state transition cannot appear, so the test is not really a test of memory. We introduce MIST, an evaluation that separates the change from the memory it should leave behind. The model first sets the scene while the change stays hidden, and only later is asked to show it, with leakage before that point kept under control. This separation locates the bottleneck: the model sets the scene but does not carry out the instructed change. The same gap appears in general memory evaluations. On that basis we propose M3-Agent (Make the Missing Move), which recursively revises the details of the input instruction so that the model successfully carries out the requested instruction. Relative to the baseline, it more than triples the MIST reveal score. On the benchmarks, WRBench's score T rises by 26.1% and MBench-T's score U by 52.7%. The method meets the problem from the execution side: it shows what the frozen model can do once the move is stated, and it sets a stricter baseline.

Momentum: Persistent Dynamics for Video World Models 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Video world models must capture how a scene evolves beyond the current camera view to generate consistent observations when its content reappears. Existing approaches rely on observation history, static point clouds, or image caches constructed from past frames. These representations are often expensive and, more importantly, cannot update dynamic scene content outside the field of view: generated vehicles and pedestrians may be forgotten after leaving the field of view or reappear in physically inaccurate states. We represent an evolving scene through synchronized videos from reference-camera rigs, each comprising eight cameras that jointly cover the surrounding scene. We connect these rigs in a graph and propagate video generation between neighboring rigs to coordinate scene dynamics across locations and time. We introduce Momentum, a two-stage pipeline: WorldUpdate jointly generates reference-camera videos as a shared memory of scene dynamics, and WorldInteract grounds camera-controlled video generation in this evolving memory, allowing moving content to remain represented outside the target view and reappear consistently. To support training and evaluation, we develop a CARLA pipeline that captures synchronized videos from reference camera rigs and a moving target camera, together with an evaluation framework measuring consistency within the generated world state and the target observations.Experiments on generated street scenes show that grounding target videos in scene dynamics generated by Momentum improves dynamic scene consistency by 126.2% in unseen scenes over static image conditioning, while also improving visual fidelity. Our models and source code will be made publicly available.

LithoWorld: An Endpoint World Model for Lithography Process-Window Recovery 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Lithography process drift can move a fabricated pattern outside its process window, producing unqualified scanning electron microscopy (SEM) morphology. We present LithoWorld, an endpoint world model for SEM-guided parameter recovery. Interior representative targets jointly determine qualified reference appearance and correction supervision. A causal predictive branch aligns currentimage features with endpoint representations, while a conditional flow policy generates a two-dimensional correction as an action chunk. The model learns from 173 groups of measured SEMs organized as focus-exposure maps, preserving unmeasured cells as unknown. On 200 test pairs from the 73-group subset, LithoWorld achieves 88.5% qualified-region success and 46.0% interior success, exceeding the strongest comparison method by 2.5 and 6.0 percentage points, respectively. Ablations separate the effects of target selection, endpoint alignment, and output parameterization. Qualitative SEM retrieval illustrates recovery to qualified interiors without requiring exact reference-coordinate matching.

AWAM: Active Trajectory-Conditioned View Imagination for Robust Robot Manipulation 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Rapid progress in embodied intelligence has enabled increasingly capable robots to follow language instructions and manipulate diverse objects. However, strong results in structured environments do not automatically transfer to open-world conditions, where clutter, occlusion, and changing viewpoints can obscure the spatial relationships required for action. To address this perceptual gap, a virtual active-perception paradigm is introduced. AWAM, an Active World-Action Model actively plans where to observe and imagines what will be seen along the planned trajectory before acting. Task-related virtual camera motion is predicted and used as a structured query into a geometry-conditioned world model, producing a reusable spatial prior for manipulation, which is later compressed into a fixed memory and fused with live observations through cross-attention for diffusion-based action prediction. AWAM achieves competitive success rates with 75.9% on LIBERO-Occ, 62.4% on LIBERO-Plus, 76.5% on RLBench-OG Occlusion1, and 94.4% in real-robot trials. These results support improved robustness to the evaluated occlusions and distribution shifts, addressing part of the gap between structured manipulation and less controlled environments.

Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate AI research, including language-model (LM) development, offering a potential path toward recursive self-improvement. However, turning experimental feedback into a learned ability to propose effective improvements across models faces two challenges. First, training such an ability with real feedback is expensive: reinforcement learning (RL) requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, each potentially requiring a full model-training run. Even at ten GPU-hours per run, reward collection alone would consume hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, motivating a cheaper surrogate. Second, surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts. To address these challenges, we propose LM2LM, which trains LLMs with RL to generate effective LM improvement proposals. To provide scalable feedback, we use a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records provided in context, as the reward model for RL. To maintain feedback reliability, we periodically execute current-policy proposals ranked highly by the RWM and add their measured outcomes to its context. Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks show that LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol, even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost. LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

LAW: Unifying Language, Action, and World Modeling for Agentic Physical Intelligence 2026-10-16
Show abstract

World-action models jointly predict actions and future world states, but long-horizon manipulation further requires language reasoning to plan subtask objectives, assess execution progress, and guide action selection. We present LAW, a closed-loop VLM--WAM framework that unifies language reasoning, action generation, and world modeling through semantic subtask progress. Within the LAW agent, a hybrid-expert vision-language model plans the remaining subtasks, estimates the values of and fuses actions proposed by a world-action model, and asynchronously assesses execution status to support continued execution and subtask transitions. Within the world-action model, Mixture-of-Transformer Experts integrates modality-specific and shared computation for joint video--action modeling. Dual-stream hierarchical visual memory maintains temporal context for both models, while subtask-local value learning aligns action evaluation with semantic objectives and preserves supervision from successful prefixes of failed trajectories. LAW achieves 94.21% average success across 50 RoboTwin tasks. Replacing the WAM backbone with OpenWAM-$alpha$ further raises the average success rate to 95.56%, establishing a new open-source state of the art on RoboTwin. Despite having only 2B parameters, our VLM also outperforms variants using Embodied-R1.5 and GPT-5.5 High. On three long-horizon real-robot tasks, LAW achieves 65% average success, compared with 28.33% for $pi_{0.5}$ and 25% for Fast-WAM.

Towards Unified World Models for Visual Navigation via Memory-Augmented Planning and Foresight 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted to ECCV 2026. 22 pages, 12 figures, code: https://github.com/UWMILab/UniWM
Show abstract

Enabling embodied agents to imagine future states is essential for robust and generalizable visual navigation. Yet, state-of-the-art systems typically rely on modular designs that decouple navigation planning from visual world modeling, which often induces state-action misalignment and weak adaptability in novel or dynamic scenarios. We propose UniWM, a unified, memory-augmented world model that integrates egocentric visual foresight and planning within a single multimodal autoregressive backbone. UniWM explicitly grounds action selection in visually imagined outcomes, tightly aligning prediction with control. Meanwhile, a hierarchical memory mechanism fuses short-term perceptual cues with longer-term trajectory context, supporting stable and coherent reasoning over extended horizons. ORD-WM: Learning Object-Centric Rigid Dynamics for 3D World Modeling in Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-16
Show abstract

World models provide robots with a predictive mechanism for reasoning about how actions change object states in manipulation tasks. However, existing image-based and dense 3D world models do not explicitly impose object-level rigid motion, leaving rigid-object geometry preservation to be learned implicitly and making multi-step rollouts susceptible to shape drift or object-level motion inconsistency. To address this, we propose ORD-WM, a 3D world model that learns object-centric rigid dynamics for robotic manipulation. ORD-WM learns a vector-neuron variational autoencoder supervised by signed distance fields, which encodes each object point cloud as a geometry-aware latent representation composed of 3D vector features, and uses an action-conditioned dynamics model to predict object-wise SE(3) motion increments. Rather than generating unconstrained next latent states, ORD-WM analytically applies predicted object-wise rigid transformations to the current latent states, separating persistent object geometry from pose changes. Experiments show that ORD-WM enables stable, geometry-preserving multi-step prediction and supports efficient real-time replanning for closed-loop model predictive control in simulated and real-world manipulation scenarios.

BeJEPA: A Goal-Conditioned Humanoid Behavior World Model via Unsupervised RL 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Humanoid control requires coordinating whole-body posture with position and heading in the world frame. Mainstream controllers imitate trajectories in the root frame, but struggle to regain the target world pose after displacement. We introduce BeJEPA, a goal-conditioned Behavior World Model learned through unsupervised reinforcement learning from human motion priors and robot interaction. BeJEPA learns a shared representation of body configuration and world placement through a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA). A Dynamic Predictor predicts the next state embedding under an action, while a Successor Predictor summarizes a goal-conditioned policy's expected discounted future state embeddings in a single vector. During joint training, Dynamic grounds the initial representation in observed physical transitions, while Successor increasingly organizes it around long-term behavior. Their composition guides policy improvement through one physical prediction and one behavioral summary, without long imagined rollouts. In 20-second motion-tracking experiments, BeJEPA with online action refinement using Dynamic outperforms all evaluated baselines in global body-position error, reducing it on AMASS by 61.7% relative to BFM-Zero with world-pose inputs and 58.7% relative to SONIC. Physical deployment on a Unitree G1 demonstrates joint attainment of posture, world position, and heading goals, recovery from the ground, and closed-loop return after disturbances and goal changes. Schrödinger's Navigator: Imagining an Ensemble of Futures for Zero-Shot Object Navigation 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted to the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) 2026
Show abstract

Zero-shot object navigation (ZSON) requires robots to find target objects in unseen environments without task-specific fine-tuning or pre-built maps, a key capability for general-purpose service robots. Yet methods that perform well in simulation often degrade in cluttered real-world scenes with severe occlusion and latent hazards, where large unseen regions make single-scene inference brittle and unsafe. We propose Schrödinger's Navigator, a belief-aware framework that reasons at inference time over multiple trajectory-conditioned imagined 3D futures. Given candidate paths, a trajectory-conditioned 3D world model predicts hypothetical observations and maintains a superposition of plausible scene realizations rather than committing to one map. An adaptive occluder-aware sampler directs imagination to uncertainty-critical regions, while a Future-Aware Value Map (FAVM) aggregates imagined futures for robust, proactive action selection. Experiments in simulation and on a physical Go2 quadruped show that Schrödinger's Navigator outperforms strong ZSON baselines, improving hidden-target discovery and risk-aware waypoint selection in occlusion-heavy navigation scenarios. These results highlight imagined 3D futures as a scalable and generalizable strategy for zero-shot navigation in uncertain real-world environments.

Show abstract

Multiplayer world models must ensure that independently controlled views remain consistent with a single shared and persistent world. WorldOracle: Benchmarking Action-Conditioned World Models against Real-World Executions 2026-10-16

We introduce WorldOracle, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) against paired real-world executions. Given the same initial observation and low-level action sequence, WorldOracle asks whether a predicted rollout reproduces what happens in the real world, including physical consequences and task-state transitions. We evaluate models along four complementary dimensions: physical state correctness, interaction spatial alignment, key interaction area fidelity, and multi-view action coherence. MAIA: Anticipating Interaction Anomalies in Contact-Rich Manipulation via Multimodal World Models 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Reliable contact-rich manipulation requires detecting execution deviations early enough for corrective action. Yet most monitoring methods are reactive, identifying anomalies only after they become observable. Anticipating such anomalies is challenging because visual observations only partially reveal physical interaction, while planned actions describe intended motion rather than their actual physical consequences. We address this problem with MAIA, a multimodal framework for anomaly anticipation. Its vision-action-force world model learns nominal interaction dynamics and predicts the visual and force responses of planned actions, while a lightweight anomaly head predicts impending interaction anomalies. To enable systematic evaluation, we introduce CRIA, a real-world multimodal dataset of 2,852 trajectories spanning 18 contact-rich manipulation tasks, with frame-level annotations of anomaly onset and evolution. Experiments on both seen and novel tasks show that MAIA achieves more accurate anomaly discrimination than representative baselines while providing earlier warnings, with bACC scores of 0.765 and 0.710 and median anticipation lead times of 28.0 and 25.1 steps, respectively. Moreover, using these predictions for anomaly-aware action selection improves end-to-end task success from 52.6% to 61.5% without retraining the underlying manipulation policy. These results highlight the value of modeling future physical consequences for proactive anomaly anticipation.

World Action Critic (WAC) Model: Joint Foresight and Criticism for Policy Learning 2026-10-16
Show abstract

World models have shown considerable promise in sequential decision-making by anticipating the consequences of actions and providing predictive foresight for policy learning. However, learning to reason about counterfactual actions remains challenging, since predictive objectives may underemphasize information critical for action evaluation, while unexecuted actions lack observed futures for direct supervision. To address these challenges, we propose the World Action Critic (WAC) Model, which proposes multimodal action candidates and jointly learns candidate-conditioned future prediction and consequence evaluation. In our proposed framework, critic supervision directly shapes intermediate predictive representations during rollout, encouraging them to retain information needed to distinguish action quality. Observed futures supervise prediction for executed actions, while external task-quality targets provide evaluation signals for counterfactual candidates. Through joint optimization, critic signals from these candidates propagate into predictive representations without requiring their observed futures. Our theoretical analysis characterizes critic-guided feature retention and bounds candidate-selection regret. Applied to autonomous driving, WAC achieves 93.4 PDMS on NAVSIM-v1, demonstrating the effectiveness of jointly learning predictive foresight and consequence evaluation.

AnyRank: Reliable Anytime Policy Ranking via Sequential Testing 2026-10-16
📝 CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Due to the high cost of hardware experiments, robot policies are predominantly evaluated by comparing their empirical performance on a limited number of hardware trials. However, finite-sample statistical uncertainty can lead to erroneous conclusions, especially when ranking multiple policies using a fixed batch of evaluation data. We propose AnyRank: a sequential method for multi-policy ranking that (i) adaptively allocates trials to hard-to-separate policies, (ii) provides (partial) rankings at any stopping time while bounding the probability of an incorrect ranking, and (iii) optionally exploits prior information to prioritize hardware evaluations more efficiently. This prior information could be informed from expert intuition, a small set of held-out hardware evaluations, or from policy rollouts in a simulator or an action-conditioned video world model. Notably, the prior can be imperfect; while a strong prior can provide efficiency gains on hardware, any inaccuracies in prior policy rankings do not invalidate the statistical assurances of AnyRank. We achieve the aforementioned properties through novel theoretical contributions that combine sequential testing with multiple-hypothesis graphical testing approaches. Experiments in simulation and real-world policy evaluation benchmarks show that AnyRank requires up to 50% fewer hardware evaluations compared to existing statistically rigorous approaches, while guarding against the incorrect rankings produced by heuristic methods used by state-of-the-art benchmarks.

STRIPS-WM: Learning Grounded Propositional STRIPS-style World Models from Images 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted at the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) 2026. Project website: https://elpislab.org/projects/strips-wm/
Show abstract

Robots performing long-horizon visual manipulation observe high-dimensional images, but successful plans depend on action-relevant facts: what can be done now and what changes afterward. A useful planning representation should discard irrelevant visual details while preserving action applicability and effects. Classical task planners exploit this structure through symbolic operators with preconditions and effects, but obtaining such representations from raw visual experience remains challenging. We study a visual task-planning setting in which a robot receives only image transitions: the current image, executed high-level action, and the resulting image. At test time, given a start image and a goal image, the robot must produce a sequence of high-level actions that reaches the goal. To address this problem, we introduce STRIPS-WM, a framework for learning image-grounded STRIPS-style world models directly from visual transitions. STRIPS-WM first induces a finite abstract transition graph from images, then learns latent binary predicates and one grounded propositional operator per action label. The learned operators form a symbolic action model with sparse preconditions and add/delete effects. Finally, the learned predicates are distilled into a visual encoder, enabling classical planning directly from novel start and goal images. Experiments on visual rearrangement tasks show that STRIPS-WM improves image-to-plan success over the tested visual rollout, latent graph-search and latent-symbolic baselines.

Is a Linear Probe Evidence of a Linear Representation? 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

The linear representation hypothesis holds that pretrained vision models encode generative factors as linearly decodable directions in feature space, and comes with a standard methodology: train a linear probe, declare a factor represented if accuracy is high. We show this test is too permissive. Asymptotically, any sufficiently rich feature map, including a random one, admits a linear readout, so high probe accuracy is consistent with two very different geometries: a true linear world model, where the latent-aligned subspace dominates the representation, or a kernel machine, where the latents lie linearly inside a much larger curved manifold. This distinction matters because the curved structure orthogonal to the probe is relevant for out-of-distribution generalization, robustness, and compositional transfer. We propose three asymmetric diagnostics that distinguish the two cases: reverse predictivity, spectral concentration, and out-of-distribution generalization. We validate them in a fully observed simulation where ground truth is known. Applied to six pretrained vision transformers on SVG-World, our deterministic generative benchmark with paired visual styles, and to two of them on CelebA, they show pretraining produces structure beyond a random kernel but well short of a linear world model. On dSprites, 3D Shapes and MPI3D, forward decodability and latent-aligned variance dissociate. Thus, forward decoding alone does not show that the probed factors align with the dominant geometry of the representation. In summary, our work cautions about the success of linear probes as opposed to truly linear and low dimensional representations.

T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Predicting how the world responds to an action requires more than what can be seen: robots must account for environmental factors that are neither directly observable nor explicitly specified, from physical properties such as friction and mass to human-defined environmental rules. We introduce T2-Z for test-time optimization of environment latents, which augments a robotic world model with a learnable environment latent to condition world predictions. This compact latent captures underlying environmental variation without supervision of what environmental factors exist or what values they take. During training, T2-Z alternates between two stages: a world-model optimization stage that fixes the latents and learns general action-conditioned dynamics across environments; and an environment-latent optimization stage that freezes the world model and optimizes the conditioning latents, allowing latent structure to emerge purely through world prediction. At test time, the world model remains frozen and a newly initialized environment latent is optimized from one or more test-time interactions, adapting the model to previously unseen environmental configurations. Across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks, T2-Z reduces object prediction error metric from 15.20 to 7.00 and raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96%. Its emergent latents capture structured representations of individual and compounded factors across diverse visual scenes. Ablations further show that T2-Z can efficiently infer environment latents from as little as a single test-time interaction trajectory.

OmniVerifier-C1: Decomposed Graded Verifier for Rule-Governed Generative World Modeling 2026-10-15
📝 NeurIPS 2026. Project: https://github.com/Cominclip/OmniVerifier
Show abstract

Generative visual verifiers play a crucial role in advancing multimodal intelligence. Expert demonstrations show how to complete contact-rich tasks, but provide limited coverage of how interaction forces respond to different actions. A world model learns from successful demonstrations to jointly predict future video and six-axis force/torque trajectories.
