T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Predicting how the world responds to an action requires more than what can be seen: robots must account for environmental factors that are neither directly observable nor explicitly specified, from physical properties such as friction and mass to human-defined environmental rules. We introduce T2-Z for test-time optimization of environment latents, which augments a robotic world model with a learnable environment latent to condition world predictions. This compact latent captures underlying environmental variation without supervision of what environmental factors exist or what values they take. During training, T2-Z alternates between two stages: a world-model optimization stage that fixes the latents and learns general action-conditioned dynamics across environments; and an environment-latent optimization stage that freezes the world model and optimizes the conditioning latents, allowing latent structure to emerge purely through world prediction. At test time, the world model remains frozen and a newly initialized environment latent is optimized from one or more test-time interactions, adapting the model to previously unseen environmental configurations. Across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks, T2-Z reduces object prediction error metric from 15.20 to 7.00 and raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96%. Its emergent latents capture structured representations of individual and compounded factors across diverse visual scenes. Ablations further show that T2-Z can efficiently infer environment latents from as little as a single test-time interaction trajectory.

OmniVerifier-C1: Decomposed Graded Verifier for Rule-Governed Generative World Modeling 2026-10-15
📝 NeurIPS 2026. Project: https://github.com/Cominclip/OmniVerifier
Show abstract

Generative visual verifiers play a crucial role in advancing multimodal intelligence. To enhance the robustness of visual verifiers in complex multimodal scenarios involving compositional requirements, we move beyond conventional binary verifiers that produce only True/False discrete judgments and introduce a verifier that automatically decomposes complex prompts or environmental rules into fine-grained components and assigns pointwise scores to provide a continuous and more informative critique signal. Our experiments further demonstrate that these structured and interpretable outputs improve the verifier's generalization to out-of-domain multimodal critic tasks. To train this verifier, we introduce a Decoupled RL training strategy that jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring, using precise local feedback to develop fine-grained verification capabilities that provide a foundation for reliable continuous scoring at the global level. Under a general reinforcement learning framework, we first empirically demonstrate that the proposed pointwise graded verifier significantly outperforms binary verifiers in traditional text-to-image scenarios. We then extend our investigation to rule-governed generative world modeling where generative models are leveraged for world simulation and world reasoning under explicit rules. From a theoretical standpoint, we further show that a pairwise verification paradigm yields more accurate critiques than the pointwise formulation when ground-truth images are available, with empirical results on diverse structured world-modeling tasks such as Maze and Sudoku further validating the effectiveness of our approach. Overall, our findings suggest a key insight: the transition from generative models to structured world-modeling agents critically hinges on the availability of accurate pairwise graded visual verifiers.

GeoState: Learning View-Decoupled Geometry States for 3D World Modeling 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Recent 3D world models increasingly use features from geometry foundation models (GFMs) as predictive world states, benefiting from their rich geometric priors. However, the view dependence of these features entangles scene dynamics with camera-induced variation, making the resulting state evolution harder to predict. We propose GeoState, a view-decoupled geometry representation for predictive 3D world modeling that separates scene-state evolution from camera-dependent observation. Specifically, a scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view GFM features into a view-decoupled scene state, which a geometry decoder queries using camera rays. An autoregressive world model then predicts future scene states, while camera motion is modeled separately. The predicted states can therefore be decoded under either predicted or externally specified cameras, while retaining a single context-derived scale throughout the rollout without per-frame scale alignment. Experiments show that GeoState outperforms prior 3D world models on Waymo, supports geometrically consistent cross-view querying, and generalizes zero-shot to KITTI and nuScenes. Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-15
Show abstract

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a multifingered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world experiment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-specific demonstrations or sim-to-real transfer.

Velox: Spatial Memory Makes World Models Efficient 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. For well-covered regions, frustum slab overlap softly modulates each layer's model-derived block importance. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. Frustum slab construction runs asynchronously with denoising on the same H200. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by $4.36\times$ and denoising by $2.43\times$. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Language Models Struggle to Use Representations Learned In-Context 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Though large language models (LLMs) have enabled great success across a wide variety of tasks, they still appear to fall short of one of the loftier goals of artificial intelligence research: creating an artificial system that can adapt its behavior to radically new contexts upon deployment. One important step towards this goal is to create systems that can induce rich representations of data that are seen in-context, and then flexibly deploy these representations to accomplish goals. Recently, Park et al. (2024) demonstrated that current LLMs are indeed capable of inducing such representation from context (i.e., in-context representation learning). The present study investigates whether LLMs can use these representations to complete simple downstream tasks. We first assess whether open-weights LLMs can use in-context representations for next-token prediction, and then probe models using a novel task, adaptive world modeling. In both tasks, we find evidence that open-weights LLMs struggle to deploy representations of novel semantics that are defined in-context, even if they encode these semantics in their latent representations. Furthermore, we assess closed-source, state-of-the-art reasoning models on the adaptive world modeling task, demonstrating that even the most performant LLMs cannot reliably leverage novel patterns presented in-context. Overall, this work seeks to inspire novel methods for encouraging models to not only encode information presented in-context, but to do so in a manner that supports flexible deployment of this information.

Plans Are Not Executions: Adapting Frozen World-Model Planners to Controller-Induced Execution Shift 2026-10-15
📝 8 pages, 7 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

World-model planners evaluate candidate trajectories by predicting their future consequences, implicitly relying on the plan-to-motion relationship learned during training. At deployment, a different downstream controller may execute the same planned trajectory differently, creating a planning-execution gap between the frozen model's imagined future and actual execution. A natural remedy is to replace the planned trajectory with its predicted or observed execution. However, we find that better execution fidelity does not necessarily yield better decisions: directly substituting predicted execution trajectories yields only a 0.048 PDMS improvement across 10 unseen controller configurations, far smaller than the gain from our proposed adaptation. This suggests a semantic mismatch between a planned trajectory, which serves as a reference for downstream control, and an executed trajectory, which already describes the resulting post-controller motion. We introduce ExecutionBridge, which infers controller-specific execution characteristics from planned-executed trajectory pairs and uses them to adapt the native ego latent rollout while keeping the original planned trajectory unchanged. The planner, world model, and reward head remain frozen. On NAVSIM, ExecutionBridge consistently improves planning across unseen controller configurations and multiple frozen world-model planners, with substantially larger gains in challenging driving scenes where execution mismatch more strongly affects candidate evaluation.

VeriVLA: Adaptive Test-Time Verification for Flow-Based VLAs with Latent World Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

TS-WAM: Temporal Latent Actions and World-Action Coupling for Vision-Language Robot Control 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Many vision-language-action policies rely on action-labeled robot demonstrations and lack an explicit mechanism for predicting future visual states. These limitations hinder learning from action-free videos and can encourage overfitting to demonstration conditions, weakening generalization and robustness. We present TS-WAM, a lightweight Teacher-Student World-Action Model that addresses these challenges through self-supervised temporal learning and explicit future prediction. Its vlm-jepa framework learns from heterogeneous data, including robot demonstrations and action-free human videos, using an EMA target branch without requiring a separately trained external teacher. By coupling vlm-jepa with delta-lwm, TS-WAM learns to predict latent actions from the current observation and instruction and uses them to predict future visual features that guide action generation at inference. A unified end-to-end training architecture jointly optimizes temporal representation learning, visual dynamics, and robot control without a separate latent-action or world-model pretraining stage. The EMA target branch is removed at inference, keeping the deployed policy lightweight. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and SimplerEnv, together with real-world manipulation experiments on Franka Research 3 and RevoTron, demonstrate that TS-WAM achieves strong manipulation performance, generalization, and robustness.

Scaling expert demonstrations alone does not enable generalist robot policies to improve from deployment. MAIA: Anticipating Interaction Anomalies in Contact-Rich Manipulation via Multimodal World Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reliable contact-rich manipulation requires detecting execution deviations early enough for corrective action. Yet most monitoring methods are reactive, identifying anomalies only after they become observable. Anticipating such anomalies is challenging because visual observations only partially reveal physical interaction, while planned actions describe intended motion rather than their actual physical consequences. We address this problem with MAIA, a multimodal framework for anomaly anticipation. Its vision-action-force world model learns nominal interaction dynamics and predicts the visual and force responses of planned actions, while a lightweight anomaly head predicts impending interaction anomalies. To enable systematic evaluation, we introduce CRIA, a real-world multimodal dataset of 2,852 trajectories spanning 18 contact-rich manipulation tasks, with frame-level annotations of anomaly onset and evolution. Experiments on both seen and novel tasks show that MAIA achieves more accurate anomaly discrimination than representative baselines while providing earlier warnings, with bACC scores of 0.765 and 0.710 and median anticipation lead times of 28.0 and 25.1 steps, respectively. Moreover, using these predictions for anomaly-aware action selection improves end-to-end task success from 52.6% to 61.5% without retraining the underlying manipulation policy. These results highlight the value of modeling future physical consequences for proactive anomaly anticipation.

Learning Intrinsic Dynamics in Latent World Models 2026-10-15
📝 12 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Learning intrinsic dynamics---the relationship between actions and their consequences---is essential for world models. However, visual variations unrelated to the underlying control process can obscure this relationship in learned representations. We investigate the connection between action predictability, defined as the ability to reconstruct an action from consecutive latent states, and the preservation of intrinsic dynamics. Our central hypothesis is that encouraging action predictability discourages representations from discarding the distinctions between different actions and their consequences. We provide two complementary forms of evidence for this connection. Theoretically, we derive a positive lower bound on squared inverse prediction error when the encoder merges distinguishable outcomes of different actions from the same state and appearance, and show that forward prediction can still be exact on those transitions. Empirically, controlled appearance-diversity comparisons, supervision interventions, and latent probes support the role of action prediction in preserving control-relevant transition information. We instantiate action predictability with Concat IDM, which predicts actions from concatenated latent states and updates the shared encoder using action labels already available in offline trajectories, without changing the test-time planner. Across three independent training runs on Push-T with 24 appearances, Concat IDM increases planning success from 9.1% to 77.2%; its paired improvement is 52.9 percentage points larger than that observed with a single appearance. Together, these findings support action predictability as an inductive bias toward intrinsic dynamics and demonstrate the practical benefits of Concat IDM for latent planning under visual diversity.

From Optical Flow to Safety: Visual Safety Certification via Scale-Free Relative Motion 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Safe autonomous navigation using monocular vision remains challenging because explicit obstacle states are not directly observable. This letter introduces a visual safety-certification paradigm that uses scale-free, depth-normalized relative motion encoded by monocular optical flow as the representation for collision-risk certification, without reconstructing obstacle position, velocity, geometry, or a metric world model. Unlike conventional optical-flow-based navigation approaches that rely on image motion to shape avoidance behavior, the proposed framework directly encodes collision-cone geometry and safety constraints in this relative-motion representation. An online Concurrent Learning observer estimates the normalized relative velocity and provides a computable estimation-error bound. This uncertainty is propagated into the collision cone through angular inflation and a monocular robot-footprint margin, yielding a Robust Vision-Based Collision Cone Control Barrier Function (RV-C$^3$BF). An optimal-decay CBF quadratic program minimally modifies the nominal control input to enforce the resulting robust safety constraint under the stated assumptions. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a TurtleBot3 in cluttered environments with static and dynamic obstacles demonstrate collision avoidance using only monocular vision and onboard proprioception, with the estimation-error bound containing the measured error in $98.3\%$ of simulation samples and the robot achieving collision-free navigation in $93$ of $100$ real-world trials.

Can World and Action Modeling Benefit Each Other under Unified Causal Training? 2026-10-15

Show abstract

World and action modeling are closely related, yet their reciprocal benefits under unified causal training remain unclear. We introduce OneWM, a simple framework for unified causal training that treats actions as both conditions for world prediction and targets for policy learning within a shared video-action architecture. VOiLA: Vectorized Online Planning with Learned Diffusion Models for POMDP Agents 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted at the 2026 International Symposium of Robotics Research (ISRR)
Show abstract

Planning under uncertainty is an essential capability for autonomous robots. The Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) provides a powerful framework for such a capability. Although POMDP-based planning has advanced significantly, its application to real-world problems is often limited by the difficulty of obtaining faithful POMDP models. We present Vectorized Online planning wIth Learned diffusion model for POMDP Agents (VOiLA), a framework that learns task-agnostic POMDP world models for online planning under uncertainty. Specifically, VOiLA learns transition and observation samplers using conditional diffusion models and observation-likelihood models for particle-based belief updates. In a physical robot experiment, VOiLA accomplishes the task in 10 of 10 runs while using models learned purely from simulated data for online planning.

Sign Betting for Misspecified Regression: Variance Adaptivity and Optimal Approximation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

We study bounded regression under model misspecification, evaluating a function $f$ by its $L_1(P_X)$ distance to the conditional mean $η(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]$, rather than the standard $L_2(P_X)$. This $L_1$ metric is natural for downstream decision problems such as bandits and reinforcement learning. We analyze the min--max estimator of Baraud & Maillard (2025), which we call \emph{sign-betting regression}. Our main results show that sign-betting regression achieves high-probability $L_1$ oracle inequalities under misspecification. Under realizability, our bound adapts to the average conditional variance and recovers the variance-adaptive rate of Li et al. (2026). We further characterize the optimal universal approximation factor for proper learning by showing that sign betting achieves factor 3, while no proper learner can satisfy a distribution-free, high-probability $L_1$ oracle inequality with a universal approximation factor strictly below 3. We extend the upper-bound analysis beyond finite classes and complement the theory with experiments in synthetic and real-world model-selection tasks.

When Reality Outpaces Cognition: A New Scaling Principle for Reliable Intelligence 2026-10-14
Show abstract

This paper challenges the understanding capabilities of existing AI paradigms and emerging world models by asking a fundamental question: How quickly can reality change before an intelligent system can no longer reliably perceive, reason, and act? We formulate this as a theory- and model-agnostic challenge in which systems must infer reliable actions for increasingly distant future states across a growing observation--reality gap. We operationalize this challenge through the effective Speed of Coherent Cognition (\(S_C^{\mathrm{eff}}\)), defined as the highest tested Speed of Reality (\(S_R\)) through which a system continuously satisfies a predefined behavioural reliability criterion from baseline.
