Lazy-MOSAIC: Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World Models 2026-11-16
📝 Under review
Show abstract

Long-horizon manipulation tasks require a robot to perform a sequence of physical interactions with its environment, typically combining several different skills. Planning such behaviors efficiently is challenging: given a library of parameterized skills, solutions lie in a vast hybrid space of skill sequences and their parameters, and evaluating each candidate skill's feasibility and effect with a high-fidelity world model such as a physics simulator is computationally expensive. MOSAIC, a recent skill-centric approach, lets the skills guide a non-sequential search toward the regions of this space where they are likely to succeed; however, it pays the full cost of simulation for every parameterized skill it considers. In this work, we introduce Lazy-MOSAIC, a lazy planning framework that instead searches over hypothesized skill outcomes. A cheap learned skill-effect world model predicts, in a fraction of a millisecond, a skill's feasibility and the world state it produces. Lazy-MOSAIC then uses these predictions to focus expensive simulation on promising candidate skills and, when a hypothesis is invalidated, repairs only the affected part of the search, while ensuring that every skill in a returned plan is validated in simulation. We evaluate Lazy-MOSAIC on cluttered-shelf retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty and demonstrate it on a physical robot, showing that it plans substantially faster than baselines with fewer simulator queries, while matching or exceeding their success rate in every setting. Visit skill-mosaic.github.io.

LynnReal-Hand: Unlocking World Interaction with Hand Control 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Interactive video world models enable exploration through text and camera controls, yet direct object interaction requires finer control. Hands provide a natural interface, but translating continuous hand movements into coherent visual responses remains challenging because of complex hand-object dynamics and demanding real-time computation. To address these challenges, we introduce LynnReal-Hand, a hand-controlled video world model that unifies precise interaction modeling with efficient streaming generation. We curate 100K interaction clips spanning diverse real and synthetic environments and develop hand-aware supervision to improve fine-grained hand motions and object interactions. Building on this foundation, we learn a causal flow map for few-step generation and introduce region-aware score distillation that combines complementary video and image priors to refine interaction dynamics and visual appearance. To further reduce inference latency, we propose Ultra-Fast-VAE, a compact video decoder that preserves high reconstruction fidelity with substantially lower computational cost. Extensive evaluations on HandBench demonstrate superior hand-following accuracy and visual quality compared with existing approaches. Our full-sequence model achieves an FVD of 181.67 and a MANO Dice score of 93.02, while the three-step causal model maintains a MANO Dice score of 92.49. For real-time interaction, 540p streaming on a single H200 reaches 18.03 FPS with 741ms latency and an optimized peak GPU memory footprint of only 34.89GB. We will release the datasets, model weights, and the full training and inference code to support research on hand-controlled video world models.

Fast Inference-Time Policy Refinement via Action-Affine World Models 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Imitation policies can fail through compounding errors, while existing refinement methods often require additional on-policy learning or expensive online planning. Our key insight is that an imitation policy already supplies a useful action proposal: refinement can focus on predicting how small action corrections change its outcome. We learn an \textit{action-affine world model} (A\textsuperscript{2}WM) offline from perturbed policy rollouts. Conditioned on history and the proposal, it directly predicts the future as a nominal trajectory plus a linear response to corrections, avoiding recursive error propagation. With quadratic costs and linear constraints, refinement becomes a cheap quadratic program (QP), requiring only one model evaluation and one QP solve per control step. Across four manipulation tasks, our framework achieves the highest success rate among evaluated baselines, adding only 4.2 ms of mean refinement latency per control step, excluding base-policy sampling. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to combine an action-affine world model with QP-based inference-time refinement of frozen imitation policies.

VGGT-VLA-World: Intent-Conditioned 3D World Evolution for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-16
📝 12 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

VGGT provides a strong foundation for geometry-centric world models by recovering unified 3D scene geometry from visual observations. Although recent extensions enable temporal 3D prediction, their future evolution remains weakly conditioned on driving intentions and actions, limiting their ability to model alternative action-dependent futures. We propose \textbf{VGGTWorld-VLA}, an intention-conditioned extension of VGGT-World for controllable 3D world evolution in autonomous driving. First, we introduce an action--semantic conditioning mechanism that injects complementary driving semantics and ego-motion representations into the future-token stream, enabling different future geometry predictions for the same observed scene under alternative ego actions. Second, we develop a geometry--language--action bridge that adapts historical geometry, VLA semantic features, and maneuver and trajectory representations for joint conditioning of future geometry prediction. We evaluate future geometry prediction on NAVSIM, with ablation studies examining the contributions of semantic and action conditioning. Compared with baseline, our method reduces future-depth RMSE by 13.7\% and improves $δ_1$ by 4.11 percentage points on NAVSIM motion subset. Controlled action-input interventions further show that the model uses the supplied action information. These results support semantic and action conditioning for controllable VGGT-based world prediction in autonomous driving.

SciWorldBench: Do Video World Models Really Understand Scientific Experiments? 2026-11-16
📝 Preprint. Benchmark paper with paired scientific experiments for evaluating video world models. 8 pages of main text, 16 pages including figures and references
Show abstract

While the ability of video generation models to accurately depict scientific phenomena has received growing attention, determining whether these models genuinely understand the underlying principles remains a critical challenge. Existing benchmarks typically evaluate video samples in isolation, making it difficult to expose models that simply fit superficial visual priors. In scientific experiments, a small change in one condition produces a markedly different outcome even when the rest of the setup remains fixed. Capitalizing on this sensitivity, we introduce SciWorldBench, a benchmark comprising paired scientific experiments. Each pair varies a single condition in an otherwise fixed setup, yielding two mutually exclusive expected outcomes. Each generation prompt specifies the condition but omits the expected outcome. We evaluate ten text-and-image-to-video (TI2V) models using an independent vision-language model (VLM) that assesses each video without access to the condition text or the label identifying the correct outcome. A pair is scored as correct only when both videos depict their respective scientifically correct outcomes. Across all ten models, we find that the two videos in an experimental pair frequently depict the same outcome even though the conditions require different results. This recurring failure suggests that current video world models struggle to apply the underlying scientific principles reliably during generation. We will publicly release the benchmark dataset and open-source the evaluation code.

Auditing World-Model Explanations in Retail Forecasting: Faithfulness, Accuracy and Price Response 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Forecasting models that read the price and promotion plan explain each forecast as a baseline, what would sell with nothing planned, plus the lift the plan adds. We test such explanations with eight checks, six with a pass rule fixed before any result, and find that passing them does not make an explanation right. In generated store data with known effects, a world model passes the checks of construction, stability, price direction and agreement with sales and with LightGBM, yet its lifts are 1.33 to 1.40 times the truth when merchants plan into high-demand weeks, and 1.23 to 1.42 times in eight variants of that world. It still picks the right weeks to promote, but stock ordered to its lift is off by 0.44 to 0.48 of the true lift. Its stated response to a lasting regular-price change is 4.7 to 6.5 times the true -0.5, and only a measured cause finds this. On Dominick's, 38 episodes in which a store's regular prices sat apart from its zone peers give a noisy benchmark: a pooled 8-week response of -0.33 (95% interval -0.68 to 0.01). For the final tuned world models of our companion paper on nine data sets, baseline and lift add up under each model's own plan, but a deeper price cut lowers the forecast in 29% to 83% of item-week steps on Dominick's (23% to 80% within prices seen in training), and the world model and LightGBM both state about twice the measured response. Across 703 configurations from the settings searches, checks passed were not related to forecast accuracy (Spearman 0.02, 95% interval -0.02 to 0.05); with no known answer for those versions, this concerns accuracy, while whether an explanation is right is measured only in the generated data.

LEON: Operator-Structured Latent Evolution for World Modeling 2026-11-16
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Modeling action-conditioned physical evolution is fundamental to latent world models. World Action Models (WAMs) couple such models with policy learning through predictive supervision or future-conditioned action generation. Yet their latent transitions typically rely on transformer-based predictors, leaving dynamical structure to be learned implicitly from data. We derive a latent-transition formulation from controlled mechanical evolution. A common finite-dimensional projection of observable dynamics yields a shared operator family affine in physical control and an explicit projection residual. Guided by this principle, we introduce the Latent Evolution Operator Network (LEON), which adapts pretrained visual features into learned observables and evolves them through an affine family of shared operator matrices and complementary additive updates. Coefficients inferred from evolving observables and action-related context modulate the operators. We instantiate LEON in two WAM architectures while preserving their distinct original prediction targets and policy interfaces. Across single-arm and bimanual manipulation, LEON improves average policy success over corresponding WAM baselines, including under diverse perturbations. Ablations support its observable adaptation and operator-structured evolution, while controlled-system evaluations demonstrate improved extrapolation beyond training conditions.

World Models, Trees and Foundation Models for Retail Forecasting: Evidence from Four Public Data Sets 2026-11-16
📝 37 pages, 18 figures, 26 tables. Code: https://github.com/karthik-iyer-au/retail-world-models, archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.23448056
Show abstract

A retailer makes different decisions at different grains of the product, store and time hierarchy: store orders for an item in a store, supplier funding for an item across stores, category performance by store, and the financial plan for the chain. Which forecasting model to use depends on the grain. This paper compares models at each grain on four public data sets (M5, six Dominick's categories, Breakfast at the Frat, and Corporación Favorita, the last kept out of all development), over rolling forecast origins with paired tests and model confidence sets. It covers price and promotion through the plan inputs these data record, not product, assortment or place. The models are a world model that carries each item's state forward under the price and promotion plan, the same network without that state, LightGBM, the Temporal Fusion Transformer, TimesFM, Chronos-Bolt, Timestral, seasonal naive, and an equal-weight average of the world model, LightGBM and TimesFM given the plan. The answer changed with the grain. For an item by store and an item across stores, the average had the lowest error on seven and eight of nine data sets, and the 90% confidence set held a median of 1 and 2 models. For a category by store, a category and the chain total, the lowest-error model differed between data sets and a median of 4 of nine models were not separated by the four to six windows available. For category-by-store planning cost, the average or LightGBM was lowest on seven of eight data sets, and the world model was lowest only on M5; it was behind LightGBM item by store on six of eight. Every trained model used the plan, and on the weekly promotion-driven data pretrained models were less accurate without it. These results hold for these data sets; a forecast solved across all four Ps, or a retailer's own data, may give different results. Every model, test and change that lost is reported.

PhysCapBench: A Cross-Domain Benchmark for Physically Grounded Video Captioning 2026-11-16
📝 Updated author order. Project page: https://hfwang2001.github.io/physcapbench-site/
Show abstract

Physical AI aims to learn transferable physical knowledge from real-world experience to understand, predict, and act in the physical world. Internet videos and embodied demonstrations provide complementary observations of motion, interaction, and state evolution. A foundational data challenge is to measure and preserve this physical information as training supervision. In video-language pretraining, captions express observed processes in language, yet a semantically correct description can omit the interactions and transitions that define those processes. Rich physical experience in video does not automatically translate into sufficient language supervision. This gap calls for a yardstick to assess annotation quality, compare captioning strategies, and guide training-data refinement. Motivated by our data construction for world-model pretraining, we introduce PhysCapBench, to our knowledge the first unified physical-caption benchmark spanning all three domains: third-person scenes, egocentric manipulation, and handheld-gripper demonstrations. Training-instruction enrichment improves both PhysCapBench scores and policy success, including a 7.5-percentage-point gain for pi0.5 on a RoboTwin 2.0 subtask. PhysCapBench makes the physical content of language supervision inspectable and comparable, providing a cross-domain basis for its evaluation and improvement.

XMimic and XSoma: Learning Humanoid Motion Tracking and Locomotion in Under a Minute 2026-11-16
Show abstract

We present XMimic and XSoma, which learn humanoid motion tracking and locomotion on rough terrain in under a minute of training. Both follow SuperMimic, which learns a world model of the robot and optimizes the policy with analytic gradients through short rollouts imagined in this model. Existing implementations of this method do not make use of larger GPUs, as every training iteration is a long chain of small GPU operations that run one after another. XMimic and XSoma therefore shorten this chain and run the steps of every iteration at the same time on up to four GPUs. We compile the complete training loop with JAX, including the MuJoCo Warp simulator. For locomotion, XSoma leaves only the dynamics of the robot to the world model and computes everything else exactly, including the terrain height under the feet. It learns with a horizon of one second and without a critic. On four H100 GPUs, XMimic learns to track all 40 LAFAN1 clips with the Unitree G1 in 0.86 minutes, 3.7 times faster than BlinkMimic on one GPU. XSoma reaches the tracking target that Holosoma's tests set for FastSAC in 0.77 minutes, 12.3 times faster than FastSAC on one GPU, while needing only an eighth of its environment steps.

One from Infinity: Actualizing Futures from Pretrained World Models into Robot Actions 2026-11-16
Show abstract

A pretrained video world model admits many plausible futures for a scene, but a robot must realize the exact task-conditioned one. To turn world models into executable robot policies, existing methods fine-tune the heavy world model backbone using large-scale robot data and computational resources. Challenging this status quo, we argue that the expensive part has already been paid in the world model pretraining since the representation space of a video world model encoder lays out the diverse potential futures. In this case, what remains is to determine the future that accomplishes the task and to read out the actions that realize it. We formalize this task as actualization, which learns a task-conditioned realization on top of a prior supplied by a frozen world model encoder. This can be solved by a tiny actualizer model. We implement RoboActualizer with as few as 60M parameters on top of a frozen world model encoder. The actualizer is composed of two lightweight DiT experts that jointly predict future latents and actions by flow matching. The model can be trained entirely on a single GPU with 32 GB peak memory. With up to 100x fewer trainable parameters than existing WAMs and VLAs, RoboActualizer reaches great performance on simulation benchmarks including LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0 and five tasks on two real-world platforms, with a low latency of 39 ms that allows real-time control.

InertiaMPDD: Multimodal Personalized Depression Detection via Emotional Inertia-Inspired Dynamics Modeling 2026-11-16

Multimodal depression screening estimates risk from speech, visual behavior and language. Current methods mainly predict labels, with limited evidence of departures from a non-depressed reference. Recording-level supervision also leaves fine-grained emotional dynamics unannotated. Motivated by emotional inertia, the persistence of affect, InertiaMPDD learns healthy next-state patterns using a world-model-inspired latent predictor. ODEWorld: A Continuous Predictive Architecture via Physical-Time Flow 2026-11-15
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

In the physical world we inhabit, space and time are fundamentally continuous. However, existing machine learning paradigms for world modeling are largely confined to discrete-time prediction, thereby exhibiting significant inefficiency in capturing the dynamics of physical world. We introduce Physical-Time Flow (PT-Flow), a novel approach that learns a continuous latent velocity field operating in physical time. Crucially, the underlying dynamics of sequential data are parameterized by an ordinary differential equation (ODE) embedded in a well-structured representation space. Under this paradigm, the prediction of future can be recast as temporal integration via an ODE solver in the compressed latent space. Building upon PT-Flow, we construct ODEWorld, a continuous-time latent world model that is both efficient and versatile. By extracting time-variant features and enforcing ODE properties on both the dynamical representation space and the latent velocity field, ODEWorld effectively addresses the long-standing representation collapse issue in latent world model literature. This also enables high-quality image reconstruction even after long-horizon prediction. Moreover, its continuous nature allows for arbitrary temporal resolution and even backward prediction, which is impossible for most discrete-time models. Lastly, ODEWorld can provide rich planning-oriented information to facilitate downstream policy learning. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that ODEWorld successfully reconciles planning-conducive dynamics abstraction with visual realism, excelling in both video generation and robotic control. Project page: https://dstate.github.io/odeworld_website/.

Flying on Imagination: DreamerV3-Style World Models under Environment Distribution Shift for Quadrotor Navigation 2026-11-15
Show abstract

DreamerV3-style reconstructive world models offer a route to vision-based navigation for aerial robots through learned latent dynamics. Yet their behavior under shifts in the training environment and their ability to support navigation using imagined observations, predictions generated by rolling the latent dynamics forward without new sensory input, remain insufficiently characterized. We study four training-environment regimes for depth-based quadrotor navigation, spanning a fixed layout, a finite set of layouts, low-discrepancy continuous obstacle sampling, and i.i.d. uniform sampling. We introduce WorldOracle, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) against paired real-world executions. Given the same initial observation and low-level action sequence, WorldOracle asks whether a predicted rollout reproduces what happens in the real world, including physical consequences and task-state transitions. Driving World Models Move the Camera, Not the Scene 2026-11-15
📝 23 pages. Project page: https://joefeng0571.github.io/retime-compose/
Show abstract

Plan-conditioned driving world models are used to compare candidate plans, so their forecast should change with the plan as the real scene would, yet widely used control metrics check only whether the camera follows the command. Recorded drives already contain real outcomes of plans that were never driven: retiming a log gives pose-matched images and LiDAR for other speeds, and a stereo rig records a laterally shifted view. Against these references, released world models move their camera but barely move the scene: their scene response, the share of the real change in distance that the forecast reproduces, reaches at most about a third of real video's, and the trajectory score cannot tell an executed speed command from an ignored one. Interventions inside the generator trace the failure to the plan's channel. The forecast geometry follows the context frames; once they carry the planned geometry, plan tokens add little. ReTime-Compose therefore delivers the plan as geometry, without training. It transports the frames of one logged-speed rollout to the planned cameras, and the frozen model completes only what no frame covers. Frozen generators then follow a 0.54 m lateral shift nearly as the real scene does on KITTI, nuScenes and Argoverse 2, and respond more than re-sampling to slow-down plans in three generators and to speed-up plans in two.

SAFER: Navigating the Exploration-Exploitation Dilemma in World-Model-Based Planning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-15
Show abstract

An autonomous vehicle must foresee the consequences of its actions, especially in the rare, time-critical situations where crashes happen. A world model offers this foresight, but only within the coverage of its training data, which poses an exploration-exploitation dilemma. Within this coverage a planner can hardly improve on the data, and beyond it the model's errors mislead it. Logs of competent driving rarely contain the failures that would widen the coverage, and agents that learn from interaction gain them only by failing themselves, without being shown what to do instead. We propose SAFER, a framework that learns offline from failures that others make, judged by an expert, and keeps learning online from its own driving. Offline, partially trained agents and scripted, recoverable perturbations Stage failures in simulation, and an expert Annotates every visited state with its action and value, so that much of what the planner explores is covered before it drives. In planning, a chunked latent world model Foresees the consequences fast enough for real-time use, and a risk-aware sampling-based planner Evaluates candidate maneuvers by their return minus a bounded collision penalty. Online, a loop continuously Refines the model wherever this driving leaves the coverage of the offline data. In closed-loop evaluation against the compared methods, SAFER reaches the highest route completion and reward per step on the CARLA Leaderboard routes, 0.95 and 0.77 from a privileged bird's-eye view, and the highest episode reward in MetaDrive, 53.4 in regular and 49.0 in safety-critical traffic. Project page: https://ys-qu.github.io/safer-website.

Latent World Modeling for Point-Cloud Video Pretraining 2026-11-15
Show abstract

Masked modeling is the dominant self-supervised paradigm for point-cloud videos. However, it leaks the information it is meant to predict, whether it reconstructs masked points as in MAE-style methods or predicts their latent features. Because point-cloud tokens are anchored at points sampled from each frame, querying a target at its own position reveals where its geometry has moved, in reconstruction and latent prediction alike. For latent predictors, random spatio-temporal masks introduce a further shortcut, as visible frames on both sides of each target allow interpolation rather than forecasting. The pretext task thus reduces to completion. Once the target positions are withheld, a masked latent predictor performs only slightly better than copying the last frame. To remove this leakage, we propose PointWM, a latent world model that turns completion into forecasting by predicting the features and geometry of future frames from strictly earlier context. Each target is queried only with its last-seen position and timestamp, while a latent action learned without annotations is meant to capture the part of the future that the past leaves undetermined. On HOI4D action segmentation with a P4Transformer backbone, PointWM improves F1@50 over training from scratch by 24.9 offline and 29.1 under strictly causal inference. These results show the promise of latent world models as a leakage-free pretraining paradigm for point-cloud videos.Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/InitalZ/PointWM.

Cooperative speed control by connected and automated vehicles can reduce congestion at lane drop bottlenecks. However, effective coordination depends on vehicle roles and execution timing, while initial holding costs may be offset by later traffic benefits. This paper proposes an offline plan search framework that combines world model guidance with simulator verification. A traffic world model incorporating physical dynamics guides evolutionary search over vehicle roles, target speeds, holding durations, and start times. Linear Recurrent Memory Suffices to Distil a World-Model Policy for Robot Air Hockey 2026-11-15
📝 10 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Does memory-dependent control need nonlinear recurrent dynamics? We study simulated air-hockey defence under temporary loss of puck tracking. A DreamerV3 teacher outperforms a memoryless policy under tracking loss, and erasing its recurrent state at the moment tracking fails cuts its save rate sharply, so the task requires memory. We distil this teacher into compact recurrent policies with a 64-dimensional state, combining a diagonal linear recurrence with an optional rank-$k$ nonlinear innovation while the observation encoder and action head stay nonlinear. Across five matched seeds, the purely linear recurrent model ($k=0$) matches both the GRU baseline and the teacher throughout the tested range of tracking loss. Under 5\,mm observation noise, without retraining, the linear student keeps a higher save rate than the GRU baseline, while using fewer recurrent parameters and less computation. These results suggest that, for this memory-dependent control task, nonlinear representation learning around a simple linear memory can be sufficient, and that nonlinear recurrent dynamics are not necessarily required.

DeformMaster: An Interactive Physics-Neural World Model for Deformable Objects from Videos 2026-11-15

Show abstract

Continual learning treats any drop in performance on previously seen data as forgetting, a convention inherited from settings where the prediction target is stationary. World models violate this assumption: their target is the environment, which changes, so knowledge that was accurate when learned can later become false, and revising it is correct behavior rather than a defect. Whether an action-conditioned world model responds to actions is usually checked by replacing or removing the actions and measuring the loss in accuracy. Taking biological experiments as the guiding case, we show that such diagnostics report action effects where there are none: under autocorrelated designs, future actions stand in for history the model does not see. When the design law is known, a conditional randomization test written as an e-value is valid in finite samples whatever the model.
