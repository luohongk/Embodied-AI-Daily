YANAMI: A Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset and Benchmark for Multiturn Stateful Interaction 2026-11-03
📝 Project page: https://rowletqwq.github.io/YANAMI
Show abstract

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) integrate visual understanding, generation, and editing within a single unified framework. However, existing unified multimodal datasets and benchmarks remain dominated by single-turn tasks. Even in recent multi-turn datasets, many turns can be solved independently without tracking information across turns. Consequently, they provide limited supervision and evaluation for multimodal stateful interaction, which requires models to understand, maintain, update, and faithfully realize evolving dialogue states across turns. To address this gap, we introduce YANAMI, the first large-scale multimodal dialogue dataset and benchmark for stateful interaction, comprising YANAMI-400K and YANAMI-Bench. YANAMI-400K contains 400K multi-turn multimodal dialogues, with more than 2M images and 2.6M dialogue turns. The dialogues span four representative scenarios: Multi-Image Fusion, Image Editing, World Modeling, and Visual Reasoning, and require models to continuously track, preserve, and revise evolving multimodal dialogue states across turns. YANAMI-Bench comprises 400 multi-turn dialogues for systematically evaluating multimodal stateful interaction. It jointly evaluates Dialogue State Understanding and Visual Realization, characterizing the gap between models' ability to understand evolving dialogue states and their ability to faithfully realize them in visual outputs. Experiments show that fine-tuning BAGEL on YANAMI-400K improves image editing, generation, and multimodal stateful interaction by 10.3%, 2.0%, and 13.23%, respectively, while preserving its general visual understanding capabilities. Evaluations on YANAMI-Bench further reveal that existing UMMs still struggle to maintain, update, and visually realize evolving multimodal dialogue states.

Trajectory Evaluation via Rollout-Free World Model for End-to-end Autonomous Driving 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Although existing candidate trajectory evaluators have substantially improved end-to-end planning accuracy, their underlying mechanism remains suboptimal. Model-based evaluators typically make scoring future-aware by explicitly predicting candidate-conditioned future states over the planning horizon, which we term rollout. However, rollout incurs high inference cost and accumulates prediction errors. We argue that planning does not require reconstructing a full future scene for each candidate, but only future-relevant information for reliable comparison. We therefore propose RFWorld, a Rollout-Free World model for end-to-end trajectory evaluation. RFWorld constructs a shared scene memory and shapes it into a future-readable representation via lightweight training-time temporal readout with semantic supervision, keeping training overhead low. At inference time, candidate trajectories directly query the shared memory via sparse spatial readout for scoring, reducing computation by roughly the planning horizon times the number of candidate trajectories, while avoiding recursive rollout errors. Experiments on NAVSIM and Bench2Drive show that RFWorld learns a more planning-aligned world representation and achieves state-of-the-art performance.

World-Model-Augmented Visual Locomotion for Humanoids on Foothold-Constrained Terrain 2026-11-03
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables. Yuxi Liu and Lijun Han contributed equally

Show abstract

Foothold-constrained terrain limits feasible footholds to sparse, discontinuous, or narrow support regions, as in gaps, stepping stones, stairs, and balance beams. In such settings, a single misplaced step can cause a fall. Reliable traversal therefore requires precise foot placement and anticipation of upcoming terrain. To address this challenge, we present World-Model-Augmented Visual Locomotion (WM-LOCO), which augments a visual locomotion policy with predictive context from a jointly trained world model. Given onboard depth observations and proprioception, a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) learns action-conditioned latent dynamics that predict future proprioceptive states, egocentric observations, and task rewards. The resulting recurrent feature is then fed into the proximal policy optimization (PPO) actor-critic, providing predictive context for anticipatory foot placement on foothold-constrained terrain. WM-LOCO achieves average success rates of 96.0% on gaps, 96.9% on stepping stones, 94.0% on stairs, and 92.1% on balance beams, outperforming the evaluated baselines while improving gait efficiency and smoothness. Furthermore, WM-LOCO achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1, with 92.5% success across these four terrain classes.

Trusting Imagined Futures: Auditing Imagined Rewards for VLA Post-Training 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Video world models enable candidate actions to be evaluated through imagined outcomes, but high predicted rewards can favor trajectories with poor execution quality. We introduce Trusting Imagined Futures (TIF), an offline supervision selection method for vision-language-action (VLA) post-training that audits predicted rewards using execution feedback. A multidimensional reward model predicts a utility score for candidate ranking and five dimensions of execution quality. An auditor uses reward predictions and uncertainty to identify overestimation patterns learned from paired imagined videos and real executions. TIF accepts the candidate with the highest predicted utility when its advantage over the existing trajectory exceeds a margin and its audit risk passes a threshold; otherwise, it retains the existing supervision. The retained real trajectories then supervise behavior cloning. Across four LIBERO suites and three training seeds, TIF achieves a mean success rate of 76.25%, improving over training on the existing trajectories by 3.00 percentage points with the same trajectory count and training budget. Adding audit gating yields 1.47 percentage points with the reward model and advantage margin held fixed. The gains persist against advantage ranking and logistic regression risk selection at matched replacement counts. On recorded videos from three real robot tasks, TIF increases clean completion among selected trajectories from 58.33% to 70.00% across 60 candidate pools.

PrimeSim: A Generalist World Model Simulator from Large-Scale Cross-Embodiment Demonstrations 2026-11-03
Show abstract

A robot world model that serves as a simulator must do more than generate plausible videos: it must faithfully follow commanded actions, maintain consistency across camera views, and sustain coherent long-horizon rollouts. We present PrimeSim, an action-conditioned, multiview world model simulator that addresses these challenges through progressive midtraining and posttraining. Starting from a pretrained video generator, we midtrain on 30,110 hours of robot demonstrations spanning 22 platforms. To improve action following, we introduce a unified cross-embodiment action representation and frame-aligned dual-path conditioning to strengthen action-frame correspondence. To maintain multiview consistency, we develop view-aware interleaved attention with explicit camera identities, enabling cross-view information exchange while preserving view-specific representations. We further introduce a fast-video, slow-memory posttraining framework that transforms the bidirectional video generator into an efficient autoregressive simulator. By combining fast causal generation, self-rollout training, and hierarchical temporal memory, PrimeSim mitigates error accumulation from self-generated histories and supports coherent long-horizon simulation. PrimeSim runs at 10.1 FPS, remains stable over long-horizon rollouts on rigid and deformable manipulation tasks, and transfers to held-out platforms within 100 to 200 gradient steps where the pretrained backbone does not. Against five open-source action-conditioned world models it leads four of the five WorldSimProbe suites and posts the best overall EWMBench score, while fine-grained local calibration still trails the field; its rollouts are faithful enough to support GRPO-based policy optimization. We release the model weights, inference code, and training code.

PRIME W0: Physical Priors from World Models for Embodied Manipulation 2026-11-03

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel at instruction following, yet most remain feed-forward: they condition actions only on the current observation and discard both long-horizon temporal context and the hierarchical structure of VLM representations. We present PRIME W0, a hierarchical conditional flow-matching framework that closes this gap through three synergistic designs: (1)per-layer KV injection from a pretrained VLM into an Adaptive MoE action expert; (2)dynamics-aware conditioning via asynchronous world-model subgoal-image prediction and a DINOv2 historical-frame semantic bridge; and (3)Action Dynamic Manifold (ADM) flow matching with covariance-structured noise that respects robot kinematic coupling. A research world model of power engineering learns its dynamics and reveals what-ifs 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Decisions about the direction of research in power engineering determine which technologies mature and, through them, shape infrastructure that lasts decades, yet their consequences cannot be observed before they are taken, and existing forecasts of research fields extrapolate a single trajectory. Here we introduce research world models, which treat a research field as a dynamical system that can be acted upon, and establish one for power engineering from 1.1 million papers. A distilled language model reads each paper into role-typed concepts, two sophisticated, high-fidelity graph-temporal neural network cores learn how the resulting concept network evolves, and operators learned from natural experiments in the publication record estimate the effects of interventions. In temporally held-out tests, the model forecast years ahead which concepts would grow and which method-target pairs would be joined for the first time, outperforming tuned machine-learning baselines in most settings, and its estimated intervention effects tracked those realized in natural experiments it had not seen. Its training scheme and three of its operators replicated in two fields withheld from development. Questions posed in natural language are compiled by a domain-specialized 27-billion-parameter language model into executable actions and answered as paired simulations with and without the intervention. The answers were rated 4.1 of 5 for faithfulness by an independent language-model judge. By revealing the latent dynamics of a research field and its response to interventions, research world models offer a new way to simulate and test research decisions before they are made.

Hybrid Particle-Voxel Dynamics for Multimodal 3D World Simulation 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Predicting how the world evolves in response to robot actions is central to policy learning, evaluation, and planning. However, many action-conditioned world models predict future observations in 2D image or video space without explicitly modeling the underlying 3D scene, making spatial consistency across viewpoints and over long-horizon interactions challenging. We introduce 3D World Simulator, an action-conditioned multimodal world modeling framework that jointly predicts appearance, geometry, and tactile observations from a unified 3D representation. Our approach combines a hybrid particle-voxel autoencoder that compresses point clouds into compact 3D voxel latents with an action-conditioned latent dynamics model that predicts their evolution. The shared 3D representation supports geometrically consistent rendering from arbitrary viewpoints and flexible integration of spatially aligned tactile signals. Our experiments demonstrate interactive, long-horizon, multimodal prediction at 50 Hz. We then show how our model enables scalable data generation for policy training and evaluation using tactile and multi-view visual observations, including wrist-mounted and novel camera views. Furthermore, we scale our method to large datasets containing diverse objects and demonstrate model-based planning with unseen objects and robot configurations. Together, these results highlight the potential of hybrid particle-voxel dynamics as a foundation for multimodal 3D world simulation.

Embodied AI for Scientific Discovery: From Physical Action to Scientific Evidence 2026-11-03
📝 46 pages, 10 figures, 14 tables. Review article
Show abstract

Scientific experiments are increasingly selected, executed and interpreted by learned systems that act through physical interfaces. Robot learning builds physical competence, whereas AI for science chooses which experiment to run, each reporting different endpoints. This review treats their interface as one problem, embodied AI for science, where the experiment itself is partially observed: specimen state, apparatus state and measurement processes are latent, actions are costly and irreversible, and missingness depends on execution and outcome. We organize the literature by three closures operating at distinct levels: operational closure (the experiment realized is the one intended), epistemic closure (the result updates beliefs or selects subsequent experiments) and evidential closure (the record supports a claim). We analyze workflows through a record-linked evidence cycle - Record, Diagnose, Calibrate, Confirm - as analytic functions with feedback. A taxonomy crosses these closures with six delegated decisions and five embodiment classes. Our formulation separates commanded, realized and observed conditions and models diagnosis with explicit resource, risk and sample-change costs. We compare experiment-selection methods by terminal decision, review perception, protocol compilation, visuomotor policies, world models, recovery and transfer by evidence emission requirements, and compare chemistry, materials, biology, microscopy and field workflows by what their records establish. An appendix provides an evidence map of 28 workflows (26 physical, two software-boundary), characterizing validation pathways and claim confirmation requirements. We propose evaluation that separates actions, valid experiments, campaigns and confirmed claims, framing open challenges as controlled comparisons with measurable failure conditions.

Medical world models in healthcare: capabilities, evidence, and trustworthy clinical translation 2026-11-03
📝 Updated title, author order, and abstract; expanded the source corpus to 300 sources
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Medical world models offer a framework for extending medical artificial intelligence beyond static prediction by representing evolving patient states and modelling how they change over time and in response to clinical interventions. This Review uses structured narrative synthesis and reproducible evidence mapping to define conceptual boundaries, technical foundations, application domains, and clinical evidence requirements. We screened 1,455 unique records and assembled a corpus of 300 sources, including 14 studies that met a strict empirical definition of a medical world model. The field is organized around four capabilities: patient state representation, temporal dynamics modelling, intervention-conditioned simulation, and clinician-supervised planning. Evidence spans medical imaging, longitudinal electronic health records, treatment response modelling, physiological and multimodal state modelling, ultrasound and surgical interaction, and population and health-system simulation; clinical digital twins are treated as a cross-cutting integration framework. Studies provide early evidence of technical feasibility for trajectory forecasting and comparison of candidate interventions, but most remain retrospective, task-specific, or preclinical. We distinguish functional capability from clinical evidence maturity and specify evidence needed to support progressively stronger clinical claims, from temporal forecasting to intervention comparison and planning-oriented decision support. These requirements include precise intervention representations, robust causal and mechanistic grounding, calibrated trajectory-level uncertainty, safety-constrained planning, external validation, and prospective multicentre evaluation against clinically meaningful endpoints. This framework aims to distinguish technically plausible simulation from clinically supportable prediction and decision support.

Distill to Think, Foresee to Act: Cognitive-Physical Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Current end-to-end autonomous driving models are fundamentally constrained by the behavioral cloning ceiling of imitation learning. While reinforcement learning offers a path to smarter autonomy, it demands two missing pieces of infrastructure: (1) a cognitive foundation that understands traffic semantics and driving intent, and (2) a foresighted physical environment that can anticipate the consequences of candidate actions. To this end, we propose CoPhy, a CognitivePhysical reinforcement learning framework for autonomous driving. To distill to think, we distill VLM knowledge into the BEV encoder and then discard the VLM entirely, retaining cognitive ability at zero inference cost while releasing the cognitive channel as a pluggable interface for optional human language commands. To foresee to act, we build an auto-regressive BEV world model that explicitly predicts future semantic maps conditioned on candidate actions, serving as an interpretable physical sandbox from which safety metrics are directly derived. Built upon this dual infrastructure, we optimize the driving policy via GRPO with a novel dual-reward mechanism: a physical reward derived from BEV rollouts enforces hard safety constraints, while a cognitive reward from a language-aligned scorer ensures intent compliance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoPhy not only achieves state-of-the-art results on NAVSIM v1 and v2 benchmarks, but also enables safer driving via cognitively informed scene compliance and flexible intent control through user-defined language instructions. World models allow agents to anticipate action consequences, but proactive assistance also requires interpreting incomplete observations and deciding whether to intervene before a user asks for help. Flow Equivariant World Models: Structured Memory for Dynamic Environments 2026-11-03
📝 Accepted at ICML 2026
Show abstract

Embodied systems experience the world as 'a symphony of flows': a combination of many continuous streams of sensory input coupled to self-motion, interwoven with the dynamics of external objects. These sensory streams and the underlying dynamics of the world obey smooth, time-parameterized symmetries which existing world models ignore. Without a memory that respects this structure, partial observability presents a major obstacle to existing methods: each observation reveals only a fraction of the world, while unobserved regions continue to evolve. In this work, we introduce Flow Equivariant World Modeling, a framework that leverages time-parameterized symmetries within a latent memory for stable and accurate dynamics prediction over long horizons. The latent memory shifts and transforms equivariantly with self-motion and inferred external object motion, keeping information about out-of-view regions aligned as time progresses. We demonstrate the advantage of this framework over state-of-the-art diffusion, memory-augmented, and recurrent world model architectures on 2D and 3D partially observed video world modeling benchmarks. More broadly, our results suggest that predictive representations become more powerful when they are organized in line with the temporal and dynamical structure of the world they model. Computer-Use Agents Gain Little Benefit from a World Model When Conditioned on Tutorials 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Recent work has improved computer-use agents (CUAs) by combining retrieved task-relevant tutorials and look-ahead planning using world models with MLLM judges. However, the contribution of each component -- tutorials, the judge, and the world model -- to overall improvement has not been evaluated in isolation. To fill this gap, we first construct OSWorld-Tutorial and ScienceBoard-Tutorial: two datasets for CUAs with curated multimodal tutorials, covering everyday and scientific domains. We then propose RAG-PolicyJudge (RAG-PJ), a policy-judge framework for CUAs conditioned on task-specific tutorials: the policy proposes candidate actions, and the judge selects among them. To isolate the effect of world models given tutorials, we compare RAG-PJ with and without common world models: using either zero-shot text-based descriptions of next states or an oracle that returns ground-truth visual observations. We find that RAG-PJ gains little benefit from these rollouts when tutorials are present, even with perfect visual futures. This suggests prior world-model gains in tutorial-conditioned methods come mainly from tutorial understanding and the judge's ability to select the proposed actions, rather than from predicted rollouts. Error analysis reveals that the bottleneck is not rollout fidelity, but the judge's ability to reason over future states and recognize task completion. This highlights the need to improve not just next-state prediction in world models, but also reasoning over their predicted rollouts. Datasets and code are available at https://github.com/realjoenguyen/RAGPJ-official.

Compression Forcing: Hierarchical History Tokenization Enables Flexible World Models 2026-11-02
📝 37 pages, 16 figures
Show abstract

Effective world modeling requires compressing salient history information that facilitates future prediction. However, most existing approaches inherit tokenization and conditioning mechanisms from video generation, where objectives are centered around perceptual realism rather than predictive utility. This discrepancy limits the world model's effectiveness, especially in environments dominated by visual nuisances or task-irrelevant background details. To this end, we introduce Compression Forcing (CF), a general framework that aligns history compression with the predictive objective of world modeling. Compression Forcing features Dynamics-Aware Tokenization (DAT), a novel tokenization method that compresses history observations into a sequence ordered by decreasing dynamics saliency. Built on a diffusion backbone, this structure enables Hierarchical History Guidance (HG-H), allowing selective amplification of dynamics-salient information during inference. Extensive experiments across various environments demonstrate improved prediction fidelity, physical realism, and downstream decision-making performance, establishing Compression Forcing as a highly flexible and effective approach for world modeling.

Physical Experience Builds Continual Spatial World Models 2026-11-02
Show abstract

World models are increasingly used to support prediction and planning in embodied agents, but the knowledge is usually established before deployment and then carried into later encounters. Continual operation faces a different problem. A familiar environment may change, and the agent must use previous experience without allowing old expectations to override current physical evidence. This study asks what repeated physical experience must contribute for a spatial world model to become progressively more useful rather than simply more persistent. A physics-grounded dynamic maze separates recurring spatial change from the continuous consequences of action. Cross-episode Spatial Change Memory learns relations among environmental transformations, while state-action-outcome experience refines predictions of motion. Structured experience progressively reduces the need for future route correction and transfers to spatial transitions not encountered during learning. The advantage disappears when past changes contain no information about future ones. Continuous navigation further shows that predictive memory changes behavior when a previous decision becomes invalid but leaves behavior unchanged when the decision remains correct. Physical tests expose a complementary limit: correct topology does not guarantee correct action prediction, and accumulated physical experience reduces this mismatch within the modeled dynamics. The results characterize continual world knowledge as experience that improves future prediction and action beyond stored episodes while remaining revisable by the physical outcomes it is intended to predict.

Show abstract

Modern learning systems increasingly combine pretrained models with downstream heads. Despite the growing importance of such modular designs, how the downstream model co-adapts to the geometry of pretrained representations remains relatively underexplored. We study this problem through vision-language-action models (VLAs), where a pretrained vision-language model (VLM) conditions an action model for control. We find that the action model progressively concentrates its sensitivity on high-variance dimensions of the VLM outputs. Through controlled experiments across different backbone-head models, we further show that counteracting this sensitivity concentration can be beneficial under representation--task mismatch settings. Our results highlight interface co-adaptation as an important optimization problem when coupling pretrained representations with downstream models.

Toward Uncertainty-Aware, Physics-Grounded Human World Models 2026-11-02
📝 CoRL2026 UncertainWM Workshop
Show abstract

A robot that acts around people needs a world model of the human: a belief over the person's body state and a dynamics model that predicts where that state goes next. This abstract argues that two published components form exactly these two halves, and specifies how to couple them. KNOWN [1] reconstructs the 3D human body while estimating both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in real time (11.6 ms per frame); its aleatoric term identifies unreliable observations, its epistemic term identifies inputs from low-density regions of the training data, and refinement driven by the epistemic term reduces the error on such inputs from 81.3 to 70.3 mm. No new experimental results are reported here.

TurboWAM: Learning What Matters for Fast World Action Models 2026-11-02
📝 Technical report. Project page: https://turbowam.github.io/
Show abstract

World action models have demonstrated promising performance in robot policy learning, yet their high inference latency remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment. Recent approaches have sought to improve inference efficiency by eliminating test-time future-frame imagination or accelerating action prediction. However, aggregating representations from all video world model layers can introduce redundant computation, while standard backpropagation may distribute action-supervision gradients unevenly across layers, limiting the effectiveness of world representation learning. In this work, we propose TurboWAM, a fast world action model that learns to efficiently aggregate and optimize world representations for action prediction. Specifically, TurboWAM introduces an efficient world representation aggregation mechanism that restricts aggregation to a bounded range of relevant world model layers identified through layer relevance analysis. Within this bounded range, TurboWAM estimates aggregation weights for different world model layers based on the current action denoising state and combines them with learnable layer-wise weights to fuse world representations. Moreover, TurboWAM employs a world model gradient modulation mechanism that decomposes action-supervision gradients into a layer-shared component and layer-specific residual components, enabling more balanced gradient propagation across world model layers. This mechanism operates exclusively during training and introduces no additional inference computation. Together, these designs enable more efficient world representation learning and low-latency action prediction. Extensive experiments on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks show that TurboWAM achieves low inference latency while maintaining competitive task success rates compared with state-of-the-art methods.

SPT-Flow: One Integration, Many Futures in Continuous-Time World Models 2026-11-02
📝 19 pages, including appendices; updated Mingyang Lyu's affiliations. No changes to the scientific content
Show abstract

Continuous-time world models generate a latent trajectory through a single ODE integration, but adding stochastic conditioning does not ensure that the model uses it. In our teacher-forced baseline, posterior means vary by only about 1% of the prior scale, and matched and swapped latents yield indistinguishable reconstruction PSNR at the reported precision. We study a supervision-shortcut hypothesis: ground-truth intermediate states can reduce the need for trajectory-associated latent information. We introduce SPT-Flow, which samples one latent per trajectory and matches a free-running ODE rollout to demonstration states at multiple readout times. Re-anchoring experiments support the proposed mechanism. Large video diffusion models provide rich spatiotemporal priors for autonomous driving, but existing world-action models often inherit the cost of iterative future-video generation even though deployment only requires an ego trajectory. We ask a more basic question: how much of a video diffusion model must be executed to make a reliable driving decision? Through a controlled study of video denoising timesteps and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) depth, we find that planning performance is largely insensitive to the tested video-noise levels, whereas strong trajectories can already be decoded from intermediate layers. Based on this observation, we introduce Adaptive-WAM, a quality-aware multi-exit planner built on a Wan2.2-5B backbone. Trajectory diffusion heads are attached to selected DiT blocks, and a lightweight trajectory-quality scorer terminates inference once the best trajectory decoded so far satisfies a quality threshold; otherwise, computation continues from the cached hidden state to a deeper exit. The deployed planner therefore avoids the iterative classifier-free denoising loop and VAE decoding required for future-video synthesis, while dynamically allocating backbone depth according to trajectory quality. On NAVSIM, the adaptive single-trajectory planner achieves 90.8 PDMS; a separate fixed-exit variant reaches 92.6 PDMS with 64 proposals. It further obtains 89.9 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, yielding the best reported results among the compared front-view video world-model planners. Without target-domain fine-tuning, Adaptive-WAM transfers to nuScenes with 0.88 m average L2 error and a 0.08\% collision rate. On an A100, adaptive routing improves PDMS from 90.62 to 90.79 while averaging 170 ms end-to-end planning latency, approximately 10\% below the 190 ms fixed block-15 planner and 47\% below the 320 ms fixed full-depth planner. Embodied world models should not only imagine plausible futures, but also perceive the geometric, semantic, and motion structure of the evolving world. We present GenSight, a unified embodied world model that brings future video generation and dense perception into a single pretrained video diffusion transformer. GenSight uses a unified spatiotemporal representation for video, depth, embodiment segmentation, and first-frame-relative 2D flow. Built on a shared DiT backbone, GenSight uses lightweight task modulation to predict all modalities within the same architecture. All tasks are jointly optimized within a unified velocity prediction space. At inference, GenSight supports two complementary modes: multi-step future video generation with aligned dense predictions and single-step dense perception from an observed video. By unifying modality representation, model architecture, and prediction space, GenSight avoids separate modality-specific pipelines and couples appearance, geometry, semantics, and motion in a shared representation. It promotes cross-modal consistency and allows dense perception to benefit from the spatiotemporal priors learned through video generation. Trained on embodied manipulation data spanning multiple robot embodiments and human-hand interactions, GenSight demonstrates strong performance in both video generation and dense 4D perception, highlighting the effectiveness of unifying seeing and imagining for embodied world modeling.

AI has made remarkable progress in mathematics, solving challenging problems with machine-checkable proofs in Lean, yet typically relying on predefined mathematical definitions and formal specifications. This raises a natural question: can it reason rigorously about an unknown world? We introduce the Algebraic World Model (AWM), a framework that turns interaction with unfamiliar environments into explicit mathematical models supporting formal reasoning. AWM explores the environment and develops executable Python hypotheses, continually testing and revising them against observations. It then structures these hypotheses into typed objects and shared rules, simplifies their structure through category-theory-guided transformations, and formalizes the resulting model in Lean 4 to prove reachability and impossibility. AWM can formally prove that a goal is unreachable, even beyond explored trajectories. On two impossible game levels, it constructs closed, kernel-checked Lean proofs excluding every legal winning sequence in all six runs, while Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, equipped with coding tools, produce none in twelve baseline runs. Four of AWM's six initial bounds are correct, compared with one for Opus 5.5 and none for GPT-6 Astra; given only feedback that a cheaper win exists, AWM revises its rules and constructs new proofs, reaching six of six correct bounds. Moreover, AWM produces interpretable mathematical models whose rules can be inspected and tested step by step. Across all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, these models enable Lean to replay the winning trajectories of all 183 levels and generalize beyond observed play, predicting unseen transitions and detecting changes in game mechanics. With Claude Opus 5, AWM clears all levels with a score of 99.9, while its certified winning trajectories use only 43% of the human baseline's actions.

Motion planning in clutter is embodiment-specific: the feasible free space and reachable commands depend on each robot's geometry and dynamics. Existing cross-embodiment policies output normalized commands that disregard these differences, or compensate through per-robot training and distillation. World models can in principle pool cross-embodiment data, yet command-conditioned models bind their latent dynamics to a single action space, while pose-conditioned models omit the mapping from commands to body-specific motion. On flat ground, however, embodiment affects the planner's observations only through the camera ego-motion. This letter proposes Hourglass, an hourglass-shaped architecture that factorizes cross-embodiment motion planning at this interface: a body-agnostic world model and each robot's differentiable ego-dynamics, which map commands to that ego-motion, are coupled only through it. The world model is trained on data pooled across embodiments and camera mounts; each robot's ego-dynamics is a reduced-order template with identified physical parameters. A generalist parameter-conditioned policy is learned once at this junction: each imagined step rolls out the template and then the world model, and imagination training through each robot's template makes the policy body-aware. On seven simulated robots of five locomotion types, the generalist policy outperforms imitation-learning and diffusion-policy baselines on the same world model by 3.8--10.6 percentage points in success rate and matches per-robot specialists without dedicated training, while approaching its parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher within 1.4--2.8 points without an online map. After three minutes of teleoperated driving for identification, the synthetic-data-trained model transfers zero-shot to a real skid-steer robot unseen in training, navigating unseen indoor scenes.

Learned world models ship as software components whose model versions, preprocessing, and test harnesses change between releases. Work on model updates already counts per-sample regressions, or negative flips, that an accuracy check hides. Two problems remain: a test can pass under one configuration and fail under another, and a harness fault can produce erroneous scores and wrong verdicts without a crash. We present TetraState, a regression-testing protocol for paired possible/impossible video tests. Each test runs under four fixed model-native configurations; its number of correct verdicts assigns one of four outcome classes. Inconclusive tests and failed executions are reported separately alongside the verdict rate. Digital medicine now captures data that traces individual health trajectories and reveals population health dynamics through electronic health records, claims, remote monitoring, wearable devices, mobile devices, social sensing data, medical images, etc. But much of its modeling still centers on task-specific, outcome-specific, and prediction-specific estimation. The next frontier is not simply better prediction, but action in an evolving health world shaped by multiple decision-makers, heterogeneous populations, resource constraints, and feedback in care delivery. This is why digital medicine now needs world models: not only to estimate what will happen, but to represent how actions change what can happen next -- whom to prioritize, how to allocate scarce resources, how to adapt care across subgroups, and how interventions reshape future health trajectories and population dynamics. We call such a model population health world model (PHWM): a more foundational representation of an evolving health world in which health states, individual trajectories, population health dynamics, interventions, constraints, and feedback are intrinsically coupled. We argue that addressing these gaps requires three minimal capabilities: representation learning under action-dependent observation, multi-scale intervention intelligence, and validation with explicit decision boundaries, while highlighting the importance of social, contextual, and public-health sensing data for action-oriented digital medicine.

Learning Goal-Conditioned Subgoals through Frozen Hierarchical World Models 2026-10-30

Hierarchical world models plan over long horizons by passing predicted states from a coarse model to a lower-level controller. Choosing these states can be expensive: the upper-level planner repeatedly differentiates through candidate macro-action trajectories. We replace this search with a lightweight goal-conditioned network trained through the frozen high-level predictor. The network supplies a conditioning vector, while the predictor generates the next latent waypoint. Training uses hindsight goals and state-prediction losses on offline trajectories, without search-solution targets or macro-action labels. Inference retains an autoregressive high-level rollout, but requires no high-level candidate optimization. We evaluate this approach on the shared-latent HWM checkpoints distributed with H-JEPA, using three backbone seeds and three head initializations per backbone. With a common evaluation protocol, mean success is 70.4% on Cube, 48.7% on Push-T, 100.0% on FourRoom, and 71.1% on Visual AntMaze. The method improves over the corresponding HWM search baseline on three environments and has the same mean on Push-T. Dedicated measurements on an NVIDIA L40S give 1.28-4.39x end-to-end speedups for batched evaluation. Ablations identify state-adaptive conditioning as a consistent contributor, while predictor-mediated subgoals and unrestricted conditions have environment-dependent benefits.

Before We Adapt: The Zero-Shot Generalization Bottleneck in Continual World Models 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted to Continual World Models workshop @ NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Continual reinforcement learning (CRL) requires agents to operate under environmental changes while reusing prior knowledge whenever possible. We argue that model-based reinforcement learning provides a promising framework for this setting because learned world models (WMs) can, in principle, support change detection, knowledge reuse, adaptation, and acquisition of new knowledge. However, such benefits depend heavily on whether the learned model can generalize beyond the contexts in which it was trained. In this work, we propose to formulate this problem with a contextual Markov decision process in which context is decomposed into behavioral and physical components. This view organizes continual WM learning into reuse, adaptation, and acquisition of new knowledge. As a first step, we study direct reuse when behavioral rules remain unchanged. Using Dyna-Q and TD-MPC in controlled FourRooms tasks, we find that zero-shot reuse can degrade substantially under physical-context changes and that this behavior depends strongly on the observation representation used by the WM. Additional ablation studies and latent-space diagnostics suggest that learned representations do not automatically provide the invariances needed for reuse. These results identify representation generalization as an important bottleneck for reliable world-model reuse in CRL.

Dreams Are Cheaper Than Flights: Curated World-Model Data Improves Aerial Detection 2026-10-29
📝 6 pages, Corl 2026
Show abstract

Robot perception is increasingly data driven, yet field data is slow, costly, and sometimes disruptive to collect. Video world models offer a cheaper source: seeded with real frames, they extend a recorded scene into new footage with the deployment's sensor, altitude, and environment. We ask whether such synthetic data matches the utility of real field data and whether detectors trained with it perform in the field. Seeding a 7B-parameter world model with wildlife drone video, we more than double the training set with curated frames. On held-out videos from the same drones, mAP@50--95 rises 4.3\% when these frames are added to real data and 10.3\% when they replace it, and up to 33.5\% on an urban benchmark. Although an expert rejects an estimated 87.5\% of generated clips, the data costs about \$1,100 of GPU time, against more than \$3,200 of field labor alone for a less useful real training set. Synthetic data thus shifts data costs from fieldwork to compute.
