Non-penetrating World Models 2026-11-20
📝 20 pages, 13 figures. Code: https://github.com/yuhangdai427/NoPeWorld
Show abstract

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

Going with the Flow: Koopman Behavioral Models as Pseudo Planners for Visuo-Motor Dexterity 2026-11-20
📝 Accepted by CoRL 2026. Website: https://k-ubm.github.io/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Contemporary visuo-motor dexterity models often rely on expressive policy classes with diffusion and transformer backbones to achieve strong performance. However, these architectures require significant data and computational resources, and remain far from reliable, particularly for multi-fingered dexterity. Importantly, they model skills as reactive mappings and rely on fixed-horizon action chunking, creating a rigid trade-off between temporal coherence and reactivity. To address these issues, we first introduce Unified Behavioral Models (UBMs), a framework to represent dexterous skills as coupled dynamical systems that capture how visual features of the environment (visual flow) and proprioceptive states of the robot (action flow) co-evolve. As such, UBMs ensure temporal coherence by construction rather than heuristic averaging. Unlike world models that attempt to predict the impact of arbitrary robot actions on the environment, UBMs target behavioral dynamics that encode how demonstrated robot behavior is related to desired impacts on the environment. A UBM can be viewed as a pseudo planner: given an initial condition, it computes the desired robot behavior over the entire skill horizon, while simultaneously "imagining" the resulting flow of visual features. We propose Koopman-UBM as a first instantiation of UBMs in the form of a structured latent linear system. K-UBM's inherent structure enables reactivity and adaptation via automated online replanning: the model acts as its own runtime monitor, automatically triggering replanning when predicted and observed visual flow diverge beyond a threshold. Across seven simulated tasks and four real-world tasks, our approach performs comparably to state-of-the-art baselines, while offering considerably smoother executions, fast inference, efficient learning, robustness to occlusions, and automated online replanning.

What Must a Model Tell a Planner? Outcome Interfaces for Risk-Aware Planning After Robot Damage 2026-11-20
Show abstract

When a robot is damaged, it must decide which routes it can still afford, and the amount of risk it may accept can change from one mission to the next. We argue that whether a learned model helps such a robot depends on its decision interface, that is, on what the model hands to the planner. A decision policy tells the planner what to do, whereas an outcome model, a kind of world model, predicts the consequences of each route. We study simulated Unitree Go2 quadrupeds and H1 humanoids with weakened motors, added payloads, or reduced foot friction. We compare four interfaces. The first is a decision policy, and the second is the same policy trained with fixed penalties for infeasible routes. The third is an outcome model that predicts the feasibility and travel time of each route. The fourth, which we call a capability contract, is an outcome model that predicts the chance of success and the travel time of the robot on each terrain and composes them along a route. All share the same collected experience, matched tuning and risk calibration, and an uncertainty guard. Two comparatively low-risk probes let the capability contract rank hazardous terrains about as well as four new trials on every hazard. At a calibrated 5% limit on selecting empirically infeasible routes, outcome models cut declined missions from about 32% to 7-8% on Go2 and from 57% to 35-36% on H1 compared with the plain policy. Fixed penalties narrow this gap, but the best penalty changes with the risk target. Predicting outcomes lets the planner preserve missions by changing routes as risk requirements tighten.

Beyond Visual Fidelity: Comparing Categorical and Continuous World Models 2026-11-20
📝 19 pages, 12 figures, including appendices
Show abstract

Visual world models can now generate compelling interactive worlds, but visual realism does not guarantee faithful dynamics. We systematically compare how categorical and continuous prediction shape learned dynamics under both next-token and iterative whole-frame generation. These choices are often bundled in approaches such as autoregressive token models and whole-frame flow matching. We design controlled 2D and 3D environments and evaluate dynamics validity, calibration, and visual quality. In 2D with lossless visual encoding, categorical models achieve higher rollout validity with less training compute, particularly for stochastic entities. Among categorical models, greedy decoding produces the highest rollout validity, while sampling from a next-token model yields the best-calibrated stochastic predictions. Failures in continuous models, such as disappearing game characters, echo those seen in more complex environments. Here, known dynamics let us measure their impact. In 3D, categorical models also achieve stronger long-horizon performance with less training compute. Even models with lossy tokenization remain competitive on semantic accuracy and FVD, despite lower next-frame RGB accuracy. Together, these findings make a strong case for categorical prediction as a compute-efficient route to learning world dynamics.

La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling 2026-11-20
📝 31 pages, 6 figures. v2: full-RoboTwin results with scaling to 2.1B parameters, leave-one-out ablation, revised figures and text. Code: https://github.com/mt-clemente/lajepa

World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to $π_{0.5}$. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction improves the policy even when skipped at inference. Tailoring the policy's design, including its latent space, to control can thus matter more than the knowledge transferred from a pretrained generative backbone, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

Object-Centric World Models for Causality-Aware Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-20
📝 Accepted by AAAI-26. Code is available at https://github.com/nishimoto0430/STICA. v4: Fixed the mean score of STICA in Table 1 and the hyperparameters in Table 3. Updated Figure 7
Show abstract

World models have been developed to support sample-efficient deep reinforcement learning agents. However, it remains challenging for world models to accurately replicate environments that are high-dimensional, non-stationary, and composed of multiple objects with rich interactions since most world models learn holistic representations of all environmental components. By contrast, humans perceive the environment by decomposing it into discrete objects, facilitating efficient decision-making. Motivated by this insight, we propose \emph{Slot Transformer Imagination with CAusality-aware reinforcement learning} (STICA), a unified framework in which object-centric Transformers serve as the world model and causality-aware policy and value networks. STICA represents each observation as a set of object-centric tokens, together with tokens for the agent action and the resulting reward, enabling the world model to predict token-level dynamics and interactions. The policy and value networks then estimate token-level cause--effect relations and use them in the attention layers, yielding causality-guided decision-making. Experiments on object-rich benchmarks demonstrate that STICA consistently outperforms state-of-the-art agents in both sample efficiency and final performance.

Trust What Repeats: Capped World-Model Policy Gradients for Humanoid Motion Tracking 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Training a humanoid motion tracker by backpropagating imagined returns through a learned world model is fast, because the model gives a first-order policy gradient. We ask how much of that gradient holds in the simulator. On a tracker for the Unitree G1, the $0.5\%$ of imagined rollouts with the largest gradients carry $22\%$ of the summed gradient norm, yet neither a second rollout from the same state nor the simulator confirms them. When the actions are moved along the model's gradient, the simulator returns 94% of the predicted change in return where the gradient norm is below the batch median, 48% between 3 and 10 times the median, and 1% above 30 times; the part of a gradient that either check confirms stops growing at a few times the median. We therefore cap each rollout's action-gradient norm at 10 times the batch median, draw imagination starts in proportion to their predicted gradient norm so that the cap removes less, and reset episodes where the world model and critic predict the lowest return. Trained on 223 hours of motion and tested on 353 motions that no actor performed in training, the method raises the normalized return of SuperMimic, the baseline it builds on, from 0.953 to 0.967 and the lowest $1\%$ of test clips from 0.65 to 0.76, halves the test clips that fall persistently, from 15--18 to 7--10, and reaches the baseline's final return in 40% of the baseline's iterations and half its training time. On LAFAN1, trained and evaluated on the same clips as prior work, it reaches 0.935 against 0.906.

Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Subchunk Rollouts in Latent World Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can improve pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies through imagined experiences. However, existing VLA-MBRL methods primarily rely on large-scale image or video generation world models, incurring large computational overhead. Latent world models offer a more efficient alternative but face two challenges: compounding errors during recursive prediction and incompatibility with VLAs' visual input interfaces. In this work, we propose LS-MBPO, a lightweight residual MBRL framework that leverages latent world models to improve upon pretrained VLAs. LS-MBPO introduces two key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Forcing, which combines dense causal supervision with autoregressive feedback to mitigate compounding prediction errors; and (2) Latent Subchunk Rollouts, which leverage action chunks from a frozen VLA policy and a latent residual policy for successive subchunk refinement, bridging the gap between latent imagination and VLA policy execution. We further introduce a Progress-Conditioned Branch-Start Curriculum that progressively shifts rollout starting points from later to earlier task stages, enabling effective policy learning from short imagined rollouts. Extensive experiments on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and a real-world ALOHA robot show that LS-MBPO achieves superior task performance and training efficiency compared to existing methods.

SOAR: Training-Free Rank-Consistent Rollout Compression for Latent World Model Planning 2026-11-20
📝 16 pages, including supplementary material. Anqi Li and Kai Liu contributed equally
Show abstract

Latent world models plan by rolling out hundreds of candidate action sequences through a transformer predictor, so a single decision can take tens of seconds. Existing accelerations prune spatial tokens as in vision-language models, yet a sampling-based planner uses predicted costs only to rank candidates, so a rollout can be compressed freely as long as the ranking survives. We propose SOAR, a training-free method that adapts rollout compression to each planning decision. At every decision, SOAR probes a few candidates under several token budgets and keeps the smallest budget that preserves their ranking, so it compresses as far as the current decision allows, without task labels. A closed-loop study of more than 80,000 episodes fixes the rest of the rollout: the first step, which alone reads a real observation, stays exact, and the context frame is cached rather than recomputed. On seven navigation and manipulation tasks with frozen checkpoints, SOAR matches dense-level planning success at 2.3x speedup and retains 98% at 2.5x. Our code is available at https://github.com/starrywiki/SOAR.

WAM-Sim0.1: World Anchored Memory Meets Online RL for Consistent and Realistic Driving Simulation 2026-11-20

Autoregressive driving world models systematically suffer from structural drift, identity drift, and quality decay over long rollout horizons due to transient conditioning signals that lack persistent state. We present WAM-Sim, a driving world model anchored by a persistent hierarchical memory and optimized via online flow-matching RL. WAM-Sim structures persistent context across three temporal and semantic scales: pose-aligned structural tokens encoding persistent road topology, read-only scene tokens supplying geolocated background appearance, and write-once instance tokens preserving individual traffic participant identities across occlusions and chunk boundaries. To restore visual sharpness and physical plausibility degraded by multi-scale conditioning, we introduce an online post-training framework that optimizes self-generated rollouts via flow-matching RL guided by hierarchy-aligned visual rewards and a vision-language model physical judge. On the nuScenes benchmark, WAM-Sim achieves a superior generation fidelity (FID=3.47, FVD=25.72) and strong downstream perception performance (24.16 mAP, 71.23 road mIoU). On minute-scale rollouts of self-collected data, WAM-Sim substantially mitigates structural and identity drift, preserving high scene similarity and subject identity coherence across extended horizons. GameScrub: Learning HUD Removal from Real Gameplay with Recovered Targets 2026-11-20
📝 24 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

Gameplay videos are a rich source of experience for learning virtual-world dynamics, yet their frames contain heads-up displays (HUDs) that follow interface rules rather than scene dynamics. Removing HUDs would make this footage directly usable for learning world dynamics. However, training a removal model requires paired clips with and without HUDs, whereas real recordings contain only HUD-overlaid footage. Existing work synthesizes pairs by compositing HUD graphics onto HUD-free footage, requiring a separately curated clean corpus and producing HUDs that only approximate real ones. Models trained on such pairs leave 40 to 50% of real HUD elements untouched. We instead recover pseudo-clean targets directly from real recordings by inpainting HUD regions with a pretrained video diffusion model. Preventing HUD traces from leaking into recovered targets is challenging because latent video autoencoders mix information across mask boundaries. Our training-free recovery pipeline, GameScrub-Data, pre-fills HUD regions with flow-guided background estimates before encoding, excludes HUDs from scene conditioning, and confines restoration to masked regions through pixel-space compositing. The resulting pairs train GameScrub, a video diffusion removal model that handles diverse HUDs without inference-time masks or clean reference images. On the in-the-wild Game2World benchmark, GameScrub achieves an artifact-adjusted removal score of 71.95, compared with 57.86 for the strongest reference-free GameCleaner configuration, and removes 90.06% of annotated HUD elements versus 59.07%. These results demonstrate that real recordings with recovered targets can support effective HUD removal, advancing HUD-free gameplay data for world models and controllable video generation.

Embodied world models should not only imagine plausible futures, but also perceive the geometric, semantic, and motion structure of the evolving world. We present GenSight, a unified embodied world model that brings future video generation and dense perception into a single pretrained video diffusion transformer. GenSight uses a unified spatiotemporal representation for video, depth, embodiment segmentation, and first-frame-relative 2D flow. Built on a shared DiT backbone, GenSight uses lightweight task modulation to predict all modalities within the same architecture. Beyond Future Views: Exploring Future-State World Models for Vision-Language Navigation 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Vision-Language Navigation(VLN) requires an embodied agent to follow language instructions through unseen 3D environments, where the target spaces often lie beyond the current observation. Navigation world models address this challenge by predicting future views either as pixels or latent representations, providing valuable look-ahead information for action decision. Both paradigms define the future primarily through what the agent may observe, raising a fundamental question: is predicting future views the ultimate form to represent the future for VLN? Cognitive studies show that the human hippocampus prospectively represents both future goals and intervening locations along planned routes. Inspired by this predictive-map view, we organize the navigation-relevant future around goal-directed spatial states and route structure. We instantiate this perspective with two complementary abstractions: a short-horizon waypoint that specifies the next local state to reach, and a mid-horizon corridor that captures the spatial support of the upcoming route. Based on this design, we propose FS-Nav, a future-state world modeling framework that predicts waypoint-corridor states from spatial memory and language instructions, and communicates them to a VLM navigator through top-down and first-person interfaces. Experiments on standard VLN benchmarks demonstrate that FS-Nav achieves state-of-the-art performance. This highlights navigation-relevant future-state modeling as a promising direction for representing the future in VLN.

Learning Interaction Dynamics via Motion-Region Guidance for Robotic Video World Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Action-conditioned video world models imagine future observations under candidate robot actions, providing a promising visual tool for robot policy evaluation and interaction simulation. However, existing video world models often optimize global reconstruction errors instead of emphasizing interaction-relevant regions. As a result, they may preserve overall scene appearance but overlook local interaction details, leading to physically implausible outcomes such as hallucinated contact effects and inconsistent robot-object interactions. We introduce MoReWorld, a unified motion-region-guided framework that uses a shared motion region, the part of the environment that moves in response to the robot's actions, to focus dynamics learning and video generation on interaction-relevant regions. Testing World Models on Actions They Have Never Seen: Held-Out Plans, Forecast Accuracy and Price Rules in Retail 2026-11-20
📝 12 pages, 3 figures, 5 tables. Companion to arXiv:2611.20811
Show abstract

A world model is trusted to answer "what if": how its world responds to an action it has never seen. We give a test of that trust for any action-conditioned world model: actions held out of training by design and scored against a known answer; whether forecast accuracy predicts accuracy on those actions; and the same structure given to simpler models. We run it in retail, where the action is a price and promotion plan, across 2,853 fitted models. First, forecast accuracy is a fair guide between kinds of model (rank correlation with error on new plans 0.32 to 0.66) and a poor guide between versions of one world model: where merchants plan into high-demand weeks the link reverses, down to -0.69. Second, training the world model to learn the effect of actions (JEPA pretraining, a latent consistency check, adaptation at the plan date) leaves its error on new plans unchanged. Third, in generated worlds the recorded regular price and three price rules improve it. Given the same rules, a log-linear model is best when its curve can match the truth; in a world whose curve lies outside the rules, the world model with the rules is ahead of the log-linear model with the rules in 36 of 40 worlds, and does as well without its recurrent cell. On real data (Breakfast at the Frat, The Complete Journey and M5) the rules lower the world model's error on pure price cuts deeper than any it saw (0.63 to 0.56 on the Frat; 0.45 to 0.44, within chance, on The Complete Journey), but not on lasting price moves. Fourth, a small share of random test plans helps every model a little. In a generated dosing world, a stress test in a second domain, the first finding repeats, and the built-up drug level lowers the world model's error (2.86 to 1.91) though it still predicts only 0.12 to 0.32 of the true gain. The test carries to any domain where a world model is asked about actions outside its history.

PACE-WM: Parallel World Modeling with Action History for Visual Planning 2026-11-20
Show abstract

JEPA-style world models support visual planning by predicting future representations conditioned on future actions. However, similar observations can conceal different motion states, making temporal context essential for interpreting future action effects. We propose PACE-WM, a parallel world model that uses executed-action history to contextualize future prediction and planning. Specifically, PACE-WM maintains a recurrent memory of executed actions and uses this memory to condition a causal Transformer. PACE-WM predicts multiple future representations in parallel without recursive predicted-state feedback within each prediction window. PhysForce: Physics-aware Force Control for Interactive Video World Model 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Interactive video world models aim to predict how visual environments evolve in response to user interventions, including physically consistent state changes induced by applied forces. Existing methods often represent forces as motion trajectories, which may introduce inappropriate motion priors, causing the model to over-rely on encoded displacement patterns. More fundamentally, the model's internal representations do not reliably localize force-induced state changes, making it difficult to associate applied forces with their resulting physical responses. Also, limited force-annotated data make it difficult to produce physically consistent and temporally stable predictions in unseen scenarios. To address these limitations, we propose PhysForce, a two-stage framework that integrates supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning through three complementary components. During supervised fine-tuning, (1) the Latent Force Intervention Encoder maps force location, direction, and magnitude into the video latent space without exposing future motion; and (2) the Physics Attribution Network associates force interventions with localized changes in the latent state, strengthening the learning of physical responses to applied forces. During reinforcement learning, (3) Physics-Aware Reinforcement Learning uses self-supervised rewards on out-of-distribution videos to improve physical consistency and generalization. Extensive experiments show that PhysForce improves force responsiveness, physical consistency, and generalization, demonstrating its effectiveness for interactive video world models.

Where Structure Enters a World Model: From Predictive Fit to Physical Rollout 2026-11-20
📝 36 pages, 8 figures, 46 tables (including appendices)
Show abstract

Predictive world models learn to forecast observations, but accurate short-term predictions do not necessarily produce physically coherent behavior over longer horizons. We investigate how incorporating physical structure can strengthen world models while retaining their underlying predictive objectives. Across token-based models of planetary motion and JEPA-style visual models of mechanical systems, we study conserved-quantity supervision, symmetry-aware representations, temporal consistency, and learned structured dynamics. In planetary motion, angular-momentum supervision improves autonomous next-token forecasting, while the benefits of temporal regularization depend on the accompanying physical objective and representation. In visual models, generating rollouts through supervised energy-based dynamics heads produces more faithful trajectories and phase-space evolution. Our study supports physical structure as a practical means of improving predictive world models and provides a unified framework for understanding its effects on representations, learned transitions, and autonomous rollouts. The central lesson is that effective physical inductive biases should help models both represent physical regularities and use them to generate future states.

A Plan or an Adversarial Example? A Round-Trip Check for Latent World Models 2026-11-20
📝 12 pages, 5 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

If we optimize an image until a classifier is confident it shows a cat, we may get an adversarial example, not a cat. Planning with a latent world model is a similar search for actions whose predicted future is closest to the goal. Is the result a plan, or an adversarial example of the world model? We show that it can be either. On the released DINO-WM and LeWM, more iterations of the cross-entropy method (CEM) lower the predicted cost but lose up to half of the problems CEM had already solved. The model's own predictions do not reveal these failures. We propose a simple test that executes the plan after every iteration and counts the problems a planner solves and then loses. Unlike an image, a plan comes with its actions, which our round-trip check reads back from the predicted states with a small network, penalizing mismatches.
