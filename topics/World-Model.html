RecallWorld: Episodic Memory for Persistent Embodied Video World Models 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Video world models generate plausible novel views but often lack persistent memory, failing to preserve a place's geometry and appearance when an agent revisits a scene. A central challenge is how previous views should be effectively used for creating memory. Reconstructing them independently underutilizes geometric constraints between nearby views, while repeatedly reconstructing a growing history can propagate later prediction errors into earlier geometry. We introduce RecallWorld, a persistent embodied video world model that jointly reconstructs short multi-view episodes into Scenelets while keeping geometry independent across episodes. Starting from a single source image, camera-aware ray lifting places perspective and native fisheye images in a common world frame, retaining wide-field content for perspective generation. Each generated segment extends the Scenelet memory and conditions subsequent generation. Coverage-based retrieval selects complementary Scenelets from the full history, while target-view visibility is resolved geometrically before appearance fusion, providing consistent memory conditioning for guiding subsequent generation. Across four datasets, RecallWorld improves revisit PSNR over the strongest evaluated baseline by 2.6 dB on partial revisits and 3.9 dB on full cycles, demonstrating substantially stronger preservation of scene identity across exploration and return.

Persuasion from Within: Model-Based Insider Attacks in Multi-Agent LLM Debate and Consensus 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems for collective decision-making, such as multi-agent debate and consensus games, in which agents exchange messages and update their answers over multiple rounds and a resolution mechanism aggregates them into a final outcome. Most multi-agent LLM frameworks assume that every participant shares the system objective. In practice, however, a malicious agent can join as a legitimate member of the group and persuade the other agents toward a hidden adversarial goal. We formalize such attacks as a sequential decision-making problem in which the insider seeks to degrade the utility of the collective outcome. We propose a model-based insider attack that learns to persuade a group of agents: the insider first learns a world model of the benign agents from offline trajectories and then uses it both to train a persuasion policy and to plan its messages. In two case studies involving a numerical consensus game and a multi-agent debate, the learned insider is the most effective and the stealthiest attack we evaluate, outperforming both prompt-based and trained LLM persuaders from prior work. The attack also transfers to other target models, larger groups, and unseen datasets, and remains effective under existing Byzantine-resilience defenses. The learned insider also enables a new defense: adversarially training benign agents on the attack data largely removes both our attack and the strongest prior persuasion attack. These results show that combining world models with reinforcement learning and planning is an effective route to both attacking and hardening language-based multi-agent systems.

WorldCrafter: Consistent Video World Model with Implicit 3D-aware Memory 2026-11-21
📝 Project webpage: https://drexubery.github.io/WorldCrafter
Show abstract

Video world models enable interactive exploration of dynamic environments, yet struggle to respect prior observations over long horizons and across viewpoints. We present WorldCrafter, a video world model that learns a camera-queryable implicit 3D-aware memory for this purpose. The key insight is to let the requested viewpoint shape how multi-view evidence is compressed into the video generator's limited token budget. Trained jointly with the video generator, a memory encoder and pose-conditioned readout module integrate historical observations into a fixed set of target view-specific tokens before denoising, without explicit depth-based correspondences. By combining this memory with recent temporal context and few-step distillation, WorldCrafter enables streaming scene exploration from a single input image or text prompt. Experiments across static and dynamic scenes show substantial gains in long-horizon consistency and camera-control accuracy while preserving visual quality during minute-scale exploration.

HaM-World: Soft-Hamiltonian World Models with Selective Memory for Planning 2026-11-21
📝 27 pages including references and technical appendix. Accepted as a poster at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/HaoyunT/HaM_World
Show abstract

World models enable model-based planning through learned latent dynamics, but imagined rollouts become unstable as the planning horizon grows or the dynamics distribution shifts: an unconstrained predictor feeds each predicted state back as input, so the latent distribution contracts until distinct states merge. We argue that this drift and contraction reflect two missing structures in planner-facing latents: a history-conditioned predictive state summarizing past observations and actions, and geometric organization that separates configuration, momentum, and task semantics. We propose HaM-World, a structured world model that decomposes the latent state into a canonical-like (q,p) subspace and a context subspace c, while using Mamba selective state-space memory as the history-conditioned input to the same latent dynamics. The (q,p) subspace evolves through an energy-derived Hamiltonian vector field plus learnable residual/control dynamics, while c captures semantic, dissipative, and non-conservative factors. The planner uses this single latent state for prediction, reward/value estimation, imagined rollouts, and CEM search. On six DeepMind Control Suite tasks, HaM-World ranks first on four and second on the other two, with the highest six-task Avg. AUC (91.9, +10.3% over TD-MPC2). Within the horizons the planner unrolls, it reduces imagined-rollout error to 45% of TD-MPC2's and wins 11/12 k in {3,5,7} MSE cells. Under 12 OOD perturbations spanning dynamics shifts, action delay, and observation masking it attains the highest return in every condition (+10.2% on Finger Spin, +13.6% on Reacher Easy). Ablations show that both selective memory and the Soft-Hamiltonian prior contribute to the gains. Mechanism diagnostics support the intended design, showing bounded action-free energy drift, structured energy variation under policy rollouts, and coherent control-induced energy transfer.

Visual Foresight for Agentic Reasoning Before Action in Urban Navigation 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Urban navigation requires embodied agents to pursue long-horizon goals through local decisions based on egocentric observations. However, existing agentic navigation methods often struggle to translate distant goals into coherent local decisions. Their reliance on linguistic reasoning over transient observations or limited history constrains anticipation of action consequences, despite the importance of such foresight for navigating long and complex urban routes. To bridge this gap, we propose PreAct-Nav, a training-free agentic navigation framework that equips frozen policies with visual foresight for reasoning before action. Our central idea is to use predicted future views to assess proposed actions in the context of persistent medium-horizon subgoals and observed experience. At its core, a navigation memory module maintains the active subgoal and relevant experience across decisions, translating distant goals into actionable intermediate objectives. To enable visual foresight, a predictive world sandbox uses an action-conditioned world model (AC-WM) to generate future views of proposed movements, providing visual cues for reasoning about their consequences beyond the current observation. A vision-language model (VLM) reasoner interprets these predictions using the subgoal and accumulated experience to retain or revise actions. After execution, real observations are used to assess outcomes, correct inconsistent assumptions, and update memory to continue or reformulate the subgoal. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that the proposed PreAct-Nav improves action selection through memory updates and visual prediction, with more pronounced gains on longer routes and routes with more turns.

WorldWeave: Rethinking Embodied Navigation through Cross-Task World Modeling 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Mobile embodied agents continuously alter their observations through their own motion, while external dynamics further shape future interactions. Different mobile embodied tasks therefore capture complementary patterns of state evolution, yet jointly learning world dynamics across such interactions remains less explored. We investigate this problem through Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT), which respectively involve navigation toward a stationary goal and interaction with a moving target. We propose WorldWeave, a cross-task world model that couples shared predictive dynamics with task-specific adaptation. Instead of conditioning state transitions on heterogeneous physical actions, WorldWeave learns latent actions from observed state changes via inverse-forward dynamics, establishing a shared transition representation across tasks. The resulting future-state predictions are adaptively integrated into task-specific representations for downstream decision making. Experiments across multiple VLN and EVT benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over specialized baselines.

PhysPPF: Learning Robotic Manipulation with Past, Present, and Future Physical Representations 2026-11-21
📝 11 pages, 5 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have advanced robotic manipulation, yet complex tasks requiring both precise 3D contact and long-horizon coordination remain challenging. Most existing studies enhance spatial perception or temporal reasoning in VLAs separately. Spatial methods primarily inject geometric or affordance priors into current-frame perception without tracking multi-step transitions, while history-aware and world-model VLAs typically capture past context or future dynamics in 2D feature or pixel spaces, either lacking explicit 3D contact cues or incurring high generation overhead. To address this, we propose PhysPPF, a VLA framework that performs unified spatiotemporal modeling in a compact physical token space. Spatially, WWH (What-Where-How) distills pretrained perception teachers exclusively during training into compact token groups capturing what 3D structures occupy the scene, where to interact, and how the gripper should approach, without test-time 3D encoders. Temporally, operating directly in this WWH space without pixel reconstruction, PPF (Past-Present-Future) consolidates executed states into a bounded past physical memory and jointly infers present and future physical tokens via an asymmetric world model. Parallel gated branches then inject these past, present, and future representations into a flow-matching action expert to guide manipulation without cross-temporal interference. Extensive experiments on SimplerEnv, CALVIN, and LIBERO show that PhysPPF consistently outperforms existing spatial, history-aware, and world-model VLAs with modest latency and parameter overhead, improving over the StarVLA baseline by +17.2 points on SimplerEnv, +29.3 points on CALVIN, and +3.5 points on LIBERO.

AlpaSafe: Risk-Aware Trajectory Selection for Vision-Language-Action Models in Autonomous Driving 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Reasoning-based driving vision-language-action (VLA) models propose multiple candidate trajectories at each decision, yet only one is executed. Standard evaluation scores the best candidate, assuming an oracle selector that deployment lacks, and leaves the risk of the executed candidate unexamined. Existing online evaluators typically close this gap with a purpose-trained scene or world model, often rolling out each candidate through it at inference time. We introduce AlpaSafe, a risk-aware trajectory selector that reuses the scene representation a frozen driving VLA computes while proposing its candidates. AlpaSafe reads the last hidden token of the VLA's prompt prefill. A lightweight head, trained on the closed-loop simulation outcomes of the candidates, fuses this token with each candidate trajectory and predicts a relative score and collision/offroad logits. A fixed rule removes candidates dominated on the two risk logits and selects the highest-scoring candidate among those remaining, adding neither rollouts nor VLA training. Experiments on 525 AlpaSim scenes show that AlpaSafe outperforms Alpamayo's default first-sample rule and uniform random selection in selected regret and top-1 agreement, and reduces the collision-at-fault or offroad rate from 6.41% and 6.10% to 4.93%. The selector has 1.7M parameters and takes 0.75 ms per decision.

State of Mind: Learning and Evaluating World Models of Naturalistic Brain Dynamics 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Understanding naturalistic brain activity requires going beyond the stimulus alone, as external inputs continuously interact with an individual's evolving brain state. A world model captures such dynamics in a latent state by inferring it from neural observations, predicting stimulus-driven evolution, and updating with new observations. However, existing brain world models fail to maintain a persistent latent state over time, and standard metrics of neural prediction accuracy cannot tell whether a model tracks an individual's brain state or relies solely on the stimulus. To directly test state inference, prediction, and updating, we introduce BrainStateBench, which evaluates a model's latent state through a frozen linear probe on whole-cortex, movie-driven fMRI. Its State task tests whether a model captures trial-to-trial variability in an individual's responses to repeated stimuli, while Forecast and Updating evaluate future activity predictions without and with new observations, respectively. To track individual brain state across time, we propose Spectator, a linear latent world model trained with a joint-embedding predictive objective. Spectator encodes short- and long-horizon observation histories into a two-timescale latent state and predicts state transitions linearly from the current state and stimulus. Stimulus-only encoders reach 0.320 in fMRI prediction correlation without observing the brain (0% on State), while prior deep brain world models reach 0.239 yet score at most 0.4% on State. In contrast, Spectator achieves 0.349 correlation alongside 5.5% on State, 16.0% on Forecast, and 20.3% on Updating, outperforming all baselines while forecasting over 20x faster than deep forecasting models.

The Social World Model Evaluation Gap 2026-11-21
📝 Accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Foundation Models for Temporal Systems: From Forecasting to World Modeling (FMTS). 4 pages of main text plus references and appendix. Code and data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22773066
Show abstract

A negotiation assistant can reach a good deal without accurately predicting the other person's response. We ask whether evaluations of social world models test that prediction directly. We audit 113 works published from 1999 to 2026. 9 primary evaluations measure a real person's response to an action selected by the system or experimenter. Only 2 also compare an explicit response forecast with that response. Their predictors use information available when the action is chosen, and the evaluated responses were excluded from fitting; both randomize the intervention. A separate full-text check of 25 language-model works finds no experiment meeting these conditions. The other evaluations test current-state inference, task success, or prediction from recorded actions. We propose a 1-step negotiation experiment that separates these questions. The findings describe the audited sample, not the prevalence of this evaluation gap across the field.

Knowing What You Carry: Scalable Physics-Anchored World Models for Robotic Planning with Liquid Payloads 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Payload-carrying robots, such as delivery robots and autonomous trucks, must choose motions that keep them and their payload safe, so their planners predict the outcome of each candidate motion. Modern planners compare thousands of candidates per step on a GPU, using world models such as batched simulators or learned predictors. Most of them assume a rigid payload, but a liquid payload keeps moving after a maneuver, and its state is rarely measured. Near a safety limit, small prediction errors can make an unsafe plan look safe. Coupled fluid simulations are accurate but too costly for every candidate, while low-order slosh models are cheap but accurate only away from resonance. We study this trade-off on a ground vehicle with a partly filled tank near its rollover limit. First, we build PLiMB (Parallel Liquid-MultiBody), a GPU-parallel slosh simulator that, to our knowledge, couples FLIP liquid two-way with many multibody vehicles for the first time. It provides training data and a high-fidelity reference. Second, we propose a scalable physics-anchored world model that combines a modal-slosh and roll prior, an estimate of the hidden liquid state from the vehicle's own motion history (no sensor in the tank), and a learned correction. Without simulating the fluid, it scores thousands of candidate plans in one batched pass within a 20 Hz control tick on one GPU. We judge each world model by the plans it leads a planner to choose. Near the rollover limit, planners that treat the liquid as a rigid payload or use published low-order slosh models repeatedly pick unsafe plans when a safe one exists. Ours never does in our standard tank and makes about half as many in a wide tank whose sloshing mode lies in the maneuver band. There, the liquid state left by earlier driving can change the best plan, and reading it from the vehicle's history halves our model's near-limit prediction error.

CausalNTP: A World-Model Recipe for Causal Next-Token Prediction under Noisy Training-data 2026-11-20
Show abstract

This paper shows that transformer networks trained with next-token prediction (NTP) and gradient descent do not learn to make predictions using only causally-relevant tokens in the left context, irrespective of the causal ordering of tokens during training, and identifies the learning deficiencies responsible. We introduce a causal world-model setup, for which, we characterize the Bayes-optimal predictor under the causal view of the data. We then prove that, a function that distills its predictions to non-causal views of data, when realized under Occam's Razor, recovers the true causal predictor. Based on this analysis we propose CausalNTP, a practical training algorithm that approximates this theoretical recipe and trains transformers to depend only the causally relevant tokens in left-context. Empirically in the world-model setup, CausalNTP achieves out-of-distribution generalization that vanilla NTP cannot due to its theoretical limits, regardless of data and model scale. We show similar gains and behavior in the natural language question-answering setting as a proof-of-concept.

Two Actions from One State: Fork Futures Teach JEPA World Models to Compare 2026-11-20
Show abstract

JEPA world models are trained to predict logged transitions but used to compare counterfactual actions from one state. The logged behavior data show one action per state, and under a local linear approximation we prove the action response is unidentified along any direction the policy never varies within a state. We therefore supplement behavior data with measured outcomes of alternative actions from the same state. Our fork futures execute two actions from one restored state, and their outcome difference cancels the state term, identifying the action response on the sampled span. The resulting model, Fork-JEPA, matches predicted to measured differences, anchors the logged successor with behavior prediction, and adds a lookback head that keeps the predecessor recoverable, all at training time, leaving planning unchanged. On three visual control tasks, behavior-only training leaves the predicted action effect no better than a constant guess. Fork-JEPA makes it readable and raises planning success by up to 14\% over LeWM, its base model. Where the action response changes across hidden zones, it nearly doubles cross-zone success, and 1,024 fork pairs beat 1.2M extra behavior steps. Our project is available at https://fork-jepa.github.io/.

Toward Adversarially Robust World Models 2026-11-20
📝 10 pages
Show abstract

Latent dynamic world models have proven to be highly versatile tools for embodied artificial intelligence systems, enabling online action planning, safe policy evaluation, and scalable policy learning. However, this versatility makes world models a vulnerable and centralized point of failure as a compromised model can impact planning, evaluation, and training at once. Therefore it is critical that the security and robustness properties of world models are properly understood before they are universally deployed in embodied AI systems. In this work we take a unifying approach towards this understanding, developing two novel attacks against world models, named state hallucination and dynamic shift attacks, aimed directly at impacting downstream systems, and proposing a new theoretical lens through which to study the robustness of latent dynamic world models. Through our analysis we identify robust latent space encoders as the key component of robust world models. We empirically validate this finding on four separate latent dynamic world model families, studying Dino World Model, Le World Model, r2Dreamer, and Cosmos 3, demonstrating significant improvements in robustness through adversarial encoder model training.

Learning a Food World Model That Predicts the Next State from Production Data 2026-11-20
📝 12 pages, 5 figures, 9 tables: a 4-page paper with appendices. This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication
Show abstract

Food assembly robots pick ingredients from a pan, and every pick must deliver a target weight. We learn a world model of food picking from 61,773 production picks, labeled automatically by a camera and a scale (self-supervised learning). From the pre-pick heightmap of the pan on a 2.5 mm grid, the commanded pick action, the food and the utensil, the model predicts the post-pick heightmap and the picked mass with uncertainty. We evaluate the mass prediction on 12,294 held-out picks of nine foods. The production controller keeps each pick near its commanded target. The model's root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 10.93 g is 9% lower than that of the commanded target or of a per-food mean. This RMSE is 24% lower than that of the controller's internal mass estimate. Gradient-boosted trees on 20 hand-crafted features have a 0.2 g lower RMSE, but they predict only the mass, not the next state that planning and policy learning need. Using the predicted uncertainty, we decline the most uncertain picks and answer 89.4% of picks with an RMSE of 10.28 g. The prediction barely responds to the commanded volume and depth, because they vary little in closed-loop production. The model also fails without warning at target weights not seen in training. Future directions include action-primitive conditioning and a more general model across foods and utensils. We give recommendations for learning world models from production data.

Scalable Beckmann Maps Improve One-step Generation 2026-11-20
📝 40 pages, 15 figures, 12 tables. Code and checkpoints: https://tullebulle.github.io/projects/sbm/
Show abstract

Beckmann Transport Models (BTM) learn one-step generative maps end-to-end from data, without a teacher, a distillation stage, or pretrained features. We introduce Scalable Beckmann Maps (SBM), which bring to BTM the two ingredients behind the quality of modern multistep generators: classifier-free guidance (CFG) and a training recipe that scales, while keeping sampling at a single network evaluation. CFG combines conditional and unconditional velocity or score predictions, which a Beckmann map does not have. Our guidance rests on one observation: the BTM current is a positive multiple of an autonomous denoising field, to which CFG applies as it does to a velocity. With exact denoisers this gives guided BTM. In practice we substitute the map's own conditional and null predictions for the denoisers, which requires no additional network and introduces a bias that we characterize. We show that the guided update is consistent with a guided conservation equation, and that guidance can move the boundaries between basins, sending more noise to the modes a condition favors. Trained from scratch in the latent of a representation autoencoder, SBM reaches a one-step FID of 1.6 on ImageNet 256x256 without a teacher or a feature loss, outperforming the Improved-MeanFlow baseline with 3.2x fewer FLOPs. In text-to-image generation it reaches a GenEval score of 0.86 with one network evaluation. As a world model, where we train without guidance, the recipe alone outperforms DriftWorld on all five metrics of two robot-manipulation benchmarks, with 1.9-2.8x less training compute and no pretrained auxiliary feature encoder.

Non-penetrating World Models 2026-11-20
📝 20 pages, 13 figures. Code: https://github.com/yuhangdai427/NoPeWorld
Show abstract

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

Going with the Flow: Koopman Behavioral Models as Pseudo Planners for Visuo-Motor Dexterity 2026-11-20
📝 Accepted by CoRL 2026. Website: https://k-ubm.github.io/
Show abstract

Contemporary visuo-motor dexterity models often rely on expressive policy classes with diffusion and transformer backbones to achieve strong performance. However, these architectures require significant data and computational resources, and remain far from reliable, particularly for multi-fingered dexterity. Importantly, they model skills as reactive mappings and rely on fixed-horizon action chunking, creating a rigid trade-off between temporal coherence and reactivity. To address these issues, we first introduce Unified Behavioral Models (UBMs), a framework to represent dexterous skills as coupled dynamical systems that capture how visual features of the environment (visual flow) and proprioceptive states of the robot (action flow) co-evolve. As such, UBMs ensure temporal coherence by construction rather than heuristic averaging. Unlike world models that attempt to predict the impact of arbitrary robot actions on the environment, UBMs target behavioral dynamics that encode how demonstrated robot behavior is related to desired impacts on the environment. A UBM can be viewed as a pseudo planner: given an initial condition, it computes the desired robot behavior over the entire skill horizon, while simultaneously "imagining" the resulting flow of visual features. We propose Koopman-UBM as a first instantiation of UBMs in the form of a structured latent linear system. K-UBM's inherent structure enables reactivity and adaptation via automated online replanning: the model acts as its own runtime monitor, automatically triggering replanning when predicted and observed visual flow diverge beyond a threshold. Across seven simulated tasks and four real-world tasks, our approach performs comparably to state-of-the-art baselines, while offering considerably smoother executions, fast inference, efficient learning, robustness to occlusions, and automated online replanning.

What Must a Model Tell a Planner? Outcome Interfaces for Risk-Aware Planning After Robot Damage 2026-11-20
Show abstract

When a robot is damaged, it must decide which routes it can still afford, and the amount of risk it may accept can change from one mission to the next. We argue that whether a learned model helps such a robot depends on its decision interface, that is, on what the model hands to the planner. A decision policy tells the planner what to do, whereas an outcome model, a kind of world model, predicts the consequences of each route. We study simulated Unitree Go2 quadrupeds and H1 humanoids with weakened motors, added payloads, or reduced foot friction. We compare four interfaces. The first is a decision policy, and the second is the same policy trained with fixed penalties for infeasible routes. The third is an outcome model that predicts the feasibility and travel time of each route. The fourth, which we call a capability contract, is an outcome model that predicts the chance of success and the travel time of the robot on each terrain and composes them along a route. All share the same collected experience, matched tuning and risk calibration, and an uncertainty guard. Two comparatively low-risk probes let the capability contract rank hazardous terrains about as well as four new trials on every hazard. At a calibrated 5% limit on selecting empirically infeasible routes, outcome models cut declined missions from about 32% to 7-8% on Go2 and from 57% to 35-36% on H1 compared with the plain policy. Fixed penalties narrow this gap, but the best penalty changes with the risk target. Predicting outcomes lets the planner preserve missions by changing routes as risk requirements tighten.

Beyond Visual Fidelity: Comparing Categorical and Continuous World Models 2026-11-20
📝 19 pages, 12 figures, including appendices
Show abstract

Visual world models can now generate compelling interactive worlds, but visual realism does not guarantee faithful dynamics. We systematically compare how categorical and continuous prediction shape learned dynamics under both next-token and iterative whole-frame generation. These choices are often bundled in approaches such as autoregressive token models and whole-frame flow matching. We design controlled 2D and 3D environments and evaluate dynamics validity, calibration, and visual quality. In 2D with lossless visual encoding, categorical models achieve higher rollout validity with less training compute, particularly for stochastic entities. Among categorical models, greedy decoding produces the highest rollout validity, while sampling from a next-token model yields the best-calibrated stochastic predictions. Failures in continuous models, such as disappearing game characters, echo those seen in more complex environments. Here, known dynamics let us measure their impact. In 3D, categorical models also achieve stronger long-horizon performance with less training compute. Even models with lossy tokenization remain competitive on semantic accuracy and FVD, despite lower next-frame RGB accuracy. Together, these findings make a strong case for categorical prediction as a compute-efficient route to learning world dynamics.

La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling 2026-11-20
📝 31 pages, 6 figures. v2: full-RoboTwin results with scaling to 2.1B parameters, leave-one-out ablation, revised figures and text. Code: https://github.com/mt-clemente/lajepa
Show abstract

World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B.
