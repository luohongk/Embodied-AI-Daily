Failure Detection for Surgical Robot Imitation Policies via Flow-Matching World Modeling 2026-11-25
📝 8 pages, 4 figures. Revised following peer review. Accepted for publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)
Show abstract

Imitation learning has shown increasing promise for autonomous robotic surgery, yet safe deployment remains challenging due to the safety-critical nature of surgical tasks and the complexity and variability of surgical environments. Failure detection is therefore an essential safeguard, but its development remains difficult due to the challenges of scarce failure data, highly variable manipulation dynamics, and the need to balance missed detections against disruptive false alarms. To address these challenges, we introduce FoMo-FD (Flow-Matching World Model for Failure Detection), a failure detection method that learns nominal short-horizon visual dynamics with an action-conditioned flow-matching world model. FoMo-FD scores the inverse-transport nonconformity of observed endpoint latents, enabling window-level detection of deviations from learned nominal dynamics without requiring failure demonstrations. Detection thresholds are obtained by conformal calibration on successful policy rollouts, yielding task-specific alarms without assuming future failure types. We evaluate FoMo-FD on four surgically relevant manipulation tasks with twenty failure modes across simulation and real-robot experiments using the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK). In the wrist view, the main model achieves a 95.6% failure detection rate (FDR) at a 2.5% false alarm rate (FAR), exceeding the FDR of the evaluated observation-based, action-based, and vision-language baselines and alternative scores from the same world model. Across four training seeds, mean wrist-view FDR and FAR are 93.4% and 4.7%, respectively. Online replay yields a mean onset-relative detection delay of 0.98 s for wrist-view failures detected offline. Project website: https://janusmaple.github.io/FoMoFD/

Not all world models can doubt: how training objective and architecture shape epistemic uncertainty in JEPA and RSSM world models 2026-11-25
Show abstract

World models predict how an environment responds to actions, so that candidate actions can be evaluated before execution. Executed actions, or decisions, taken in this manner are only as reliable as the model's predictive capabilities, and predictions in turn are only reliable within the model's experience. Epistemic uncertainty (EU) is the signal that marks where that experience ends. In this work we offer an extensive empirical study on how training objectives and architectures shape epistemic uncertainty of world models. More precisely, we present a controlled comparison of post-hoc EU (last-layer Laplace and ensembles) between two dominant models of different paradigms: 1) LeWorldModel (LeWM): A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), which predicts the next embedding without a decoder, and 2) DreamerV3: A recurrent state-space model (RSSM), which reconstructs the observation from a recurrent latent state. In ten simulated visual-control environments, we follow a controlled state perturbation of the environment state through every stage of the models up to the final EU estimate. We find that the RSSM attenuates the state perturbation through two mechanisms before it reaches an estimator on the predictor feature. Its recurrent update averages over context, so a change in a single frame reaches the state at a fraction of its size and recovers only as the change persists. Its reconstruction objective weights an entity by the pixels it covers, so a small object can be lost due to the training objective. The JEPA has neither mechanism and passes the change through all stages to the uncertainty. We further show that this translates to gaps in five uncertainty related downstream tasks, from unfamiliar-state detection to uncertainty-penalised planning: the RSSM uncertainty misses deviations that its own posterior registers, whereas the JEPA uncertainty reports what its model sees.

Was the Model Given the Right Action? Auditing and Repairing Action Interfaces in Navigation World Models 2026-11-25
📝 26 pages, 8 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

World models are evaluated by predicting future observations from actions. These evaluations assume that actions are prepared the same way as during training, but an unchecked mismatch can make a model appear less accurate than it is. We audit this assumption in Navigation World Models (NWM) and find three interacting differences between its training and rollout code. Correcting the inputs, without retraining, reduces mean visual prediction error by 42%, measured with DreamSim on the RECON navigation dataset. The correction also changes the ranking of NWM checkpoints in an exploratory analysis and removes or reduces two alternative models' measured advantages. Correcting only the normalization offset instead increases error, showing why interacting mismatches must be checked together. To test whether sensitivity to input preparation extends beyond NWM, we deliberately introduce encoding errors into two alternative models; both become less accurate. We introduce ActRosetta, a tool that checks whether action encoding matches training, identifies supported causes of disagreement, and repairs inputs when sufficient information is available. Repair helps little where predictions barely respond to actions, the tool's diagnosis has limited coverage, and improved control remains unproven. For any action-conditioned world model, the lesson is the same: before reporting rollout error, verify that evaluation encodes actions exactly as training did.

WorldInteract: Controllable Streaming Video Generation for Multi-Material Interactions 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Video world models aim to create interactive physical worlds that capture how diverse materials move and interact. Yet material-dependent dynamics remain underexplored in controllable video generation, limiting reliable control in scenes with different materials. We present WorldInteract, a streaming video generation framework that decomposes cross-material interaction into material-dependent behavior generation and contact response computation. External pushes and contact impulses are expressed through a shared velocity-increment interface, allowing material behaviors learned from interaction-free videos to be reused in interacting scenes. A physics controller predicts contacts and computes velocity increments from estimated masses and restitution coefficients, while a causal generator executes these increments using learned material behaviors. This separation enables cross-material interaction without supervised contact videos spanning diverse material pairings and contact configurations. We train the generator on 30k simulated interaction-free videos spanning 22 material classes and introduce Material-Aware RL, using verifiable physical rewards to refine material behavior and control execution. WorldInteract substantially outperforms state-of-the-art methods on Physics-IQ and delivers more reliable push control on real-world images.

ShowNow: Fine-Grained Speculative Streaming for Responsive World Models 2026-11-25
📝 23 pages, including appendix. Video examples provided as ancillary files
Show abstract

Interactive world models must generate video continuously while responding promptly to user actions. Real-time throughput alone leaves input latency unresolved: coarse generation blocks delay action updates, and generation after input adds work to the response path. We introduce ShowNow, a fine-grained speculative streaming framework that uses computation available during playback to prepare action-conditioned futures before input arrives. Single-latent commitment enables frequent decisions, while configurable preparation covers one to eight actions. We construct a matched multi-future dataset to train alternative continuations from a common state; shared-history tree execution and fused commitment make their preparation efficient. On a single H100, our 5B model sustains 480p generation at 16 fps with eight prepared futures. In ViZDoom, the complete ShowNow deployment reduces median input-to-display latency from 620 to 208 ms relative to the native-block SolarWM-FT deployment. Matched-policy experiments reduce restart empty ticks from 1.56% to 0.09% (94%) and improve on-time responses to action changes. These results establish complementary roles for fine-grained commitment and advance preparation, with measured trade-offs in visual fidelity and long-horizon consistency. ShowNow turns playback-time computation into responsive visual interaction. Project: https://github.com/showlab/ShowNow.

Do We Still Need Reinforcement Learning When We Can Read the World Model? 2026-11-25
📝 44 pages, 10 figures, 24 tables
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning has achieved remarkable success in sequential decision-making, but learning effective policies often requires substantial interaction experience. Model-based reinforcement learning alleviates this dependence by leveraging world models, which are typically used to support planning or policy optimization. In this paper, we investigate an alternative paradigm in which autonomous LLM-based coding agents directly synthesize control policies by reasoning over readable world models. By examining environment source code, these agents translate environmental knowledge into executable decision rules and iteratively refine their policies using online feedback or offline data, without task-specific RL training. Experiments across diverse decision-making benchmarks show that synthesized policies match or outperform conventional RL methods while requiring substantially less experience. Further analyses reveal interpretable decision rules and how they evolve through feedback-driven refinement. Comparisons with policies generated without source code access and experiments under challenging conditions further demonstrate the benefits of readable world models. These findings suggest that direct policy synthesis can reduce reliance on trial and error, challenging the necessity of task-specific RL training.

Motion Beyond Pixels: Learning Future Visual Representations for Object-Centric Dynamics 2026-11-25
📝 Project page: https://xyz1206.github.io/MotionRep/
Show abstract

Many embodied world models predict future states in latent spaces optimized for RGB reconstruction, which do not explicitly prioritize object-level semantics and geometry relevant to motion generation. We present MotionRep, a visual representation world model that jointly generates future DINO representations and 3D object flow, coupling semantic and geometric information with object-centric dynamics. To make high-dimensional DINO features amenable to generative modeling, we introduce DINO-VAE, which combines temporal and channel compression with latent regularization to produce compact latents while preserving semantic and geometric information. Conditioned on an initial observation, object query points, and a language instruction, a unified diffusion transformer predicts future visual representations and 3D object flow through joint attention. MotionRep employs asymmetric modality-specific noise schedules to keep the visual stream ahead of 3D object flow during joint denoising, allowing progressively refined future visual representations to guide flow generation. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that MotionRep outperforms pixel-based baselines in 3D object flow accuracy and generalization to unseen scenes, while requiring only three denoising steps for inference. These results support predictive visual representations as an effective future-state space for object-centric dynamics modeling.

DiffGR00T: Execution-Aware Differentiable Training for Humanoid Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-25
📝 14 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables. Conference preprint. Corresponding author: Hesheng Wang
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have achieved strong results in arm-centric manipulation, yet humanoid whole-body control (WBC) requires dynamic balance and tightly coupled full-body coordination. Existing hierarchical humanoid VLAs typically decouple motion generation from physical execution, leaving tracking and balance errors unable to directly optimize the VLA generator. We propose DiffGR00T, an end-to-end differentiable training framework that couples GR00T N1.7 with SONIC, a pretrained whole-body behavior foundation model. To enable execution-aware differentiable training, we replace the non-differentiable simulator with a learned, action-conditioned world model, allowing multi-step body-execution losses to backpropagate through the frozen whole-body controller and the flow-matching sampler directly into the VLA action head. On four HumanoidArena simulation tasks, DiffGR00T improves average task success by 12.5 percentage points over the supervised base VLA (59.2% to 71.7%), demonstrating that execution-aware gradients effectively align high-level motion generation with closed-loop whole-body dynamics.

$μ$MPC: Microsecond-Scale Nonlinear and World Model Control from Edge to GPU via Koopman-Linearized Precompilation 2026-11-25
📝 9 pages, 4 figures. Project Website: https://www.deveshnath.com/projects/micrompc/
Show abstract

We present $μ$MPC, a fast model predictive control (MPC) framework that reduces online computation using learned Koopman-linearized dynamics. By precomputing finite-horizon control-to-output maps, $μ$MPC eliminates lifted-state trajectories, yielding an action-only Koopman-Compiled QP (KC-QP) with $Hn_u$ decision variables, independent of lifted dimension. A fixed, precomputed primal operator enables efficient primal-dual iterations using only matrix-vector products and projections, accommodating changing goals and constraints without refactorization. This efficiency also enables control-aware dynamics learning through differentiable KC-QPs in the training loop and batched DAgger-style data aggregation. $μ$MPC achieves solve times as low as $23μ$s on a desktop CPU and 24 ms onboard a 168 MHz Crazyflie microcontroller. It scales to 4096D quadruped control in under 5 ms, visual planning with 2048D world models in 0.4 ms, and collision-aware planning for 200 quadrotors at over 30 Hz on the GPU. Across these benchmarks, $μ$MPC achieves up to $108\times$ faster constrained MPC and $1907\times$ faster visuomotor planning than competing methods.

PrimeSim: A Generalist World Model Simulator from Large-Scale Cross-Embodiment Demonstrations 2026-11-25
Show abstract

A robot world model that serves as a simulator must do more than generate plausible videos: it must faithfully follow commanded actions, maintain consistency across camera views, and sustain coherent long-horizon rollouts. We present PrimeSim, an action-conditioned, multiview world model simulator that addresses these challenges through progressive midtraining and posttraining. Starting from a pretrained video generator, we midtrain on 30,110 hours of robot demonstrations spanning 22 platforms. To improve action following, we introduce a unified cross-embodiment action representation and frame-aligned dual-path conditioning to strengthen action-frame correspondence. To maintain multiview consistency, we develop view-aware interleaved attention with explicit camera identities, enabling cross-view information exchange while preserving view-specific representations. We further introduce a fast-video, slow-memory posttraining framework that transforms the bidirectional video generator into an efficient autoregressive simulator. By combining fast causal generation, self-rollout training, and hierarchical temporal memory, PrimeSim mitigates error accumulation from self-generated histories and supports coherent long-horizon simulation. PrimeSim runs at 10.1 FPS, remains stable over long-horizon rollouts on rigid and deformable manipulation tasks, and transfers to held-out platforms within 100 to 200 gradient steps where the pretrained backbone does not. Against five open-source action-conditioned world models it leads four of the five WorldSimProbe suites and posts the best overall EWMBench score, while fine-grained local calibration still trails the field; its rollouts are faithful enough to support GRPO-based policy optimization. We release the model weights, inference code, and training code.

Escaping Latent Traps by Remembering: Training-Free Episodic Kernel Repulsion for World-Model Planning 2026-11-25
📝 29 pages, including references and appendix
Show abstract

Latent world models plan towards a goal image by minimising the distance between predicted and goal latents. Because this distance is not geodesic, it creates latent traps---local minima or flat basins behind walls, at corners and in dead ends---where memoryless receding-horizon replanning keeps revisiting states far from the goal. We propose Episodic Kernel Repulsion (EKR), a training-free, test-time cost term that penalises candidate plans via a top-$k$ Gaussian kernel to a novelty-gated memory of the latents the agent has actually visited, normalised by the current batch base cost so a single strength transfers across models. Across two JEPA world models and four benchmarks, EKR improves success in all eight settings without changing the model, planner or search budget, matching or exceeding $4\times$ samples at $0.8$--$1.5\times$ compute in seven of eight settings while adding $\sim 25$ ms ($<1\%$) per replan. Controlled trap-fork interventions confirm that the memory causes escape, and an untuned configuration transfers directly to DINO-WM with CEM (e.g. $44.7\%\to 100.0\%$ on long PointMaze, $+19.3$ points on long Wall) and to JEPA-WM on Metaworld mw-reach-wall ($25.3\%\to 40.7\%$).

Long-Horizon Planning with Latent World Models via Graph Anchors 2026-11-25
📝 Project page: https://syed-nazmus-sakib.github.io/graph-anchors/ | Code: https://github.com/syed-nazmus-sakib/graph-anchors
Show abstract

Latent world models plan in a compact latent space by predicting the outcomes of actions. Their planners judge a plan by the latent distance between its predicted end state and the goal image. We show that this distance fails in two distinct ways. First, it loses directional signal a few steps from the goal, causing distant goals to fail. Second, it weighs the robot arm over the manipulated object, causing manipulation to fail at contact. We measure both failures directly on recorded data by asking whether a lower latent distance corresponds to true progress toward the goal. Across five environments, six visual representations (including raw pixels and V-JEPA 2), and nine Meta-World tasks, this ranking test cleanly separates the two failures, and the graph distance to a goal predicts where closed-loop planning breaks down. Both failures share a single remedy: replacing the distant goal with a nearby target that lies on a recorded trajectory. We realize this principle with Graph Anchors, a training-free planner for frozen latent world models. It builds a sparse graph once over the model's own training frames and aims each planning step at a node a few moves ahead along the shortest path to the goal, keeping paths on recorded transitions wherever contact matters. On goals up to 400 steps away, Graph Anchors raise LeWM's success from 13% to 85% on PointMaze-Medium, from 8% to 87% on PointMaze-Large, and from 5% to 71% on Push-T, where stronger search toward the goal fails and where prior training-free planners break down under missing step horizons, subsampling shortcuts, or fragmented contact.

Most of the Way There: Turning a Strong Video Prior into an Action-Conditioned World Model 2026-11-25
📝 Project Page: https://xmz111.github.io/Dianjing/
Show abstract

Modern video generators excel at producing visually plausible futures guided by text and image prompts. Yet their emphasis on semantic plausibility does not guarantee fidelity to the physical consequences of specific actions. Action-conditioned world models (ACWMs), in contrast, aim to simulate how the world responds to robot actions, enabling scalable policy evaluation and improvement without costly real-world rollouts. The challenge is not merely to generate a plausible future, but to predict what follows from a specific physical action. As video priors grow stronger, we ask how little adaptation is truly needed to bridge this gap. We introduce Dianjing, a minimal recipe that preserves and unlocks the capabilities of strong video priors through unified physical action conditioning, training concentrated at the high noise levels where motion and layout, and thus the consequences of actions, are decided, and native history conditioning. On WorldOracle, which evaluates predictions against paired real-world executions, Dianjing achieves the best scores on four of the five evaluation tracks and ranks second on AgiBot-Long. Our findings suggest that strong video priors already bring us most of the way to world modeling; lightweight physical action grounding can unlock their potential for real-world prediction.

Can Visual World Models See Force? 2026-11-25
Show abstract

World models and vision-language-action policy models are increasingly proposed as foundations for robot manipulation. They predict how scenes evolve and which actions to take, yet successful manipulation depends not solely on following observable trajectories but also on the hidden variable of interaction force. Neurophysiology has shown that humans possess a visual prior for this hidden variable, motivating us to ask the question: Do the representations of visual foundation models also capture the forces involved in an interaction? We define force awareness as the ability of representations to (1) indicate when contact force is applied, (2) track how it changes during an interaction, and (3) reflect how large it is. We probe frozen image, video, world- and policy model representations with a lightweight readout trained on synchronized video and force measurements from two manipulation datasets. Our experiments show that most models capture when contact occurs but not how much force is being applied. Surprisingly, robot post-training does not improve this either, highlighting an important gap in current models capabilities.

Co-World Model for Human-Video-to-Robot Transfer via Co-Constructed Shared Latent Dynamics 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Human videos are abundant and cheap to obtain, whereas robot trajectories remain scarce and costly, making zero-shot skill transfer from a single human demonstration an appealing route to scalable robot learning. Existing methods, however, either retarget human-specific geometry or match only static visual goals, and thus stumble over the cross-embodiment dynamics mismatch and the ungrounded action execution. We present the Co-World Model (CoWM), a predictive framework jointly optimized from paired yet unsynchronized human and robot streams. To bridge the cross-embodiment dynamics gap, CoWM disentangles each observation into a shared object-relation state and embodiment-specific context. It aligns latent actions across embodiments using effect-swap and action-sensitivity objectives,such that actions are matched by their predicted effects on the manipulated objects rather than by embodimentdependent kinematics. For grounded execution, a phase-aware receding-horizon controller estimates demonstration progress, projects the inferred human action prior onto the robot action-support density, and translates the resulting latent dynamics into executable robot commands. Extensive experiments in simulation and on two physical robot platforms demonstrate the effectiveness of CoWM, its cross-platform generalizability, and the contribution of each component. Rehearse in 3D, Render in 2D: Physically Consistent and Editable Robot World Models 2026-11-25
📝 Includes supplementary material
Show abstract

Embodied world models aim to predict future interactions that are both visually realistic and physically consistent. Yet explicit 3D motion prediction alone does not guarantee that object structure and contact relationships are preserved. Beyond motion prediction, 3D representations provide an explicit state space for imposing structural and physical constraints. Building on this insight, we introduce RehearsalWorld, an editable robot world model driven by 3D Interaction Rehearsal (3DIR). Given an initial RGB-D observation and robot actions, Interaction-Consistent Rehearsal (ICR) uses a pretrained 3D point-flow model as a motion prior. Compared with PointWorld on real DROID grasping sequences, RehearsalWorld reduces gripper-relative offset by approximately 79% to 84% across deformable, articulated, and rigid objects and lowers reference motion error. Loop4D: Embodied World Modeling with 4D VAEs and Looped Diffusion Transformers 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Predicting the outcomes of robotic manipulation requires modeling both visual appearance and evolving 3D geometry. This poses two challenges: video compression can obscure fine-grained robot motion, while jointly predicting RGB and geometry demands sufficient generative capacity. We introduce Loop4D, a framework that addresses these challenges through motion-preserving 4D representations and recurrent generative refinement. Our 4D VAE encodes RGB and pointmap sequences into compact spatiotemporal latents while preserving motion details and geometric structure. Our RGB VAE combines geometry-guided supervision with predictive reconstruction to reduce compression-induced motion artifacts. Our geometry VAE uses lightweight adapters to adapt pretrained RGB representations while preserving their priors. Building on these representations, our Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Transformer maintains separate appearance and geometry streams with shared Transformer weights and cross-modal connections. Experiments demonstrate high-quality 4D generation and validate Loop4D in simulated robotic manipulation tasks.

Video-based world models hold significant potential for generating high-quality embodied manipulation data. CARE-WM: Same-Horizon Self-Conditioning with Constraint-Adaptive Regularization for Latent World Models 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Planning with latent world models requires both accurate multi-step predictions and latent distances that reflect physical proximity to the goal. Large language models (LLMs), when acting as agents, are expected to take observed data in context, infer the latent state space underlying the world, and leverage it for downstream prediction. However, prior work demonstrated that LLMs struggle to use representations learned in context on a graph tracking task, where the model needs to construct a representation of the graph governing data generation process and use it for subsequent predictions. In this paper, we show that extending this to few-shot settings, where each demonstration is generated from a different world with either the same or different graph topologies, enhances its prediction on 9 models from 7 model families. To understand this improvement, we linearly probe a low-dimensional world representation that encodes graph information in the hidden states. Notably, we find that few-shot demonstrations relocate the world representation and increase its predictive use. Specifically, for each model, these world representations shift in directions nearly orthogonal to their original subspace, and interventions on these representations selectively impair performance more than interventions on other subspaces. Consistent with this insight, we show that few-shot demonstrations with observations from different worlds improve performance on ARC-AGI-1&2, web agent tasks, and Othello. Our findings elucidate the role and internal mechanisms of few-shot demonstrations in in-context world modeling and provide implications for their further improvement.

Reactive robotic manipulation is acutely sensitive to simultaneous sensing noise, measurement dropouts, joint execution errors, and contact parameter variations. While single-frame frameworks like Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS) achieve strong robustness under isolated uncertainties (86.4%), they degrade severely when perception and actuation uncertainties co-occur (27.6% across 10 coupled stress scenarios) due to memoryless encoding and fixed high-stiffness PD control. Where a person looks indicates their focus of attention, and how their gaze shifts between targets reveals how that attention responds to a changing scene. Existing gaze target estimation methods largely operate frame by frame and do not model these shifts. Gaze shifts are often driven by dynamic changes in the scene, which is the kind of information world models are designed to encode. This relationship is subtle, however, since an event far across the frame may produce only a slight movement of the person's head or eyes. We therefore introduce the Gaze World Model (GWM), which co-trains latent scene prediction with person-conditioned gaze supervision on a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; in our ablations, the latent prediction objective improves both shift detection and gaze localization. Given a video clip and the person's head box, GWM estimates gaze locations and detects gaze shifts in the observed clip, and forecasts future gaze and shifts from predicted scene features. To train and evaluate these tasks, we augment VideoAttentionTarget and ChildPlay with event-level gaze-shift annotations. GWM achieves state-of-the-art shift detection on these benchmarks, about 10 points higher in four-class AP than the strongest baseline, while remaining comparable to the strongest image-based models in gaze target estimation, and outperforms the compared baselines on future gaze localization and shift forecasting.

Interactive video world models generate navigable worlds frame by frame, but a persistent world requires previously observed scenes to remain recoverable as users move away and return. Existing memory evaluations, however, often focus on seen-view revisits and full rollouts, leaving cross-view scene recovery underexplored while entangling memory with trajectory-following errors and accumulated generation degradation. We introduce SceneMemBench, a benchmark for scene memory in interactive video world models. It evaluates two complementary dimensions: seen-view recall versus novel-view recovery, and controlled diagnosis versus end-to-end interaction. We introduce Continued Rollout from a shared ground-truth observation history, alongside Full Rollout over complete model-generated interactions. As live video streams grow longer, repeatedly encoding and processing their full histories becomes increasingly costly. Answering questions as they arrive therefore calls for visual memory built in one pass before future questions are known. Existing methods mainly keep tokens that differ from earlier ones or match surrogate queries fixed in advance. Without modeling world dynamics, these criteria struggle to tell subtle surprising details from expected change. We introduce Dreamcatcher, a training-free framework and, to our knowledge, the first to use predictive signals from an independently pretrained, frozen video world model for online memory formation in streaming video question answering. Conditioned on recent history, the world model predicts incoming features using learned world priors. Its normalized prediction errors yield temporal and spatial surprisal signals that guide online segmentation and the retention of a frozen Video-LLM's native visual tokens, balancing surprising details with broad visual coverage under budgets that decay with segment age. At query time, an evidence gate admits a segment from a similarity-based shortlist only when the Video-LLM judges that it adds evidence for the question beyond the current view. On OVO-Bench, Dreamcatcher achieves the highest overall accuracy among the compared training-free methods for each of four frozen Video-LLMs, reaching 78.9% with Qwen3.8-27B. With the three Qwen Video-LLMs, it also reduces average input tokens for answer generation by more than 80% relative to their base models at 1 fps.

Autoregressive video world models enable temporally coherent generation for a single observer. Extending them to multiple agents requires consistency across independently controlled views and temporal gaps under causal streaming. We present ConsistWorld, a multi-agent world model that generates camera-controlled video streams of a static scene from one shared image. We formulate consistency as routing evidence from committed multi-agent history and concurrently generated peer views to the tokens being generated. Pose Conditioned Memory Retrieval selects relevant historical observations from all agents, recovering evidence beyond the recent context window. Visibility-Gated Peer Sharing regulates current peer information according to estimated historical coverage and current-view overlap. Together, they determine which historical observations enter the context and where concurrent peer information contributes, supporting long-term recall and coordinated exploration. World models facilitate planning and control in robotic manipulation by enabling agents to anticipate the consequences of their actions. However, they typically rely on action-labelled interaction data that is costly to acquire and difficult to scale. These models are also often tied to the specific embodiment they were trained on, limiting transferability to previously unseen robot morphologies. We introduce a 3D latent-action world model that learns continuous action representations directly from point-cloud observations of retargeted human manipulation demonstrations. A contrastive objective is used to encourage consistent representations of motion across embodiments. The resulting unified latent action space serves directly as the search space for model-predictive control, where sampled latent actions condition particle-based future predictions. Executable robot commands are then recovered from the predicted geometric motion without requiring an embodiment-specific action decoder. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that the learned latent actions exhibit controllable and compositional motion, transfer to unseen robot embodiments, and support zero-shot manipulation with unseen gripper geometries. Following task-agnostic adaptation, our model achieves 90%, 92%, and 60% success on reaching, grasping, and placing, respectively, compared with DexWM's reported 72%, 58%, and 28%. Finally, we demonstrate zero-shot real-world manipulation with an unseen dexterous hand, achieving an 86% success rate across 50 trials on five diverse tasks involving pushing, grasping, and lifting everyday objects. These results highlight the potential of geometry-grounded latent actions as a unified, transferable interface for world-model-based robotic manipulation. Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.
