$Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://junjieye.com/Z-sight/
Show abstract

We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM's native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

Render the Robot, Generate the World: One Video World Model Across Embodiments 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://doppelwm.github.io/
Show abstract

Predicting the consequences of an agent's actions requires modeling both the body's motion and the environment's response. Most video world models attempt to learn both from numerical actions, even though geometry and kinematics already specify the intended motion. We introduce Doppel, an embodiment-agnostic video world model conditioned on camera-aligned renderings of future body configurations and numerical action trajectories. By explicitly providing the intended motion, Doppel can focus on predicting object motion and interaction-driven changes in the environment. Rendered motion also provides a common visual interface across different embodiments and action spaces. To train on robot datasets without camera extrinsics, we recover camera poses and align rendered trajectories with recorded observations. We jointly train a single shared video backbone and conditioning adapter on eight datasets spanning eight robot and human embodiments, from single- and bimanual robot arms to a dexterous humanoid and human hands and bodies, using about 600 hours of video without embodiment labels. Across three robot-arm datasets, Doppel improves PSNR over action-only baselines by 1.00 dB on held-out trajectories and 1.20 dB under out-of-distribution evaluation, while predicting more consistent object motion. Training on all eight embodiments (Doppel-8) improves robot predictions over the three-robot model (Doppel-3) on four of six evaluation sets. Project page: https://doppelwm.github.io/.

WorldRuler: Interactive World Modeling under Configurable Rules 2026-11-18
📝 15 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Rules govern how player actions change interactive worlds. Different rules create distinct gameplay experiences within similar scenes and action spaces. For customizable gameplay, generative world models must follow user-defined rules beyond real-world behaviors. However, realizing user-defined rules presents two challenges: Rule-State Persistence and Rule-to-Visual Realization. The former requires preserving rule definitions, dependencies, and world states across saving and loading, so that restored worlds follow the established rules. The latter requires accurately realizing rule-induced changes on designated objects and attributes, particularly when affected objects are unseen. In this paper, we propose WorldRuler, which organizes rule definitions, dependencies, and object states in an executable 3D rule graph and connects rule execution, video generation, and perceptual feedback in a closed loop. The graph supports joint saving and restoration of rules and world states, while dynamic object binding grounds rule-specified entities in visual instances and uses stored states to constrain generation from their first appearance. We further introduce object attribute state replacement and counterfactual reconstruction training, enabling accurate attribute control and compatible appearance and motion changes while limiting interference with unrelated content. We develop an automated data collection and annotation pipeline using GTA-V and Habitat to construct RuleVerse, a dataset of rule-driven interactions with detailed state and event annotations, including cases where rules take effect before affected objects become visible. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of WorldRuler in preserving rule-state consistency across saving and loading and accurately realizing rule outcomes on designated objects and attributes.

Does the Action Matter? Probing Action-Conditioned Semantic Forecasting as a World Model for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables. Accepted at the WAM-AD Workshop, WACV 2027. Code: https://github.com/binayksingh/does-action-matter
Show abstract

World models are increasingly used in autonomous driving to predict how a scene evolves under the ego vehicle's actions, supporting counterfactual reasoning and model-based planning. However, existing evaluations focus on prediction fidelity and planning performance, and rarely verify that the predictions depend on the conditioning action. In this paper, we study action dependence in action-conditioned semantic forecasting, which predicts future semantic segmentation from past segmentations and the ego action. We compare a forecaster with feature-wise and input-level action conditioning against an identical model without action input, and measure action dependence with test-time action interventions scored by standard segmentation metrics. The evaluation is validated in a synthetic environment with known action-dependent dynamics and applied to Cityscapes and BDD100K, both within each dataset and across the two vehicle platforms. Experiments show that the executed action improves mid-term mIoU by at most 2.5 points on Cityscapes and 0.6 points on BDD100K, whereas a wrong action reduces it by up to 23.2 and 7.0 points, below the model without action input. On real data, feature-wise conditioning depends 1.6 times more on the action than input-level conditioning at similar accuracy, whereas both depend on it equally in the synthetic environment, where the history does not determine the action. These results show that an accuracy gain indicates that a driving world model uses its action, but not how strongly its forecasts depend on it. Code is available at https://github.com/binayksingh/does-action-matter.

DiWALI: Diffusion Policy Learning in World Models via Latent Action Inference from Human and Robot Play 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Learning robot manipulation is constrained by the availability of action-labeled robot data, for both imitation and world models. Humans can demonstrate skills in seconds, but their demonstrations lack the required robot actions. We introduce DiWALI, a framework that learns and improves diffusion policies within a world model trained on human and robot play. DiWALI infers latent actions from consecutive frames using an inverse dynamics model, enabling world model training without action labels, and grounds them in robot commands through a decoder trained separately on robot play. Skill policies are trained over latent actions from action-free human demonstrations and improved with reinforcement learning in imagination. On the CALVIN benchmark, DiWALI policies trained solely from human demonstrations substantially outperform baselines trained with robot demonstrations. Incorporating human play into the world model training also yields stronger policies after fine-tuning than using the same amount of robot play alone. Experiments on a physical robot show that skills learned primarily from human demonstrations are further improved in imagination with a world model trained on human and robot play. Together, these results demonstrate that human experience can support both manipulation skill acquisition and subsequent policy improvement, reducing reliance on action-labeled robot data. Videos, dataset and code are available at https://diwali.cs.uni-freiburg.de.

Counterfactual World Models: JEPAs That Reason About "What If" 2026-11-18
Show abstract

World models operate in a world they cannot fully observe. When a hidden disturbance, such as a current pushing a boat, changes the outcome of an action, a counterfactual question follows: what would have happened under a different action, subject to the same disturbance? Its answer separates bad decisions from bad luck. We prove that world models trained on factual transitions cannot answer it on their own, whatever their data or capacity. For many physical systems, the observed outcome and a simple property of the dynamics suffice: natura non facit saltus, nature makes no leaps. When actions and disturbances act as separate forces, their interaction is second-order in time, so the counterfactual outcome is, to first order, the observed outcome plus the difference between the world model's predictions under the alternative and factual actions. This yields a counterfactual world model with no counterfactual data, extra parameters, or retraining. For transitions of duration $h$, we prove an $O(h^2)$ bound on the counterfactual error relative to the disturbance variance, establish a matching minimax lower bound from unpaired factual data, and characterize failures at discontinuities, coordinate folds, and disturbances that scale the command. We validate these predictions from pixels with JEPA world models on frozen DINOv2 and masked autoencoder (MAE) encoders. On four rendered simulators, our approach improves counterfactual $R^2$ from $38$--$46\%$ to $86$--$96\%$ with the same world models; on a fifth, an underwater vehicle, it reaches $90$--$94\%$ with sixteen times more factual data. Four further frozen encoders, DINOv3, SigLIP~2, CLIP and EVA-02, give $81$--$95\%$ on the same simulators. In hindsight planning, it finds the action that would have reached the goal in $35$--$69\%$ of events, against $14$--$23\%$ for an oracle planner that knows the simulator but not the outcome.

HandWorldBench: A Unified Benchmark for Hand-Centric World Models 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Recent advances in world models are opening new possibilities for fine-grained embodied tasks, with hand-object interaction offering a particularly demanding setting. Modeling these interactions involves capturing how hand actions change objects and how the resulting states persist over time. Yet systematic evaluation tailored to these hand-centric capabilities remains underdeveloped. To address this gap, we introduce HandWorldBench, a unified benchmark that systematically evaluates hand-centric world models across human hands, simulated dexterous hands, and real dexterous hands along six dimensions: visual quality, hand fidelity, appearance consistency, instruction following, physical consistency, and temporal coherence. To construct the benchmark, we develop a data curation pipeline that extracts hand-object interaction clips and screens them for quality, yielding 20,500 high-quality egocentric clips from over 600 hours of footage. We then manually select 500 clips covering seven task families for evaluation. Benchmarking eleven models under a unified protocol reveals that stronger hand-pose control does not consistently translate into coherent object-state evolution and persistence or robust cross-embodiment performance, with several specialized hand-control models underperforming general-purpose text/image-to-video generators on dexterous-hand tasks.

Masked Swingers: Harnessing Data Augmentation to Advance Autoencoders for Self-Supervised Learning 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Self-supervised learning (SSL) trains on inputs alone, allowing us to make models that are more useful in more settings. The autoencoder SSL framework learns by reconstructing its own input after information loss through a bottleneck or noise injection. Masked autoencoders (MAE) are the most successful instantiation of this framework: they encode a random subset of patches, then decode the masked-out patches. In this work, we introduce key modifications to improve MAEs. Our method augments an image in two different ways, then masks and encodes each view separately. It then exchanges the global representations (CLS tokens) between views before decoding the masked patches. By design, our Masked Swingers encourages learning a view-agnostic summary of the image for efficient transfer. We perform extensive experiments, and find Masked Swingers outperforms the official MAE checkpoint by +10.7% averaged over 10 frozen-feature tasks at equal pre-training compute. Gains are highest on fine-grained tasks, e.g., instance retrieval. Swingers also reduces error by 64% relative to MAE on three new state-probing datasets for world modeling, while keeping the fine-tunability of MAE. Post-training by swinging nearest neighbors gains again, e.g., by +5% on IN-1K kNN, opening the door to learning invariances via autoencoding.

Comparing Each Generation with Independent Siblings Gives Video World Model Post-Training a Proper Objective for Outcome Frequencies 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Video world models are used as simulators, so when a first frame leaves several physical outcomes open, repeated generations should produce each outcome as often as it occurs in the data. Physics post-training scores every generation against the single recorded future of its history, which makes single videos more faithful, but the expected score is linear in the model distribution and is maximized by always producing the most likely outcome. The log-likelihood of the recorded outcome is maximized by the data distribution, and its gradient weights agreement with the record by the inverse of how often the model produces the generated outcome, a frequency that other generations of the same history with independent noise, its siblings, can estimate. To train with this weight from a finite group, we propose SibPO, which performs Sibling-paired Policy Optimization: siblings estimate a truncated inverse-frequency weight for agreement with the record, and a second term subtracts the same weight times the agreement with the siblings, which makes the expected objective a strictly proper divergence over outcome cells at every group size. On PisaBench, SibPO lowers the KS distance to simulated landing times by 9.1-10.2% relative to the strongest method outside its reward family on three of four video models without degrading single videos, and it lowers the error of estimated robot policy success rates by 13%.

When to Refine: Selective Search for Frozen Visual World Models 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Planning with a visual world model requires deciding both which actions to execute and how long to search for them. We introduce Selective CEM Refinement (SCR), a learned rule for deciding whether to continue cross-entropy method (CEM) search with a frozen LeWorldModel. After five it- erations, the gate measures how far the mean first action chunk has moved, how much the mean terminal cost of elite plans has fallen, and how widely their first chunks vary. It returns the current plan for execution or continues the same search for ten more iterations. The gate requires no addi- tional world-model rollout. On 16 PushT scenes evaluated with two planning seeds, SCR succeeded in 25 of 32 trials, compared with 28 of 32 for fixed 15-iteration search (Fixed-15). All 25 SCR suc- cesses occurred in trials that Fixed-15 also solved, yield- ing 89.29% paired success retention. Across complete episodes, including replanning, SCR evaluated 23.81% fewer candidate plans and reduced mean controller time per eight-environment batch by 17.22%. Its observed suc- cess rate was 5.36 percentage points above an analytic mix- ture of the two fixed-depth controllers at the same expected candidate workload, although the difference changed sign across planning seeds. Compared with gating only at the initial solve under the same random-draw padding proto- col, gating again at replanning produced nine additional early exits. This reduced total candidate evaluations by 15.79%, while successes fell from 27 to 25.

Considering Context: When World Models Need Context Encoders 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Methods for generalization in model-based reinforcement learning typically assume that an agent cannot recover the latent context governing the environment dynamics from its own experience, and therefore supplies it externally. We formalize and test this assumption with \emph{predictive sufficiency}, which quantifies what access to the context adds to next-step prediction under the visitation distribution an agent induces, and separates that quantity into a history-recoverable part and a residual requiring the true context, both distinct from a finite model's deficit. We classify context-aware algorithms by the predictive risk their conditioning set can target and demonstrate across environments of increasing identification difficulty that the headroom does not follow the MDP class. The same task under different priors leaves predictive headroom in one setting and nothing distinguishable from zero in another, where the agent's behavior implicitly identifies the context and any benefit of such a mechanism cannot be attributed to missing one-step predictive information. Where headroom persists, the learned state exposes it only partially, and adding the true context still lowers the risk. Our contribution is an analytical framework, instantiated by a practical criterion for matching contextual mechanisms to the predictive information available to them, estimated from the ordinary trained agent.

ShoWorld: Shape-Faithful Multi-Hypothesis Occupancy World Model 2026-11-18
📝 10 pages
Show abstract

Occupancy world models struggle to forecast moving objects due to motion uncertainty and shape distortion. A single trajectory can average plausible futures into an implausible path, and repeated re-voxelization can erode or inflate object shape even under correct motion. We introduce ShoWorld to address these challenges through separate motion and shape representations. The motion forecaster predicts probabilistic trajectories conditioned on scene layout, nearby agents and the future ego trajectory. Each hypothesis is rendered into occupancy and fused according to its probability to retain alternative futures. The shape component predicts sub-voxel points with occupancy probabilities and offsets from local geometry. These points move rigidly with the object, and voxel occupancy follows the probability that at least one occupied point falls within it. This representation reduces shape distortion under motion. ShoWorld achieves state-of-the-art results on Occ3D-nuScenes. With ground-truth occupancy and ego trajectory, it improves mIoU and IoU by 6.56 and 9.16 points overall and by 6.06 and 5.53 points on dynamic classes.

Risk-Triggered Search: Behavior-Preserving Safe Control 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Pre-trained visual world models generate multi-step action plans from current and goal observations, yet terminal-proximity objectives fail to prevent forbidden-zone traversal or rigid-body collision sliding. Purely visual interfaces may lack analytical dynamics, structured states, or explicit collision geometry, requiring internal prediction of action-conditioned process risk. We propose Risk-Triggered Search (RTS) for frozen Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world models. Rather than relying on static state features that cannot distinguish candidate plans sharing an observation, RTS unrolls action-conditioned future latent prefixes in the frozen world model and trains a lightweight risk head with process-violation supervision. To intervene selectively while preserving safe nominal behavior, RTS uses the same predicted prefix-risk criterion as both the search trigger gate and hard feasibility gate: safe nominal plans execute directly, whereas alarms trigger local Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) search to minimize nominal deviation within the feasible set. To prevent isotropic Euclidean minimal modification from shrinking obstacle-directed actions into zero-amplitude stalling, we introduce an amplitude-preserving metric $d_{\mathrm{AP}}$ that retains tangential progression while maintaining a unique zero at the nominal plan. Under unified gating and this unique-zero condition, bypassing safe-state search yields closed-loop trajectories strictly identical to every-step same-kernel search. Evaluated under a process-safety benchmark and Safe Arrival Rate (SAR) metric across five tasks, RTS improves SAR over Direct by 12.67--41.67 percentage points on navigation and articulated control and +2.08/+4.17 pp on Cube/PushT boundary cohorts, maintaining 100% action identity with every-step search while cutting controller time by 17.71%--70.41% across 1,968 paired trajectories.

StrideWorld: Benchmarking Scene Rigidity and Action Consistency in Interactive Video World Model 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Interactive video world models turn user commands into the exploration of a generated scene, which requires the scene to keep its 3D structure as the camera moves and each action to keep a stable effect. Existing benchmarks mostly evaluate these properties against references fitted to the video under test, either a scene reconstructed from its own frames or a camera trajectory aligned to the command path with a free scale. Such references can absorb gradual scene deformation and changes in pace and route length, so these errors can pass undetected. We therefore introduce StrideWorld, a benchmark whose references are fixed before the motion they judge. It evaluates scene rigidity against the ground-truth geometry of the start view, and action consistency against the stride of each action, the camera motion of one command. The stride is measured on the model's first steps and then used to test the steps that follow, so each model is judged only against its own steps. Its measurements are validated on rendered videos with known camera motion, and its scores agree with human judgments. Across \nvideos{} videos generated by \nmodels{} open models from $140$ start views, all models but one deform their scenes as the camera moves, with offsets up to $7.3$ times those of a rigid rendering along the same camera path. Most models keep a steady pace when a single action is held. Along composed routes, several models often stall, and most completed routes end away from the positions predicted by each model's own strides. Only the two models that scale commands by the estimated scene depth bring most routes close to these positions. Fine-tuning two models on a few rendered clips removes most of their mid-route stalls. Code, data, and a leaderboard are publicly available at https://github.com/chuangwang123/strideworld.

CommDreamer: Radio-Geometric World Models for Mapless Cellular-Connected UAV Trajectory Planning 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Reliable cellular connectivity and collision-free flight are essential for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) operations in low-altitude urban canyons, where dense 3D buildings obstruct flight clearance, shadow serving base stations, and create strong co-channel interference at street intersections. Existing planners either require prior 3D city meshes and channel knowledge maps unavailable onboard, or rely on model-free reinforcement learning with low sample efficiency and poor cross-city generalization. We propose CommDreamer, a mapless radio-geometric world model that fuses raw onboard 3D LiDAR scans, multicell cellular reports, and shift-invariant relative kinematics to jointly infer local obstacle clearance and directional signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) dynamics. ProbeShare: Probing a Few, Sharing Across Many for Fast World-Model Planning 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Visual planning with a frozen joint-embedding world model supports robotic reaching and object manipulation by evaluating candidate action sequences against an image goal before execution. Standard cross-entropy method (CEM) planners roll out every candidate independently through a billion-parameter predictor, even when nearby actions induce similar scene changes. This redundant computation grows with population size and search rounds, slowing closed-loop replanning. We observe that candidates within a planning step share an observation history and thus a common action-to-feature map. Based on this insight, we propose ProbeShare, which measures local responses with a few representative probes and reconstructs each candidate's feature grid through scene-conditioned, geometry-aware weighting. When promising candidates move beyond existing probe coverage, ProbeShare evaluates a new probe to rescore nearby candidates. Across four RoboCasa tasks, ProbeShare accelerates episode planning by 43.8--128.9 times over dense RoboJEPA and at least $14.2\times$ over competitive baselines, with greater mean goal progress in a matched-time comparison.

Beyond the Camera: A Tri-Modal Driving World Model for Future RGB, Thermal, and LiDAR Forecasting in Adverse Weather 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Driving world models forecast what a vehicle's sensors will record next. Most forecast camera video and some add LiDAR, but thermal cameras, which still see in darkness and some adverse weather, are rarely a forecast target, and to our knowledge no world model forecasts all three sensors. We study joint forecasting of RGB, thermal, and LiDAR on real adverse-weather sequences of the DSERT-RoLL dataset. We post-train a pretrained video world model for each sensor and represent LiDAR as a depth video aligned with the RGB camera, with densification and encoding chosen by what the model's video tokenizer can reconstruct. A tri-sensor model sees all three sensors as one video. We score LiDAR in meters against measured returns and compare against persistence baselines passed through the same tokenizer as the model. With this matching, an apparent loss of the thermal model to persistence in fog disappears. Blanking one sensor degrades the forecasts of the others more in snow than in fog.

Target-Consistent Action Guidance via Semi-Unbalanced Optimal Transport for Drifting World Models 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Action-conditioned world models let robots imagine the outcomes of their actions for planning and offline policy evaluation, but these uses need many rollouts, which multi-step diffusion makes expensive. Drifting models move the iteration from inference to training and generate samples in one network pass. DriftWorld brought this paradigm to action-conditioned world models; to strengthen action following, it repels predictions from the current frame, a construction borrowed from classifier-free guidance. We show that this accentuation has a target bias: the push persists even when a prediction matches the recorded future, in kernel, balanced, and unbalanced Wasserstein drifting fields alike. We introduce Mass-Share Accentuation (MSA) to remove this bias. MSA treats the current frame as a counterfactual destination competing with the recorded future in a semi-relaxed optimal transport problem; subtracting the closed-form share of the correct future makes the guidance vanish at the target, and the remainder is shared by all predictions. We prove that the recorded future is the unique fixed point along the motion direction for collapsed predictions, and that the guidance fades with prediction error. On Push-T, Robomimic, and BridgeV2, where trained models under-react rather than overshoot, MSA improves action ranking, response slope, and moving-region error over latent-space baselines at no inference cost, though these benchmarks do not isolate bias removal as the cause.

RiskWorld: Risk-Aware World Modeling with Flow-Guided Occupancy Evolution for Selective Trajectory Planning in Automated Driving 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Safe motion planning in automated driving requires anticipating evolving traffic risks and deciding when to revise the current planned trajectory. We introduce RiskWorld, a risk-aware world modeling framework for shared occupancy forecasting and selective trajectory replacement. Spatial risk fields and temporal actor context are fused with visual bird's-eye-view features. Flow-guided evolution transports occupancy and scene features, while signed residuals correct occupancy after transport. One forecast is generated per planning step and reused across candidates. Each candidate is compared with a current-state persistence reference, yielding a nonnegative collision-score correction. The trajectory selected by current-world evaluation serves as the planning anchor and is replaced only when additional predicted risk triggers intervention and an alternative satisfies component-wise constraints on predicted risk and trajectory error. Candidate geometries remain unchanged. We evaluate RiskWorld for open-loop planning on nuScenes using camera features, annotation-derived current and historical actor states, and dataset-provided map context. RiskWorld achieves the lowest collision rate at a long evaluation horizon of 3 s, and the second-best average L2 error among various state-of-the-art baselines, while running at 11.5 FPS on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with 90.81 M parameters. Within-setting ablations show that RiskWorld achieves lower collision rates than the current-state rescoring baseline, while forecast reuse enables additional candidates to be evaluated at low marginal computational cost.

WM^MLLM: Turning MLLMs into World Models 2026-11-18
📝 25 pages, 14 figures, 8 tables; project page: https://ztmotalee.github.io/wmmllm/
Show abstract

Video world models keep the world in the context of a generator, so a revisit is synthesized again and earlier edits can drift or disappear. We present WM^MLLM, the first interactive world model built on multimodal large language models (MLLMs), for persistent, editable world generation from a single image. WM^MLLM keeps the world state outside every generator, as a panorama with a revision history, and lets the MLLMs write it: they turn each request into operations on this state, select where a local edit is written, and check every candidate before it becomes the next state. Image generators only propose views, and video only presents them. Users can walk to new places and return, edit objects through text or a box, place subjects, and change lighting, weather or season. By construction, a revisit at the same revision reproduces the earlier view exactly, a local edit leaves every panorama pixel outside its region unchanged, and each request starts from the result of the previous one. Against six world model baselines on MBench-A, WM^MLLM has the highest mean on all four environment-recovery metrics (Epipolar 91.5 versus 0.1 to 74.0) and completes the most camera round trips (85.3% versus at most 50.9%). It has the best value in all 15 CompBench columns against 15 baselines. On 112 local edits judged blind, selecting the region to write after generation raises completion from 34.8% to 83.0%. On 258 WBench and MBench-A items, all 46 revisits recover their earlier views, and all 97 local edits that pass the check leave panorama pixels outside their region unchanged.

Worth Another Look:Constrained World-Model Planning for Visual Inspection 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Visual inspection in field robotics and environmental monitoring must localize multiple targets under strict compute and sensing caps. Exhaustive sweeps waste calls on absent classes, while myopic and search-free policies stumble over heterogeneous tariff cliffs or discard viewport edits whose value emerges only after re-querying.
