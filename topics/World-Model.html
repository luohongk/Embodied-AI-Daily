OnlyAct: Extracting Action Representations from Foundation Models for World Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Video world models promise general-purpose interactive simulators, but their training is limited by action supervision: action labels are scarce, fragmented across incompatible specifications, and do not scale with the unlabeled video pretraining corpus. A common workaround jointly trains an action encoder to compress consecutive frames into a latent action and a decoder to reconstruct the next frame from the previous frame and that latent, forcing the latent to capture only new information. This has two limitations: learning the action space from scratch can limit generalization to unseen domains, and the bottleneck trades expressiveness for separation, with tighter settings losing nuance and looser ones risking scene leakage under domain shifts. We argue for a different starting point: rather than learning actions from scratch, we start from a pretrained foundation model (V-JEPA 2), whose embeddings of a frame pair already carry substantial action information after large-scale, diverse video pretraining. The remaining challenge is to extract the action while discarding the scene. We propose a label-free contrastive adaptation framework that pulls together frame pairs sharing the same motion under a different scene and pushes apart pairs from the same scene with different motion, reshaping the geometry of the pretrained space without imposing a reconstruction bottleneck. We introduce a unified evaluation protocol along three axes (action expressiveness, scene invariance, and action-conditioned video generation quality) across robot manipulation, camera navigation, game, and driving benchmarks, most of which are unseen during adaptation. Compared with annotation-free latent-action methods, our representations are substantially more expressive while matching their scene invariance, and they yield world models that transfer demonstrated actions to new scenes more faithfully.

Evaluating LLM-Generated World Models for Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Formal world models can support planning, verification, and safety reasoning, but constructing them from open-ended natural-language situations is difficult, particularly in multi-agent settings where several formalizations may be plausible. We study whether five LLMs can generate such world models as partially observable stochastic games and whether mechanical validity is sufficient to establish that the resulting model faithfully represents the intended decision problem. We introduce a benchmark of 44 scenarios spanning game, situational, and cultural settings and evaluate generated world models along three dimensions: mechanical validity, reference-relative structural agreement, and semantic criteria. Across three empirical tracks, we analyze 410 first-pass generations. In the reference-backed game track, 134/135 generated world models pass a strict mechanical audit, yet mean permutation-invariant structural F1 is only 0.366, with just 28/134 mechanically valid models exactly matching the authored reference. In the cultural track, explicitly naming the country changes the generated structural representation for most LLMs while producing only small changes in automated semantic scores. These results show that executability is a necessary but insufficient criterion for LLM-generated world models: models that are mechanically valid can still differ substantially in the structure used by downstream neuro-symbolic reasoning and planning.

An Energy-Based Perspective on Machine Learning 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Energy-based models (EBMs) define a distribution through a scalar energy, $p_θ(x)\propto\exp(-E_θ(x))$, and are usually presented as one model family among many. This tutorial has a broader goal: beyond introducing EBMs, it uses them as a single lens that connects the different aspects of machine learning. Any EBM is specified by three choices, the energy, which determines what the model represents; the inference procedure, which samples from or optimizes the energy; and the learning method, which fits the energy to data. We first develop these components, including sampling, variational inference, and learning by maximum likelihood, noise contrastive estimation, pseudolikelihood, score matching, and adversarial training, and show that the learning methods share one contrastive structure. We then show that discriminative models, generative models such as GANs, normalizing flows, diffusion and autoregressive models, representation learning methods such as contrastive learning, word2vec, BERT, denoising auto-encoders, and diffusion representations, reinforcement learning with EBM policies and world models, inverse reinforcement learning, and planning, as well as inference-time computation with verifiers, are all EBMs that differ in these three choices, mostly in how they handle the partition function and sampling. This view explains existing methods, organizes them into a common framework, and suggests new algorithms by replacing one component while keeping the others, with composition of energies as a principled way to combine learned models at inference time.

From Video Frames to Robot Meta-Frames: A Unified Latent Interface for World Action Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) offer a powerful foundation for World Action Models (WAMs), which jointly predict robot actions and future observations. Building such models requires injecting low-dimensional robot signals (e.g. proprioception, actions, and values) into a latent space shaped by natural videos, and a key design choice is the interface through which these signals enter: existing schemes treat it as a formatting detail, spending a separate latent frame on each signal or bolting on dedicated encoders and decoders. We argue that signal injection is instead a distribution-alignment problem: how well a representation matches the prior of the pretrained VDMs during denoising. Using the relative reconstruction error of a frozen VDM denoiser as a probe, we find that conventional spatial encodings sit far off this prior, whereas frequency-domain packing yields latents that the denoiser corrects far less. Motivated by this diagnostic result, we propose MetaWAM, which folds all robot signals into a single unified meta-frame: each signal is assigned to a disjoint frequency-domain channel band and lifted to the spatial latent by an inverse DCT, yielding a decodable interface aligned with the pretrained VDM prior without any added module or architectural change. A fixed band layout together with slot-aware routing lets the same meta-frame support policy, world-model, and value prediction over a shared set of robot variables. On RoboCasa, MetaWAM reaches 68.9% success while collapsing the per-signal frames into one and cutting inference latency by 39%, and attains 85.0% success on three real-world Franka tasks. Code and models are available at https://github.com/showlab/MetaWAM

LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning 2026-11-10
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. At deployment, only the policy is retained, operating on RGB observations, language, and robot state without depth input or explicit future prediction. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. Few-Shot Residual Adaptation of Pretrained Latent World Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Pretrained latent world models can support visual planning and control, but their predictions may fail when physical dynamics change at deployment. We study whether such models can adapt from only a few interactions without modifying their pretrained weights or identifying the changed physical parameters. We introduce a few-shot residual adaptation approach that learns self-supervised corrections from latent prediction errors. The corrections are applied both inside the latent predictor and directly to its predicted latent state, while the pretrained world model remains frozen. We evaluate the approach in simulation and on a real robot under center-of-mass and friction shifts. These changes reduce the success of the frozen model to near zero. Using three interaction episodes for adaptation, our dual-site approach recovers 90\% success under the center-of-mass shift and 100\% under the modified friction on the real robot. Ablations show that correcting both locations outperforms single-site adaptation. Our diagnostics further suggest that much of the learned correction reduces a general prediction mismatch, while successful control also depends on shift-specific information captured during adaptation.

Stand-In Effects and Pooling Amplification: Lessons from Biological World Models for Testing Action Effects 2026-11-10
📝 v3: adds a second registered test on two further published world-model families (IRIS, TD-MPC2; OSF j75g2), exploratory studies of score rule, sampling noise, regimes and validation tuning on ERK data, and corrections. 100 pages
Show abstract

Action-conditioned world models are increasingly trained on data from randomized experiments, and whether their predictions respond to actions is usually checked by replacing or removing the actions and measuring the loss in accuracy. Taking biological experiments as the guiding case, we show that such diagnostics report action effects where there are none: under autocorrelated designs, future actions stand in for history the model does not see. When the design law is known, a conditional randomization test written as an e-value is valid in finite samples whatever the model, provided the resampler conditions on the full state the design used. A second failure arises whenever many sites share one action sequence, as in world models: the pooled score turns small model errors along the action contrast into overwhelming evidence against a true effect. In a pixel world, its median log e-value fell from -17.3 to -6832 as frames grew from 64 to 4096 pixels, while the oracle's rose. Tempering the score changes the bet, not the validity. Our own preregistered analysis of optogenetic ERK experiments failed in exactly this way; a tempered statistic, registered before the data were obtained, then rejected the null on a never-opened field (E = 1.1e10). Every single tempering factor we tried, estimated from residuals or tuned on validation data, collapsed in some setting; mixtures over a fixed grid of factors never did. In registered tests on three world-model families trained by others, the pooled test rejected in 0 of 96 units of a diffusion model in which actions mattered, and reached the registered power bar in 10 of 26 Atari games under IRIS's own token likelihood and 1 of 6 control tasks under TD-MPC2; mixtures fixed in advance reached it in all 96 units, 24 of 26 games and 6 of 6 tasks. Under exact nulls the statistics made 4 rejections in 22,848 tests.

GEMOR-GS: Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Photorealistic and metrically consistent world representations are a primary requirement for LiDAR--inertial--visual SLAM systems that serve robots over continuous operation. 3D Gaussian splatting makes such representations attainable by supplying an explicit, renderable, and queryable scene model. We present GEMOR-GS (Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM) to make the Gaussian map geometrically accurate, globally refined, and re-enterable, providing a better appearance for robot perception and decision-making. To improve the 3DGS map, the LiDAR--camera extrinsic is first estimated offline by direct-method alignment over dedicated calibration segments and reused by all following online and offline processes. Under the calibrated geometry, keyframe poses and Gaussian parameters are refined alternately under a gated differentiable-rendering and LiDAR--projection objective. The final global reconstruction is then rebuilt from the refined keyframe schema with all online constraints lifted. For the robot SLAM lifecycle, the method recovers the pose from the 3DGS map after tracking loss through an XFeat bypass map, RANSAC-PnP coarse localization, and Gaussian RGB-D warp refinement. Experiments on one self-collected and five public sequences quantify each element: the offline reconstruction wins all paired keyframes against the online map by 14.3--19.9 dB, improves PSNR by approximately 9 dB over Gaussian-LIC2, and achieves 86--100% held-out relocalization success tied to extrinsic quality. The results show that GEMOR-GS improves the geometric accuracy, appearance fidelity, and re-enterability of the 3DGS map, serving as a dependable robot world model for SLAM and downstream tasks.

Cold-Start Personalization via Bayesian Adaptive Questioning 2026-11-10
📝 26 pages, 4 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

Cold-start personalization requires inferring preferences from minimal interaction when no user-specific historical data is available. The space of possible preferences is vast, yet users care about only a sparse subset and rarely articulate them upfront; combined with limited interaction budgets, this makes preference elicitation challenging. Our key insight is that preferences exhibit predictable structure across populations; e.g., users who want detailed explanations often also value worked examples. We propose CAPE (Cold-start Adaptive Preference Elicitation), which learns a structured world model of preference correlations offline from complete profiles, then performs training-free Bayesian inference online to select informative questions and predict complete preference profiles, including dimensions never asked about. Even simple belief model instantiations (e.g., linear regression) substantially outperform end-to-end RL. Across medical, mathematical, social, and commonsense reasoning, CAPE achieves 80.8% alignment with ground-truth user preferences versus 68.5% for RL, requires 2.5x to over 15x fewer interactions, and adapts its questions twice as often as RL. Our contribution is a principled decomposition of cold-start personalization that makes Bayesian preference elicitation practical at scale for LLM systems.

Distill to Think, Foresee to Act: Cognitive-Physical Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Current end-to-end autonomous driving models are fundamentally constrained by the behavioral cloning ceiling of imitation learning. While reinforcement learning offers a path to smarter autonomy, it demands two missing pieces of infrastructure: (1) a cognitive foundation that understands traffic semantics and driving intent, and (2) a foresighted physical environment that can anticipate the consequences of candidate actions. To this end, we propose CoPhy, a CognitivePhysical reinforcement learning framework for autonomous driving. To distill to think, we distill VLM knowledge into the BEV encoder and then discard the VLM entirely, retaining cognitive ability at zero inference cost while releasing the cognitive channel as a pluggable interface for optional human language commands. To foresee to act, we build an auto-regressive BEV world model that explicitly predicts future semantic maps conditioned on candidate actions, serving as an interpretable physical sandbox from which safety metrics are directly derived. Built upon this dual infrastructure, we optimize the driving policy via GRPO with a novel dual-reward mechanism: a physical reward derived from BEV rollouts enforces hard safety constraints, while a cognitive reward from a language-aligned scorer ensures intent compliance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoPhy not only achieves state-of-the-art results on NAVSIM v1 and v2 benchmarks, but also enables safer driving via cognitively informed scene compliance and flexible intent control through user-defined language instructions. Action-Guided Reachability for Deterministic World Models of Vision-Based Systems 2026-11-10
Show abstract

End-to-end vision-based controllers map raw camera images directly to control actions, yet verifying their closed-loop behavior remains challenging: no analytical model maps a physical state to a camera image, and propagating a set of states through high-dimensional images yields loose reachable sets. We address both challenges with two techniques. First, we propose a Deterministic World Model (DWM), a latent-free decoder that generates camera images only from the physical state, trained with a saliency-weighted reconstruction loss and a control-consistency loss. The DWM replaces the camera in the closed loop, so a sound and tight reachable tube of the surrogate closed loop can be computed without bounding any latent variables. Second, we propose an action-guided partition-and-verify scheme that certifies as many initial sets as possible within a fixed computation budget. A Gaussian process learned from cheap simulations orders the cells and splits those that lie across the property boundary, while every other unknown cell is split along the state dimension that most changes the controller's actions along the simulated closed loop, i.e., the one that most reduces the pixel uncertainty reaching the controller. On a CARLA braking system and three Gym benchmarks, the DWM is more accurate to the real camera than a cGAN surrogate and yields 1.9x to 69x narrower robustness intervals. The vision part causes most of the remaining overapproximation, and our splitting reduces the unknown fraction of the four-dimensional CartPole initial set from 21.88% to 8.18% within the budget.

Does intelligence require the ability to reason about phenomena beyond direct experience? It is natural to suspect that some complex thought cannot be captured through language alone. However, of particular concern to this work, is whether visualising counterfactual events can complement language as a mechanism for complex thought. 📝 64 pages, 5 main figures, 5 supplementary figures

Protein engineering requires navigating rugged fitness landscapes, where small sequence changes can produce large functional differences, but experimental screening can only sample a small fraction of sequence space. Thus, a central challenge is to efficiently traverse the large combinatorial space of mutations in silico. We introduce ProTEUS, a mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution. Pretrained on deep mutational scanning assays, ProTEUS learns a joint representation of sequence, evolutionary, and structural information using MutationFormer, a mutation-biased attention architecture. Variants are represented as states in this joint latent space, and mutations define actions whose functional consequences are predicted. ProTEUS provides a world-model framework that connects virtual evaluation of mutational outcomes with experimental learning for efficient protein evolution.

SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model 2026-11-09
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 (Oral). Project website: https://supermimic.github.io
Show abstract

Tracking large human-motion datasets has become the dominant route to general, expressive humanoid whole-body control, but scaling across many motions is expensive. Current pipelines use model-free reinforcement learning, either merging per-motion specialists through lossy distillation, or brute-forcing a single policy with cluster-scale compute. SuperMimic amortizes learning across all motions with one differentiable world model of their shared dynamics, fit from logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator. The world model and the tracking policy train together in one loop, and backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts gives the policy analytic gradients. Fast imagined rollouts and analytic gradients make policy learning efficient. In 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs. It reduces GPU-hours by more than $1000\times$ and fails on only 25 of 129,514 benchmark motions, versus 179 failures for the baseline. After 9.8 hours on two GPUs, SuperMimic achieves zero failures. From 1 to 264 hours of motion, the world model's prediction error stays stable, and GPU-hours to a target return grow slowly. Our policy transfers to a physical Unitree G1 across diverse and acrobatic motions. SuperMimic removes compute as the bottleneck to scaling humanoid whole-body control, making it practical to measure motion-tracking scaling laws.

Efficient robotic 3D reconstruction requires selecting views that contribute new surface measurements with few physical captures. Pretrained world models can guide this choice, but a predicted surface may not produce valid depth measurements under the operating conditions of the deployed sensor. Building World Models from Intrinsic Motivation 2026-11-09
📝 27 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

In the absence of extrinsic rewards or task supervision, human behavior is guided by intrinsic motivation---neural signals that drive curiosity, creativity, play, and other essential facets of intelligence. Intrinsic motivation plays an important developmental role in building robust world models that enable generalization and adaptation. Yet existing studies leave open how a broad range of intrinsic motivations interacts with world-model representation learning to support downstream control. This limits our understanding of which intrinsically collected experiences build reusable models. To address these gaps, we integrate 24 intrinsic objectives, including established methods and explicit variants spanning curiosity, entropy, and competency, into a common model-based reinforcement learning framework. These findings motivate designing intrinsic motivation and representation learning jointly. Our results demonstrate world models as a powerful tool for measuring the quality of intrinsically motivated experience, and positions intrinsic motivation as a key ingredient for building world models autonomously.

Library of Hands: Fix the Hands, Not the World Model 2026-11-09
📝 27 pages, 8 figures, 20 tables
Show abstract

A world model predicts how a scene changes after an action, and a planner uses it to search for actions that reach a goal. If the camera moves or the controller changes, the same command moves the robot differently: when its commands are turned by 90 degrees, a frozen planner with the LeWM world model that succeeds in 77.5% of episodes succeeds in only 7.8%. Current fixes retrain the model, need training data, or forget a set-up when it returns. We propose Library of Hands (LoH), a training-free method that keeps the world model frozen and corrects only the map from commands to applied actions, which we call the hand. LoH reads the hand through the frozen model, with no extra sensor: it finds the action that best explains the change between two camera images (a reading). It stores every hand it identifies, so a returning set-up is recognised rather than re-learnt. In closed-loop tests, LoH more than doubles success on three simulated tasks, from 28.6% to 62.3%, and loses only 0.6 percentage points when nothing changes. It also works, unchanged, with two other world models and on two of three models pretrained on real robot data, and its gain grows with the quality of its readings.

When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model? 2026-11-09
📝 NeurIPS 2026 camera-ready. 52 pages, 22 figures, 14 tables. Code: https://github.com/klindtlab/lejepa-identifiability
Show abstract

A representation that scrambles the true degrees of freedom of the world cannot support reliable planning or compositional generalization. We prove that LeJEPA (alignment plus Gaussian regularization) linearly recovers the world's latent variables from nonlinear observations, a property known as linear identifiability, in a class of worlds where latents evolve under stationary, additive-noise transitions. Our main result is that among all such worlds, the Gaussian is the unique latent distribution for which this guarantee holds. The forward direction rests on a spectral decomposition in which each degree of nonlinearity is strictly penalized by alignment, making the linear map the optimum; the converse rules out every non-Gaussian alternative. We further prove an approximate identifiability result where the guarantee degrades gracefully, and show that linear, orthogonal identifiability enables optimal latent-space planning. We validate the theory with experiments ranging from 2D examples to 4096-dimensional latents, including ablations, pixel-based robotic control, and natural images from ImageNet-X. Our theory turns an empirically successful recipe into a mathematical guarantee, providing the foundation for building World Models that provably recover the structure of the world.

WanderBench: Can World Models Explore Dynamic Worlds from Video? 2026-11-09
📝 10 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Video world models conditioned on an input video make it possible to explore real, recorded dynamic scenes, enabling re-shooting of footage, free-viewpoint replay, and real-to-sim environments for embodied agents. Such uses require the generated rollout to stay faithful to the recorded world, yet existing benchmarks start from a single image or a text prompt, and video-conditioned generation is evaluated mainly on perceptual quality and camera following. These evaluations do not directly assess whether exploration preserves subject identity, event progression, and scene consistency upon revisiting a location. We introduce WanderBench, a benchmark for exploring dynamic worlds from video. It comprises 745 single-shot source videos of real and AI-generated content in three difficulty tiers (3-25 s), paired with camera routes given as keyboard actions, and evaluates six dimensions with 24 human-validated sub-metrics. Benchmarking eight models, we find that as clips grow longer, models keep the static scene and visual quality but lose camera control and the source dynamics, and that none keeps revisited places consistent. We further propose WanderLand, a training-free framework that conditions each frame of a frozen model on the same-instant source and, on revisits, on its own earlier static content. WanderLand improves five models on every dimension, by up to 8.3 points in source-dynamics fidelity.

Web World Models 2026-11-09
📝 Project Page: https://github.com/Princeton-AI2-Lab/Web-World-Models
Show abstract

Language agents increasingly require persistent worlds in which they can act, remember, and learn.
