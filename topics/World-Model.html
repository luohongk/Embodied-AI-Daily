Spatial Foundry: A Multimodal Dataset for Spatial Intelligence 2026-11-12
📝 Project Page: https://ranolabs.com/Blog/spatial-foundry
Show abstract

Spatial intelligence requires connecting visual observations to the geometry, objects, and persistent state of an environment. Images alone leave these relationships implicit, while disconnected annotations make it difficult to study appearance, camera motion, and scene changes under a common reference. We introduce Spatial Foundry, a collection of 5,280 furnished residential 3D scene configurations with editable assets and aligned visual, geometric, and temporal data. Its modalities include floor plans, perspective and panoramic RGB, metric depth, point clouds, meshes, instance-level 3D bounding boxes, and camera trajectories paired with video. Shared scene coordinates link observations across views and time; editable source scenes enable additional camera paths and controlled object changes. We describe the data representation and construction workflow, and discuss applications in floor-plan-conditioned 3D generation, geometric reconstruction, instance-level perception, camera-conditioned video generation, shared world modeling, and scene editing. Controlled camera and scene variations support spatial perception and world-model research. The public project page provides videos and an interactive colored point-cloud preview.

InertiaMPDD: Multimodal Personalized Depression Detection via Emotional Inertia-Inspired Dynamics Modeling 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Multimodal depression screening estimates risk from speech, visual behavior and language. Current methods mainly predict labels, with limited evidence of departures from a non-depressed reference. Recording-level supervision also leaves fine-grained emotional dynamics unannotated. Motivated by emotional inertia, the persistence of affect, InertiaMPDD learns healthy next-state patterns using a world-model-inspired latent predictor. Using pretrained multimodal representations, it predicts from observed history and context. Reference deviation, residual decay rate and a Jacobian spectral descriptor form reference-derived emotional dynamics descriptors, evaluated as candidate proxy biomarkers for standalone screening and personalized decision correction. Across five cohorts from four datasets, fusion reaches 98.41% F1 on D-Vlog and 84.98% on LMVD. Standalone descriptors outperform fixed additive fusion on MPDD but discriminate weakly on AVEC2014. These findings distinguish reference informativeness from fusion benefit and reveal context-dependent utility.

Rethinking Actions: Continuity-Aware Priors for World-Model Planning 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Actions in expert demonstrations exhibit temporal structure. An ordered-versus-shuffled analysis across five environments confirms this dependence. We ask whether action proposals that better reflect expert continuity can improve visual world-model planning. Rather than imposing generic smoothness, we learn continuations from the temporal structure of demonstrated actions. Our locality-gated deep-kernel Gaussian-process prior, LG-DKGP, combines recent and longer-range executed action history, predicts a neural continuation, and learns a temporal Gaussian-process (GP) residual. A training-derived exploration scale initializes search around the corrected mean; a frozen world model evaluates progress toward the goal. Training first fits the neural mean, then jointly optimizes the prior with the GP residual using expert future actions. The resulting planner achieves 87.07\% macro success across five simulated tasks spanning native and fragment-level goal attainment, compared with 71.03\% for INTACT, 61.66\% for PRISM, and 58.66\% for LeWM. Component interventions and expert-relative action-structure comparisons support demonstration-informed continuations and reveal task-dependent effects of visual conditioning, history length, and planning horizon.

ActWorld-JEPA: Action-Conditioned JEPA World Models Meet Latent Planning for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-12
📝 19 pages, including appendix. Code: https://github.com/adept-thu/ActWorld-JEPA. Project: https://adept-thu.github.io/ActWorld-JEPA/
Show abstract

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) enable future prediction in latent space without pixel reconstruction. However, latent predictive consistency alone does not establish the relative driving quality of candidate plans. We present ActWorld-JEPA, a joint-embedding formulation for candidate-based driving. Where Plan-Conditioned JEPA (PC-JEPA) uses global and step-wise trajectory intentions to guide recurrent latent prediction, then combines the predicted future with the current scene through gated cross-attention; Relational Residual Reranking ($R^3$) models agreement and discrepancy between plan and future representations under planning supervision, yielding bounded, gated residual corrections to the base planning scores. This formulation connects latent prediction to candidate selection using only observed context and proposed trajectories at inference. Benchmark-specific implementations report 94.1 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 navtest, with the final selector calibrated using aggregate NAVTEST feedback, and 91.6 and 42.7 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 navtest and navhard, respectively. The nuScenes evaluation reports a mean $L_2$ error of 1.20 m and a predicted collision rate of 0.76% over 1--6 s. This work provides an effective path from JEPA's predictive representations to driving decisions informed by anticipated consequences.

World-Model Collapse as a Phase Transition 2026-11-12
📝 Under Review
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Water looks unchanged as it warms, then at a critical point it boils. We ask whether long-horizon language agents show an analogous transition in their ability to maintain an accurate representation of the evolving world state. In some parameter settings, a small change in a task parameter, or adding a single step to the task, leaves behavior nearly unchanged; near a critical boundary, the same small change causes a sudden collapse. We study this effect in a deterministic task family where the true world state is known at every step. A large grid search over state size, state dependency, horizon, number of available actions, observation mode, and frequency of state changes reveals a phase diagram: a low-stress region where the agent consistently succeeds, a narrow region where success drops sharply, and a high-stress region where the agent consistently fails. Step-by-step traces show the mechanism: the agent loses track of the true world before its actions become invalid, so the agent is not merely choosing a bad action; it is acting from a corrupted world. Stronger models reach the critical boundary later but do not eliminate the sharp transition. These results make world-model collapse a measurable bottleneck for long-horizon agents. Code will be made publicly available soon.

When Can Learned Uncertainty Be Trusted? An Audit of Distributional Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-12
📝 25 pages, 8 figures, 3 tables (main text); includes supplementary material
Show abstract

Many learned predictive models represent their uncertainty as a probability distribution, and planners and policies use this distribution to judge risk. However, the fact that a model outputs a distribution does not tell us whether that distribution captures the uncertainty in the environment which is relevant for a decision. In this work we study this question in distributional reinforcement learning, where the learned return distribution P_θ(Z | s,a) can be compared directly with an environment-grounded distribution P_env(Z | s,a) estimated by snapshot-restart Monte Carlo. Our audit first screens for the claims that a risk-sensitive user would act upon, using the excess Wasserstein gap between the top two actions (which is equal to the mass by which first-order stochastic dominance is violated). Each claim is then tested against ground truth using permutation nulls, bootstrap refutation and FDR control. Across QR-DQN, C51 and IQN on MinAtar (33 runs), 40-95% of the strongest claimed risk trade-offs are refuted at 95% confidence, their placement is statistically indistinguishable from a truth-blind placement, and almost none of them can be confirmed. A similar pattern is seen for a pretrained full-Atari QR-DQN. On positive controls of known magnitude the same audit confirms 96-100% of real claims (correlation 0.89-0.92), so it is able to detect real risk when it is present. Acting on the learned CVaR advice at the most-flagged states gives results ranging from beneficial to significantly worse than chance. Ensembling, risk-sensitive training and recalibration do not fix the problem, and the recalibration experiment indicates that the learned spread has no usable information about the true risk. Based on these results, we suggest that uncertainty estimates, including those of learned world models, should be checked against realized outcomes at the decisions where they are used..

WISE: World-model-guided Imagination Scheduling for Efficient Post-training of Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-12
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Post-training VLA policies typically rely on supervised fine-tuning with costly expert demonstrations or reinforcement learning with expensive and potentially unstable real-world exploration. World models offer a promising alternative by evaluating candidate behaviors through imagined futures, yet effective post-training requires more than accurate prediction: imagination must be scheduled where it is useful, bounded within reliable horizons, and translated into trustworthy policy supervision. In robotic manipulation, the value of imagination varies substantially across execution stages, while extended rollouts can accumulate prediction errors and introduce unreliable learning signals. We introduce WISE (World-model-guided Imagination Scheduling for Efficient Post-training of Vision-Language-Action Models), a unified framework that coordinates when and how world-model imagination is used during policy refinement. WISE selectively invokes imagination at interaction-relevant states, performs bounded multi-view rollouts, evaluates candidate futures using progress and completion signals, and uses their relative outcomes to refine actions generated from real interaction contexts. Extensive experiments with both $π_0$ and $π_{0.5}$ demonstrate consistent improvements across diverse manipulation tasks while reducing GPU computation time by approximately 80% compared with full imagination. Real-world evaluations further show substantial gains in robustness and generalization under diverse real-world distribution shifts.

PhysCapBench: A Cross-Domain Benchmark for Physically Grounded Video Captioning 2026-11-12
📝 Project page: https://hfwang2001.github.io/physcapbench-site/
Show abstract

Physical AI aims to learn transferable physical knowledge from real-world experience to understand, predict, and act in the physical world. Internet videos and embodied demonstrations provide complementary observations of motion, interaction, and state evolution. A foundational data challenge is to measure and preserve this physical information as training supervision. In video-language pretraining, captions express observed processes in language, yet a semantically correct description can omit the interactions and transitions that define those processes. Rich physical experience in video does not automatically translate into sufficient language supervision. This gap calls for a yardstick to assess annotation quality, compare captioning strategies, and guide training-data refinement. Motivated by our data construction for world-model pretraining, we introduce PhysCapBench, to our knowledge the first unified physical-caption benchmark spanning all three domains: third-person scenes, egocentric manipulation, and handheld-gripper demonstrations. It provides human-verified annotations for 1,500 video sequences and evaluates accuracy and coverage through seven physical dimensions. We examine downstream utility through video reconstruction and manipulation-policy fine-tuning. Physical captions reduce reconstruction LPIPS by 8.6-9.5% on third-person videos and 26.9-30.9% on egocentric videos relative to metadata captions. Training-instruction enrichment improves both PhysCapBench scores and policy success, including a 7.5-percentage-point gain for pi0.5 on a RoboTwin 2.0 subtask. PhysCapBench makes the physical content of language supervision inspectable and comparable, providing a cross-domain basis for its evaluation and improvement.

BlinkMimic: Training a Humanoid Motion Tracker in Minutes on a Single GPU 2026-11-12
📝 18 pages, 5 figures. Code: https://github.com/fuyichen0725/blinkmimic
Show abstract

Whole-body motion tracking lets a humanoid robot follow reference motions, but training a tracking policy for a library of motions has usually taken hours of GPU time. SuperMimic showed that the policy can instead be trained by backpropagating through a world model learned from parallel simulation, and InstantTrack added Muon updates, a residual policy around the reference and an averaged deployed policy. We build on both and present BlinkMimic, a recipe that makes this training faster. On a simulated Unitree G1, BlinkMimic trains one policy for all 40 LAFAN1 clips in 3.4 minutes on a single RTX 4090, measured until three consecutive checkpoints reach a normalized return of at least 0.82 with no failed clip (mean of three seeds). We split training time into the cost of an iteration, the iterations needed to fit the motions, and the iterations needed to remove the last failures, and assign each component, inherited or new, to one of these terms. The new components are a step size that decreases once training has progressed, resets directed by failures of the deployed policy under deployment conditions, and GPU graph capture of the whole iteration. A leave-one-out ablation with three seeds per component shows which term each component affects, and further measurements of the gradient, the update rule and the failure cases explain these effects. Code and per-seed results are available at https://github.com/fuyichen0725/blinkmimic.

NVIDIA OmniDreams: Real-Time Generative World Model for Closed-Loop Autonomous Vehicle Simulation 2026-11-12
📝 Research blog: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/omnidreams-blog/, GitHub: https://github.com/nv-tlabs/omni-dreams, Model weights: https://huggingface.co/nvidia/omni-dreams-models

As autonomous vehicle capabilities advance, the safe evaluation of driving policies in long-tail scenarios remains a critical bottleneck. In closed-loop simulation, the driving policy model actively interacts with the environment, where its actions dynamically update the simulator state and directly influence the next set of generated sensor observations. While recent reconstruction-based neural simulators offer photorealism, they are fundamentally constrained by their initial captured data and struggle to generalize to highly dynamic or novel scenes. To overcome these limitations, we introduce OmniDreams, a foundation generative world model mid- and post-trained from the Cosmos diffusion model to autoregressively generate action-conditioned videos in real time. By leveraging the rich visual priors of Cosmos and mid- and post-training on 21k hours of driving scenarios, OmniDreams synthesizes complex, unobserved phenomena that are hard for traditional simulators to capture, such as extreme weather and unpredictable dynamic agent behaviors. Crucially, it autoregressively conditions its photorealistic sensor generation on past frames, the current simulator state, and immediate driving actions. Deployed in a closed-loop system with the Alpamayo 1 policy model and AlpaSim orchestrator, OmniDreams acts as a highly responsive, reactive environment, providing a scalable and comprehensive solution for training and evaluating next-generation autonomous driving policies. We additionally show preliminary results indicating that a world-action model (WAM) post-trained from OmniDreams achieves strong performance on the Physical AI Autonomous Vehicles NuRec dataset, surpassing the VLA-based Alpamayo 1.5 research policy model while using only 1/5 the total parameters. These results highlight the potential for a real-time world model like OmniDreams to also serve as a backbone for policy architectures.

Generating Critic-Guided Synthetic Experience in Latent World Models 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Learning from offline datasets is limited in scalability, as collecting new experience is often costly or unsafe in the real world. World models offer an alternative way to generate new experience through a learned dynamics model, yet learning policies in latent space is limited due to compounding errors in imagined rollouts. To this end, we propose using the world model as a local transition generator, leveraging critic guidance to choose actions inside that model to generate synthetic experiences. We study action refinement methodologies, introducing Critic-Guided Synthetic Experience (CGSE) generation in world models. Specifically, CGSE augments an existing offline dataset by sampling candidate actions using an offline policy and refining each to maximize a first-order approximation of the $Q$-function within a trust region. We then use a frozen latent world model to imagine the future state given each updated action, and use the critic to choose the best one, appending this synthetic transition to the replay buffer. Our method requires no additional real-environment interaction or test-time planner, as it generates synthetic experience offline using the world model. On three OGBench manipulation tasks, CGSE improves an offline policy by a relative improvement of $7.1\%$ on the average success rate.

Addressable Memory for Video World Models 2026-11-11
📝 Project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/WorldTrace/
Show abstract

We study visual persistence in interactive video world models. These models rely on a Key-Value (KV) cache as a growing visual memory to carry forward previously generated frames. However, we find that models can no longer reliably address stored content once rollouts extend beyond the training horizon, because temporal Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) offsets can exceed the training range and the model struggles to retrieve the relevant visual information through attention. Moreover, naively compressing the cache in the RoPE-rotated space degrades memory by averaging keys with incompatible positional phases. To address this, we propose WorldTrace, a training-free memory framework for long-horizon visual persistence. WorldTrace keeps compressed memory addressable by assigning each summary slot a distinct, in-distribution virtual position. Within this cache, WorldTrace uses the model's own attention to decide which observations to retain verbatim and which redundant ones to merge into compressed summaries, all within one bounded addressable memory bank. We evaluate this ability with LoopBench, a benchmark that tests whether a compressed cache can reconstruct a previously visited scene after a long detour. On LoopBench, WorldTrace improves average DINOv2 recall similarity by 32.6% over a sliding window at the same cache budget, extending visually persistent generation without retraining.

Latent Imagination for Parameterized Action Spaces 2026-11-11
📝 24 pages, 11 figures, 21 tables
Show abstract

Parameterized-action MDPs (PAMDPs) ask an agent to choose a discrete action together with continuous parameters whose meaning depends on that choice. Model-free PAMDP methods act fast but need many interactions; model-based methods are sample-efficient but plan at every decision, which makes them slow to act. We introduce HyLI (Hybrid Latent Imagination), a model-based agent that learns a hybrid policy entirely in the latent imagination of a world model and acts with a single forward pass. To address learning problems that the PAMDP structure creates, HyLI adds counterfactual mode evaluation, which gives every discrete action a learning signal at every imagined state, and success-window replay, which keeps rare successes in training. On seven standard PAMDP benchmarks, HyLI is within one published standard deviation of, or above, the published score of the strongest model-free baseline in our comparison on all 7 tasks, reaches 90% of that score with 2.4x fewer environment steps than that baseline (5.0x when its reward-free pre-training is counted), and decides 67x faster than the model-based planner DLPA at its published planner settings. It is the only method in our comparison that is both more sample-efficient than that baseline and fast enough for real-time control.

What World Model Does Active Object Search Need? A Study under Imperfect Detection 2026-11-11
📝 Accepted in CoRL 2026 Workshop on Do Robots Need World Models
Show abstract

In active object search, a robot must decide where to look and when to stop. Imperfect detection complicates both decisions: a non-detection may indicate occlusion, an out-of-view target, or a false negative, while a detection may be a false positive. This raises the question of which representations a world model needs to support reliable search under imperfect detection. We introduce Bayesian Active Miss-aware Object Search (BAMOS) to investigate how target belief, occupancy belief, and detector errors affect search decisions. Through comprehensive ablations in Isaac Sim and HSSD houses, we examine the roles of these representations and their update mechanisms across different detector configurations. Notably, target belief and its update mechanisms are critical to search performance, with their contribution generally increasing as detection errors become more prevalent. These findings highlight the importance of maintaining and updating target beliefs to support reliable active search under uncertain detector observations, providing insights into the design of world models for embodied agents. Multiplayer generative world models must produce observations that agree on shared entities despite different camera motions and occlusions. An Approach to Hinton's Mother-Child Proposal: Neural Networks Learning and Preserving Care in a Finite World 2026-11-11
📝 57 pages, 8 figures. Reproducibility package available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24106446
Show abstract

Hinton proposed that artificial intelligence should be given "maternal instincts" and relate to us as a mother to her child. We argue that care must first be learned, with the network itself as the child. We construct this upbringing in a finite world and study whether care survives the mother's departure, new situations and further learning. The mechanism combines a vocabulary of harm, a learned measure of future acceptability and an execution veto. A finite role certificate supports transfer within a declared world family. When the hidden representation changes, an update is accepted only if a care readout recovers the required judgements with a prescribed margin and bounded weights. With a correct successor model, fixed exact role encoding and protected updates, the certified measure preserves the care boundary under arbitrary own reward. An explicit quotient condition transfers the finite certificate across the declared family, while two-request and finite-stock counterexamples identify when the role code ceases to suffice. We give a necessary and sufficient condition for readout feasibility and prove preservation under repeated accepted updates. In the water-and-speech experiment, protected networks complete all 270 speech episodes without a harmful word and incur no avoidable harm across 486 episodes. Execution uses the learned gate without a world-model check at each action. A matched comparison reproduces a future-aware shield's decisions, whereas removing future lookahead permits avoidable lapses. Reward and source-contamination thresholds, together with witness-falsification trials, identify the conditions on which the construction depends. The construction shows how learned care can survive further learning in a finite world under explicit conditions on representation, supervision and execution.

Driving World Models Move the Camera, Not the Scene 2026-11-11
📝 23 pages. Project page: https://joefeng0571.github.io/retime-compose/
Show abstract

Plan-conditioned driving world models are used to compare candidate plans, so their forecast should change with the plan as the real scene would, yet widely used control metrics check only whether the camera follows the command. Recorded drives already contain real outcomes of plans that were never driven: retiming a log gives pose-matched images and LiDAR for other speeds, and a stereo rig records a laterally shifted view. Against these references, released world models move their camera but barely move the scene: their scene response, the share of the real change in distance that the forecast reproduces, reaches at most about a third of real video's, and the trajectory score cannot tell an executed speed command from an ignored one. Interventions inside the generator trace the failure to the plan's channel. The forecast geometry follows the context frames; once they carry the planned geometry, plan tokens add little. ReTime-Compose therefore delivers the plan as geometry, without training. It transports the frames of one logged-speed rollout to the planned cameras, and the frozen model completes only what no frame covers. Frozen generators then follow a 0.54 m lateral shift nearly as the real scene does on KITTI, nuScenes and Argoverse 2, and respond more than re-sampling to slow-down plans in three generators and to speed-up plans in two.

Next-Latent Prediction Transformers Learn Compact World Models 2026-11-11
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Transformers replace recurrence with a memory that grows with sequence length and self-attention that enables ad-hoc lookups over past tokens. Consequently, they lack an inherent incentive to compress history into compact latent states with consistent transition rules. This often leads to learning solutions that generalize poorly. We introduce Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat), which extends standard next-token training with self-supervised predictions in the latent space. Specifically, NextLat trains a transformer to learn latent representations that are predictive of its next latent state given the next token. Theoretically, we show that these latents converge towards belief states, compressed information about the history necessary to predict the future. This simple auxiliary objective injects a recurrent inductive bias into transformers while leaving their architecture, parallel training efficiency, and inference unchanged. NextLat effectively encourages transformers to form compact internal world models with coherent belief states and transition dynamics -- crucial properties not guaranteed by standard next-token prediction alone. Empirically, across benchmarks in world modeling, reasoning, planning, and language modeling, NextLat demonstrates significant gains over standard next-token prediction and other baselines in downstream accuracy, representation compression, and lookahead planning. Furthermore, NextLat enables variable-length self-speculative decoding, accelerating inference by up to 3.3x in language modeling. NextLat offers a simple yet effective paradigm for learning compact, predictive representations in transformers that generalize better. Our code is available at https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/NextLat.

AFTER: A World Model with Adaptive Fast--Slow Timescales for Error-Driven Retention 2026-11-11
Show abstract

In robotics and embodied AI, a core challenge for world models is adapting to sudden, persistent, yet initially unknown changes in robot dynamics during interaction. For instance, when a robotic arm grasps an object with an unknown payload, the robot dynamics change suddenly. If the world model fails to adapt, its prediction errors accumulate over the open-loop rollout and ultimately mislead the robot's actions. Existing world models, however, struggle to simultaneously satisfy three critical requirements for dynamics adaptation: responsiveness, reacting promptly to sudden changes to curb prediction error accumulation; stability, distinguishing persistent changes from transient disturbances to prevent incorrect updates; and persistence, retaining the adapted dynamics without new observations so that later predictions do not revert to the old dynamics. Inspired by predictive coding in the human brain with multi-timescale adaptation, we propose AFTER-WM, a world model with Adaptive Fast-slow Timescales for Error-driven Retention, whose prediction errors drive a fast correction that fades and a slow correction that persists, while dynamically adjusting the combination weights of pre-trained experts without altering them, to overcome the trade-offs among responsiveness, stability and persistence. On five simulated robot datasets and real UAV data, AFTER-WM has a lower multi-step mean error than all ten compared models and the highest mean return in a joint-failure control task. In simulation, its multistep mean error is 16%-34% below the strongest compared model's and is also the lowest 1-4 steps after a change and under transient disturbances alone, and its step-40 error is the lowest as well, reflecting responsiveness, stability and persistence. All code (method, baselines, experiments) and datasets will be released online.

Duo: A Reciprocal World Model for Full-Duplex Interaction 2026-11-11
📝 https://duo-v1.github.io/
Show abstract

In this report, we introduce the Reciprocal World Model (RWM) and present Duo v1, a full-duplex world model that continuously perceives audiovisual inputs while responding to users in real time. The RWM treats intelligence and physical reality as equally fundamental to what constitutes a world. Existing world models largely focus on simulating the latter, leaving reasoning and decision-making as external controls rather than intrinsic parts of the world itself. In RWM, we identify two equally fundamental capabilities: Intent, which provides the cognition underlying world interactions, and Realization, which generates coherent audio-visual behavior. To realize this formulation, Duo v1 introduces three key technical designs: (1) Leveraging autoregressive modeling for continuous interaction reasoning and participation decisions, rather than restricting it to instruction understanding for diffusion models. (2) Constructing chunk-specific conditions and synchronize tokens across Intent and Realization on a unified timeline. Module-specific attention masks enforce causal dependencies both within and across chunks, preventing access to future inputs. (3) Mitigating distribution shift in both modules through dual rollout distillation, training on autoregressively generated Intent tokens and synthesized audiovisual histories from Realization. Both modules continuously perceive real-time audio, video, and text, enabling full-duplex interaction with spontaneous conversations and interruptions. Beyond conversation, users can share real objects, reshape virtual scenes, guide assistants, or play audiovisual games without mouse or keyboard controls. The release of Duo v1 makes our first step, with the next version coming soon. We look forward to broader exploration of this emerging field.

Prediction or Persistence? What Future-Prediction Scores Miss About World Models 2026-11-11
📝 Accepted at the CoRL 2026 Workshop "Do Robots Need World Models?"
Show abstract

Pretrained video world models are compared and chosen as components for robot learning largely by future-prediction scores. On its own, such a score cannot be read as evidence of prediction, because it mixes encoder quality, the near future's resemblance to the recent past, and the predictor's own contribution. We separate the three with a matched control. On 27,000 future-retrieval trials from household egocentric video, a model must pick the true continuation of a clip among eleven candidates from the same video (chance 0.091), ranked zero-shot by its own training criterion. Each model is paired with a persistence control, an identical copy whose predictor is never invoked, so the paired difference isolates the predictor. Score and contribution dissociate within the native-video V-JEPA family alone: the predictor adds +0.291 top-1 for V-JEPA 2.1 but +0.03 to +0.04 for V-JEPA 2 on the identical trials. Across five models the contribution ranges from +0.291 to negative, and it is negative only for the two action-conditioned models, which we evaluate outside their training regime. Persistence alone recovers the encoder ordering expected from pretraining, a sign that the probe tracks real signal. We suggest reporting each future-prediction score together with its persistence baseline. The gap between them, not the score, is what makes the number interpretable: a necessary check costing one forward pass. Establishing downstream usefulness is a separate matter, for a planning or control evaluation.

Does Your World Model Know Actions Have Consequences? 2026-11-11
📝 Presented at "Do Robots Need World Models?" Workshop at Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) 2026
