CoTAMP: Cooperative Task and Motion Planning for Quadruped Robots with Large Language Models 2026-11-25
📝 8 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

Some environments cannot be traversed by a single quadruped robot, for example a ledge that one robot can climb only from its partner's back or a gap that must be bridged with boxes. Solving them requires cooperative task and motion planning (TAMP). Previous works are limited to the motions of a single robot or only select actions from a predefined set of skills. We propose a framework with two parts: task planning that invents motions not defined in advance, and motion generation that realizes the invented motions. Both parts consist of large language model (LLM)-based modules and code-based modules. Task planning divides the trajectory of the whole task into steps and fixes the robot poses at the step boundaries. Motion generation produces keyframes that describe the motion of each step and turns them into a continuous trajectory through kinematic synthesis. The code-based modules check every plan and motion and return failures to the LLM-based modules for correction. The whole trajectory is then refined by trajectory optimization to satisfy the dynamics. On three simulated courses with two RAIBO2 robot models, our framework produced kinematically valid references in all 30 trials, all of which converged in trajectory optimization. Compared with several baselines, our framework showed advantages in success rate, dynamic feasibility, execution time, and generation time and cost. Project page: https://woojin8098.github.io/CoTAMP/

Follow-Bench 2.0: An End-to-End 3D Benchmark for Socially-Aware Robot Person Following 2026-11-24
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (ED Track). Project page: https://medlartea.github.io/follow-bench-v2/
Show abstract

Socially-aware robot person following (RPF) requires a mobile robot to follow a designated person in dynamic human environments while maintaining target identity, avoiding obstacles and pedestrians, and preserving socially comfortable formations. Existing benchmarks only partially evaluate this coupled problem: embodied visual tracking benchmarks emphasize target visibility with limited social interaction, person re-identification benchmarks study perception without closed-loop control, and motion-planning benchmarks often assume ground-truth target and pedestrian states. We introduce Follow-Bench 2.0, an end-to-end 3D benchmark for evaluating socially-aware RPF under coupled perception--planning challenges. Built in Unreal Engine, Follow-Bench 2.0 provides difficulty-leveled scenarios with diverse pedestrian flows, lighting and weather conditions, cluttered layouts, bottlenecks, queueing behaviors, distractors, and temporary target occlusions. The benchmark supports modular perception--planning pipelines, end-to-end visual policies, and foundation-model-based active trackers under a unified closed-loop protocol. It further evaluates both back- and side-following configurations and reports safety--comfort metrics covering task success, target visibility, target recovery, social-space intrusion, following formation, and motion smoothness. Experiments show that RPF-oriented modular pipelines substantially outperform current end-to-end visual policies on close and socially comfortable following, but also reveal that socially-aware RPF remains far from solved in interaction-heavy environments and especially in side-following. Follow-Bench 2.0 therefore provides a diagnostic platform for studying how perception errors, occlusions, crowd interactions, and following configurations jointly affect robot person following.

📝 8 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) delivery is an important application of the low-altitude economy, enhancing the flexibility and accessibility of last-mile logistics. Existing UAV delivery methods generally rely on precise terminal coordinates, predefined terminal specifications, or task-specific localization procedures, limiting the scope and flexibility of delivery. As UAV logistics becomes more intelligent, open-ended semantic descriptions offer a new paradigm for specifying terminal destinations. However, conventional aerial navigation methods have limited ability to generalize to delivery scenarios that require open-vocabulary terminal navigation. To enable this broader delivery paradigm, we propose CoFiNav, a VLM-guided coarse-to-fine UAV semantic navigation framework that progressively grounds relation-defined terminal destinations through hierarchical semantic search and active 3D observation. A geometry-aware module converts the semantic observation request into feasible viewpoints using RGB images and LiDAR, while conventional motion planning handles safe execution. Experiments across multiple urban environments show that CoFiNav outperforms representative adapted baselines in success rate while requiring shorter flight paths and fewer observations.

Vector-Quantized World Model: Latent Feedback Motion Planning for Robot Manipulation 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Visual world models have shown strong potential for robot planning, yet long-horizon manipulation remains challenging due to compounding prediction errors, large search complexity, and latent plans that the low-level controller cannot reliably reach. In this work, we introduce Vector-Quantized World Model (VQ-WM), a latent feedback motion planning framework that integrates discrete long-horizon search with continuous visual feedback control: a discrete latent predictor models transitions between vector-quantized latent states, allowing the planner to look far ahead and find a shortest path to the goal through discrete search and a continuous learned controller sequentially reaches the planned states through visual feedback, correcting deviations to mitigate compounding errors. Whereas most vector quantization uses Euclidean distance, we use a novel estimator of the first-passage-time as a quasi-metric, which aligns the planning abstraction with the controller's capabilities. We evaluate on the physical robot manipulation of the Tower of Hanoi puzzle: whereas existing state-of-the-art foundation models (VLAs, WAMs) only succeed when the solution trace in their training dataset, our method achieves 100\% success rate with only short-play data, demonstrate its ability to compose long-horizon solutions beyond the short demonstrated trajectories and execute them reliably. Our solution also outperforms strongest world-models by 13.3--85.3 percentage points on simulation benchmarks spanning manipulation and locomotion, demonstrating consistent progress and new capabilities.

Real-Time Kinodynamic Multi-Agent Motion Planning 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Coordinating large robot fleets, from warehouses to quadrotor swarms, requires collision-free trajectories that satisfy dynamic constraints and stay within speed and acceleration bounds. Existing kinodynamic multi-agent planners enforce these bounds during the search or through trajectory optimization after it, which limits fleet size and speed. We present Tickwise Actions to Kinodynamic Trajectories (TAKT), a framework that accounts for the dynamics before the search and reduces planning to a lattice search for double-integrator robots. TAKT analytically bounds the clearance lost when a discrete plan is smoothed and reserves this margin on the lattice. A closed-form convolution provably converts any valid plan into dynamically feasible trajectories with continuous-time collision avoidance. For robots spanning many lattice vertices, we introduce a diamond formulation that permits one-tick sideways yielding and adapt the Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) solvers PIBT and LaCAM to it. With 50 robots, our planners run over 2,000 times faster than the strongest baseline in 2D halls and over 100 times in 3D, with comparable or lower makespan and cost. They plan about a thousand robots in seconds in 2D, and tens of seconds in 3D, and replan up to a thousand robots in real time.

Decision-making and motion planning constitute critical components for ensuring the safety and efficiency of autonomous vehicles (AVs). Existing methodologies typically adopt two paradigms: decision then planning or generation then scoring. However, the former architecture often suffers from decision-planning misalignment that incurs risky situations. Meanwhile, the latter struggles to balance short-term operational metrics (e.g., immediate motion smoothness) with long-term tactical goals (e.g., route efficiency), resulting in myopic or overly conservative behaviors. GeoSDF-Diff: Geometry-Aware Diffusion Planning for Collision-Free Trajectories 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Diffusion models have recently attracted increasing attention in motion planning due to their ability to model high-dimensional and multi-modal trajectory distributions. However, existing diffusion-based planners still struggle to reliably satisfy geometric feasibility in unseen cluttered environments with complex obstacle layouts. To address this challenge, we propose GeoSDF-Diff, a geometry-aware diffusion planner that incorporates multi-scale SDF conditioning into trajectory denoising. The key idea is to provide the denoiser with both route-level geometric context and fine local clearance evidence, enabling generated trajectories to better adapt to unseen complex obstacle layouts. Specifically, a Geometric Encoder extracts multi-scale SDF features and injects them into a DiT-X denoiser through cross-attention. We further introduce a trajectory-level training objective that combines SDF clearance, violation emphasis, smoothness, and path-length regularization, so that local infeasibilities are penalized during training rather than corrected only at inference time. Experiments on held-out cluttered environments show that, without post-hoc trajectory repair or sampling-time guidance, GeoSDF-Diff achieves a 65.2% average success rate across diverse test scenarios, outperforming strong diffusion baselines by 25.4% to 28.5%.

Verify Before You Generate: Sketch-Guided Efficient Motion-Planning for Video Generation 2026-11-20
📝 Accepted by WACV 2027, website: https://sketchverify.github.io/
Show abstract

Recent video generation approaches increasingly rely on planning intermediate control signals like object trajectories to improve temporal coherence and motion fidelity. However, these methods mostly employ single-shot plans that are typically limited to simple motion instructions, leading to suboptimal motion quality, or iterative refinement which requires multiple calls to the video generator and incurs high computational cost. To address this trade-off, we propose SketchVerify, a training-free framework whose key insight is to verify motion plans using lightweight video sketches before invoking the expensive video generator. This lightweight planning space enables efficient comparison of multiple candidate trajectories and bounded iterative refinement before a single final video-generation pass. Given a prompt and a reference image, SketchVerify samples multiple candidate trajectories and renders each as a lightweight video sketch by compositing the moving objects over a static background. A vision-language verifier then ranks these sketches according to instruction alignment and visual motion consistency, allowing unsuitable trajectories to be rejected before full video synthesis. The motion plan is iteratively refined until a satisfactory one is identified, then passed to a trajectory-conditioned generator for final synthesis. Experiments on WORLDMODELBENCH and PHYWORLDBENCH demonstrate consistent improvements in instruction following and temporally coherent, visually plausible motion. Using the same ATI-14B generator, SketchVerify improves the aggregate WorldModelBench score from 7.71 to 8.71. Compared with full-video iterative refinement, it achieves a 13.1x planning-stage speedup and a 3.6x end-to-end speedup.

Staleness Deadlines for Motion Plans during Onboard Reasoning 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Language models may enable general autonomy in robot planning, but onboard their reasoning can run for most of a minute while the robot and nearby pedestrians keep moving. The robot must then decide how long to follow a plan that the pending answer may overturn. Here we present staleness deadlines, which hand that decision to the robot's clock. Calibrated prediction regions for pedestrians and deviation tubes for the robot set the deadline. When the language model answers, a graph planner rebuilds the route, which the robot adopts only after it passes the same clearance check against walls and predicted pedestrians. Exact binomial tests determine which prespecified budget-selection policies keep close calls, wrong decisions and late arrivals below their targets at each inference profile. Candidate policies either fix one budget or set each query's budget from how long the robot can continue its plan before it must brake. We study corridor signs that close routes and delivery requests whose destination the language model must infer. In 81,000 simulated missions with recorded Qwen3-4B answers, full reasoning cuts wrong decisions about twentyfold, and tracking until the deadline cuts late arrivals by up to 64%. The robot never collides while it moves, although pedestrians who never yield still walk into the stopped robot in 23% of offline and 32% of live missions. On a Jetson AGX Orin under perception load, short budgets fail the wrong-decision target, and full reasoning the lateness target. Between these extremes, a fixed-budget policy passes for corridor signs and per-query policies pass for deliveries. Live Orin runs match offline replay on all three losses in 24 of the 32 missions where either run records a loss. The deadline turns slower reasoning into measurable mission outcomes, which let designers test how much reasoning each inference profile can support.

Search-Informed MPPI for Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Monte Carlo Tree Expansion 2026-11-19
📝 24 pages, 12 figures, 2 tables. Project page with videos: https://ihabmohamed.github.io/SI-MPPI/
Show abstract

Kinodynamic motion planning in large-scale, cluttered environments remains challenging, as dynamically feasible, collision-free trajectories must be computed efficiently over long horizons. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) can strategically explore long-horizon alternatives but faces large branching factors in continuous control spaces and often relies on random default-policy rollouts, whereas Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) efficiently optimizes local trajectories yet remains susceptible to obstacle-induced local minima. To overcome these challenges, we propose Search-Informed MPPI (SI-MPPI), a unified framework that embeds MPPI trajectory optimization within MCTS-based tree search. The core concept is to fuse the expansion and default-policy simulation stages of MCTS into a single MPPI expansion stage guided by structured intermediate targets, termed baits. During each expansion, an adaptive bait-handling rule retains geometrically valid baits as local targets for GPU-batched MPPI optimizations that generate dynamically feasible candidate motion segments. Feasibility gating then checks these candidates for collision safety and sufficient progress, and top-$k$ selection admits the lowest-cost accepted candidates as tree edges, yielding sparse, directed tree growth; thereafter, effective sample size (ESS)-guided adaptation warm-starts subsequent expansions, while cost-based backpropagation updates tree statistics. Extensive simulations across four large-scale, cluttered 2D occupancy-grid maps demonstrate that SI-MPPI consistently produces the smallest search trees and smoothest angular control profiles compared with three kinodynamic baselines, while maintaining competitive planning times and path lengths. Ablation results further highlight the importance of GPU batching, adaptive bait handling, and retaining multiple expansion candidates for planning efficiency and robustness.

PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced rapidly in language-conditioned robotic manipulation, yet existing benchmarks mainly evaluate gripper--object contact and largely ignore how objects interact with the environment through gravity, friction, inertia, and mass distribution. As a result, it remains unclear whether current policies truly understand physics or merely interpolate from their training data. We address this gap through the lens of dynamic extrinsic dexterity and introduce PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark in which a robot must infer an object's mass, center of mass, and friction to send it to a destination beyond its kinematic workspace. PhyDEX contains 35K trajectories (143 hours) across nine tasks and 100 scenes, covering uniform, irregular, and dynamic-mass objects with static, multiple, and moving destinations. Our analysis reveals two key bottlenecks: limited robustness of pretrained models, which causes most failures before the throw, and physical blindness, as providing ground-truth physical properties roughly doubles success. Experiments on a humanoid robot and in the real world show the same failure patterns. We hope PhyDEX will drive the development of physically grounded robot policies.

RMRRT: Riemannian Barrier Metric RRT for Inequality-Aware Steering on Equality Manifolds 2026-11-19
📝 20 pages, 10 figures, 8 tables. Accepted to IEEE Transactions on Robotics (T-RO). Project page: https://rcilab.khu.ac.kr/rmrrt/
Show abstract

This paper presents a motion planning framework that unifies equality and inequality constraints within a single geometric formulation for sampling-based planning in high-dimensional robotic systems. In conventional sampling-based planners, equality constraints are typically enforced through projection, whereas inequality constraints are handled separately through binary validity checks such as collision testing, often leading to inefficient exploration. To address this limitation, we propose Riemannian Barrier Metric RRT (RMRRT), which constructs a unified local geometry for planning on equality-constrained manifolds. RMRRT first builds an ambient barrier metric from inequality-sensitive barrier terms and then induces a tangent-space metric via a G-orthogonal projection associated with the equality constraints. The resulting tangent-space metric is used consistently in both steering and nearest-neighbor selection, biasing exploration away from nearby inequality boundaries while preserving first-order equality consistency. In this work, the metric is instantiated from signed-distance-based geometric proxy inequalities to provide collision-informative tangent-space directions; hard feasibility is enforced separately through standard validity checks. Experimental results show that RMRRT achieves a 100% success rate across diverse constrained manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world settings, while reducing planning time relative to representative constrained planning baselines. Ablation studies further demonstrate that the proposed metric improves exploration quality by reducing rejected samples and shortening path length. Experiment videos and source code are available at: https://rcilab.khu.ac.kr/rmrrt/

📝 Project page: https://spatial-trajectory-diffusion.github.io/
Show abstract

Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) requires trajectories that avoid environmental obstacles and collisions between robots in a shared workspace. Diffusion-based planners often address obstacle avoidance through sampling-time guidance, while some conditioned approaches aggregate map geometry into a global representation. We present Spatially Conditioned Trajectory Diffusion (SCTD), a map-conditioned trajectory generator for multi-robot planning. SCTD retains multiple position-aware occupancy tokens accessed through cross-attention throughout a temporal U-Net, with hard endpoint inpainting enforcing start and goal states. The resulting multi-robot system achieves higher team success than the compared diffusion baselines, with experiments covering teams of up to 15 robots.

Modeling the Frictional Contact of Continuum Robots with Environments through Linear Complementarity Problem 2026-11-18

Show abstract

Accurate friction modeling in continuum robots is essential for precise motion control and path planning. However, current approaches make predictions only for the mechanism's internal friction that is limited by the boundary forces of the Coulomb friction cone, which fail to characterize the static friction and motion hysteresis. In this paper, we propose a novel friction model based on complementarity constraints under the Coulomb friction assumption. EmbodiedLab: Beyond Manipulation, Benchmarking Embodied Agents That Act to Know 2026-11-18
📝 Technical report. 23 pages, 9 figures, 15 tables. Project page: https://hexafuture.ai/benchmarks/embodiedlab/ Code: https://github.com/HexaFuture/EmbodiedLab
Show abstract

Physical action not only changes the state of an environment but is also the way to know its hidden properties: what cannot be seen must be inferred from the feedback to the change. As embodied agents advance from executing procedures to pursuing goals, success depends on properties neither given nor visible, so they must act to know. Existing benchmarks separate changing from knowing: manipulation benchmarks give the target state, so nothing has to be found out, whereas exploration and discovery benchmarks let the agent find out by looking or querying, so nothing physical has to be changed. Quantitative science experiments offer a natural testbed: the properties sought are new in every instance, appear only in instrument readings under conditions the agent sets up, and can be checked exactly. We introduce EmbodiedLab, a simulated laboratory of 35 such experiments. Given only a goal, the agent must decide what to measure, operate the apparatus with a robot arm, read instruments through cameras and judge when its readings suffice. Its 30 determination tasks specify what to find out, whereas its 5 optimization tasks ask only for the best result the apparatus allows. With native control, the strongest of seven frontier agents succeeds on 41.4% of the tasks. Ablations reveal two bottlenecks: contact-rich manipulation, where ground-truth depth and motion planning change the success rate by at most 10 points, whereas executing every operation for the agent on five of the tasks raises it from at most 20% to at least 70%, so what an agent can know is bounded by how reliably it acts; and closed-loop experimental rigor, where with manipulation supplied, agents fail to gather sufficient readings or read instruments accurately, and most optimization runs stop at one design. Beyond evaluation, EmbodiedLab offers a resamplable environment with verifiable rewards for training embodied agents.

RiskWorld: Risk-Aware World Modeling with Flow-Guided Occupancy Evolution for Selective Trajectory Planning in Automated Driving 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Safe motion planning in automated driving requires anticipating evolving traffic risks and deciding when to revise the current planned trajectory. We introduce RiskWorld, a risk-aware world modeling framework for shared occupancy forecasting and selective trajectory replacement. Spatial risk fields and temporal actor context are fused with visual bird's-eye-view features. Flow-guided evolution transports occupancy and scene features, while signed residuals correct occupancy after transport. One forecast is generated per planning step and reused across candidates. Each candidate is compared with a current-state persistence reference, yielding a nonnegative collision-score correction. The trajectory selected by current-world evaluation serves as the planning anchor and is replaced only when additional predicted risk triggers intervention and an alternative satisfies component-wise constraints on predicted risk and trajectory error. Candidate geometries remain unchanged. We evaluate RiskWorld for open-loop planning on nuScenes using camera features, annotation-derived current and historical actor states, and dataset-provided map context. RiskWorld achieves the lowest collision rate at a long evaluation horizon of 3 s, and the second-best average L2 error among various state-of-the-art baselines, while running at 11.5 FPS on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with 90.81 M parameters. Within-setting ablations show that RiskWorld achieves lower collision rates than the current-state rescoring baseline, while forecast reuse enables additional candidates to be evaluated at low marginal computational cost.

Contact-based optimization for collision-free motion planning for a million robots 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Multi-agent robotic systems have attracted growing interest in recent years, with applications ranging from coordinated drone shows to autonomous operations on land, in the air, and on water. At the planning level, robot motion is often modeled using double-integrator dynamics (with low-level controllers handling the physical dynamics during execution) with a minimum-energy objective subject to collision-avoidance constraints and limits on position, velocity, and acceleration. The complexity of the problem resides in the constraints, which couple the trajectories and make the optimization problem nonconvex. Existing numerical approaches typically discretize and optimize entire trajectories, which becomes expensive for large fleets. We exploit the structure of optimal trajectories by organizing optimization around contacts, at which robots reach the minimum allowed separation. We formulate a coupled algebraic system in contact variables and reconstruct trajectories analytically between contacts. Our algorithm, sequential contact insertion (SCI), starts from unconstrained trajectories, inserts contacts at separation violations, and jointly adjusts their times and states. Numerical experiments demonstrate planning for one million robots in minutes and thousands of robots in fractions of a second. On problems feasible for a fixed-grid sequential convex programming baseline, SCI achieves median speedups of several orders of magnitude at matching solution accuracy.

Continuous-Time Safe Planning and Control for Agile Quadrupeds 2026-10-26

Agile quadruped navigation in dynamic environments demands trajectories that are both dynamically feasible and provably collision-free. Most real-time planners achieve tractability through a convex reduction that requires simplified kinematic models, discarding motion that the robot can readily execute, and certify safety only at discretization points, leaving open possible inter-sample collisions with moving obstacles. We present a real-time motion planning framework that retains the fully-actuated $SE(2)$ model of a quadruped, with its anisotropic, heading-coupled velocity bounds, and accepts the resulting nonconvexity. By parameterizing trajectories as composite Bernstein polynomials, we exploit the convex hull property to certify collision avoidance and the input bounds over the entirety of each planning horizon. Solved at online replanning rates, the optimization runs in closed loop, commanding body velocities to the locomotion controller. We validate the approach in simulation against ablations and external baselines, and in hardware experiments on a quadruped navigating among unknown moving obstacles, recovering both lateral and heading-coupled motion that simplified models exclude.
