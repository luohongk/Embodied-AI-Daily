Computing Invariant Reachable Sets through Sum-of-Squares Programming 2026-11-03
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 3 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Control Systems Technology. Code: github.com/khalid2696/computeSOSFunnels
Show abstract

Funnels, time-varying invariant sets around nominal trajectories, certify that a closed-loop system tracks its plan, and can be composed into verified motion plans. Their computation by sum-of-squares (SOS) programming is well established in formulation but not in practice: the result depends on implementation choices such as the initial guess, Lyapunov function normalization, and termination rule, which the literature documents only briefly. This paper presents a self-contained treatment of SOS-based funnel computation, implemented in the open-source package computeSOSFunnels. We propose a minimum-volume formulation with inlet and outlet constraints under which funnels with coinciding nominal endpoints compose by construction. Numerical experiments on a unicycle, with timing studies on a cart-pole and a 12-state quadrotor, characterize the pipeline: infeasible initial guesses are partly decidable before solving, the certificate shows no violations among 39,000 boundary samples per funnel under the true dynamics, and constructing the semidefinite program, not solving it, dominates computation time.

OmniScribble: Mixed-Initiative Authoring of Animated Sketches through Inspectable and Repairable AI Decisions 2026-11-03
Show abstract

AI-assisted creative tools reduce the effort of authoring content, but delegating creative decisions to AI can make it difficult for authors to understand, correct, or redirect work. We present OmniScribble, a system for animating multi-object sketches that treats object decomposition and motion planning as editable artifacts. Rather than generating animation end-to-end, OmniScribble presents AI proposals at verification checkpoints, where authors can accept, repair, or regenerate them. Both manual and AI-assisted authoring operate on the intermediate representation and rendering pipeline, allowing us to isolate AI delegation. In a within-subject study with eight participants, AI-assisted authoring yielded a median within-participant reduction in completion time of 71.4\%, while perceived control and motion-intent alignment remained high. Interaction traces and interviews show that participants adapted to AI errors by repairing local issues, regenerating mismatches, and accepting proposals. These findings suggest that inspectable, repairable delegation enables generative automation while maintaining control over the outcome.

Grasping and Stabilizing Tumbling Objects with Rotating-Base Multi-Arm Space Manipulators 2026-11-03
Show abstract

This paper presents an innovative guidance and control method for a space manipulator that actively maneuvers its base to hold a tumbling object stationary relative to the base during proximity operations. This simplifies motion planning, reduces collision risk, and enables impact-free capture using single- or multi-arm configurations, with or without predefined grasping interfaces. The proposed approach involves two phases. In the pre-grasping phase, a synchronization control aligns the target's center of mass (CoM) with the system's instantaneous center of rotation (ICR), virtually rigidizing the target-servicer system to keep the object stationary for reliable grasping. In the post grasping phase, a control strategy based on Hamiltonian dynamics and optimal control stabilizes the combined system by minimizing time, fuel, or energy, while respecting base torque limits and multiple grasping constraints. For structured targets, applied forces and torques stay within their bounds; for unstructured ones, friction constraints prevent slipping. A case study demonstrates the method's effectiveness in enabling both the subsequent grasping and detumbling phases.

h-NESDF: An Infinitely Differentiable Signed Distance Between Two Sets with Robotic Applications 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Computing distances between sets is important in many robotic applications, such as motion planning and control. In many applications, derivatives of the distance are needed as well. It is well known that the Euclidean distance is not always differentiable, which create issues to algorithms that need a derivative. In this paper, we introduce NESDF, the Normal Euclidean Signed Distance Function between two general (with mild regularity assumptions but possibly non-convex) sets. This metric is still not differentiable, but it allows us to introduce a generalization, $h$-NESDF, which has a smoothing parameter $h$ such that the metric is infinitely differentiable for all $h>0$ and that reduces to the NESDF when $h$ goes to $0$. The metric is amenable to GPU parallelization, and thus can be computed efficiently. We show extensive simulation results to evaluate its viability in motion planning.

TAMPAgent: Agentic Belief-Space Planning for Mobile Manipulation 2026-11-02
📝 Website link: https://umishra.me/TAMPAgent/
Show abstract

Open-world robotic mobile manipulation requires acting in physical environments with only partial information. While recent Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) agents show strong open-world reasoning capabilities, existing architectures that rely on direct tool calling struggle with geometric uncertainty and thus are limited to fully-observable tabletop environments. In contrast, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems can solve long-horizon tasks under partial observability but incur high computational costs and require closed worlds. We propose \textbf{TAMPAgent}, an agentic planning framework that unifies the reasoning of LVLMs with the structured constraint satisfaction of TAMP systems. We endow an LVLM agent with a belief-space planning model operating over an extensible API of perception, planning, and control tools. The agent formulates a partial task plan composed of parameterized observation and execution actions, which is subsequently completed using classical search algorithms. After extracting the plan's geometric constraints, the agent leverages its tool API to bind valid parameter values, satisfying feasibility requirements and producing an executable motion plan. Upon execution, the agent continuously updates its belief history and repeats this closed-loop process by interleaving observation and manipulation actions. Evaluated across complex household mobile manipulation tasks on Rainbow Y1 and Unitree G1 platforms, TAMPAgent significantly outperforms direct tool-calling agentic baselines, demonstrating that integrating TAMP constraint satisfaction into agentic planning loops is essential for unstructured, partially observable environments.

Sampling-Based Motion Planning with Scene Graphs Under Perception Constraints 2026-11-02
📝 8 pages, 5 figures, Accepted to R-AL
Show abstract

It will be increasingly common for robots to operate in cluttered human-centered environments such as homes, workplaces, and hospitals, where the robot is often tasked to maintain perception constraints, such as monitoring people or multiple objects, for safety and reliability while executing its task. However, existing perception-aware approaches typically focus on low-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems or only consider a single object in the context of high-DoF robots. This motivates us to consider the problem of perception-aware motion planning for high-DoF robots that accounts for multi-object monitoring constraints. We employ a scene graph representation of the environment, offering a great potential for incorporating long-horizon task and motion planning thanks to its rich semantic and spatial information. However, it does not capture perception-constrained information, such as the viewpoints the user prefers. To address these challenges, we propose MOPS-PRM, a roadmap-based motion planner, that integrates the perception cost of observing multiple objects or humans directly into motion planning for high-DoF robots. The perception cost is embedded to each object as part of a scene graph, and used to selectively sample configurations for roadmap construction, implicitly enforcing the perception constraints. Our method is extensively validated in both simulated and real-world experiments, achieving more than ~36% improvement in the average number of detected objects and ~17% better track rate against other perception-constrained baselines, with comparable planning times and path lengths.

Most common tasks for robots in dynamic spaces require that the environment is regularly and actively perceived. The perception task considered in this work can represent a broad range of robot perception objectives, including object detection, human activity recognition, and human face detection. For example, a service robot may need to continuously localize an object during manipulation, while an assistive robot may need to reliably perceive a human face or activity for interaction and safety. These tasks impose perception constraints on robot motion. However, solving motion and perception tasks simultaneously is challenging, as their requirements often conflict. Furthermore, robots must react quickly to environmental changes, while directly evaluating perception quality (e.g., object detection confidence) is often expensive or infeasible at runtime. This problem is especially important in human-centered environments, such as homes and hospitals, where effective perception is essential for safe operation. In this work, we address motion planning for high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robots from a start to a goal configuration with continuous perception constraints in static and dynamic environments. Our solution is a GPU-parallelized perception-score-guided probabilistic roadmap planner with a neural surrogate model (PS-PRM). Unlike existing active perception-, visibility-aware, or learning-based planners, our work jointly considers perception tasks and constraints when searching for a motion-planning solution. Our method uses a neural surrogate model to approximate perception scores, incorporates them into roadmap planning, and leverages GPU parallelism for efficient online replanning. We demonstrate that our planner outperforms RL- and trajectory-optimization-based baselines in static and dynamic environments in simulation and real-robot experiments.

Control Barrier Functions for Safety-Aware Latent-Space Motion Planning 2026-11-01
📝 8 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Latent-space path planning (LSPP) compresses the configuration space of a robotic manipulator into a compact, differentiable manifold learned by a variational autoencoder, enabling fast gradient-based planning without explicit inverse kinematics. Existing LSPP frameworks enforce obstacle avoidance with a binary collision classifier, whose saturated gradients near obstacle boundaries and lack of temporal structure cause the optimizer to stall and provide no forward-invariance guarantee. We replace the classifier with a neural control barrier function (CBF) learned directly in the latent space. The barrier is trained with a three-term objective that combines safe and unsafe sign constraints with a discrete-time decrease condition evaluated on transitions of an obstacle-blind nominal planner, and it is enforced at planning time by a closed-form, first-order correction that projects each goal-directed latent step back onto the CBF constraint. On a 7-DoF Franka Panda manipulator, the CBF planner achieves a 75.6\% success rate on a fixed-obstacle task, 12.0 percentage points above the best of 35 tuned classifier configurations, and 83.8\% on a multi-position task. A sweep of the decay rate $α$ characterizes the safety--performance trade-off, with the best operating points in $α\in [0.10, 0.20]$. In a fully general random-obstacle setting, success drops to 33.0\%, which we trace to covariate shift between the obstacle-blind training transitions and the states visited by the corrected planner.

Macro-Operator Generation and Predicate Selection for TAMP Operator Learning 2026-10-31
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Creating symbolic operators by hand is one of the main bottlenecks in deploying Task and Motion Planning systems (TAMP). Recent works show that these operators can instead be learned from demonstration data. Existing methods, however, typically learn each action in isolation and cannot capture the recurring multi-step structure of manipulation tasks, so the search becomes intractable on long sequential tasks. A further inefficiency arises in the symbolic state: every provided predicate is evaluated at every search node, even when it never appears in any learned operator. We present a system that addresses both problems. Its central component is the automatic generation of macro-operators, composite actions that compress a recurring sequence of individual actions into a single planning step. Our system discovers causally linked action pairs from the training data, where one action produces exactly the condition that the next one requires, and turns each pair into a new operator. Alongside this, our system prunes every predicate that no learned operator references, which shrinks the symbolic state evaluated at each search node. Together, these changes shorten the effective planning horizon, and the benefit they bring grows with the length of the task. Across four TAMP domains, our method reaches up to a ~4.6x planning speedup compared to the baseline method, namely Learning Operators for TAMP. More importantly, it solves a long sequential task that the baseline cannot solve. Macro-operator discovery thus not only accelerates planning but, in our long-horizon domain, determines solvability in practice. Our code is available at https://github.com/canemirbora4/macro-tamp

Interaction-Aware Sampling-Based Motion Planning for Autonomous Surface Vessels in Urban Waterways 2026-10-30
📝 This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication
Show abstract

Autonomous surface vessels operating in urban waterways must coordinate with other vessels in confined spaces while complying with traffic regulations, often without knowing their intentions. We present a decentralized motion planner based on model predictive path integral control, a sampling-based model predictive control scheme. The planner jointly computes a trajectory for the ego vessel and a prediction of the trajectories of the other vessels, and therefore accounts for their interaction. Traffic regulations are encoded as discontinuous costs, which the sampling-based formulation admits without a smooth approximation. We evaluate the method in maps taken from real sections of Amsterdam's canals, in encounters between two vessels and in scenarios with four interacting vessels. Across 780 randomized runs the method reaches every goal without collisions or deadlocks, and outperforms an optimization-based planner designed for the same domain, as well as a version of the proposed planner that does not account for the interaction. We further validate the planner on two scaled vessel prototypes in an outdoor environment, where planning and localization run onboard at 10 Hz, and head-on and crossing encounters are resolved without collisions or rule violations.

A complete analytical characterization of optimal collision-free motion for two robots 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Collision-free motion planning for multi-agent robotic systems remains an active research area because of the complexity of coordinating robot trajectories, with applications ranging from coordinated drone shows to autonomous operations on land, in the air, and on water. Importantly, the analytical difficulty of nonconvex collision-avoidance constraints has so far driven the development of exclusively numerical methods. We derive a complete analytical characterization of the optimal trajectories for two robots that start and end at rest, under double-integrator dynamics and a squared-acceleration objective. We prove that these trajectories belong to eight distinct contact families and determine which family applies for each configuration of initial and final positions. We analyze the geometrical properties of the optimal trajectories, provide a numerical algorithm for computing them, and discuss how these insights may improve the performance of existing numerical trajectory optimizers.

Contact-based optimization for collision-free motion planning for a million robots 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Multi-agent robotic systems have attracted growing interest in recent years, with applications ranging from coordinated drone shows to autonomous operations on land, in the air, and on water. At the planning level, robot motion is often modeled using double-integrator dynamics (with low-level controllers handling the physical dynamics during execution) with a minimum-energy objective subject to collision-avoidance constraints and limits on position, velocity, and acceleration. The complexity of the problem resides in the constraints, which couple the trajectories and make the optimization problem nonconvex. Existing numerical approaches typically discretize and optimize entire trajectories, which becomes expensive for large fleets. We exploit the structure of optimal trajectories by organizing optimization around contacts, at which robots reach the minimum allowed separation. We formulate a coupled algebraic system in contact variables and reconstruct trajectories analytically between contacts. Our algorithm, sequential contact insertion (SCI), starts from unconstrained trajectories, inserts contacts at separation violations, and jointly adjusts their times and states. Numerical experiments demonstrate planning for one million robots in minutes and thousands of robots in fractions of a second. On problems feasible for a fixed-grid sequential convex programming baseline, SCI achieves median speedups of several orders of magnitude at matching solution accuracy.

Decompose and Reorganize: Planning with Primitives and Visuomotor Policies Learned from Demonstrations 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted by T-RO. Website: https://dr-lfd.github.io/DR-LfD-website
Show abstract

Successfully automating dexterous, long-horizon bimanual manipulation requires frameworks capable of both high-level reasoning and fine-grained execution. Traditional task and motion planning (TAMP), while excellent at symbolic planning, is often brittle in contact-rich operations. Simultaneously, imitation learning (IL), while effective in manipulation tasks with visual feedback, is limited by its low capability in spatial generalization and multi-stage operation. To reconcile their complementary strengths and limitations, we propose DR-LfD (Decomposed and Reorganized Skills Learned from Demonstrations), a framework that integrates visuomotor policies into a TAMP-gated decision-making system. DR-LfD transforms the learning problem from one requiring exponential demonstration data over possible skill sequences to one whose demonstration burden scales with the number of distinct skill types, with limited data for each skill. Real-world and simulation benchmarks demonstrate data-efficient skill composition under unseen setups and physical constraints. On two DexMimicGen tasks, DR-LfD with 100 demonstrations outperforms diffusion-based imitation policies with 1000.

Scaling Trusted Autonomy: Towards Generally Capable Verifiable Robots 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Robots powered by foundation models are increasingly able to interpret open-ended language, recognize unfamiliar objects, and operate across diverse environments. Yet broader capability does not establish that a robot has understood a request, that its actions will produce the intended effects, or that its internal representation still corresponds to reality. Classical symbolic planning provides explicit structure for composition and verification, but traditionally depends on hand-engineered skills and representations. Learned policies can broaden the robot's repertoire, but provide weak guarantees about what will happen and why. This thesis argues that scaling trusted autonomy requires a neurosymbolic approach that unifies the generality of learned models with symbolic structure for robust robot behavior. It explores three complementary directions. First, LIMP uses foundation models and spatial reasoning to ground natural-language instructions in explicit task constraints and instruction-specific semantic maps, enabling task-and-motion planning while exposing the robot's interpretation for inspection. Second, REALM maintains an editable, photorealistic 3D Gaussian scene, predicts the consequences of planned manipulation, and locally reconciles the scene so that subsequent instructions obtain their grounding and planning geometry from an updated state. Third, PACTS learns visuomotor skills that jointly generate action trajectories and predictions of their symbolic effects, supporting skill composition, progress monitoring, and recovery through replanning. Together, these contributions preserve explicit structure for reasoning and verification while broadening how it is obtained, providing a foundation for generally capable robots whose behavior can be assessed under the assumptions of their models.

HIPC: Humanoid Iterative Planning and Control via Goal-Conditioned Motion Closure 2026-10-30
📝 Website: https://hipc-humanoid.github.io/
Show abstract

Recent generative models are widely used to provide whole-body motion for humanoid tracking. Current methods combine motion generators with controllers in an open-loop paradigm, which leads to critical issues: tracking errors can accumulate into spatial drift, and the planner cannot adapt to unexpected physical disturbances without feedback from physical execution. However, directly feeding realized states back to the planner in a closed loop can cause discontinuities, resulting in physical jitter and even falls, since such states are out of distribution during training. To bridge this gap, we propose HIPC, a real-time Humanoid Iterative Planning and Control framework that couples a generative model with motion tracking into a unified planning-and-control paradigm. HIPC introduces a goal-conditioned motion planning trained with a canonical motion representation that avoids gimbal-lock singularities under extreme orientations and supports motion generation on diverse geometric and semantic conditions. Then, HIPC adopts two strategies, deviation-decayed recovery and history re-anchoring, to mitigate the out-of-distribution shift caused by realized-state feedback. Finally, HIPC operates a real-time asynchronous planning-and-control system, with latency compensation to reduce temporal mismatch for inference delay. Extensive experiments confirm the effectiveness of HIPC under switching and composition of diverse conditions. Results show that HIPC achieves state-of-the-art goal-reaching accuracy among existing methods while simultaneously demonstrating robustness to physical perturbations and the ability to recover from falls.

Cross-Embodiment Motion Planning by Coupling Body-Agnostic World Model with Differentiable Ego-Dynamics 2026-10-30
📝 8 pages
Show abstract

Motion planning in clutter is embodiment-specific: the feasible free space and reachable commands depend on each robot's geometry and dynamics. Existing cross-embodiment policies output normalized commands that disregard these differences, or compensate through per-robot training and distillation. World models can in principle pool cross-embodiment data, yet command-conditioned models bind their latent dynamics to a single action space, while pose-conditioned models omit the mapping from commands to body-specific motion. On flat ground, however, embodiment affects the planner's observations only through the camera ego-motion. This letter proposes Hourglass, an hourglass-shaped architecture that factorizes cross-embodiment motion planning at this interface: a body-agnostic world model and each robot's differentiable ego-dynamics, which map commands to that ego-motion, are coupled only through it. The world model is trained on data pooled across embodiments and camera mounts; each robot's ego-dynamics is a reduced-order template with identified physical parameters. A generalist parameter-conditioned policy is learned once at this junction: each imagined step rolls out the template and then the world model, and imagination training through each robot's template makes the policy body-aware. On seven simulated robots of five locomotion types, the generalist policy outperforms imitation-learning and diffusion-policy baselines on the same world model by 3.8--10.6 percentage points in success rate and matches per-robot specialists without dedicated training, while approaching its parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher within 1.4--2.8 points without an online map. After three minutes of teleoperated driving for identification, the synthetic-data-trained model transfers zero-shot to a real skid-steer robot unseen in training, navigating unseen indoor scenes.

AffordSim: A Scalable Data Generator and Benchmark for Affordance-Aware Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Many everyday robot manipulation skills are affordance-dependent, with success determined by whether the robot contacts the functional object region required by the subsequent action. Current simulation data generators obtain contacts from generic grasp estimators or per-object manual contact annotations, but generic estimators rank stable grasps without task semantics and often select contacts that are misaligned with the downstream action, while manual contact annotations must be rewritten for each new object and task. To solve these challenges, we introduce AffordSim, a scalable data generator and benchmark that integrates open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction into simulation-based trajectory generation. Given a natural-language task description, AffordSim synthesizes a task-relevant scene, emits affordance queries, grounds them on object surfaces, samples region-conditioned grasps, and selects executable candidates with motion planning. It further randomizes object pose, texture, lighting, image noise, and cross-viewpoint backgrounds for sim-to-real transfer. We instantiate AffordSim as a 50-task benchmark across diverse manipulation skills, five robot embodiments, and 500+ rigid and articulated objects. AffordSim achieves 93% of the trajectory collection success rate of manual contact annotations on affordance-critical tasks and 89% on hard composite tasks. Vision-language-action policies trained on AffordSim data transfer zero-shot to a real Franka FR3, reaching 24% average success.

Evidence-Gated Task and Motion Planning with Vision-Language Models 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Robots executing long-horizon manipulation tasks from natural-language instructions must reason about both semantic task structure and geometric feasibility. Hierarchical approaches combining Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) offer a promising solution, but VLM-generated subgoals may rely on prior knowledge without sufficient observational support. This can lead to the omission of task-relevant subgoals or attempts to execute instructions whose requirements cannot be met in the environment. We propose Evidence Acquisition and Feasibility Gating (EAFG), a framework for evidence-gated task and motion planning that acquires visual evidence to specify appropriate task plans and assess whether given instructions can be fulfilled. EAFG first acquires evidence by having a VLM generate subgoals and a robot execute action sequences planned by TAMP for these subgoals. We evaluate EAFG in three cooking scenarios involving explicitly specified required objects, underspecified instructions, and instructions requiring an absent object. EAFG improves task completion over a VLM-TAMP baseline in the explicit and underspecified scenarios and enables appropriate halting when a required object is absent.

Simplified Algorithms and Improved Bounds for Motion Planning 2026-10-28
📝 10 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

We investigate coordinated motion planning (CMP) for unit square robots in the plane under the $L_1$ metric. However, when the current solution path contains redundant or tortuous segments, the resulting informed subset may remain unnecessarily large, slowing convergence. Our prior work addressed this issue by introducing the greedy informed set, which reduces the sampling region based on the maximum heuristic cost along the current solution path. In this article, we formally characterize the behavior of the greedy informed set within Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT*)-like planners and analyze how greedy sampling affects exploration and asymptotic optimality. We then present Greedy RRT* (G-RRT*), a bi-directional anytime variant of RRT* that leverages the greedy informed set to focus sampling in the most promising regions of the search space. Experiments on abstract planning benchmarks, manipulation tasks from the MotionBenchMaker dataset, and a dual-arm Barrett WAM problem demonstrate that G-RRT* rapidly finds initial solutions and converges asymptotically to optimal paths, outperforming state-of-the-art sampling-based planners.

Diffusion models provide expressive trajectory priors for multi-robot motion planning (MRMP), but scaling them to large teams requires coordinating individual motions without expensive joint computation. We introduce Akule, a sparse interaction diffusion framework that augments a frozen single-robot prior with three learned modules: a reusable pairwise correction model $R_φ$, a contextual selector $U_ψ$ that determines which corrections to evaluate, and a composer $G_ω$ that combines their effects through context-dependent signed weights. This decomposition separates which interactions merit computation from how their effects should be combined into coordinated denoising updates. We train $R_φ$ using matched pairwise trajectory optimizations, then learn $G_ω$ and $U_ψ$ from physically optimized joint trajectories without requiring a prescribed ground-truth interaction graph. The resulting joint proposals are passed to a downstream search-based planner for validation and repair. Across diverse MRMP benchmarks, Akule achieves competitive planning success, complete-planner speedups of up to $25\times$, zero-shot population transfer, and successful planning with up to $256$ robots. Ablations further demonstrate that learned selection and composition reduce pre-repair conflicts and downstream repair effort relative to heuristic selection and uniform summation.
