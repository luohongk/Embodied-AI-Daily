Pedicel-Targeted Strawberry Harvesting via Imitation Learning on a Low-Cost Teleoperated Robotic Arm 2026-10-16
📝 4 pages, 3 figures. This paper was peer-reviewed and accepted in the IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS), "From Innovation to Adoption: Hardware, AI, Policy, and Funding Challenges in Agricultural Robotics Workshop," Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, 2026
Show abstract

Precise pedicel-targeted grasping in strawberry harvesting remains a critical bottleneck due to the pedicel's small diameter (1.3-3 mm), color similarity with surrounding foliage, and precise force control required for clean abscission zone detachment. As a market-grade crop, strawberry aesthetic value makes bruise-free pedicel detachment essential over direct fruit grasping. Classical approaches struggle to unify vision models, motion planning, and contact manipulation into a robust framework, particularly in occluded canopy environments. Therefore, this work presents a visuomotor imitation learning pipeline for pedicel-targeted strawberry harvesting that unifies perception, motion planning, and contact manipulation from collecting human teleoperation demonstrations, training an Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy with a ResNet-50 visual backbone, and deploying the learned policy autonomously on a Jetson AGX Orin edge platform at 15 Hz. The trained policy achieved consistent loss convergence from 2.84 to 0.091 over 100K gradient steps on 130 demonstrations. We have also established a reproducible open-source pipeline with publicly released dataset and model weights for the agricultural robotics community. Full dataset is available at, https://huggingface.co/datasets/nitindominicrai/strawberry_pedicel_grasp_teleoperation and trained policy at, https://huggingface.co/nitindominicrai/act_strawberry_pedicel. To replicate this work, all code, configurations, and deployment instructions are publicly available at, https://github.com/nitin-dominic/autonomous_strawberry_pedicel_harvesting.

Shape-Aware Neural Time Fields for Rigid-Body Motion and Manipulation Planning 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Rigid-body motion planning is fundamental to robotic transport and manipulation, where both object position and orientation determine whether a body can move through cluttered environments. This coupling makes planning chal- lenging because translation and rotation can be constrained very differently by surrounding geometry. We propose Shape- Aware Neural Time Fields, a physics-informed neural motion planning framework that learns a reusable travel-time, or cost- to-go, representation for rigid-body motion while explicitly accounting for these distinct geometric restrictions. The learned field captures local motion difficulty in translation and rotation and is paired with a sampling-based planner that balances progress toward the goal with the cost of reaching nearby poses. We evaluate the framework across 2D and 3D rigid- body planning tasks and non-prehensile manipulation through object pushing in diverse environments. Results show substantial improvements in planning success and inference efficiency over physics-informed neural planning baselines, while remaining competitive with classical sampling-based planners at significantly lower planning times. Ablation studies further validate the contributions of both the learned representation and the proposed planning strategy. Finally, closed-loop pushing experiments under execution deviations and external disturbances demonstrate that fast replanning enables reactive and adaptive task execution.

Bi-Level Optimization for Contact and Motion Planning in Rope-Assisted Legged Robots 2026-10-15
Show abstract

This paper presents a planning pipeline framework for locomotion in rope-assisted robots climbing vertical surfaces. The proposed framework is formulated as a bi-level optimization scheme that addresses a mixed-integer problem: selecting feasible terrain regions for landing while simultaneously optimizing the control inputs, namely rope tensions and leg forces, and landing location. The outer level of the optimization is solved using the Cross-Entropy Method, while the inner level relies on gradient-based nonlinear optimization to compute dynamically feasible motions. The approach is validated on a novel climbing robot platform, ALPINE, across a variety of challenging terrain configurations.

Auditable and Deployable Multi-Agent Motion Planning via Posetal Games from Legal Guidelines 2026-10-15
Show abstract

When robots are deployed in the real-world, they must interact with other agents and accomplish their own goals while adhering to legal constraints. These potentially conflicting objectives are often only partially prioritized, making scalarized objectives or fully specified hierarchies unsuitable. We present a framework for multi-agent motion planning under legal requirements based on games with partially ordered preferences (posetal games). Scenario-independent legal guidelines order abstract legal concerns, from which we automatically derive agent preferences and a legal game for any concrete scenario. Agents may refine their legal preferences with private objectives, and regulators may refine the guideline as laws evolve. We prove that such customizations can only remove equilibria and thus preserve legality. To coordinate agents without explicitly communicating private preferences, we introduce belief play, which provably recovers the true equilibria from conservative local games, and a distributed iterative best-response protocol requiring less information. We apply the framework to maritime navigation, systematically lifting pairwise traffic rules to multi-vessel encounters. On seven encounters with up to four vessels, every rule violation of the committed equilibria is certified as forced by the legal hierarchy or unsatisfiable, and defectors are identified from public information in all tested cases. These results indicate that guideline-based posetal games offer an auditable route from partially specified laws to multi-agent motion planning.

Safe and Fuel-Efficient Motion Planning for Lunar Propulsive Drones 2026-10-15
📝 This work has been submitted to the 2027 IEEE Aerospace Conference
Show abstract

Propulsive drones are a promising new technology for generating high-resolution terrain models of the Moon. In future lunar missions, propulsive drones will complete a series of hops across the lunar surface while capturing images with downward-facing cameras. We present an autonomous motion planning framework that uses a vision-guided optimal control strategy for performing safe, efficient hops between specified waypoints. The drone plans parabolic hops between waypoints generated by a global-level planner; if a nominal waypoint is unsafe or unreachable, the drone then completes a diversion maneuver to safely land. To evaluate the validity of potential landing sites, we develop a custom semantic segmentation model for identifying obstacles from aerial images, and we introduce an approach for rapidly calculating reachable terrain. We test the optimal hop planner in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. Our results demonstrate that our unified motion planning framework yields safe, fuel-efficient hop trajectories which can be tracked rapidly and with low error via MPC.

Learning from demonstration is an effective strategy for endowing robots with manipulation capabilities but requires training on datasets comprised of massive amounts of demonstrations. Existing demonstration collection approaches either require constant human involvement, for example, via teleoperation or are specialized for a single task. Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems are able to autonomously solve and therefore generate demonstrations for general-purpose tasks; however, learning from their demonstrations has proven difficult due to multi-modality in their solutions. CLIS-MPPI: Closed-Loop Model Predictive Path Integral Control With Initial-State Sampling Under Execution Delay 2026-10-15
📝 65th IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC 2026)
Show abstract

Sampling-based motion planning such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control has become attractive for robotics due to its ability to handle nonlinear dynamics and non-smooth objectives without gradient information, while leveraging parallel rollout computation for real-time performance. However, in real-world robotic systems, the optimized control input is often applied after a non-negligible planning delay, causing a mismatch between the state used in forward simulation and the actual state at control execution, which can significantly degrade planning accuracy and may even lead to unsafe behavior such as collisions. This paper proposes Closed-Loop MPPI with Initial-State Sampling (CLIS-MPPI), which explicitly addresses execution-time state mismatch by modeling planning delay as a distribution over plausible execution-time initial states, performing rollout optimization from sampled initial states, and applying a rollout-based local feedback correction during control execution. The proposed method is validated through real-world experiments on a 6-DoF UR5e manipulator in a narrow-passage task involving obstacle avoidance and end-effector target reaching. Across 5 real-world runs of the same task, standard MPPI collided in 2 runs, whereas CLIS-MPPI remained collision-free in all 5 runs. Averaged over all collision-free runs for each method (3 for MPPI and 5 for CLIS-MPPI), CLIS-MPPI reduced the average task completion time from 25.4s to 24.4s and the average end-effector path length from 0.456 m to 0.425 m. It also reduced the overall joint-position and joint-velocity root mean square errors (RMSEs) from 0.100 rad and 0.108 rad/s to 0.017 rad and 0.011 rad/s, respectively. These results demonstrate improved safety, planning-execution consistency, and motion smoothness.

SPARC: Reliable Spatial Annotations from Robot Demonstrations at Scale 2026-10-14
📝 CoRL 2026

This work introduces Spatial Annotations from Robot Demonstrations with Reliability Calibration (SPARC), a risk-aware framework that automatically labels robot demonstrations with structured spatial annotations and assigns each annotation a reliability score. Structured spatial annotations, such as bounding boxes, object trajectories, and manipulation phase labels, benefit a broad range of robotics applications from training grounded robot policies and embodied foundation models to motion planning and hierarchical task composition. Existing automated pipelines generate such annotations at scale but provide no reliable quality signal: detector confidence is poorly calibrated for annotation correctness, forcing a choice between accepting noisy labels or discarding useful samples. In contrast to existing automated pipelines, SPARC leverages the spatio-temporal structure inherent to robot tasks to generate a reliability signal, reducing noisy labels and retaining more useful samples. We further introduce Interaction-Aware Bench (IA-Bench), a benchmark that measures model accuracy in grounding the locations of interacted objects in robot demonstrations. On 1.7k human-annotated demonstrations spanning diverse embodiments and scenarios, SPARC significantly outperforms detection-only baselines in localization accuracy while retaining three times more samples at high-precision operating points. Our experiments demonstrate that models finetuned on our annotations achieve state-of-the-art results on object-grounding and pointing benchmarks among similarly sized models, while remaining competitive on broader spatial-reasoning suites without manually verified or annotated training data. Furthermore, policies trained on SPARC-generated annotations outperform baselines in cluttered, visually ambiguous real-world scenes. Code, data, and models are available at intuitive-robots.github.io/sparc-labeling.

FPTAS for Polygonal Multi-Robot Motion Planning 2026-10-14
📝 To appear in SODA 2027
Show abstract

We study multi-robot motion planning in a polygonal environment with polygonal robots. The setting of the problem consists of a polygonal domain $\mathcal{W}$ with $n$ vertices and $k$ robots $R_1,\dots,R_k$ translating in $\mathcal{W}$ each of which is represented by a polygon of constant complexity. Given a source configuration $\mathbf{s}$ and a target configuration $\mathbf{t}$ of the robots in $\mathcal{W}$, the goal of the problem is to find a collision-free motion plan from $\mathbf{s}$ to $\mathbf{t}$ that is shortest with respect to some cost function. We present the first fully polynomial-time approximation schemes (FPTAS) for motion planning with any fixed number $k$ of robots, under the min-sum objective, the min-max objective, and the min-makespan objective. Here, the min-sum variant aims to minimize the total traveling distance of all robots, the min-max variant aims to minimize the maximum traveling distance of a single robot, and the min-makespan variant aims to minimize the total time cost under the assumption that all robots move with a maximum speed $1$. The algorithm directly generalizes to the problem in any fixed dimension $d$, with a polytope environment and polytope robots. Our result settles an open question of Agarwal, Halperin, Sharir, Steiger [SODA'24] and Agarwal, Holmgren, Steiger [SODA'26], who gave an FPTAS for $k=2$ unit-square robots and a bicriteria-approximation algorithm for a fixed number $k$ of unit-square or unit-disk robots, respectively. Compared to the previous results, our algorithm is substantially simpler.

Uncertainty-Driven Self-Play for Robust Multi-Agent Driving Policies 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Learning robust driving policies that generalize beyond their training domain remains a central challenge in motion planning. We propose an uncertainty-driven self-play scheme, in which half of the agents are rewarded for increasing their neighbors' epistemic uncertainty, while the other half learn to drive safely as the exploring agents continuously seek out their weaknesses. Epistemic uncertainty indicates where the policy is least confident and serves as an automatic proxy for scenario difficulty. Exploring agents use this signal to seek out hard cases, generating a challenging curriculum without manual scenario design. The resulting policy outperforms standard self-play, achieving best-in-class zero-shot transfer to nuplan Val14 (99.12% vs. 97.97%) and Argoverse 2 (82.39% vs. 78.03%) without fine-tuning, while also improving performance over standard self-play on Waymo. Our diversity-driven approach achieves 85% higher LSTM hidden-state coverage than the baseline, indicating a much richer set of behaviors. Further analysis shows that epistemic uncertainty serves as a calibrated difficulty detector, with highest-uncertain agents exhibiting 14.8 times the combined failure rate across collision, off-road, and DNF, of lowest-uncertain agents, enabling automatic prioritization of challenging scenarios without ground-truth labels.

MobiGuide: Vocabulary-Guided Online Recovery for Mobile Manipulation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

A central challenge in mobile manipulation is preserving multiple plausible action modes while remaining reactive during execution. For example, grasping a bottle in a cluttered scene can be achieved through multiple physically feasible base-arm motion plans. Deterministic VLA policies trained with $L_1$ regression are efficient, but they tend to average across valid modes and may produce physically invalid actions. Diffusion-based generative policies can better represent multimodal action distributions, yet their proposals may be weakly constrained when generated from pure noise, and iterative sampling can increase inference cost. Action chunking helps amortize inference, but it also introduces partially open-loop execution, allowing small mismatches to accumulate into drift. We present MobiGuide, a generative VLA model for efficient mobile manipulation, where action generation is guided by a structured trajectory vocabulary. Constructed from expert demonstrations, this vocabulary encodes plausible whole-body motion patterns and provides structured priors for feasible action generation. Given the current observation and instruction, MobiGuide locally refines these reference trajectories into context-compatible action proposals and selects the most suitable trajectory for execution. This preserves multimodal action candidates while keeping generation constrained around feasible expert-like motion modes. To mitigate chunk-induced drift, we further introduce a lightweight residual correction module that performs high-frequency, per-step adjustments during rollout. Across diverse mobile manipulation tasks, MobiGuide improves success and stability under execution disturbances while maintaining low-latency inference.

We devise a dual-arm cooperative planning framework for wiping table surface in the presence of scattered objects. Unlike conventional surface-cleaning strategies that only cover free areas and detour around obstacles, our framework explicitly assigns one arm to rearrange objects while the other performs coverage wiping, thereby enabling access to previously obstructed regions. We present Keypoint Constraint Learning (KCL), a framework for learning task-relevant motion constraints from visual demonstrations and for planning safely with the extracted constraints. To make constraint learning tractable from RGB-D observations, we represent demonstrations using sparse 3D object-centric keypoints and semantic mask centroids extracted through segmentation, visual feature clustering, and point tracking. Monte Carlo Tree Search with Tensor Factorization for Optimization Problems in Robotics 2026-10-13
📝 29 pages, 18 figures
Show abstract

Many robotic tasks, such as inverse kinematics, motion planning, and contact-rich manipulation, can be formulated as optimization problems. Solving these problems requires addressing inherent nonlinear kinematics, complex contact dynamics, long-horizon correlations, and multi-modal optimization landscapes, each posing distinct challenges for state-of-the-art optimizers. While existing methods tackle these issues through problem-specific strategies, such specialization inherently limits cross-task generalization, requires heavy engineering effort in problem reformulation, and hinders multi-task autonomy. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) offers a compelling framework that generalizes across diverse robotic tasks via strategic exploration of the solution space. However, it typically suffers from combinatorial complexity when applied naively, resulting in slow convergence and excessive storage space in high-dimensional domains. To address this limitation, we propose Tensor Train Tree Search (TTTS), which leverages tensor factorization to exploit implicit correlations among different branches within the decision tree. By utilizing the resulting compact, linear-complexity representation, TTTS significantly reduces both computation and storage overhead, thereby enabling highly efficient global decision making. Experimental results across inverse kinematics, motion planning around obstacles, legged robot manipulation, multi-stage motion planning, and bimanual whole-body manipulation demonstrate the efficiency of TTTS for generalized robot optimization over a diverse set of tasks.

MOSAIC: Mode- and Support-Aware Inference-Time Control of Robot Diffusion Models 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Generative policies such as diffusion models provide expressive data-driven priors for robot behavior, yet the situations a robot faces at execution time rarely match its training data and impose constraints and objectives the policy never learned. Existing inference-time guidance methods either rely on gradients that can push samples away from the learned prior, or on sampling procedures that can be inefficient, collapse modes, or average them into out-of-distribution predictions. We introduce MOSAIC, a mode- and support-aware framework for gradient-free inference-time control of robotic diffusion models. MOSAIC treats the initial noise and all stochastic reverse-step perturbations as a joint input to the pretrained denoising process and adapts a distribution over this input using importance weights computed from fully denoised outputs. Physical feasibility and consistency with the learned support are balanced using adaptive penalties, while mode-wise importance-weighted updates preserve modes and avoid destructive averaging. The same formulation can jointly steer multiple independently trained diffusion models through shared coupling costs, enabling coordinated behavior without requiring a joint model. We evaluate MOSAIC on navigation, dexterous grasping, motion planning with unseen geometry, and collaborative box manipulation with independently trained robot priors. Across these settings, MOSAIC adapts and composes frozen diffusion priors under new deployment constraints without retraining.

Efficient Hybrid Hard--Soft Signal Temporal Logic MPC for Driving under Multimodal Uncertainty 2026-10-11
📝 8 pages
Show abstract

Motion planning for automated vehicles in interactive traffic has to simultaneously ensure safety, respect traffic rules, and maintain comfortable progress under multi-modal behavior uncertainty. Imposing all these requirements as hard constraints reduces feasibility, whereas treating them all as costs allows safety-critical requirements to be traded against progress. We propose a hybrid hard--soft Signal Temporal Logic (STL) MPC framework that optimizes a single shared ego trajectory against every retained behavior hypothesis: minimum-distance and speed predicates are imposed as explicit hard constraints, while soft STL robustness terms shape clearance, comfort, and interaction quality within the resulting feasible driving corridor. This retains robustness to discrete interaction modes without branch-specific contingent ego policies. We further identify substantial symbolic graph-construction overhead and introduce reachability-certified relevance screening and vectorized symbolic construction to reduce computational cost while preserving the feasible set and objective. Closed-loop Monte Carlo evaluation over six highway and intersection scenarios shows the highest strict success rate among the compared planners with no observed collisions or rule violations, at roughly a quarter of the planning latency of the unoptimized formulation. An ablation study separates the two layers: the hard constraints define the safety envelope, while the soft terms improve clearance and motion quality within it.
