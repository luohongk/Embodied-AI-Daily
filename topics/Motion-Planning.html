SIGMO: Sigmoid Gated Interaction-Aware Motion Planning 2026-11-10
📝 Submitted to IEEE
Show abstract

Despite the widespread adoption of autonomous vehicle technologies, reliable navigation within cluttered environments remains arduous due to the necessity of interaction-aware strategies; example scenarios include: narrow passages with multiple agents, lane changing in dense traffic, and crossing unsignalized intersections. These scenarios often require highly proactive strategies, simultaneously demanding interpretability and quick adaptability, which challenge end-to-end and data-driven methods. An effective strategy requires: scalability to multiple agents, adaptability to changes in other agents' behavior, and harmony with surrounding traffic without freezing or becoming overly conservative. To meet these requirements, we present SIGMO, a sigmoid-gated interaction-aware motion planner, with two main contributions. First, we formulate polynomial sigmoid functions that analytically estimate yield scores of surrounding agents using physics-grounded interaction features that capture driving intuitions. Second, we integrate the polynomial sigmoid functions into a receding-horizon planner, which facilitates interaction-aware planning with arbitrary trajectory predictions. We validate SIGMO in interaction-intensive simulations and hardware experiments. The results show that SIGMO balances proactive and defensive behavior while remaining computationally lightweight, serving as a strong baseline in a mobility stack. The simulation and hardware experiment videos are available here: https://sites.google.com/view/sigmoid-polygate.

Learn-To-Propagate: Distributionally Robust Motion Planning for Nonlinear Uncertain Systems 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Motion planning for stochastic systems aims to find a sequence of control inputs that drives a system to a goal set while avoiding unsafe states with high probability. Existing approaches, however, are either restricted to linear dynamics with perfect knowledge of the underlying distributions or lack formal correctness guarantees. To address these limitations, we propose \emph{Learn-To-Propagate Distributionally-Robust Forest (L2P-DR-Forest)}, a distributionally-robust sampling-based planning algorithm for \emph{nonlinear} stochastic systems with \emph{unknown} initial state and process noise distributions. Our approach leverages recent advances in optimal transport to learn ambiguity sets that contain the true system distributions with high confidence. We develop an efficient method for propagating these sets through the system dynamics and for verifying their safety. We then integrate these techniques into a novel sampling-based planning algorithm to grow a forest of search trees in the system's state space while accounting for and mitigating the growth of ambiguity-sets radii. We prove that the algorithm is correct, i.e., every returned plan satisfies the specifications with the prescribed confidence, and, under some feasibility and distributional assumptions, is probabilistically complete. Evaluations on several robotic benchmarks demonstrate that our algorithm significantly outperforms existing methods while providing formal guarantees with high confidence.

A computational framework for understanding embodied plans 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Reasoning about people's plans and goals based on their actions is fundamental to human social interaction and essential for cooperative, embodied artificial intelligence. Formalizing this capacity in naturalistic, embodied settings has been a longstanding challenge. Here, we present GenEA, a novel agent representation capable of reasoning over multi-step, long-horizon embodied plans. At the heart of GenEA lies a multilevel state-space that couples the coarse biomechanics of an articulated body and its interactions with nearby objects by extending the task-and-motion planning framework from robotics with simulation-based intuitive physics. By embedding this state-space in a generative model, GenEA yields a joint probability distribution over goals and embodied plans. Across several experiments spanning the qualitatively distinct domains of object manipulation and navigation, featuring diverse tasks and multiple behavioral measures, we show that GenEA, through simple transformations of this joint distribution, recapitulates how humans reason about embodied plans. Crucially, GenEA consistently outperforms strong alternative models, including plausible heuristics, standard task-and-motion planning, and a state-of-the-art vision-language model. GenEA thereby offers a new perspective on the architecture of human reasoning and social cognition, suggesting that embodied artificial intelligence systems seeking to seamlessly interact with humans should adopt GenEA-like generative models of action planning.

EgoHMP: Achieving Precise Human Motion Prediction in 3D Scenes via Egocentric Cues 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Main Conference)
Show abstract

The complex interactive dependencies between human behavior and 3D scenes make accurately predicting human intent a major challenge for human motion prediction (HMP). While recent studies use human gaze to infer intent, gaze signals suffer from ambiguity, drift, and a reliance on eye-tracking hardware. Research in cognitive science indicates that egocentric views inherently encode potential interactions, while head motion serves as a crucial precursor in motion planning. Motivated by this, we propose EgoHMP, a novel framework that uses egocentric views and head motions as robust carriers of interaction intent. By translating this intent into an envisioning of future interactions, EgoHMP achieves precise HMP in 3D scenes. EgoHMP first employs a vision-motion alignment encoder to align the visual and motion feature spaces. Based on these aligned features, an egocentric intent predictor with a modality-aware modulation mechanism adaptively suppresses invalid interaction cues to predict an accurate interaction probability map. Furthermore, to enhance the stability and realism of the generated motions, we propose a contact-aware motion decoder. Specifically, inspired by the human cognitive process of planning trajectories prior to action execution, we first predict a global trajectory to serve as a self-prompt, effectively mitigating the optimization conflict in diffusion models. Subsequently, we introduce a multi-stage denoiser, which relieves motion artifacts by incorporating human contact modeling through a multi-stage optimization strategy. Finally, a GCN-based motion decoder is employed to synthesize physically plausible and semantically consistent motions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EgoHMP achieves state-of-the-art performance.

We present cuRoboV2-Fast, a GPU-accelerated planner that reduces both planning latency and execution time for collision-free, payload-aware, torque-limited motions. B-spline trajectory optimization reduces the required iterations, while accelerated robot computations and differentiable inverse dynamics reduce refinement cost and accommodate changing payloads. Show abstract

Wearable robots depend on a software chain that links body-worn sensing to gait interpretation, assistance generation, and actuation. Personalization further requires each measured response to remain associated with the assistance parameters that generated it. This paper presents ExoStack, a modular ROS 2 framework for implementing that chain across embedded hubs and a host computer. Its Embedded Sensing and Actuation (ESA) library configures ESP32 boards as microROS sensing and actuation hubs. ROS2MR: Connecting ROS 2 Motion Planning to Mixed-Reality Trajectory Visualization 2026-11-09
📝 Pre-print submitted in Elsevier
Show abstract

Mixed reality (MR) allows users to inspect planned robot motion within their physical surroundings, but displaying trajectories from robotics software requires converting their joint configurations and timing into a format the MR application can play back. This paper presents ROS2MR, an extension of the MR-Robotics LAB platform that converts YAML-serialized ROS 2 joint trajectories, including MoveIt motion plans, into the platform's existing JSON playback format. The converter validates the input, maps arm joints by name, and resamples the trajectory at a nominal rate of 30 Hz, using cubic Hermite interpolation when joint velocities are supplied and linear interpolation otherwise, while binary gripper commands are sampled separately using a zero-order hold. A Python export helper combines sequences of motion plans and gripper states into a single trajectory and checks positional agreement at segment boundaries. The MR application was also adapted for Apple Vision Pro in addition to Meta Quest 3. A technical evaluation showed that the converter preserved the normalized duration and endpoint configurations for dense, sparse, and irregularly timed inputs. With supplied velocities, the position RMSE was approximately 2.91x10^-5 degrees for all sampling patterns; without them, sparse input increased it to 0.85 degrees, and close positional agreement did not guarantee accurate velocity profiles. A pick-and-place sequence retained its open--closed--open gripper order, although off-grid transitions were shifted to the output sampling grid. The resulting file-based pathway enables inspection of ROS-generated trajectories in MR without a live ROS connection.

Graph-Sparse Distributed Predictive Control for Multi-Robot Motion Planning vianEdge-Consensus ADMM 2026-11-09
Show abstract

This paper presents a distributed model predictive control (MPC) architecture for collision-avoiding motion planning of planar robots with double-integrator dynamics. To reduce coordination demands, a time-varying predicted conflict graph selects relevant pairwise interactions. The associated collision-avoidance constraints are convexified once per MPC update using warm-start trajectories, yielding a fixed convex quadratic program (QP). The resulting QP is then decomposed using an edge-consensus formulation of the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), enabling parallel robot-local optimization and independent closed-form projections onto separating half-spaces. Execution is selectively supervised by a centralized high-order control barrier function (HOCBF) QP when the distributed candidate does not satisfy the direct-execution conditions. Evaluated in C++ simulations involving 8, 16, and 32 robots, the proposed architecture achieves collision-free execution and substantially improves computational scalability while maintaining task-level performance comparable to a centralized complete-graph baseline.

Autonomous UAV exploration is increasingly used for large-scale surveying and inspection in inaccessible or disaster-affected environments, where onboard memory and computational resources are often severely constrained. Most existing UAV exploration systems maintain globally queryable geometric map representations for motion planning and exploration decision-making, causing memory consumption and spatial-query costs to increase with the explored area. A complete analytical characterization of optimal collision-free motion for two robots 2026-11-08
Show abstract

Collision-free motion planning for multi-agent robotic systems remains an active research area because of the complexity of coordinating robot trajectories, with applications ranging from coordinated drone shows to autonomous operations on land, in the air, and on water. Importantly, the analytical difficulty of nonconvex collision-avoidance constraints has so far driven the development of exclusively numerical methods. We derive a complete analytical characterization of the optimal trajectories for two robots with zero initial and final velocities, under double-integrator dynamics and a squared-acceleration objective. We prove that these trajectories belong to eight distinct contact families and determine which family applies for each configuration of initial and final positions. We analyze the geometrical properties of the optimal trajectories and provide a numerical algorithm for computing them. These insights have already been adopted to improve the performance of numerical methods for multi-agent motion planning by several orders of magnitude.

Relative Contact Velocity-Controlled Hand-Object Mechanism for Dexterous Tool Manipulation 2026-11-08
Show abstract

This work investigates how to enable general multi-finger robotic hands to perform the complete tool manipulation process, which entails picking up a tool, loading it into a suitable pose, and then wielding it. Inspired by human tool manipulation and mechanical design principles, we model the hand and the tool as a unified hand-object mechanism (HOM) composed of sub-assemblies. Specifically, we define a HOM as consisting of the hand, the object, and the generalized contact frames, allowing the HOM's motions to be expressed with the same set of Cartesian-space relative contact velocities, irrespective of the hand's kinematics and geometry. Then, we define a HOM's sub-assemblies as relative contact velocity and contact force constraints between fingers. Building on these definitions, we developed a lightweight and physically interpretable motion planning and contact estimation framework using least squares and a complementary filter. We evaluated our framework in simulation by teleoperating five different robotic hands. The results show that our framework enabled all five hands to execute the complete tool manipulation process, achieving dexterous behaviors even from identical, simple reference trajectories. Furthermore, the results showcase our framework's adaptability to different hands, tools, and tasks, enabled by its kinematic and geometric foundation. Sampling-based Model Predictive Control as Sequential Monte Carlo Inference 2026-11-07
📝 8 pages, 5 figures. Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L). Code: https://github.com/CoMMALab/PFMP
Show abstract

Sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) is treated as a family of distinct algorithms: predictive sampling, MPPI, the cross-entropy method, and their biased, colored-noise, and annealed variants all sample control sequences and reweight them by cost. We show a single Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) inference algorithm implements them all, specified by three independent axes: the proposal, the update rule that reduces cost-weighted particles to a nominal plan, and the effective-sample-size threshold for resampling. We evaluate SMC-based planning on MuJoCo non-linear control, open-loop navigation through obstacle fields, 7-DoF motion planning, and non-prehensile push-T, in simulation and on a Franka FR3. Resampling combats particle degeneracy at long horizons. At a 28-knot horizon in our navigation domain it cut the collision rate by 30% over non-resampling baselines. Programmable proposals improve push-T success over perturbative baselines, improving from 1% to 100% in simulation and 10% to 90% on hardware.

Auditing Grasp Feasibility Predictors with Planner-Verified Counterfactuals 2026-11-06
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027. 10 pages main paper + 8 pages supplementary
Show abstract

Grasp-feasibility predictors report near-perfect accuracy on Task and Motion Planning benchmarks, but high accuracy on these benchmarks does not verify that a predictor uses the collision geometry that the motion planner checks. We propose a planner-verified counterfactual (CF) test: holding the grasp pose fixed, we insert a solid obstacle into the approach corridor, add its points to the model input, and let a planner re-derive the label, which flips from feasible to infeasible. Across eight predictors spanning point clouds, voxels, depth, and object tokens, CF-pair AUROC falls from high natural AUROC (0.920-0.996) to chance (0.470-0.504), and the flip holds across four planners, two arms, and a real-object obstacle. Predictors fail at different resolutions, from a bounding box to a 12.5 cm voxel. In the point-cloud models we dissect, the obstacle either moves the representation in a direction unrelated to whether it blocks the grasp or is not registered at all. A clearance-aware read of the corridor points, trained on the unmodified benchmark with no counterfactual scenes, reaches 0.983 CF-pair AUROC, but it remains bound to its trained obstacle placements; a placement-invariant clearance representation is still open. We release the counterfactual labels, metrics, datasheet, and relabeling pipeline at https://github.com/codewithsajid/graspfc-cf.

GraspBench: Execution-Aware Cross-Robot Evaluation of 6-DoF Grasp Generation in MuJoCo 2026-11-05
📝 7 pages, 6 figures, accepted for publication in ICARCV 2026
Show abstract

Modern 6-DoF grasp generators are often evaluated using grasp scores, dataset-level metrics, or single-robot demonstrations, yet proposal quality alone does not determine whether a grasp is executable by a particular robot. We present GraspBench, a controlled MuJoCo protocol for evaluating complete grasp-generation pipelines after robot-specific inverse-kinematics filtering, motion planning, and fixed approach-close-lift execution. GraspBench evaluates six configured pipelines across four robot embodiments, five diagnostic objects, and a structured tabletop pose grid, yielding 540 scenarios per method and 3,240 method-scenario evaluations in total. Missing outputs are conservatively counted as failures, and success follows the binary lift criterion produced by each method's execution runner. GraspGen achieves the highest overall success rate (73.1%), followed by EconomicGrasp (63.5%), while robot- and object-conditioned analyses reveal substantial variation across embodiments and object geometries. GPG produces the largest native candidate sets but achieves 44.3% success, indicating that proposal volume alone does not guarantee robot-executable grasps. Cluster-aware paired intervals support the principal method differences while not estimating repeated-execution uncertainty. The results characterize complete configured pipelines on fixed scenarios rather than intrinsic model quality or stochastic repeatability, showing that grasping performance depends jointly on the generator, robot embodiment, scene, and execution protocol.

World Models as Adversaries: Multi-Agent Self-Play Fine-Tuning for Robust Motion Planning 2026-11-05
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Robust motion planning in dense traffic requires autonomous vehicles to interact in rare and safety-critical scenarios that are underrepresented in naturalistic driving data. Although adversarial training offers a feasible solution, existing methods often rely on external scenario generators, heuristic perturbations, or simulator-heavy rollouts, which makes them difficult to integrate with modern autoregressive planners. Here, we cast adversarially robust planner learning as a constrained min-max game and propose Adversarial World Modeling (AWM), a theoretically grounded multi-agent self-play fine-tuning framework. Since solving the exact game is intractable, AWM introduces a principled decoupled solver. In the inner minimization, the planner's predictive world model is converted into a role-conditioned adversary that learns sparse, scene-adaptive attack coalitions via counterfactual credit assignment. In the outer maximization, the ego planner optimizes a regret-aware robust best response against the frozen AWM, utilizing tail-risk weighting and reference-anchored trust regions to improve hard-case recovery while preserving nominal driving behavior. Experiments on the nuPlan and InterPlan benchmarks demonstrate that our method generates transferable adversarial interactions and yields a robust planner that achieves competitive closed-loop performance in both nominal and highly interactive long-tail scenarios. Theoretical analysis justifies the decoupled solver and the main optimization components.

Vision-Guided Reorientation and Color Sorting of Writing Utensils Using a Low-Cost 4-DoF Arm 2026-11-04
📝 Accepted manuscript in the authors' formatting, 23 pages, 7 figures, 1 table. Published in the American Journal of Student Research, 2026;4(6):74-88. v1 was titled "Tabletop Pen Manipulation With a Vision-Guided 4-DoF Arm"
Show abstract

Low-cost four-degree-of-freedom (DoF) arms are among the most accessible robotic platforms. But they are one joint short for picking up in situations where objects are at arbitrary orientations, a task that appears to require five degrees of freedom: the planar position (x and y), the height (z), a wrist rotation to align the gripper with the object, and gripper actuation, of which a four-DoF arm lacks the wrist rotation. This work uses perception and motion planning so that such an arm, a roughly $200 Waveshare RoArm-M2-S under a fixed overhead camera, can detect and color-sort writing utensils without that joint. A YOLO11n-OBB (You Only Look Once, oriented bounding box) detector locates each writing utensil; camera intrinsics and an ArUco reference pose convert its pixel coordinates to robot coordinates; and a color classifier labels it. In 196 single-utensil trials, the 98 that started beyond 45 degrees from that direction all became graspable within four sweeps (130 total). Overall, 176 (89.8%) ended in the correct bin; 19 of 20 failures were slipped or missed grasps, one a mislabeled color. For the seven utensils tested in this setup, corrective sweeps brought every steeply misaligned utensil into a graspable orientation on an arm with no wrist joint.

Computing Invariant Reachable Sets through Sum-of-Squares Programming 2026-11-03
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 3 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Control Systems Technology. Code: github.com/khalid2696/computeSOSFunnels
Show abstract

Funnels, time-varying invariant sets around nominal trajectories, certify that a closed-loop system tracks its plan, and can be composed into verified motion plans. Their computation by sum-of-squares (SOS) programming is well established in formulation but not in practice: the result depends on implementation choices such as the initial guess, Lyapunov function normalization, and termination rule, which the literature documents only briefly. This paper presents a self-contained treatment of SOS-based funnel computation, implemented in the open-source package computeSOSFunnels. We propose a minimum-volume formulation with inlet and outlet constraints under which funnels with coinciding nominal endpoints compose by construction. Numerical experiments on a unicycle, with timing studies on a cart-pole and a 12-state quadrotor, characterize the pipeline: infeasible initial guesses are partly decidable before solving, the certificate shows no violations among 39,000 boundary samples per funnel under the true dynamics, and constructing the semidefinite program, not solving it, dominates computation time.

Grasping and Stabilizing Tumbling Objects with Rotating-Base Multi-Arm Space Manipulators 2026-11-03
Show abstract

This paper presents an innovative guidance and control method for a space manipulator that actively maneuvers its base to hold a tumbling object stationary relative to the base during proximity operations. This simplifies motion planning, reduces collision risk, and enables impact-free capture using single- or multi-arm configurations, with or without predefined grasping interfaces. The proposed approach involves two phases. In the pre-grasping phase, a synchronization control aligns the target's center of mass (CoM) with the system's instantaneous center of rotation (ICR), virtually rigidizing the target-servicer system to keep the object stationary for reliable grasping. In the post grasping phase, a control strategy based on Hamiltonian dynamics and optimal control stabilizes the combined system by minimizing time, fuel, or energy, while respecting base torque limits and multiple grasping constraints. h-NESDF: An Infinitely Differentiable Signed Distance Between Two Sets with Robotic Applications 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Computing distances between sets is important in many robotic applications, such as motion planning and control. In many applications, derivatives of the distance are needed as well. It is well known that the Euclidean distance is not always differentiable, which create issues to algorithms that need a derivative. In this paper, we introduce NESDF, the Normal Euclidean Signed Distance Function between two general (with mild regularity assumptions but possibly non-convex) sets. This metric is still not differentiable, but it allows us to introduce a generalization, $h$-NESDF, which has a smoothing parameter $h$ such that the metric is infinitely differentiable for all $h>0$ and that reduces to the NESDF when $h$ goes to $0$. The metric is amenable to GPU parallelization, and thus can be computed efficiently. We show extensive simulation results to evaluate its viability in motion planning.

TAMPAgent: Agentic Belief-Space Planning for Mobile Manipulation 2026-11-02
📝 Website link: https://umishra.me/TAMPAgent/
Show abstract

Open-world robotic mobile manipulation requires acting in physical environments with only partial information. While recent Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) agents show strong open-world reasoning capabilities, existing architectures that rely on direct tool calling struggle with geometric uncertainty and thus are limited to fully-observable tabletop environments. In contrast, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems can solve long-horizon tasks under partial observability but incur high computational costs and require closed worlds. We propose \textbf{TAMPAgent}, an agentic planning framework that unifies the reasoning of LVLMs with the structured constraint satisfaction of TAMP systems. We endow an LVLM agent with a belief-space planning model operating over an extensible API of perception, planning, and control tools. The agent formulates a partial task plan composed of parameterized observation and execution actions, which is subsequently completed using classical search algorithms. After extracting the plan's geometric constraints, the agent leverages its tool API to bind valid parameter values, satisfying feasibility requirements and producing an executable motion plan. Upon execution, the agent continuously updates its belief history and repeats this closed-loop process by interleaving observation and manipulation actions. Evaluated across complex household mobile manipulation tasks on Rainbow Y1 and Unitree G1 platforms, TAMPAgent significantly outperforms direct tool-calling agentic baselines, demonstrating that integrating TAMP constraint satisfaction into agentic planning loops is essential for unstructured, partially observable environments.

This study introduces TRAIN: TeRrain-Aware Interactive Navigation, a deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based framework for quadrupedal navigation across unstructured terrain and human-populated environments. On the other hand, end-to-end methods are more integrated but require high-frequency sensing, which is often noisy and computationally costly. (2) TRAIN-Nav applies a symmetric AC framework for navigation with interactive pedestrians, directly mapping transformed LiDAR data to ego-agent actions under differential-drive kinematics. (3) A unified pipeline, TRAIN, integrates TRAIN-Loc and TRAIN-Nav, supporting terrain-aware quadrupedal navigation in uneven and interactive environments. CLIS-MPPI: Closed-Loop Model Predictive Path Integral Control With Initial-State Sampling Under Execution Delay 2026-10-15

Show abstract

Sampling-based motion planning such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control has become attractive for robotics due to its ability to handle nonlinear dynamics and non-smooth objectives without gradient information, while leveraging parallel rollout computation for real-time performance. However, in real-world robotic systems, the optimized control input is often applied after a non-negligible planning delay, causing a mismatch between the state used in forward simulation and the actual state at control execution, which can significantly degrade planning accuracy and may even lead to unsafe behavior such as collisions. This paper proposes Closed-Loop MPPI with Initial-State Sampling (CLIS-MPPI), which explicitly addresses execution-time state mismatch by modeling planning delay as a distribution over plausible execution-time initial states, performing rollout optimization from sampled initial states, and applying a rollout-based local feedback correction during control execution. The proposed method is validated through real-world experiments on a 6-DoF UR5e manipulator in a narrow-passage task involving obstacle avoidance and end-effector target reaching. Across 5 real-world runs of the same task, standard MPPI collided in 2 runs, whereas CLIS-MPPI remained collision-free in all 5 runs. Averaged over all collision-free runs for each method (3 for MPPI and 5 for CLIS-MPPI), CLIS-MPPI reduced the average task completion time from 25.4s to 24.4s and the average end-effector path length from 0.456 m to 0.425 m. It also reduced the overall joint-position and joint-velocity root mean square errors (RMSEs) from 0.100 rad and 0.108 rad/s to 0.017 rad and 0.011 rad/s, respectively. These results demonstrate improved safety, planning-execution consistency, and motion smoothness.
