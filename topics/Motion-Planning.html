Dispersive Tree Agreements for Multi-Robot Coordination 2026-10-22
📝 19 pages, 5 figures, 7 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Robotics
Show abstract

Multi-robot coordination of nonlinear systems is naturally formulated as joint-space optimal control subject to constraints over groups of robots. Existing methods that approach joint optimality remain largely centralized, offline, and confined to collision-free motion planning, while decentralized, real-time counterparts target safe navigation without pursuing the joint optimum. This paper develops a decentralized, real-time planner that approaches the joint optimum as an anytime process. We extend a recent single-robot framework that searches dispersive trees of forward-propagated commands for differentially flat systems to coordinate a multi-robot team near-optimally. We first show that the inter-robot coupling can be effectively replaced by some local constraints, under which locally grown dispersive trees contain a near-optimal agreement. It is this structure that the robots pursue by decentralized column generation, negotiating rents on the shared space that guide the search of each robot's tree. The negotiation settles on an agreement whose near-optimality is certified at quiescence. Our protocol, WarpWeaver, implements this negotiation as an asynchronous real-time loop over a receding horizon, and demonstrates effective closure to quiesced agreements even when real-time deadlines cut the negotiation short. In simulation, WarpWeaver reduces total travel time by up to 22% against centralized offline planners on the Dynobench suite and by up to 37% against a decentralized real-time baseline, and nearly doubles the success rate of a task-specific planner on graph connectivity. Hardware experiments on six robots planning onboard demonstrate real-world implementability.

SCOPE: Scene-graph Construction Optimized by Plan Error-correction for Robot Long-term Planning Datasets 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages. Accepted at IROS 2026. Project page: https://scope-page.github.io/
Show abstract

Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) stands as a fundamental challenge in robotics, essential for enabling autonomous agents to execute complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse environments. While Large Language Models (LLMs) introduce a new paradigm for TAMP by leveraging extensive common sense, their inherent reasoning limitations hinder direct application, and fine-tuning strategies to enhance these capabilities are currently bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality datasets. Existing automated generation methods often suffer from homogeneity, limiting model generalization, and unsolvability, where hallucinated scene graphs create physical mismatches with task goals. To address these issues, we propose Scene-graph Construction Optimized by Plan Error-correction (SCOPE), an automated framework designed to generate diverse and executable TAMP datasets. SCOPE incorporates multi-level diversity metrics to maximize the information entropy of scenarios. Crucially, it introduces a Plan Feedback Error-correction pipeline that utilizes planner execution failures as feedback signals to retroactively rectify logical inconsistencies in scene graphs, ensuring physical feasibility. Experimental results demonstrate that SCOPE exhibits greater robustness to erroneous scene graphs and generates higher-quality data. When utilized for LLM fine-tuning, SCOPE-generated data yield a downstream TAMP model that achieves an absolute 12.33% gain in success rate over its counterpart fine-tuned with data generated by DELTA, a state-of-the-art PDDL-based planner.

V2-STRep: VLM-Grounded Structured Task Representations for Reusable Robot Skills Acquired from Generated Videos 2026-10-22
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Human manipulation videos provide rich motion and interaction cues for acquiring robot skills without robot demonstrations. Video generation models synthesize such demonstrations from an initial scene image and task instruction, avoiding the need to record demonstrations for each task. However, the recovered motion captures only one scene-specific realization, leaving task structure, geometric relations, and constraints implicit. We present V2-STRep, a zero-shot framework that converts generated video motion into reusable robot skills through VLM-grounded structured task representations. The representation specifies motion phases, references, and task-relevant constraints, with targets described by minimal geometric structures: points, point-normals, axes, planes, and full 6D poses. VLM-provided 2D image-space cues are lifted into 3D using RGB-D observations to reconstruct task geometry and candidate grasp poses. Geometry-specific rules transfer motion to new scenes, while task-constrained trajectory optimization couples grasp selection with complete robot motion planning. It preserves task requirements while using remaining rotational freedom to accommodate joint limits. Updating deployment grounding and constraints enables reuse under new compatible instructions without generating another video. Experiments on six real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate improved execution success over baselines, reliable cross-scene transfer of successfully acquired skills, and adaptation to changed deployment instructions. Details are available on the project website: https://tuwien-asl.github.io/V2-STRep/.

ReactiveBFM: Reactive Closed-Loop Motion Planning Towards Universal Humanoid Whole-Body Control 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted by CoRL 2026. Project page: https://xiao-chen.tech/reactivebfm/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

While current Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) provide robust control priors for humanoids, they only execute pre-defined reference motions. As a result, they are vulnerable to environmental shifts and incapable of reactive whole-body coordination. Naively cascading them with generative motion planners fails to achieve true reactivity, as inevitable tracking discrepancies induce fatal cumulative exposure bias. To bridge this gap, we propose ReactiveBFM, a real-time closed-loop planning-control framework. At its core, we effectively mitigate exposure bias via a scheduled prefix sampling curriculum, forcing the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors from imperfect physical states rather than ground-truth trajectories. Systematically, to reconcile the severe latency mismatch between auto-regressive planning and high-frequency tracking, we introduce an asynchronous replanning mechanism. Combined with trajectory chunking to temporally ensemble spatial references, our system guarantees spatio-temporally fluid execution without physical jitter. Deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid, ReactiveBFM demonstrates unprecedented physical agility across a vast repertoire of text-conditioned closed-loop motions. Notably, ReactiveBFM achieves zero-shot moving target reaching, showcasing intricate whole-body coordination and on-the-fly replanning. In sim-to-sim benchmarking under severe perturbations, ReactiveBFM achieves a 93.1% success rate, significantly outperforming cascaded open-loop baselines by 28.6%.

Demonstration-Based Robotic Feeding Using Dynamic Movement Primitives 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted on RAAI 2026
Show abstract

This paper presents a demonstration-based robotic feeding framework that integrates human demonstration, Dynamic Movement Primitive-based motion planning, vision-based scene perception, and robot control. A human operator first demonstrates the scooping and feeding motions while the position and Euler-angle orientation of the spoon are recorded using a motion-capture system. The recorded trajectory is segmented into scooping and feeding motions, which are encoded using separate Dynamic Movement Primitives. Before task execution, an RGB-D camera estimates the participant's mouth position, while two AprilTags are used for robot-frame registration and tray localization. The detected tray pose is used to position the scooping motion, whereas the detected mouth position defines the positional goal of the feeding motion. The approach, scooping, and feeding trajectories are then executed sequentially by a Kinova Gen3 manipulator through a ROS~2-based framework. A real-world experiment demonstrates the feasibility of the integrated pipeline and shows that the learned spoon motions can be reproduced within the detected experimental scene.

AgentDreamer: Agentic 3D Motion Reasoning for Text-Controllable Physics Simulation 2026-10-22
📝 Project page: https://cv.snu.ac.kr/research/AgentDreamer
Show abstract

We propose AgentDreamer, an agent-guided optimization framework that aligns text instructions with simulated motion through explicit 3D motion planning. Given a text instruction and scene geometry, an off-the-shelf agent translates the instruction into a structured semantic motion prior, specifying what should move, in which direction, and in what temporal order. The agent represents this prior using parametric functions over a series of temporal chunks, yielding dense 3D trajectories. We then optimize the parameters of a differentiable MPM simulator such that its rollout best matches the prior while remaining constrained by the scene dynamics. This allows the agent to provide semantic guidance from language, while the simulator grounds it in physically feasible motion. We further introduce Motion-IG, an information-theoretic metric for text-motion alignment based on masked text recovery. By comparing the likelihood of recovering masked motion-related content with and without the simulated motion, Motion-IG measures the information contributed by the motion beyond that available from the remaining text and scene context. Experiments show that AgentDreamer improves text alignment over video-based guidance, producing simulations that more closely follow the requested physical behavior.

Bimanual dexterous grasping of large objects is a critical challenge in robotic manipulation. However, most existing studies focus on sequential manipulation rather than cooperative grasping, and methods addressing such bimanual tasks have largely been limited to simulation. These limitations stem from the difficulty of acquiring full 3D object models and generating physically plausible grasping actions. To fill this gap, we propose a real-world bimanual grasping framework that includes: a multimodal dataset capturing joint angles, visual observations and force signals; a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)-based module that generates joint-level grasp configurations from segmented point clouds; and an execution strategy that integrates motion planning with online grasp refinement to ensure physical stability and feasibility. Continuous-Time Motion Planning and Steering with Global Convergence 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Motion planning is central to robotics, and it is increasingly used to correct the motions proposed by learned planners and policies. In both uses, constraints must hold at every instant of the motion, not only at sampled times. To our knowledge, no algorithm provably converges to a globally optimal motion once a smoothness cost is combined with such constraints. We cast planning and steering as a single continuous-time program: robots avoid one another and static or moving obstacles, and a learned proposal is steered by keeping the motion close to it. The program is elementary to state, yet nonconvex. We propose GlobMP (Global Motion Planning), a gradient projection method over piecewise-polynomial curves, on which each constraint is one polynomial per piece that must stay nonnegative over the whole motion; it alternates cost reduction with feasibility restoration. Under mild assumptions, we show that, from a fine grid start, its cost approaches the global optimum as the number of polynomial pieces grows, with every constraint met to any chosen tolerance, or exactly on stable instances. We demonstrate its superior performance over strong baselines in real and synthetic experiments, including teams of up to $200$ robots, multi-robot planning on the Robotarium testbed and an extended benchmark, maze navigation, and steering tactile-reactive policies on a bimanual humanoid.

DriveMLP: When Training Strategy Outperforms Model Complexity in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-21
📝 Accepted at 2026 Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL)
Show abstract

Learning motion planning policies that remain reliable under closed-loop execution is a central challenge in Autonomous Driving (AD). Imitation Learning (IL) scales well to large AD datasets but suffers from distribution shift, causal confusion, and compounding errors under closed-loop execution. In contrast, Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically requires handcrafted rule-based rewards and massive simulation. We study a simple closed-loop IL framework that uses RL purely as an optimization tool, guided by a trajectory-distance reward derived from logged expert data. We refer to this training strategy as Expert-Guided RL (EGRL). Linear Twisting Sidewinding Gaits for Twistable Snake Robot Motion Planning on Complex Terrains 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Sidewinding is an efficient gait for snake robots to locomote on various terrains, by undulating the backbone curve and shifting the contact segments along it. However, relying solely on undulatory motion limits the locomotive capability of snake robots, and combining it with twisting motion can improve their speed and robustness. This hypothesis has recently been verified by the rolling vector approach, which locomotes the robot like a differential-drive car. Yet its twisting is concentrated in a short buffer segment, imposing most of the workload on the two twist joints in the middle, which can often overburden the motors and limit its locomotion. Vector-Quantized World Model: Latent Feedback Motion Planning for Robot Manipulation 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Visual world models have shown strong potential for robot planning, yet long-horizon manipulation remains challenging due to compounding prediction errors, large search complexity, and latent plans that the low-level controller cannot reliably reach. In this work, we introduce Vector-Quantized World Model (VQ-WM), a latent feedback motion planning framework that integrates discrete long-horizon search with continuous visual feedback control: a discrete latent predictor models transitions between vector-quantized latent states, allowing the planner to look far ahead and find a shortest path to the goal through discrete search and a continuous learned controller sequentially reaches the planned states through visual feedback, correcting deviations to mitigate compounding errors. Whereas most vector quantization uses Euclidean distance, we use a novel estimator of the first-passage-time as a quasi-metric, which aligns the planning abstraction with the controller's capabilities. We evaluate on the physical robot manipulation of the Tower of Hanoi puzzle: whereas existing state-of-the-art foundation models (VLAs, WAMs) only succeed when the solution trace in their training dataset, our method achieves 100\% success rate with only short-play data, demonstrate its ability to compose long-horizon solutions beyond the short demonstrated trajectories and execute them reliably. Our solution also outperforms strongest world-models by 13.3--85.3 percentage points on simulation benchmarks spanning manipulation and locomotion, demonstrating consistent progress and new capabilities.

FPTAS for Polygonal Multi-Robot Motion Planning 2026-10-20
📝 To appear in SODA 2027
Show abstract

We study multi-robot motion planning in a polygonal environment with polygonal robots. The setting of the problem consists of a polygonal domain $\mathcal{W}$ with $n$ vertices and $k$ robots $R_1,\dots,R_k$ translating in $\mathcal{W}$ each of which is represented by a polygon of constant complexity. Given a source configuration $\mathbf{s}$ and a target configuration $\mathbf{t}$ of the robots in $\mathcal{W}$, the goal of the problem is to find a collision-free motion plan from $\mathbf{s}$ to $\mathbf{t}$ that is shortest with respect to some cost function. We present the first fully polynomial-time approximation schemes (FPTAS) for motion planning with any fixed number $k$ of robots, under the min-sum objective, the min-max objective, and the min-makespan objective. Here, the min-sum variant aims to minimize the total traveling distance of all robots, the min-max variant aims to minimize the maximum traveling distance of a single robot, and the min-makespan variant aims to minimize the total time cost under the assumption that all robots move with a maximum speed $1$. The algorithm directly generalizes to the problem in any fixed dimension $d$, with a polytope environment and polytope robots. Our result settles an open question of Agarwal, Halperin, Sharir, Steiger [SODA'24] and Agarwal, Holmgren, Steiger [SODA'26], who gave an FPTAS for $k=2$ unit-square robots and a bicriteria-approximation algorithm for a fixed number $k$ of unit-square or unit-disk robots, respectively. Compared to the previous results, our algorithm is substantially simpler.

CAOS-SLAM: Class-Agnostic Object-Aware Real-Time vSLAM for Dynamic Environments 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Robust visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (vSLAM) in dynamic environments faces challenges due to the presence of moving objects that may introduce erroneous observations, corrupt the map reconstruction and overall degrade localization accuracy. Existing vSLAM approaches often rely on predefined semantic classes or costly pixel-wise classification, limiting their robustness or applicability in real-world scenarios. This paper presents CAOS-SLAM, a dynamic vSLAM framework that extends ORB-SLAM3 with a hybrid perception pipeline to detect, track, and filter both semantically known and previously unseen moving objects in real-time. On-demand panoptic segmentation is performed for object discovery, with candidate unknown objects instantiated through depth-based clustering of unclassified regions. Between panoptic updates, persistent object tracks are updated using lightweight detection when available or temporal mask propagation based on world-frame Kalman prediction, depth consistency, and appearance similarity. The estimated object motion is then used to reject dynamic features from the SLAM pipeline. Experimental results on multiple dynamic RGB-D datasets and real-world experiments demonstrate improved localization accuracy over state-of-the-art dynamic vSLAM approaches, achieving up to 59% lower localization error in scenes characterized by unknown dynamic objects and up to 88% lower error in heavily occluded scenes, while achieving a median processing rate of 30 FPS on a laptop. Furthermore, the tracking module provides estimates of object trajectories and velocities, supporting potential future applications in perception, navigation, and motion-planning tasks in dynamic environments. Code and dataset will be released upon acceptance.

TrajEdit: Real-Time Flow-Matching Trajectory Adaptation via FlowEdit and CBF Safety Filter 2026-10-16
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Safe, real-time motion planning is especially difficult when a dynamic obstacle actively pursues the ego agent, requiring rapid adaptation as the environment changes. We present TrajEdit, a hierarchical trajectory planner for pursuit-evasion that combines flow matching, trajectory editing, and safety filtering. Rather than regenerating trajectories from scratch at every step, TrajEdit follows the current plan under nominal conditions and adapts its remaining portion through FlowEdit as the pursuer approaches, and falls back to a control barrier function safety filter if the adversarial agent is within a safety threshold. Our use of FlowEdit for trajectory editing reduces computation time while maintaining a comparable success rate. We evaluate TrajEdit in 2D pursuit-evasion with static obstacles and an adversarial pursuer, demonstrating high success rates with low capture and collision rates, while achieving computational time savings over full replanning baselines.

FLASH-CBF-RRT: Fast Lipschitz-Adaptive Safe Horizon CBF Steering for Certified Motion Planning 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Fast motion planning requires both sample-efficient exploration and computationally efficient local steering. Conventional straight-line steering is inexpensive but wastes samples by rejecting colliding extensions. Quadratic program controllers with control barrier function (CBF) constraints can be used to achieve effective local steering but can increase the planning time due to the expensive sequential evaluations. We develop a method to accelerate CBF steering by computing a time horizon within which CBF-filtered velocity may be applied constantly using closed-form kinematic bounds on collision-clearance. Our method, FLASH-CBF-RRT, significantly reduces the number of evaluations compared to prior CBF-RRT approaches because the planner can take adaptive steering step sizes that are shorter near obstacles and longer in open space. We implement this steering procedure as an extension of RRT-Connect in OMPL. Across 689 MotionBenchMaker problems, FLASH-CBF-RRT matches RRT-Connect's 100% success rate while reducing median planning time by 3.82x, and is 3.88x faster than fixed-step CBF-RRT. Hardware experiments on a 17-DoF mobile manipulator demonstrate 2.2x faster planning and 4.2x fewer evaluations. Videos and results are available at: https://mamani828.github.io/FLASH-CBF-RRT/.

Auditable and Deployable Multi-Agent Motion Planning via Posetal Games from Legal Guidelines 2026-10-15
Show abstract

When robots are deployed in the real-world, they must interact with other agents and accomplish their own goals while adhering to legal constraints. These potentially conflicting objectives are often only partially prioritized, making scalarized objectives or fully specified hierarchies unsuitable. We present a framework for multi-agent motion planning under legal requirements based on games with partially ordered preferences (posetal games). Scenario-independent legal guidelines order abstract legal concerns, from which we automatically derive agent preferences and a legal game for any concrete scenario. Agents may refine their legal preferences with private objectives, and regulators may refine the guideline as laws evolve. We prove that such customizations can only remove equilibria and thus preserve legality. To coordinate agents without explicitly communicating private preferences, we introduce belief play, which provably recovers the true equilibria from conservative local games, and a distributed iterative best-response protocol requiring less information. We apply the framework to maritime navigation, systematically lifting pairwise traffic rules to multi-vessel encounters. On seven encounters with up to four vessels, every rule violation of the committed equilibria is certified as forced by the legal hierarchy or unsatisfiable, and defectors are identified from public information in all tested cases. These results indicate that guideline-based posetal games offer an auditable route from partially specified laws to multi-agent motion planning.

B-CTMP departs from prior CTMP in two ways: neighborhoods are constructed in object-pose space rather than robot configuration space, and coverage is established by statistical certification rather than a reachability check. A plan is cached only if repeated rollouts lower-bound its success rate above a user-specified threshold, and we prove these bounds hold simultaneously across the entire cache at a prescribed confidence level. For deterministic behaviors a single rollout suffices, recovering the binary check of prior CTMP as a special case. We evaluate B-CTMP on three manipulation tasks---shelf picking, plug insertion, and wheel replacement---in simulation and on real robots. B-CTMP's certified plans succeed consistently where baselines fail during behavior execution, and it rejects infeasible object poses in constant time.

Path finding and multi-robot motion planning require trajectories that are smooth, goal-directed, and collision-free in environments with complex geometric constraints. Recent diffusion-based planners have shown that trajectory generation can be cast as iterative denoising which has opened the doors to learning-based approaches that can handle multi-modal trajectory distributions and refine entire trajectories. However, a key limitation is that diffusion planners require training on large collections of feasible trajectories, rendering them map-specific, and difficult to deploy when high-quality demonstrations are unavailable. This paper introduces a training-free diffusion-based motion planner that replaces learned global trajectory scores with analytical local scores derived from obstacle, smoothness, velocity, and inter-agent feasibility terms. The proposed idea relies on a key observation: the score of a trajectory can be reconstructed by considering only local interactions between neighboring waypoints and nearby constraints. This structure exploitation yields a decomposed denoising procedure that retains the optimization structure of classical trajectory methods while inheriting the iterative refinement behavior of diffusion models. Experiments on a large collection of complex environments and large multi-agent planning tasks show that the proposed analytical score produces smooth and feasible trajectories within limited computational costs, for example in generating feasible paths for 300+ agents in environments containing 100+ obstacles in under 6 seconds on a GPU, outperforming strong learning-based and optimization baselines, while avoiding the data requirements of learned diffusion planners.
