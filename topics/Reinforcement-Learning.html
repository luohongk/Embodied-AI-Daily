Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking 2026-10-20
📝 Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/
Show abstract

Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.

InterWebCoder: Learning Interactive Webpage Generation from Executable Tests via Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Interactive webpages underpin modern applications, but reproducing both their visual appearance and multi-step behavior remains challenging for multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Scalable training data must capture user actions, state transitions, and expected outcomes, while real-world webpage code is often entangled with irrelevant scripts and external dependencies. Moreover, existing GUI-agent-based verification provides slow and potentially unstable functional feedback. To address these challenges, we propose InterWebCoder, an efficient framework for interactive webpage code generation. InterWebCoder introduces a Test-Specification-Guided and Execution-Verified Data Synthesis pipeline that records real-world interaction trajectories and synthesizes self-contained webpage code with executable test scripts. A failureattributed repair mechanism uses runtime evidence to locate and iteratively fix errors in either the webpage code or test script. We then combine test-ID-weighted supervised fine-tuning with group-relative policy optimization, directly optimizing functional correctness and visual fidelity using test pass rates and state-level visual similarity. This replaces costly GUI-agent verification with fast, reproducible executable feedback. Our pipeline produces 1,736 verified samples from 2,058 tasks, achieving an 84.35% synthesis success rate. On IWR-Bench and Vision2Web, InterWebCoder achieves final scores of 48.54 and 48.42 with a 9B backbone, outperforming 30B-level models and Gemini-3.7-Flash while remaining competitive with Claude-Sonnet-5. It reduces reward-computation latency by over 97%, eliminates external MLLM verification costs, and accelerates end-to-end training by up to 1.4x.

AgenticRoboRL: Bringing Coding-Agent Post-Training to Agentic Robotics 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Agentic robotics systems combine planners with robot harnesses to perform complex manipulation tasks. Recent work improves these systems through better harnesses and external memory, but relies on powerful closed-source models as planners and does not improve the planners' own capabilities. In contrast, coding agents have improved substantially by post-training the model itself. We therefore investigate whether post-training can also improve robot planners. However, applying it across robot harnesses requires accommodating different execution mechanisms, including VLA skill invocation and control-code generation. To this end, we introduce AgenticRoboRL, a framework for post-training robot planners across different harnesses. The training recipe combines supervised fine-tuning with multi-turn reinforcement learning. To accommodate different execution mechanisms, harness-specific adapters connect recorded planner interactions with task outcomes, enabling a common RL training pipeline while keeping execution components fixed. On LIBERO-PRO, AgenticRoboRL raises the success rate of a 9B-parameter planner from 18.1% to 71.0% without external memory, approaching the 72.1% achieved by Codex (GPT-5.5) with external memory. These results demonstrate post-training as an effective approach for improving robot planners. We will release the framework and trained planner checkpoints.

Reasoning with Continuous Latent Diffusion 2026-10-20
Show abstract

We introduce the Continuous Embedding Diffusion Reasoner (CEDR), a training and inference pipeline for continuous latent diffusion models that achieves strong performance on mathematical reasoning and code generation. We learn compact answer representations from three hidden layers of the autoregressive Qwen3-4B model. A staged curriculum first trains the ELF-based diffusion backbone with teacher prompt conditioning, then initializes a compact prompt encoder through imitation and jointly adapts both networks. This replaces the large teacher contextual prompt encoder at inference with a compact learned Transformer. The multilayer answer representation also enables asynchronous inference, with different components denoised under separate clocks. We further adapt DiffusionNFT to learned self-conditioning guidance, enabling reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. Controlled ablations evaluate the effects of representation design, prompt training, and denoising schedules. CEDR outperforms recent continuous-diffusion baselines at comparable backbone scales, including before reinforcement learning. With a 638M-parameter denoising backbone and DMD2-style stochastic sampling, post-NFT CEDR-L achieves {pass@1, pass@8} accuracies of {68.97%, 80.82%} on GSM8K and {32.40%, 53.80%} on MATH500 at 64 denoising steps per sample, and {37.56%, 51.00%} on HumanEval+ and {26.96%, 39.92%} on MBPP+ at 128 steps per sample. Code and model weights are available at https://github.com/chengxiang/CEDR and https://huggingface.co/xc91/CEDR, respectively.

Continuous-Time Policy Gradient from Discrete Data: Convergence Analysis and Model-Free Algorithms 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Continuous-time reinforcement learning seeks an optimal policy under unknown dynamics driven by stochastic differential equations using observations collected at discrete times. We develop a policy gradient framework that combines rigorous convergence analysis with learning from discrete data. We first establish a performance difference lemma and derive policy gradient formulas for randomized and parametrized policies. We then prove exponential convergence of an entropy-regularized policy flow to the regularized optimum. For discrete parametrized updates, we derive a convergence bound that explicitly separates the contributions of critic error, policy-regression error, and the learning step size. To enable learning under unknown dynamics, we integrate this framework with PhiBE, which constructs a continuous-time surrogate generator from discrete transition data. The resulting on-policy and off-policy model-free actor-critic algorithms preserve the continuous-time structure of the control problem. Numerical experiments on an entropy-regularized linear-quadratic regulator and a nonlinear wheeled robot demonstrate the performance and stability of the proposed methods with both known and data-driven dynamics.

SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 (Oral). Project website: https://supermimic.github.io
Show abstract

Tracking large human-motion datasets has become the dominant route to general, expressive humanoid whole-body control, but scaling across many motions is expensive. Current pipelines use model-free reinforcement learning, either merging per-motion specialists through lossy distillation, or brute-forcing a single policy with cluster-scale compute. SuperMimic amortizes learning across all motions with one differentiable world model of their shared dynamics, fit from logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator. The world model and the tracking policy train together in one loop, and backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts gives the policy analytic gradients. Fast imagined rollouts and analytic gradients make policy learning efficient. In 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs. It reduces GPU-hours by more than $1000\times$ and fails on only 25 of 129,514 benchmark motions, versus 179 failures for the baseline. After 9.8 hours on two GPUs, SuperMimic achieves zero failures. From 1 to 264 hours of motion, the world model's prediction error stays stable, and GPU-hours to a target return grow slowly. Our policy transfers to a physical Unitree G1 across diverse and acrobatic motions. SuperMimic removes compute as the bottleneck to scaling humanoid whole-body control, making it practical to measure motion-tracking scaling laws.

When Synthetic Speech Is All You Have: Better Call GRPO 2026-10-20
📝 7 pages; accepted to IEEE SLT 2026
Show abstract

LLM-based ASR adapted to regulated domains such as banking is bottlenecked by privacy: real speech is costly and legally constrained to collect, making synthetic text-to-speech (TTS) an attractive substitute. Yet synthetic speech stays acoustically mismatched with real recordings, and work on this gap has stayed within supervised fine-tuning (SFT). We instead turn to reinforcement learning, and show that Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) extracts far more from the same synthetic speech than SFT. Synthetic-only adaptation of the model with GRPO, a critic-free method rewarding low-WER hypotheses, reduces WER by 40% relative to SFT (36.71%$\to$22.09%), and an SFT-then-GRPO combination pushes this further to 45%. We trace the gain to behavior rather than representation: GRPO reduces insertion errors by improving stopping calibration and speech-to-text alignment by better anchoring attention to audio, leaving early-layer representations intact. When synthetic speech is the main resource, reinforcement learning should be preferred over supervised fine-tuning.

Answering in the User's Variety: Calibrated Accommodation Pairs for Post-Training 2026-10-20
Show abstract

People adapt how they talk to the person in front of them. However, chat models tend to respond in Standard English regardless of the dialect used by the user. A uniform answer can hide whether training treats these users alike, and output audits cannot tell as well. We trace how each post-training stage, from supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to preference optimization (DPO) and reinforcement learning, changes a model's tendency to match the user, and read what each update prefers from two content-matched replies, one matching the user and one not. Across 10 open model lineages, post-training standardizes replies to every informal cue, yet after adjusting for how likely each reply already was, the DPO update still favours matching British and Australian spelling over AAE and Singlish on 10 lineages, and models still produce AAE features when asked. We then introduce calibrated accommodation pairs (CAP), preference data that reward matching users' dialect density while preserving Standard English for Standard-English users. On a fully open model, CAP raises convergence on features it was not trained on without a detectable increase in dialect for Standard-English users or a cost in instruction following. In contrast, one-sided pairs reach a similar gain only by overshooting the user and imposing dialect. Our findings show that uniform replies can hide selective updates and audits should read the update as well as the output. Code: https://github.com/kouzhizhuo/cap-accommodation.

Fast Motion Retargeting Pipeline for Scalable Humanoid Robot Learning 2026-10-20
Show abstract

While large-scale human motion capture datasets provide a rich source of demonstrations for humanoid robot learning, retargeting the diverse motions to different humanoid robots remains a nontrivial challenge. Existing kinodynamic trajectory optimization pipelines are computationally expensive and often require manual adjustments per motion. We present FRDV (Fast Retargeting with Dynamics Validation), a scalable framework consisting of rapid constrained kinematic retargeting and an additional dynamics validation pipeline. Its kinematic stage integrates a custom Sequential Quadratic Programming solver, Real-Time Iteration, and parallel chunk-based processing, achieving up to 9,156 frames per second, approximately up to 136 times faster than GMR and ProtoMotions, while robot-specific constraints are incorporated into the optimization to reduce common retargeting artifacts, such as ground penetration and self-collision. Kamino: GPU-based Simulation of Rigid Multi-Body Physics with Kinematic Loops 2026-10-20
Show abstract

We present Kamino, a GPU-based physics solver for massively parallel simulations of heterogeneous highly-coupled mechanical systems. Implemented in Python using NVIDIA Warp and integrated into the Newton framework, it enables the application of data-driven methods, such as large-scale reinforcement learning, to complex robotic systems that exhibit strongly coupled kinematic and dynamic constraints such as kinematic loops. The latter are often circumvented by approximating the system's topology as a kinematic tree, and incorporating explicit loop-closure constraints or so-called mimic joints. Kamino aims at alleviating this burden by natively supporting these types of couplings. Moreover, Kamino supports heterogeneous worlds, allowing for batched simulation of structurally diverse robots on a single GPU. At its core lies a state-of-the-art constrained optimization algorithm based on ADMM, which computes constraint forces by solving the constrained rigid multi-body forward dynamics transcribed as a nonlinear complementarity problem. This leads to high-fidelity simulations that resolve contact dynamics without resorting to approximate models that simplify or convexify the problem. We demonstrate RL policy training on a biped with six nested kinematic loops, generating a feasible walking policy while simulating $4{,}096$ parallel environments on a single GPU.

FALCA: Falsification-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Hierarchical Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon Deep Search Agents 2026-10-20
📝 19 pages, 20 figures, 2 tables. Project page: https://douzj-llm.github.io/FALCA/
Show abstract

Long-horizon deep search requires LLM agents to iteratively decompose complex information needs, gather evidence from external sources, and maintain a reliable research state under multiple constraints. A central challenge is that early mistakes can be amplified over the search trajectory: agents may anchor on an incorrect candidate, collect partially supporting evidence, overlook contradictions, and accumulate errors that misguide subsequent reasoning. We propose FALCA, a falsification-aware framework that formulates deep search as a propose-falsify process. FALCA adopts a collaborative Searcher-Falsifier architecture, where the Searcher proposes candidate answers or sub-answers, while independent Falsifiers actively seek counterevidence, check unmet constraints, identify conflicts, and produce concise falsification reports for research-state revision. To strengthen this loop, we introduce falsification-aware training, combining SFT warmup on falsification trajectories with agentic RL based on parallel rollouts and hierarchical credit assignment over research stages, state transitions, and actions. Experiments on five search benchmarks show that FALCA improves long-horizon deep search performance over backbone and harness ablations, while reducing confirmation bias and error accumulation through more effective falsification and research-state revision.

Confessions of a Masked MDP 2026-10-20
📝 18 pages, 7 figures Submitted to AISTATS 2027
Show abstract

In reinforcement learning, one presumes the action set to be known ahead of time, and, thus, one must know if any state-action pairs are not permissible ahead of computations. In some applications, determining this action mask is the main computational bottleneck. Isolating this problem to highlight its core properties, we study how to combine the search for an optimal policy with the learning of the mask itself, under a linear MDP model in which each attempted action reveals the exact value of a linear score determining its validity. In this framework, we first construct an algorithm based on deterministic optimistic optimization and show that it is minimax optimal, which we show is also computationally efficient. However, it is sub-optimal on non-degenerate instances, as we show. We propose an alternative, which is also computationally efficient, which plans its exploration strategy. This algorithm admits a formal interpretation as an analogue of information-directed-sampling for deterministic feedback, which may be of independent interest. Finally, we study an application of this framework to transfer learning, where the invalid action set changes over time.

Reinforced Iterative Learning Control for elastic joint arms: Guaranteed cross-trajectory generalization under model uncertainties 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted for publication in Control Engineering Practice, 2026. 16 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Trajectory tracking for compliant robots is challenging due to nonlinear dynamics and modeling uncertainties, often requiring learning-based methods to avoid explicit parameter identification. Iterative Learning Control (ILC) ensures theoretically high precision and preserves the passive compliance of the robot. However, traditional ILC requires a separate learning phase for each new trajectory. Conversely, Reinforcement Learning (RL) generalizes to new trajectories, but practically suffers from high sample complexity and limited tracking precision. This work proposes Reinforced Iterative Learning Control (RILC) that combines the strengths of both approaches to achieve fast adaptation and guaranteed tracking convergence across diverse trajectories. By establishing a concurrent coupling between the two controllers, RILC overcomes the high initial errors and slow adaptation of traditional ILC, enabling high-performance tracking from the very first trials. In this architecture, ILC provides high-quality samples, while RL contributes as a generalizing best-effort policy across iterations. Additionally, a theoretical proof is presented to show that the RL component preserves ILC stability and, under specific conditions, can also reduce the error bound asymptotically. The efficacy of this framework is demonstrated through simulation and experiments on an underactuated 4-degree-of-freedom serial elastic robot using different trajectories and modeling mismatches.

Reward Misspecification Degrades Chain-of-Thought Monitorability 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Monitoring the chain-of-thought (CoT) of language models (LMs) is a key strategy for detecting and preventing misbehaviors for AI safety. As such, it is critical to identify reinforcement learning (RL) practices that lead to unmonitorable chain-of-thought. One such practice may be unavoidable: reward misspecification. As RL environments become more complex, it is infeasible to have perfect verification, and models are rewarded for behaviors that are different from the true task intent. We show that reward misspecification alone already degrades monitorability. We create a testbed with pairs of true reward aligned with task intent and misspecified rewards that favor a different behavior, so that reward hacking can be induced by training and labeled verifiably. Across three models and three tasks, CoT monitorability remains low as models' hack rates increase: the CoTs keep reasoning about the task intent while the outputs violate the intent and get rewarded. To mitigate unmonitorability without prior knowledge of the true reward or the hack, we collect rollouts with diverse rewards across training steps and use a LM to infer a reward description. We then inject the description into the input, which teaches the model to verbalize the reward. This reward description training improves monitorability by an average of 24% or 42% absolute, depending on the metric, across all models and tasks, and yields CoTs that reason about the misspecified reward explicitly. Code is available at https://github.com/Lorenayannnnn/reward-misspecification-degrades-cot-monitorability.

Adaptive-Group Process Rewards for GUI Agents 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Training graphical user interface (GUI) agents with reinforcement learning suffers from sparse outcome rewards, leading to inefficient learning. Process rewards offer a promising direction for credit assignment to intermediate actions; however, accurately evaluating an action requires identifying evidence across long-horizon GUI trajectories, as its value may depend on information beyond its immediate state and observation. In this paper, we propose an adaptive-group process reward framework that dynamically partitions a GUI trajectory into semantically coherent subgoal groups, enabling process reward estimation at both the action and group levels. At the action level, a critic evaluates each action based on the visual changes and subsequent actions within the group for action correctness and contribution to the subgoal. At the group level, the framework aggregates action-level evaluations to assess subgoal completion and contribution to the overall task, and further refines these assessments by leveraging the context of all groups in the trajectory. The process reward of each action is obtained by combining its action-level evaluation with the refined group-level evaluation. We further augment GRPO's outcome advantage with the proposed process rewards to provide finer-grained guidance for GUI agent training. Experiments on process reward benchmarks and downstream RL training demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

Hybrid Belief Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Coordinated Spatial Exploration 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Coordinating multiple autonomous agents to explore and serve spatially heterogeneous demand requires jointly learning unknown spatial patterns and planning trajectories that maximize task performance. Pure model-based approaches provide structured uncertainty estimates but lack adaptive policy learning, while deep reinforcement learning often suffers from poor sample efficiency when spatial priors are absent. This paper presents a hybrid belief-reinforcement learning (HBRL) framework to address this gap. In the first phase, agents construct spatial beliefs using a Log-Gaussian Cox Process (LGCP) and execute information-driven trajectories guided by a Pathwise Mutual Information (PathMI) planner with multi-step lookahead. In the second phase, trajectory control is transferred to a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent, warm-started through dual-channel knowledge transfer: i) belief state transfer supplies spatial uncertainty, and ii) replay buffer seeding provides demonstration trajectories generated during LGCP exploration. A variance-normalized overlap penalty enables coordinated coverage through shared belief state, permitting cooperative sensing in high-uncertainty regions while discouraging redundant coverage in well-explored areas. The framework is instantiated in two scenarios: multi-Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) wireless service provisioning, and mobility-on-demand sensing over measured New York City taxi demand. In the wireless scenario it achieves 10.8% higher cumulative reward and takes 38% fewer episodes to reach the reward level of the baselines, with ablation studies confirming that dual-channel transfer outperforms either channel alone; the taxi scenario yields consistent gains with all algorithmic components unchanged.

Reframing Object-Centric Reinforcement Learning: From Absolute to Relative Representations 2026-10-20
📝 13 pages, 7 figures, 10 pages Appendix, First two authors contributed equally
Show abstract

Object-centric reinforcement learning agents typically receive each object's position in a fixed global frame, even though their decisions usually depend on where objects are relative to one another. These absolute views often fail to capture the local context that is most relevant to the agent's behavior. As a result, an agent must relearn the same response at every location where a configuration can occur, and its performance collapses when the scene is merely shifted. In this work, we propose a shift toward Object-Relative Coordinates fixed to Anchor (ORCA), where entities are described in a relative frame of reference. To operationalize this idea, we introduce Object-Relative inductive-Bias Transformers (ORBiT), a transformer-based architecture for processing anchor-relative inputs in reinforcement learning. Our results suggest these relative representations improve performance for both MLP and transformer agents. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ORBiT outperforms the pixel-based PPO baseline, achieving an aggregate HNS IQM greater than 1.0 over 13 Atari games. ORBiT learns the game of Foosball from scratch, dominating all baselines. In our controlled Tag environment, we further show that ORCA extends to rotations, that ORBiT generalizes to unseen numbers of distractors, and that these gains require a decision-relevant anchor.

Performance-Driven Policy Optimization for Speculative Decoding with Adaptive Windowing 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by having a lightweight draft model propose speculative windows of candidate tokens for parallel verification by a larger target model. In practice, speculative efficiency is often bottlenecked by hard-to-draft positions, where an early mismatch truncates the accepted prefix and invalidates the rest of the speculative window. Most learning-based drafters are still optimized with token-level supervised objectives, even though speculative utility is inherently window-level and prefix-sensitive. We propose PPOW (Performance-Driven Policy Optimization with Adaptive Windowing), a reinforcement learning framework that shifts drafter optimization from token-level imitation to window-level optimization. PPOW combines a Cost-Aware Speedup Reward, a Distribution-Based Proximity Reward, and Adaptive Divergence-Aware Windowing, which prioritizes informative windows with high confidence-weighted draft-target divergence. PPOW achieves average acceptance lengths of 6.29-6.52 and speedups of 3.39-4.36$\times$ across multiple model families and benchmarks under a unified decoding protocol. These results show that performance-driven window-level optimization is a practical approach to improving speculative decoding efficiency.

Interruption-Aware Recurrent Reinforcement Learning for UAS Navigation Under Temporary Overrides 2026-10-20
📝 This work has been submitted to IEEE for possible publication
Show abstract

Safe autonomous navigation of UASs requires tactical deconfliction to address conflicts that strategic coordination cannot prevent. This task is challenging under partial observability, when sensor or traffic data are missing, delayed, or degraded. Geometry-based methods can offer separation assurances but face limitations in dense encounters. Recurrent reinforcement learning has therefore been proposed to learn navigation policies that integrate observation-action histories to optimise long-term safety-efficiency trade-offs. However, operational safeguards may require a remote pilot, onboard detect-and-avoid system, or runtime assurance mechanism to temporarily override the learned policy. A research gap remains in understanding how these interruptions affect recurrent policies when the agent regains authority. Benchmark evidence shows that interruptions make task variables less decodable from the recurrent hidden state and increase post-interruption collision risk. The findings suggest that altered recurrent context prolongs post-handback degradation through an action-observation feedback loop, in which changed actions alter the flight path and subsequent recurrent updates. To address this vulnerability, we propose three interruption-aware architectures that condition recurrent processing on interruption context through separate feature streams, dedicated interruption memory, or gated environmental features. Across simulations, all three architectures preserve more decodable task information than a standard recurrent policy. Moreover, the strongest architecture reduces post-interruption collisions by 74% and interruption-induced mission failures by 69% relative to the recurrent baseline, without degrading interruption-free performance. These results show that safe interruption does not guarantee safe handback, and support interruption-aware recurrent design for resilient UAS autonomy.

NestedVLA: Learning to Consolidate and Generate Skills for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-20

Vision-language-action (VLA) models can learn a wide range of robotic skills, but combining separately trained skills remains challenging. The original training data may be unavailable for joint retraining, while directly merging model weights can cause interference. We propose NestedVLA, which consolidates independently fine-tuned VLA policies without access to their original training data. Instead of merging all updates into one checkpoint, a hypernetwork embedded in the VLA generates action-expert weight updates for a selected task. KuaFu: Compressing Long User Behavior into Understanding at Billion Scale 2026-10-20
📝 12 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Learning user preferences from long behavioral sequences is fundamental to large-scale advertising, recommender systems, and conversational agents. In industrial systems, preference modeling is typically task-wise, with different downstream tasks independently processing the same user history to capture different aspects of preference. This repeated processing creates substantial redundancy at billion-user scale, especially as preferences evolve and representations require frequent updates. We present KuaFu, a unified user preference modeling system that replaces repeated user-level sequence encoding with reusable item-level compressed representations. Each behavioral item is encoded once and shared across users and downstream tasks, while a user's history is built chronologically and extended with new item representations as they arrive, letting KuaFu capture evolving preferences without re-encoding the entire history. To reduce inference and storage overhead, KuaFu introduces a two-axis projector: token-axis compression shortens behavior sequences by up to 15x and reduces each item from 150 KB to 10 KB, while width-axis compression reduces the cached representation to 0.5 KB. We further develop a four-stage training pipeline consisting of reconstruction pre-training, preference-understanding post-training, joint compression-generation training, and hallucination-aware reinforcement learning, progressively improving representation fidelity and downstream utility. Recent work has explored continuous representations in place of discrete tokens during Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, often referred to as soft CoT. This is motivated by the idea that a mixture of token embeddings can represent a superposition of multiple reasoning possibilities within a single step. However, it remains challenging to train large language models to realize this potential. Existing work has explored reinforcement learning (RL)-style training for soft CoT. However, these approaches treat soft tokens as actions in a Markov decision process rather than as differentiable intermediate computation steps. Thus, they do not propagate gradients across the soft tokens and miss out on the signal of how the choice of one soft token affects the next. In this paper, we propose a sparse relaxation method for RL (called SpaRLax), which leverages the differentiability of soft-CoT reasoning over time steps and makes training more stable and effective. We adopt an entmax sparsity method that adaptively determines the degree of softness, which truncates long-tail noise at confident steps while retaining multiple plausible candidates when uncertain. Across nine reasoning benchmarks, our method achieves the highest average performance compared with baseline methods. Further analyses validate the effective use of soft-token superposition in our approach, and also demonstrate the contribution of adaptive sparsity in our approach.

Estimating extreme risk in reinforcement learning becomes increasingly difficult as the confidence level increases, since only a small number of observations contribute to empirical estimates of Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). In this paper, we extend an Extreme Value Theory (EVT) approach for CVaR optimization to high-dimensional neural-network policies. Using a peaks-over-threshold representation and method-of-moments estimation, we obtain a differentiable tail-risk objective and show that, conditional on the selected threshold, its finite-sample gradient can be computed directly through automatic differentiation. We first validate the approach in a controlled experiment with a known optimal solution and then investigate its performance in a deep-hedging problem with rare extreme market movements. Our results show similar hedging behavior for EVT- and empirical-CVaR-trained policies under normal conditions, while EVT-trained policies exhibit lower hedging losses under the extreme-scenario evaluation across the training sample sizes considered. These results suggest that EVT can provide a practical alternative for training neural policies when the observations relevant to extreme risk are scarce.
