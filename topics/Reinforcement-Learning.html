DiWALI: Diffusion Policy Learning in World Models via Latent Action Inference from Human and Robot Play 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Learning robot manipulation is constrained by the availability of action-labeled robot data, for both imitation and world models. Humans can demonstrate skills in seconds, but their demonstrations lack the required robot actions. We introduce DiWALI, a framework that learns and improves diffusion policies within a world model trained on human and robot play. DiWALI infers latent actions from consecutive frames using an inverse dynamics model, enabling world model training without action labels, and grounds them in robot commands through a decoder trained separately on robot play. Skill policies are trained over latent actions from action-free human demonstrations and improved with reinforcement learning in imagination. On the CALVIN benchmark, DiWALI policies trained solely from human demonstrations substantially outperform baselines trained with robot demonstrations. Incorporating human play into the world model training also yields stronger policies after fine-tuning than using the same amount of robot play alone. Experiments on a physical robot show that skills learned primarily from human demonstrations are further improved in imagination with a world model trained on human and robot play. Together, these results demonstrate that human experience can support both manipulation skill acquisition and subsequent policy improvement, reducing reliance on action-labeled robot data. Videos, dataset and code are available at https://diwali.cs.uni-freiburg.de.

Data-driven simulation of the brain's response to stimulation: a system identification approach for closed-loop neuromodulation, piloted in adaptive deep brain stimulation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Closed-loop neuromodulation uses a physiomarker to control stimulation. Developing such controllers requires exploring a large space of strategies and parameters, which is often not feasible with the human-in-the-loop. Simulation environments offer an alternative. Adaptive deep brain stimulation (aDBS) for Parkinson's disease is a stereotypical application: while physiomarker identification progresses rapidly, clinically available control is limited to threshold and proportional strategies. We propose to identify dynamical models of the brain with and without deep brain stimulation (DBS) using the sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy) framework. Identified ex post from existing recordings, such models are a middle ground between abstract bottom-up models and the realistic but difficult-to-access human-in-the-loop. Forecasting neural activity from real initial conditions, the models create a simulation environment for "what if" analyses of DBS changes. In a proof-of-concept with EEG data from a single subject, we train and evaluate threshold, proportional, proportional-integral-derivative (PID), and proximal policy optimization (PPO) control, aiming to have the simulated data classified as DBS ON at minimal energy. SINDy models fitted on spatially filtered EEG maintained DBS condition-specific characteristics. All aDBS controllers delivered DBS more efficiently than continuous DBS (cDBS), with up to 20.2% energy reduction. Proportional control yielded a small but significant gain over threshold control. PID and PPO achieved a comparable reward advantage over cDBS, with PPO stimulating more conservatively and PID saving the most energy. The approach thus proved feasible, and within this simulation environment, PID control is a strong a-priori choice, while the benefit of deep reinforcement learning may only emerge with richer physiomarker signals.

Reinforcement Learning for Large-Scale Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding with Shared Spatial Fields 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Large-scale applications of lifelong multi-agent path finding (LMAPF) call for computationally inexpensive movement decisions under changing traffic conditions and across varied layouts. We propose a navigation policy in which a convolutional network computes a shared spatial field over the map once per timestep and a compact goal-conditioned operator derives agents' movement preferences. The complete policy is trained end-to-end using average-reward reinforcement learning with throughput as the reward, without planner demonstrations or a separate online path planner. For structured and unstructured layouts, we train one policy per class on pools of small maps and deploy each without retraining on larger, unseen layouts and agent populations. Across six layouts with 10,000 agents, our policies achieve throughput comparable to or higher than the strongest evaluated baselines. The proposed method has an average execution time below 10 ms per step, more than an order of magnitude faster than several established planners.

HINT-WAM: Hindsight-Informed Training for World Action Models 2026-11-18
Show abstract

World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy's own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets. We introduce \textbf{HINT-WAM}, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher's action distribution from the student's own samples rather than regressing its velocity field. The project web is available in https://tuandattt.github.io/hintwam/.

Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-18
📝 7 pages, 6 figures, 6 tables. Accepted to the CoRL 2026 Workshop on Everything Beneath the Policy. Code, specification, and every experiment's protocol, logs and findings: https://github.com/PlugRL
Show abstract

Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it. On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. A clean evaluation of all 1,060 final policies agreed with the curves: of the 728 boundary-fault runs both curve checks passed, 723 scored no more than three standard deviations below fault-free seeds, though nearly all ended on other weights and, for reward faults, logged other returns. Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

Graph Attention Reinforcement Learning for Multicast Routing and Age-Optimal Scheduling 2026-11-18
📝 25 pages. Revised the NGAT and actor-critic analyses and expanded the experimental evaluation
Show abstract

Multicast routing is essential for real-time group applications, such as video streaming, virtual reality, and metaverse platforms, where the Age of Information (AoI) acts as a crucial metric to assess information timeliness. This paper studies dynamic multicast networks with the objective of minimizing the expected average AoI by jointly optimizing multicast routing and scheduling. The main challenges stem from the intricate coupling between routing and scheduling decisions, the inherent complexity of multicast operations, and the graph representation. We first decompose the original problem into two subtasks amenable to hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) methods. We propose the first RL framework to address the multicast routing problem, also known as the Steiner Tree problem, by incorporating graph embedding and the successive addition of nodes and links. For graph embedding, we propose the Normalized Graph Attention mechanism (NGAT) framework and establish non-expansiveness of the complete embedding matrix in the Frobenius norm under fixed symmetric row-stochastic attention, while evaluating the learned model's stability and generalization empirically. We validate our framework through experiments on four datasets, including the real-world AS-733 dataset. The results demonstrate that our proposed scheme can be up to 9.85 times more computationally efficient than traditional multicast routing algorithms, achieving approximation ratios of 1.1-1.3. These results are not only comparable to state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods but also highlight its superior generalization capabilities, performing effectively on unseen and more complex tasks. Additionally, our age-optimal TGMS algorithm reduces the average weighted AoI by 38.7% and the weighted peak age by 37.1% under various scenarios.

Meta-LSQ: A Closed-Form Sobolev Inner Solver for Few-Shot Meta-Learning 2026-11-18
📝 15 pages (9 main + references + appendix), 5 figures, 10 tables. Accepted at the Northern Lights Deep Learning Conference (NLDL 2027); to appear in PMLR vol. 352
Show abstract

Gradient-based meta-learners such as model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) adapt to a new task with an inner loop of gradient descent that has to be differentiated through, and they meta-learn every network weight. Meta-LSQ instead fits each task with one regularised least-squares solve over a fixed random Fourier basis and meta-learns only three scalars, a bandwidth, a ridge and a derivative weight. Since the derivative of a sinusoid is again a sinusoid, the same solve can also take derivative observations, and the fitted model has exact closed-form derivatives of any order. On 10-shot sinusoid regression Meta-LSQ trains in 14 s and ends with a 5.5 times lower error than a first-order MAML baseline that trains 36 times longer, and a Gaussian-process control suggests that most of this gain comes from sharing one kernel across tasks. In simulated beam tuning at DESY's ARES facility with model derivatives it is slightly more accurate than an ALPaCA-style model and well ahead of gradient descent. Over DESY's full range of target beams, again in simulation and from measurements alone, its best setting has a median error of 7.3 $μ$m, against 14.4 for the published final beams of their reinforcement-learning agent, which has the lower median on their 300 trials of small targets. On real sensor-calibration data a learned prior mean puts it ahead of MAML on two of four targets, but an exact Gaussian process with the same change and an ALPaCA-style model stay more accurate on most targets, and it breaks down on non-smooth targets. On noiseless targets outside the span of the basis, in-sample criteria such as generalised cross-validation choose too small a ridge.

Depth Before Breadth: Reverse-to-Forward Curriculum with Advantage Decomposition for LLM Agent 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a common approach for training large language model (LLM) agents on long-horizon tasks. However, learning from sparse terminal rewards makes credit assignment and exploration difficult. We approach these challenges through a policy-induced capability region, the set of states from which the current policy can reliably complete a task. Near its boundary, an agent may need only a few actions to reach a more solvable state, even when the goal remains distant. This reduces long-horizon learning to two coupled subproblems of reaching that state and completing the task from there. We address them with paired advantages and a progressive reverse-to-forward curriculum. The advantages provide separate signals for reaching more solvable states and completing the task from them, while the curriculum expands the capability region backward along successful reference trajectories and then into off-reference states through on-policy exploration. On ALFWorld and WebShop, our method outperforms competitive agentic RL methods at both model scales, achieving success rates of 90.1%/96.1% and 80.2%/82.6% with Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct, respectively.

DrawScore: Evaluating Generated CAD Models against Engineering Drawings 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Automatic CAD generation is commonly evaluated with volumetric IoU, Chamfer Distance, and surface F-score, which barely penalize local errors that occupy only a small fraction of a part's volume or surface area, such as a misplaced hole or a slot on the wrong face, even though such errors are critical to manufacturing. We formalize these metrics in a unified integral form and show that the weight of a local error decays as the $(k+q)$-th power of its size, where $k$ is the dimension of the integration domain and $q$ is determined by the penalty function. Guided by this analysis, we propose DrawScore, which projects both models into three orthographic views with visible and hidden lines, as in engineering drawings, and applies a saturating penalty along the projected curves, so that the weight of a local error decays only linearly. Controlled experiments confirm the predicted decay orders, and on outputs of existing CAD generation agents, DrawScore exposes errors in outputs that IoU rates as nearly perfect. DrawScore further improves generation as a reinforcement learning reward and, for CAD generation from engineering drawings, serves as a test-time verifier without 3D ground truth or an additional reward model. Our code is available at https://github.com/UESTC-Simon/DrawScore.

Slotted ALOHA networks constitute a playground for research on machine learning (ML)-based channel access schemes. M3OPD-Reasoner: An Open Recipe for Post-Training Multimodal Reasoning with Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) enables a single student to absorb the skills of several domain teachers by learning from its own generated responses. We present Multimodal Model Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation Reasoner (M3OPD-Reasoner), a fully open recipe for post-training vision-language models (VLMs) over three reasoning domains: image STEM, image perception, and video. We curate two datasets, M3OPD-SFT-1.35M for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and M3OPD-RL-193K for reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards. On these data, we compare post-training recipes that start from Qwen3.5-9B: training one model on the mixed data of all domains (MixSFT and MixRL), and training one teacher per domain with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), directly or after an SFT warm-up, and distilling the teachers into one student with MOPD. We evaluate on 21 image STEM, image perception, and video reasoning benchmarks after we decontaminate the training data against them. The comparison establishes MOPD of teachers trained with GSPO directly from Qwen3.5-9B as the best recipe: the student closely matches each teacher on its own domain and is on average above the mixed-training baselines on the same prompts. Since MOPD already merges the domain capabilities that MixRL would otherwise have to learn, its student is a stronger starting point for RL, and we ask whether the same data can carry the model further from there: we add MixRL on top of the MOPD student, on the same prompts and with the same settings, which, to our knowledge, no prior VLM recipe has studied. The same prompts still improve the MOPD student, with no collapse, and give our main model, M3OPD-Reasoner-9B. MOPD and MixRL can therefore work as consecutive stages that get more out of the same data. We release all data, the training code, and the checkpoints of every stage.

How Far Does Online RL Generalize in Mobile GUI Agents? A Controlled Study with AndroidWorld-Generalization 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Online reinforcement learning (RL) has advanced mobile GUI agents on AndroidWorld to over 80\% success, but these systems train and evaluate in the same environment, and their training tasks are not released for auditing. How far RL generalizes beyond its training contexts therefore remains unmeasured. We formalize the mobile environment as a Contextual MDP and instantiate a ladder of three progressively distant context shifts as AndroidWorld-Generalization: Unseen Instance, Unseen Template, and Unseen Application, with separation verified manually. In addition, we provide an open RL training system that makes studies at this scale feasible. Applying the identical RL algorithm to two priors of different origin and competence, UI-TARS-7B-SFT at 23.0\% and Qwen3-VL-8B at 52.6\%, we find that RL improves both in distribution but that gains fall monotonically up the ladder (+17.5/+11.1/+4.2 and +10.3/+7.4/+0), with training performance rising throughout while evaluation performance saturates. Few-shot adaptation in the target application recovers +22.9, motivating test-time adaptation as a complement to further RL training. We release the benchmark, training system, recipes, and model weights.

FOCUS: Active Critic Learning for Long-Horizon Language Model Agents 2026-11-18
📝 32 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Sparse outcome rewards make it difficult for long-horizon language-model agents to assign credit to earlier actions. Learned critics provide value estimates for intermediate states, but these estimates can be inaccurate, while estimating intermediate values with repetitive continuation samples from points within a trajectory incurs substantial costs. To mitigate these issues, we introduce FOCUS, an RL method that uses selective branching to help the critic network learn its value estimator. The rollout controller facilitates active learning for the critic by sampling continuations from states that are expected to most reduce the critic's estimation error. These refined value estimates then score the segment between consecutive states with a TD($λ$) advantage, while the sampled evidence supplies training targets for the critic. FOCUS supports long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning through a modular context-management interface that decouples context management from rollout allocation. Our experiments show that using compaction-induced segment boundaries for value estimation and credit assignment, FOCUS outperforms GRPO and VinePPO with matched per-step generation budgets on legal reasoning, deep search, and code localization benchmarks, improving held-out accuracy at Qwen3.5-9B scale by 7.2 points over the strongest baseline on BrowseComp-Plus (55.4 vs. 48.2) and by 4.1 points on DeonticBench (56.5 vs. 52.4).

Considering Context: When World Models Need Context Encoders 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Methods for generalization in model-based reinforcement learning typically assume that an agent cannot recover the latent context governing the environment dynamics from its own experience, and therefore supplies it externally. We formalize and test this assumption with \emph{predictive sufficiency}, which quantifies what access to the context adds to next-step prediction under the visitation distribution an agent induces, and separates that quantity into a history-recoverable part and a residual requiring the true context, both distinct from a finite model's deficit. We classify context-aware algorithms by the predictive risk their conditioning set can target and demonstrate across environments of increasing identification difficulty that the headroom does not follow the MDP class. The same task under different priors leaves predictive headroom in one setting and nothing distinguishable from zero in another, where the agent's behavior implicitly identifies the context and any benefit of such a mechanism cannot be attributed to missing one-step predictive information. Where headroom persists, the learned state exposes it only partially, and adding the true context still lowers the risk. Our contribution is an analytical framework, instantiated by a practical criterion for matching contextual mechanisms to the predictive information available to them, estimated from the ordinary trained agent.

PIPETTE: Prompt Injection Purple-Teaming for Robust LLM Agents 2026-11-18
📝 18 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents routinely interact with untrusted external data, exposing them to indirect prompt injection (IPI) attacks that hijack agents into executing attacker-chosen tasks. To uncover these vulnerabilities at scale, automated red-teaming methods often leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to generate attacks that achieve high attack success rates (ASR). Model developers use the attacks discovered during internal red-teaming to harden agents through adversarial training before deployment, a collaborative offensive-defensive process known in cybersecurity as purple-teaming. However, we show that achieving high ASR during red-teaming does not guarantee robustness gains from subsequent adversarial training. Specifically, we find that two state-of-the-art RL-based red-teaming methods, RL-Hammer and PISmith, suffer from mode collapse, repeatedly discovering similar attack strategies whose additional successes add no additional robustness improvements to adversarial training. To address this gap, we introduce PIPETTE, a unified prompt injection purple-teaming framework that effectively converts red-teaming discoveries into defender robustness improvements. PIPETTE employs a dictionary learning-based reward that encourages broader coverage of attack strategies during red-teaming. It also integrates an on-policy adversarial training pipeline tailored to IPI defense, enabling agents to resist attacks while completing legitimate user tasks. Extensive evaluations across agentic benchmarks (AgentDojo and ASB) demonstrate that PIPETTE produces defenders that achieve near-zero attack success rates on static attacks and can withstand strong adaptive attacks, while maintaining benign task utility close to that of the undefended baseline.

IQS-BO: In-Context Query Selection for Bayesian Optimisation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Bayesian Optimisation (BO) is a powerful framework for optimising expensive black-box functions, but typically requires refitting a surrogate and maximising an acquisition function at every evaluation. In-context approaches based on Prior-data Fitted Networks (PFNs) amortise part of this cost by pre-training transformers on functions drawn from synthetic priors. PFNS4BO amortises the surrogate but still relies on a numerically maximised acquisition function, while FIBO performs BO fully in-context by sampling optimiser locations from a learned density, which fixes the decision rule and admits no surrogate. Learned acquisition functions score a finite candidate set with a network, but, lacking a label for the query, learn the score by reinforcement learning. We propose IQS-BO, a PFN that learns the query decision by supervised learning on synthetic priors. In a single forward pass, IQS-BO predicts the probability that each candidate is the best in the set, and we show that training minimises its expected KL divergence to the posterior probability of this event. The model can be pre-trained without a surrogate for fully in-context BO, or take the predictions of a fixed probabilistic surrogate as additional input, amortising only the decision step. Our method proposes queries at a fraction of the cost of acquisition-based methods, while matching or outperforming BO with Gaussian processes (GPs) and available in-context methods in sequential and batch optimisation, in low and high dimensions. Finally, we propose a mixture prior for pre-training PFNs which combines samples from GPs with functions exhibiting warped inputs, narrow optima, or plateaus that are poorly modelled by stationary GP kernels. We show that pre-training on this prior can improve performance and help the network generalise to a broader range of optimisation problems.

Do Thinking VLMs Need to Keep Looking at Long Videos? Write It Down, Then Look Away 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) reason about long videos over hundreds to thousands of tokens. Every decoding step re-reads the key-value (KV) cache of the entire video. HopChain has shown on still images that multi-hop data synthesis improves vision-language reasoning, because long chain-of-thought reasoning exposes errors that compound across steps, while most data used for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) rarely demands a chain of visual evidence, so these weaknesses are likely to stay unexposed. Each question chains three to six yes/no questions about moments in one video, and each yields one of two integers depending on its answer. The final answer is the sum of these integers, so an exact match on that sum gives the verifiable reward that RLVR needs.
