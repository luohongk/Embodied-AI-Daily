Agentic AI for Intent Management and Control in Autonomous Communication Networks 2026-11-23
📝 Accepted for publication in IEEE Communications Magazine
Show abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven automation will help future mobile networks translate high-level network intents into concrete network optimization or control actions. However, existing approaches often focus on intent management and orchestration, leaving the adaptation of underlying network optimization and control functions largely unaddressed. This article proposes a triadic agentic AI framework that co-designs intent management and intent fulfillment through three agents: an interpreter, powered by a language model, that translates and refines intents into machine-readable optimization templates; an optimizer that adapts controller policies to intent specifications; and a controller that performs fast decision-making. We discuss the design space of each agent and how the workflow can be embedded across network architectural domains. Finally, we develop a concrete realization combining a language-model interpreter with Bayesian optimization for the optimizer and multi-objective reinforcement learning for the controller, and evaluate it in a radio resource management case study for differentiated connectivity. Results demonstrate how the agentic AI framework can connect high-level intents with runtime network control, showing adaptability of intent specifications and control policies to service requirements and changing network conditions.

EditThinker: Enhancing Image Editors via Iterative Reasoning with a Plug-and-Play VLM 2026-11-23
📝 25 pages; revised title, manuscript, and experimental analysis; includes supplementary material. Project page: https://appletea233.github.io/think-while-edit/
Show abstract

Image editors can produce realistic images while still failing to satisfy the requested changes. Improving their instruction following requires identifying these failures and translating visual feedback into instructions that the editor can execute. We introduce EditThinker, an 8B vision-language model (VLM) that enhances existing image editors through iterative reasoning while keeping their weights fixed. EditThinker jointly critiques semantic consistency and visual quality, reasons about editing failures, and refines the instruction in a structured response. It interacts with the editor through a Critique-Refine-Repeat loop, providing a plug-and-play interface for feedback-driven editing. Learning effective refinements requires more than imitating plausible expert suggestions: the instructions must work with the capabilities of the underlying editor. We therefore combine supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations with reinforcement learning from executed editing outcomes, using format, critic, and edit rewards. To support this training, we construct ThinkEdit-167k, a dataset of 167k step-wise samples with unified supervision for critique, reasoning, and instruction refinement. Across four tested editors, EditThinker improves overall scores on GEdit-Bench-EN, ImgEdit-Bench, RISE-Bench, and KRIS-Bench. Ablations examine the joint model design, data allocation, and training across VLM backbones. In our GEdit round-budget study, refinement achieves higher scores with fewer editor calls than best-of-N selection. We will release our data construction framework, datasets, and models.

CO-GRAFT: Attributing Reinforcement Learning Gains in Search Agents via Role-Level Policy Grafting 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) improves large language model search agents by up to 32 exact-match (EM) points over their pre-RL initializations, yet overall accuracy gains do not identify which underlying functional capabilities improve or interact during training. We introduce CO-GRAFT, a reward-agnostic interventional attribution framework in which an RL-trained agent and its pre-RL initialization jointly generate a single trajectory under closed-loop execution with live retrieval. Each policy controls a mutually exclusive subset of four functional roles: intermediate reasoning, search-or-answer decision, search query generation, and final answer extraction. Evaluating all 2^4=16 role assignments without modifying weights or retraining defines a cooperative game whose Shapley values decompose the total RL gain across roles, while M"obius interaction terms distinguish standalone role improvements from multi-role coupling. Applying CO-GRAFT to Search-R1 agents across Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2 models (spanning two sizes, base and instruction-tuned initializations, GRPO and PPO optimizers, and NQ and HotpotQA datasets), we show that query generation never exceeds 15% of the total gain; instead, gains are governed by pre-RL answer validity, search retention, model scale, and fixed task-level search budgets, shifting credit from reasoning toward answer extraction in larger or well-formatted answer response checkpoints while smaller base models rely on reasoning--decision coupling.

Precision-Aware Learning Rate Transfer Across Block Count in LLM Post-Training 2026-11-23
Show abstract

The post-training of pretrained large language models, including supervised finetuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR), is very sensitive to the learning rate, and tuning it through grid search is costly in both computation and memory. In this work, we propose \textbf{TipSweep}, a method to transfer the learning rate across the number of trainable transformer blocks. To do so efficiently, we search for the best learning rate by training only the last $k_0$ blocks (in our experiments, typically $k_0=1$), and provide scaling rules that predict the optimal learning rate when training any set of $k$ blocks, including when $k=L$ (the full transformer). Across experiments with models ranging from 1 to 7 billion parameters, we observe that the optimal learning rate follows precision-dependent scaling rules: the optimal rate scales approximately as $k^{-1}$ with float32 (FP32) master weights, but as $k^{-1/2}$ when updates are applied directly to bfloat16 (BF16) weights without a master copy. We explain both rules using an analytical model that accounts for update rounding, under assumptions supported by profiling experiments. Finally, we develop two prototypes of the learning rate search for a 6.7-billion-parameter model. The first fits the search on a single consumer GPU with 16 GiB of memory. The second speeds up the search on a high-end GPU by evaluating all candidate learning rates in a single pass over the data, so that a five-candidate search takes about half the GPU hours of one full-depth ($k=L$) run.

Decoupling Solving from Abstention: Single-Rollout Conformal Risk Control for Hard-Budget LLM Reasoning 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Hard token budgets often interrupt LLM reasoning before an answer is produced. Forcing reasoning to terminate recovers correct solutions but also increases confident errors, creating a joint challenge of solving within budget and deciding when to abstain. Existing remedies are costly or lack guarantees: supervised fine-tuning offers no error control, penalizing wrong answers during reinforcement learning (RL) can suppress exploration, and multi-sample conformal methods multiply inference cost. We propose Tail-CRC, a single-rollout framework that separates candidate generation from submission control. We first adapt the solver to budget forcing with pure binary correctness rewards; after freezing it, we apply conformal risk control to upper-tail token surprise in the answer span. Each rollout yields both an answer and an uncertainty score from token log probabilities, enabling finite-sample control of expected wrong submissions under exchangeability and recalibration across risk targets or domains without retraining. We show that wrong-answer penalties create failure-only comparisons in group-relative RL, derive an exploration-starvation boundary, and identify length-induced ranking reversals and single-token spike sensitivity in answer scores. Across Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3-8B, 50 RL updates on 400 problems improve correct-submission rates by 15.0--29.6 points on DAPO and 4.3--12.8 points on out-of-domain Omni-MATH over inference-only budget forcing, while reducing generation cost by 3.5--12.6\%. On 8B Omni-MATH, target-domain calibration cuts wrong submissions from 30.3\% to 5.8\% at 4K and from 41.8\% to 6.5\% at 8K. At 4K, generation is 2.05$\times$ faster for 4B and 2.23$\times$ faster for 8B than unforced 8K decoding in local amortized batch time. Tail-CRC thus enables reliable, efficient budgeted reasoning from a single rollout.

RoboUniverse-10K: Toward the Scaling Era of Agentic Robotics via Real-to-Sim Environments 2026-11-23
📝 Project Page: https://lab.einsia.ai/robo-universe/
Show abstract

Agentic Robotics, in which foundation models act as coding agents to control robots through executable programs, has emerged as a compelling paradigm for embodied intelligence. Yet unlike software engineering, where massive executable environments power post-training, robotics remains constrained by small, manual benchmarks and in-distribution evaluation. To bridge this gap, we introduce RoboUniverse-10K, 10,807 interactive, resettable, and verifiable physics environments built from real-world videos via an automated Real2Sim and quality assurance pipeline. Each runs in an isolated, Harbor-compatible harness that hides privileged states and grading logic to mirror real-world sensing. From the same pipeline, we hold out 500 unseen tasks to establish RoboUniverse-Verified, an expert-verified zero-shot benchmark. Our model achieves substantial zero-shot gains on RoboUniverse-Verified, lifting the success rate from 0.8\% to 21.2\%, while achieving zero-shot task completions across LIBERO-MAX, RoboCasa, RoboTwin 2.0, and a real Franka arm. We open-source the RL-ready environments and model to lay the foundation for large-scale reinforcement learning in Agentic Robotics.

Generalizing Agentic Robotics via Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Building general-purpose robots is a central goal in robotics. Agentic robotics pursues this goal with planners that control robots through tool calls. However, existing methods either rely on proprietary models accessed through APIs, which incur high costs and latency, or post-train a planner with reinforcement learning (RL) in a single domain, requiring a separate specialist for each domain with incompatible tools. In large language model (LLM) post-training, multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) has been used to consolidate domain specialists into a single generalist. Yet whether MOPD can build a generalist planner for agentic robotics, how well it performs, and how it compares with other consolidation methods remain unexplored. In this paper, we systematically study these questions: we introduce Robo-MOPD, which trains a single student on closed-loop rollouts in simulation under token-level supervision from matching frozen specialists, and compare it with weight merging, off-policy specialist supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and multi-domain online RL. Experiments on LIBERO-PRO and RoboCasa365 show that a single 9B Robo-MOPD planner reaches 72.9% and 44.8% success (58.9% average), surpassing all consolidated baselines in average success and both 9B specialists on their respective benchmarks. Robo-MOPD excels on long-horizon tasks, outperforming multi-domain online RL by 13.0 percentage points on LIBERO-10.

SLICR: Strategy Logic-Guided Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Specification-guided reinforcement learning (RL) translates formal specifications of desired behavior of agents into shaped rewards. This paper introduces SLICR, a significant paradigm shift that advances the state of the art to specification-guided RL in adversarial settings under partial observability. We employ Strategy Logic (SL), which allows explicit quantification over agents' strategies and uses the linear temporal logic (LTL) to specify the agents' objectives and constraints. This enables reasoning not only about how agents should act to satisfy a specification, but also about how they should respond to the strategies and behaviors of other agents. We evaluate SLICR on three popular benchmarks: Predator-Prey, Google Research Football, and our home-grown extension of SMAC-HARD, demonstrating its effectiveness, scalability, and ability to adapt to diverse adversarial strategies.

Reinforcing Diffusion Models by Instantaneous Trajectory Distribution Matching 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Forward diffusion reinforcement learning (RL)---enabling deterministic ODE sampling without trajectory backpropagation---has grown increasingly popular for efficiently aligning diffusion models. However, we reveal that existing methods essentially rely on signed reward-weighted regression (RWR), regressing the \emph{trainable policy} velocity onto rollouts from a \emph{behavior distribution}. Unbiased baseline subtraction implicitly assumes that the policy velocity equals the behavior marginal velocity---an idealized assumption violated under classifier-free guidance (CFG) rollouts, offline/off-policy updates, and imperfect online self-consistency. This \emph{behavior--policy mismatch} breaks baseline invariance and injects \emph{baseline-induced gradient drift}, destabilizing training. To resolve this issue, we propose \textbf{I}nstantaneous \textbf{T}rajectory \textbf{D}istribution \textbf{M}atching (\textbf{iTDM}), taking the infinitesimal limit of Trajectory Distribution Matching toward a time-local reward-guided target via the posterior-score identity to yield a sample-level gradient anchored at the behavior velocity without learning an additional reward-tilted target score. Tracking this velocity with an online auxiliary denoiser guarantees exact baseline invariance at the auxiliary optimum, effectively suppressing baseline drift across both online and offline RL. Extensive experiments across compositional generation, text rendering, and preference alignment show that iTDM achieves state-of-the-art in-domain and out-of-domain performance. Notably, on the challenging GenEval2 benchmark, iTDM converges nearly $3\times$ faster than competing methods while scaling smoothly to $0.87$ without collapse. Code is available at https://github.com/Luo-Yihong/iTDM

Evaluable Language-Model Treatment Policies for Sepsis Resuscitation via Offline Value-Guided Selection 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Offline treatment learning must balance imitation, which stays close to logged evidence but ignores outcomes, against value maximisation, which uses outcomes but drifts toward treatments the records cannot verify. Numerical reinforcement learning strikes this balance during training. For large language models (LLMs), directly using sparse retrospective outcomes for fine-tuning raises challenges of delayed credit assignment and limited evidence for alternative treatments. We present R-LLMC, which strikes the balance at inference for sepsis resuscitation. A behaviour-supervised LLM, decoded into an exact distribution over 25 vasopressor-fluid actions, proposes clinician-anchored candidates; an offline critic selects among them by estimated long-term value. The policy has a closed form for off-policy evaluation, and its rationale is written after selection, so explanation cannot steer treatment. Show abstract

Precision industrial contact manipulation requires reliable robot policies under pose perturbations and contact-force constraints. Vision-language-action models offer broad generalization but often introduce high inference latency and GPU-memory cost, while vision-action chunking policies are more suitable for real-time industrial control. However, these policies are usually trained by behavior cloning and suffer from distribution shift in contact-rich tasks. This paper proposes PAC-ACT, a reinforcement-learning post-training framework for pretrained Action Chunking Transformer policies. GlanceNav: Efficient Vision-and-Language Navigation with Streaming Memory and Counterfactual Rewind 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have become strong policies for vision-and-language navigation (VLN). However, most of them encode a sample of past frames again at every decision and are trained on isolated decisions, so computation is redundant and the usable history is short. They also learn from their own mistakes through outcome rewards that give one scalar to a whole episode. We ask whether a navigator can encode each frame only once, at a constant cost per step, and still remember what matters and learn efficiently from its mistakes. We present GlanceNav, a streaming navigator built on a hybrid MLLM. Its Gated DeltaNet layers carry a recurrent state through the whole episode, while its attention layers keep a short window of recent observations and a small bank of keyframes, selected by how much each frame changes the recurrent state. This constant-size memory keeps the cost per step fixed and allows training on whole episodes with the same memory as at inference. Because the entire memory can also be copied in constant time, we further propose Counterfactual Rewind (CoRe), a reinforcement learning stage that rewinds a drifting rollout to its closest approach to the goal, lets an expert correct a few actions, and credits decisions by the resulting change in outcome. Trained only on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE training splits, with 1,000 instructions for reinforcement learning, a 4B GlanceNav reaches success rates of 71.9% and 73.6% on R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen. On R2R-CE, it outperforms the strongest previous single-RGB method trained without external data by 9.1 points, while running at 104 ms per step.

AnchorCredit: Output-Anchored Credit Assignment for Structured Generation with Multiple Verifiable Rewards 2026-11-23
Show abstract

In Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and related methods typically compute group-relative advantages from response rewards and broadcast the same advantage to every generated token in a response to guide policy updates. However, we identify a systematic problem of cross-component credit misassignment in tasks with multiple verifiable results, such as joint multi-attribute prediction: low rewards for some components can penalize a well-performing component and its associated reasoning. To address this problem, we propose AnchorCredit. The method uses the policy model's attention from output anchors to preceding reasoning sentences to estimate sentence-component associations and assign component-specific advantages to the relevant reasoning, without additional relevance annotations or a separate relevance discriminator. To support the study of credit assignment with multiple verifiable rewards, we construct ProductAttr, a benchmark of 7,200 real product image-text samples. Experiments show that AnchorCredit outperforms existing baselines on this benchmark. Experiments on the public Nutrition5k dataset further support the method's effectiveness in this setting.

Beyond Domain Randomization: Real-World Policy Adaptation in Learned Dynamics for Legged Robots 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning policies are often trained with static domain randomization for real-world deployment. However, real-world environments often exhibit unmodeled dynamic variations, such as payload changes or friction shifts, that lie outside the randomization distribution, leading to performance degradation upon deployment. We propose a framework that moves beyond static domain randomization by adapting a learned dynamics model to the deployed target conditions, with a concurrent teacher-student paradigm applied to both the policy and dynamics model. Object Count Generalization Is an Interface Problem: Warping a Frozen Skill Instead of Retraining It 2026-11-23
📝 37 pages, 15 figures, 15 tables. Submitted to International Conference on Learning Representations 2027. Code, videos and full results are available at https://hhi-robot-learning.github.io/wave-website/
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning policies for robot manipulation degrade when the number of objects N in the scene grows beyond the training range. This is a covariate shift that also changes the dimension of the state. We propose WAVE (Warping Actions and Views of an Expert), which extends a pretrained single-object skill to many objects without updating its weights. WAVE has three parts. First, a selection policy picks the one object the robot works on next. Second, a warping policy reads a local view of the scene around that object, whose size does not depend on N. It emits a multiplier and an offset for every quantity the skill reads and every action it outputs. Third, the skill acts under this affine warp. It is shown a scene that holds a single object, while the warp steers it around the objects it is not shown and edits the actions that are sent to the robot. Both the selection and warping policies are trained with PPO on the multi-object reward and use the skill as a black box, so WAVE is task-general and skill-agnostic. It requires only that the skill was trained on the same embodiment and that every object has a goal pose. We also bound the number of attempts needed to place all N objects, assuming positive expected net progress per attempt, and measure this progress for each object count. We evaluate WAVE on cube arrangement tasks with a gantry robot and with a Franka Panda arm. Trained with at most nine cubes, it generalizes zero-shot to several times its training count, on the gantry to scenes of up to thirty cubes. Published methods that learn multi-object manipulation show count generalization up to six objects. Fine-tuning the skill, learning from the same local view without it, and an entity transformer, even when distilled from the pretrained skill, all collapse within their training counts under the same training budget.

SWE-Ladder: Learning to Localize, Reproduce, and Patch via Progressive Privileged Self-Distillation 2026-11-23
📝 18 pages, 11 figures. Code:https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/SWE-Ladder
Show abstract

Resolving a repository-level issue typically requires a software-engineering (SWE) agent to localize the faulty code, reproduce the failure, and patch the code. Reinforcement learning trains such agents from a single test result on the final patch, while on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) adds token-level supervision from a teacher with privileged evidence, exposing the full reference patch at every step. Neither provides stage-specific guidance for localization or reproduction. We propose SWE-Ladder, which turns the verified repair of each training instance into stage-specific evidence: the changed files and lines for localization, the fail-to-pass tests for reproduction, and the diff for patching. A rule-based router assigns each step of an end-to-end rollout to a stage based on its tool call, a frozen teacher sees only the matching evidence, and the stages are distilled in repair order while GRPO optimizes the full trajectory. With Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on SWE-bench Verified under the R2E-Gym scaffold, SWE-Ladder improves pass@1 from 9.6% to 16.4%, compared with 10.4% for GRPO and 12.4% for OPSD with the full patch. Under the OpenHands scaffold, it improves pass@1 from 13.0% to 17.6%. Reversing the stage order under R2E-Gym reduces pass@1 to 11.4%.

ABI: Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations for Zero-Shot Test-Time Guidance 2026-11-23
📝 Project page: https://securitycfs.github.io/ABI-demo/
Show abstract

Behavior-cloning policies show strong ability in sequential control by generating coherent multi-step action chunks, yet their purely imitative objective limits zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks. While offline reinforcement learning offers principled policy improvement, existing methods either require task-specific reward supervision or remain tied to a specific actor during critic training or policy deployment, inflating training cost and restricting cross-policy generalization. We observe that evaluating actions under the data-collecting behavior largely preserves their relative value ordering across target policies, offering a way around actor coupling. Combining this property with reward-free successor prediction, we propose Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations (ABI) to learn multi-horizon action-chunk representations from unlabeled trajectories without reward supervision or actor training. At test time, pairing the frozen representation with lightweight task readouts forms a chunk critic that guides target policies without parameter updates and transfers directly across distinct unseen policies. Our analysis establishes when the value ordering is preserved across policies and when selecting among proposed chunks improves closed-loop return. Across 65 locomotion, navigation, and manipulation tasks, ABI's chunk critic guides weak imitation policies to match or surpass zero-shot RL baselines with high sample efficiency, outperforms existing zero-shot critics on identical proposals, and improves externally trained RL actors without retraining. From raw pixels, ABI delivers substantial guidance gains and surpasses prior zero-shot RL baselines on visual manipulation, highlighting its potential for visuomotor control.

GeoMerge-OPD: Balanced Skill Integration in Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-23
📝 29 pages, 4 figures, 17 tables. The first four authors contributed equally. Wenshuo Chen is the project leader. Yutao Yue and Xiaochun Cao are co-corresponding authors
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models excel at individual robotic skills, yet integrating heterogeneous skills into a single policy remains a key challenge for general-purpose robotic control, especially in whole-body tasks with differing manipulation and locomotion requirements. Common multi-skill integration strategies, such as joint fine-tuning and parameter merging, can induce unintended actions during closed-loop execution, severely degrading individual skills. Task-conditioned analysis of expert updates reveals response energy concentrated in a few dominant directions and opposing expert responses along high-energy directions within a shared, task-relevant response subspace. This structure co-occurs with unintended action shifts after merging. We propose GeoMerge-OPD, combining response-space regularization, parameter merging, and on-policy distillation. During independent expert fine-tuning, G-isometry constrains update-response gains within task-activation subspaces; the expert parameters are then statically merged into a student with preliminary multitask capabilities. Fine-tuned task experts serve as teachers, providing velocity-field supervision at the environment observations and intermediate action-flow states visited by the student to calibrate residual behavioral deviations. Experiments with two VLA backbones show strong, balanced humanoid skill integration on SIMPLE and consistent performance on LIBERO and LIBERO-PRO, outperforming joint fine-tuning and multitask reinforcement-learning baselines while maintaining robustness under perturbations.

Show abstract
