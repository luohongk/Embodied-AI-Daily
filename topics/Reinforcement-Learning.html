World Model for Energy Efficiency Maximization in Dynamic Metasurface Antennas 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Dynamic metasurface antennas (DMAs) have emerged as a promising technology for realizing large-scale antenna arrays in next-generation wireless networks owing to their ability to enable dense and energy-efficient antenna architectures. However, jointly optimizing digital beamforming and DMA element weights under time-varying channel conditions remains challenging due to their coupled effects on the received signal and interference powers. We formulate this problem to maximize time-averaged energy efficiency (EE) under time-varying channel conditions, subject to transmit power constraints, Lorentzian phase limitations, and minimum rate requirements. To enable long-term planning and improve sample efficiency compared with model-free reinforcement learning, we develop a world model framework that learns latent system dynamics from interactions with the environment and jointly optimizes digital beamforming and DMA element weights through imagined trajectories. Furthermore, we incorporate feasibility-preserving action mappings and safety cost signals to ensure constraint satisfaction during policy learning. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework improves the time-averaged EE by approximately 20% compared with the deep Q-network baseline, while achieving higher sample efficiency and more reliable constraint satisfaction.

FOCUS: Active Critic Learning for Long-Horizon Language Model Agents 2026-10-26
📝 32 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Sparse outcome rewards make it difficult for long-horizon language-model agents to assign credit to earlier actions. Learned critics provide value estimates for intermediate states, but these estimates can be inaccurate, while estimating intermediate values with repetitive continuation samples from points within a trajectory incurs substantial costs. To mitigate these issues, we introduce FOCUS, an RL method that uses selective branching to help the critic network learn its value estimator. The rollout controller facilitates active learning for the critic by sampling continuations from states that are expected to most reduce the critic's estimation error. These refined value estimates then score the segment between consecutive states with a TD($λ$) advantage, while the sampled evidence supplies training targets for the critic. FOCUS supports long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning through a modular context-management interface that decouples context management from rollout allocation. Our experiments show that using compaction-induced segment boundaries for value estimation and credit assignment, FOCUS outperforms GRPO and VinePPO with matched per-step generation budgets on legal reasoning, deep search, and code localization benchmarks, improving held-out accuracy at Qwen3.5-9B scale by 7.2 points over the strongest baseline on BrowseComp-Plus (55.4 vs. 48.2) and by 4.1 points on DeonticBench (56.5 vs. 52.4).

Where and What to Reward? Understanding Generalization Between Verifiable and Open-Ended Domains in Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is widely used to improve the reasoning capability of language models, since model-generated answers can be easily verified by a rule-based verifier. In contrast, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) is mainly used to optimize open-ended conversation with a learned reward model, because such responses are not easy to verify. RLVR and RLHF are usually trained separately with their own domain data and training protocols. How gains transfer between these domains is unclear, partly because comparisons change both where a model is trained (the prompts) and what is rewarded (by a verifier or a reward model). We separate the two in a controlled study of RL on multiple base models that fixes the optimizer and the number of training prompts, and varies only the source domain of the prompts (math, multi-domain, chat, or mixtures) and the reward. We find that RLHF on open-ended prompts substantially improves conversational ability and also brings large gains in reasoning and coding, whereas RLVR improves reasoning but its gains in open-ended conversation plateau far below those of RLHF, suggesting that RL on open-ended prompts with a reward model can serve as an effective approach for generalizing across domains.

SEMA-Auto: Sighted and Efficient Multi-Turn Attack with Autonomy 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Existing jailbreak attackers fall into two classes: static planners fix their single- or multi-turn adversarial plans in advance, remaining simple but losing sight of the victim's replies, wasting turns and limiting transferability; interactive generators read each victim reply to generate the next-turn attack but require in-loop evaluators for success signals or hints, incurring substantial costs and inflated performance. Bridging this gap, we propose SEMA-Auto, a sighted and efficient multi-turn attacker with autonomy, interacting with the victim model and terminating the session by itself upon success. To stabilize its behaviors and boost its capabilities, SEMA-Auto is trained in two stages. Synthetic bootstrap tuning installs the notion of attack targets and conforming decisions by fine-tuning the base model with bootstrapped trajectories. Decision-supervised reinforcement learning then optimizes two objectives jointly yet separately, reinforcing the attack with higher jailbreak reward while supervising the decision at each turn against an absolute target. Our method achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) attack success rates (ASR) across multiple datasets, victims, and judges, outperforming all static and interactive baselines, within almost the fewest turns, demonstrating both effectiveness and efficiency. For example, SEMA-Auto performs a $76.0\%$ ASR@1 within an average of $3.0$ turns against DeepSeek-V4-Flash on AdvBench, while the best static baseline is $54.8\%$ with fixed $7$ turns and the best interactive baseline is $43.8\%$ in $3.7$ turns. Against safety-hardened frontier models, GPT-oss-120B, GPT-5.4, Claude-Haiku-4.5, and GPT-6-Astra, our counter-trained attacker achieves an average $59.6\%$ ASR@1 on HarmBench, $13.8\%$ over prior SOTA. Our code is available at: https://github.com/microsoft/SEMA

Preference-Guided Cooperation in Multi-Objective Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-26
📝 14 pages, 4 figures, 1 table (plus 3 figures, 3 tables, and 2 algorithms in the appendix)
Show abstract

Multi-objective multi-agent reinforcement learning (MOMARL) addresses agent coordination and objective trade-offs simultaneously while optimising overall system performance. Existing work tends to treat multi-agent cooperation and multi-objective optimisation as largely independent components of a learning problem, which amounts to an exorbitant complexity except in trivial cases. We study here whether the structure provided by multiple objectives can itself be exploited to facilitate cooperation, rather than being treated as an additional optimisation challenge. We formulate MOMARL at both the team and individual levels and introduce objective-wise counterfactual credit assignment to evaluate each agent's contribution across the team objectives. We propose Meta-Gradient Informed Multi-Objective Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MIRL), with static (MIRL-S) and dynamic (MIRL-D) variants. By incorporating meta-gradient information, MIRL enables agents to learn how to prioritise objectives individually while collectively optimising the system-level objective, allowing the multi-objective structure to directly guide cooperation. Experiments on a multi-objective multiple particle environment show that our methods improve Pareto-front quality over baselines, with the dynamic variant achieving the strongest performance.

Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning Using Routed Ensembles and Trajectory Attribution 2026-10-26
📝 30 pages. To appear at NeurIPS 2026 (extended version)
Show abstract

Multi-objective reinforcement learning is a fundamental problem for modern AI, with applications spanning post-training, reasoning models, and robotic control. A central challenge is to learn policies that balance competing objectives, such as helpfulness versus honesty in safety and alignment, or speed versus energy in robotics. The optimal policy can vary sharply with objective-weighting combinations, and the extent of this variation depends crucially on the degree of conflict among objectives, making per-preference policy learning sample-inefficient. In this paper, we introduce objective-conflict coefficients and derive near-optimal regret bounds for online settings using this measure, thereby quantifying the impact of misalignment on learning efficiency. Then, we design routed ensemble policy learning, an algorithm that maintains a small set of basis policies and dynamically routes trajectories to the policy with the highest potential reward. Finally, we consider trajectory selection as another approach to mitigate objective conflict, and design a kernel surrogate trajectory attribution algorithm that estimates how feedback signals from a set of trajectories influence policy learning on other trajectories. The kernel can capture nonlinear interactions such as synergy and antagonism between trajectories. We evaluate our approach across diverse environments and benchmarks: routed ensembles improve average performance by 4.3% over baselines for online environments from Meta-World and MO-Gymnasium, and kernel surrogate attribution improves correlation with leave-one-out analysis over existing baselines by 50%. When applied to trajectory selection, kernel-based attribution yields a 27% improvement in reward over baseline attribution methods.

Audit Where Hacks Pay: Budgeted Strong-Verifier Audits Against Reward Hacking in GRPO 2026-10-26
📝 27 pages, 5 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards trains on cheap checks, such as unit tests, that a policy can learn to game, while a stronger check is too expensive to run on every rollout. We study how to spend a limited strong-verifier budget in GRPO, separating which weakly passing rollouts to audit from what reward a caught hack receives. At a fixed policy state with centered advantages, the expected update depends on a design only through each rollout's marginal audit probability and correction; reflecting a caught hack's reward about the failure reward negates the advantages of an all-hack group; and auditing whole groups with the fewest weak passes first (low-pass budgeted audits, LB) minimizes the advantage mass left on hacks among pass-count designs with a fixed correction when a label's value falls with the pass count. We tested this analysis in actual LLM training: from each run's starting state, the sign of this mass on a frozen panel predicted whether hacking would be suppressed in 33 of 39 runs, including 13 of 16 preregistered ones. In LeetCode (Wong et al., 2026) and Countdown-Code (Khalifa et al., 2026), LB drives hacking to near zero, while random audits with the same penalty and budget do not and use more labels. We also find that the randomness of the update affects training: in Countdown-Code, two unbiased corrections, per-item reward-level IPW and a group-level advantage correction, bring hacking down too but end 6.1-11.8 and 8.0-11.5 points lower in held-out accuracy than LB at the same budget. A full audit, which the group-level correction reproduces in expectation, stays within about two points of LB in three seeds, while LB uses 28% of its labels, and allocating the same correction by pass count recovers most of the loss (one seed). Training outcomes thus depend not only on the expected update but also on the randomness of its estimate.

MiRL: Error-Targeted Reinforcement Learning for Correcting Terminal Agent Misbehaviors 2026-10-26
📝 20 pages, 14 figures, 5 tables. Project page: https://github.com/runchu-tian/MiRL
Show abstract

Error-targeted learning gives a legible way to improve a model: identify specific weaknesses of the model, train the model to fix these weaknesses, and check whether the weaknesses are fixed. We study how to apply error-targeted learning to terminal agents, which solve long-horizon tasks specified in natural language by interacting with a command shell. Existing error-targeted methods are designed for single-turn tasks, and existing analyses of agent failures are used to evaluate and debug agents rather than to train them. We propose Misbehavior-driven RL (MiRL), which first profiles the errors of a policy to find generalizable misbehaviors: kinds of wrong steps that recur across many environments. MiRL then trains the policy with RL, starting rollouts at the steps where these misbehaviors occur and rewarding a rollout only if it passes the tests and avoids the misbehavior. On Qwen3.5-4B, eight misbehaviors account for 52.1% of wrong steps. After training with MiRL, the policy repeats the measured misbehaviors 43% to 57% less often on average on unseen prefixes. MiRL outperforms TMax-4B on Terminal-Bench Lite (51.8% vs. 47.4%) and matches it on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (21.3% vs. 20.6%), while using 35% of its rollouts. These results show that error-targeted learning can be applied to realistic, long-horizon terminal tasks.

EvoRouter: Cost-Efficient Multi-Agent Routing via Insight-Guided On-Policy Distillation 2026-10-26
📝 13 pages, 12 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Serving web queries at scale with multi-agent systems requires jointly configuring large language models, collaboration modes, agent counts, interaction rounds, and skills. However, the resulting configuration space grows combinatorially, while sparse execution rewards provide limited fine-grained supervision for interdependent planning decisions. We propose EvoRouter, a self-evolving multi-agent routing framework driven by insight-guided on-policy distillation. A dedicated expert periodically synthesizes performance-cost feedback from configuration trajectories across tasks into reusable conditional routing insights about capability requirements, returns from resource scaling, and interactions among configuration choices. A privileged teacher combines these evolving insights with current execution feedback to provide dense token-level supervision for the router's own planning trajectories through on-policy distillation. Cost-aware reinforcement learning jointly optimizes task performance and computational cost. Together, these training signals internalize execution insights into the routing policy. The updated policy generates new trajectories that support subsequent insight extraction, forming an execution-driven self-improvement loop. We evaluate EvoRouter on 19 in-domain datasets spanning five domains and two out-of-domain benchmarks. EvoRouter achieves an average in-domain accuracy of 68.69%, outperforming the strongest routing baseline by 7.23 percentage points while reducing inference compute cost by 50.95%. Provably Efficient Q-learning for Robust Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Standard reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms based on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are vulnerable to sim-to-real gaps. To address this issue, distributionally robust RL algorithms based on robust MDPs (RMDPs) have been proposed, for ensuring that agents can perform reliably to unpredictable conditions in real-world environments. Despite of the rich literature, \revision{an efficient model-free method is still lacking} for robust RL, particularly for Markovian data, which is more relevant in practical applications. In this work, we take a step towards settling this problem by proposing a sample efficient (asynchronous Q-learning) algorithm for robust RL. In particular, consider a $γ$-discounted infinite-horizon RMDP with state space $\mathcal{S}$ and action space $\mathcal{A}$, where the uncertainty set is specified by the total variation (TV) distance with uncertainty level $σ$. Assuming access to a single trajectory of Markovian samples induced by a behavior policy and the nominal transition kernel, the sample complexity of the proposed Q-learning algorithm --- to achieve an entrywise $\varepsilon$-approximation of the optimal Q-function --- is bounded by $$ \frac{1}{μ_{\mathsf{min}}\varepsilon^2(1-γ)^3\max\{1-γ,σ\}} + \frac{t_{\mathsf{mix}}}{μ_{\mathsf{min}}(1-γ)} $$ up to logarithmic factors. Here, $t_{\mathsf{mix}}$ and $μ_{\mathsf{min}}$ denote the mixing time and the minimum state-action occupancy probability of the sample trajectory, respectively. This result is nearly minimax optimal, with an additional factor of $\frac{1}{1-γ}$. Moreover, it achieves a significant improvement over existing bounds.

Evolve on Graph: Self-Evolving Knowledge Graph Reasoning without Question Annotations 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide reliable external knowledge for multi-hop factual reasoning with large language models (LLMs), yet most KGs in real-world domains such as finance and industry lack question--answer (QA) annotations. Without such annotations, existing methods struggle to improve LLM graph reasoning: training-free agents cannot improve their exploration through interaction; reinforcement learning (RL) agents depend on QA data annotated by humans or synthesized by closed-source models; and training data is typically built offline, so its topology mix is fixed and cannot follow the agent's ability. We propose Evolve on Graph (EoG), a framework that lets an LLM self-evolve its graph reasoning without any QA annotations. Through Graph Self-Play, a single LLM alternates between a Proposer that poses questions and a Solver that answers them. To make questions verifiable, Symbolically Verifiable Topology Synthesis (SVTS) samples reasoning topologies on the KG and executes the corresponding first-order logic queries, yielding topology skeletons with correct and complete answer sets; guided by a skeleton, the Proposer explores the graph to pose a multi-hop question. To let task topologies follow the Solver, a Boundary-Aware Graph Curriculum rewards the Proposer by the Solver's score variance, keeping questions at the Solver's capability boundary so that complex topologies enter training as the Solver improves. On six benchmarks over Freebase, Wikidata, and an industrial KG, EoG consistently outperforms prompting-, retrieval-, and agent-based methods, showing that LLM graph reasoning can improve through self-play without QA annotations. Our code is available at https://github.com/Szy5/EoG

HoloBoxing: A Simple Recipe for Humanoid Boxing with 7 Minutes of Single-GPU Training 2026-10-26
Show abstract

HoloBoxing presents a simple recipe for learning humanoid boxing by building on a pretrained whole-body controller. The recipe separates reusable motor skills from task decisions: a compact planner requests future body-point targets, while the frozen controller handles joint-level execution. A learned decoder maps 32-dimensional actions to structured target windows, and measured-state imitation initializes the planner before reinforcement learning. Task learning then refines interaction behavior while reusing the decoder and controller, avoiding the need to relearn whole-body control. This combination keeps task optimization focused on a compact upper policy and supplies an initial behavior before reward-driven exploration. Starting from that available controller, the recipe accounts for about seven minutes of additional training, including reused representation fitting and a single-GPU task run. This excludes controller pretraining, data preparation, evaluation, and search. Simulated boxing experiments and component controls evaluate the resulting behavior and the roles of imitation initialization and the target representation.

Typed decision models (TDMs), such as Jev, answer a question about an input with a probability, an option, or an ordinal level. Web services execute these decisions automatically above a confidence threshold. An attacker who writes part of the input can change a decision that the service then executes. Current adversarial benchmarks cannot measure this risk, because they judge generated text. A TDM writes no text, and its wrong decisions have a valid format. We present JevAdvBench, to our knowledge the first unified benchmark for the adversarial robustness of TDMs. It contains 862 questions with audited answers and twelve black-box attack methods that edit the input or the options by adaptive search. Beyond Reasoning Gains: Mitigating General-Capability Forgetting in Large Reasoning Models 2026-10-26
📝 Implementation is released at https://github.com/VietHoang1512/recap
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has delivered impressive gains in mathematical and multimodal reasoning and has become a standard post-training paradigm for contemporary language and vision-language models. However, the RLVR recipe introduces a significant risk of capability regression, in which models forget foundational skills after prolonged training without employing regularization strategies. We empirically confirm this concern, observing that open-source reasoning models suffer performance degradation on core capabilities such as perception and faithfulness. While imposing regularization terms like KL divergence can help prevent deviation from the base model, these terms are computed on the current task and therefore do not guarantee preservation of broader knowledge. Meanwhile, commonly used experience replay across heterogeneous domains makes it nontrivial to decide how much training emphasis each objective should receive. To address this, we propose RECAP-a replay strategy with dynamic objective reweighting for general knowledge preservation. Our reweighting mechanism adapts online using short-horizon signals of convergence and instability, shifting the post-training focus away from saturated objectives and toward underperforming or volatile ones. Our method is end-to-end and readily applicable to existing RLVR pipelines without training additional models or heavy tuning. Extensive experiments on benchmarks using Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, which not only preserves general capabilities but also improves reasoning by enabling more flexible trade-offs among in-task rewards.

Trajectory-based confidence estimation has shown promising performance for large language models (LLMs) by leveraging rich signals from their reasoning processes. Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise for training multimodal agents to reason and use tools. However, it remains unclear when fine-grained credit assignment improves learning and when it fails to improve task performance. Video editing provides a useful testbed for this question, combining multimodal perception, long-horizon planning, and tool use with delayed outcome feedback. S$^2$-RL: Sample-Set Dual Reinforcement Learning for Generative Semantic Segmentation Dataset Distillation 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project Page: https://00why00.github.io/S2RL/
Show abstract

While dataset distillation has witnessed significant advances in image classification, its extension to dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation is plagued by two core bottlenecks: bi-level optimization-based approaches suffer from prohibitive computational costs stemming from pixel-wise gradient unrolling, whereas proxy-based generative methods merely infuse visual textures into fixed semantic masks derived from real data, thus failing to condense rich semantic knowledge into a compact set of informative, novel scene generations.
