ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization 2026-11-16
📝 74 pages, 11 figures, 26 tables. Code: https://github.com/huawei-lin/ViRA
Show abstract

Visual agents answer questions about ultra-high-resolution photos by zooming into the relevant regions. However, existing agents often answer from common sense instead of reading the evidence. Reinforcement learning (RL) on the final answer cannot correct this, because a guess earns the same reward as reading. We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on contrast sets. A contrast set asks one question about several versions of an image that differ only in a small region with the evidence and have different answers, so only reading the evidence solves the whole set. CrossRPO rewards solving the whole set, and we prove that it does not reinforce a set answered from common sense. We build contrast sets for training (ViRA-Train) and evaluation (ViRA-Bench) by editing one small object in images of 21 to 120 megapixels, and we photograph further sets with real changes (ViRA-Bench-Real) to exclude editing traces. We train two Visual Reasoning Agents, ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B, with CrossRPO on only about 3,500 items of ViRA-Train each. CrossRPO achieves 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench at the maximum resolution, against 59.3% for GRPO. ViRA-9B is the most accurate open model of its size on all benchmarks, and ViRA-27B outperforms Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-Luna, both at their highest thinking level, on most benchmarks.

Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting with Physics-Indexed Specialist Memory: the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent 2026-11-16
📝 25 pages, 7 figures, 23 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/blasvicco/ena/tree/v1
Show abstract

Continual reinforcement learning requires adaptation to changing conditions while preserving performance under previously encountered dynamics. It was investigated whether specialists generated by evolutionary search can provide a reusable memory when combined with environment detection and failure-driven search. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) evolves small neural networks without gradients, archives selected policies by detected dynamic regime, and retrieves them through Bayesian policy reuse. The architecture was evaluated in non-stationary CartPole and HalfCheetah environments. In the 30-replicate CartPole ablation, the complete architecture achieved a mean success rate of 83.2% across the five test environments. Among variants that learned the harder CartPole environments, the complete architecture showed the most consistent retention. A selection ablation also showed that the retrieval rule affected performance. In the evaluated sequences, ENA adapted more slowly than PPO on CartPole and achieved lower returns than SAC on HalfCheetah. The evidence concerns the complete architecture and does not isolate gradient-free optimization as the cause of retention or establish superiority over other continual-learning methods.

Perceive-to-Reason: Decoupling Perception and Reasoning for Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning 2026-11-16
📝 Code: https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/Perceive-to-Reason
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Fine-grained visual reasoning remains challenging for vision-language models, especially when small but critical visual cues are buried in high-resolution images. Existing approaches rely on repeated cropping or test-time visual search to introduce local evidence, but they typically do not explicitly distinguish perception from reasoning. In this paper, we propose Perceive-to-Reason (P2R), a unified framework that formulates fine-grained visual reasoning as a two-stage process: the model first localizes question-relevant evidence as a Perceiver, and then answers the question as a Reasoner based on the annotated image and cropped regions. To better align training with this decoupled formulation, we further introduce Perception-Reasoning Alternating GRPO (PRA-GRPO), a role-aware reinforcement learning strategy that alternates between perception-focused and reasoning-focused updates using only final-answer supervision. Built on top of Qwen3-VL-Instruct-2B/4B/8B, P2R consistently improves performance across model scales. In particular, P2R-4B achieves 93.2% on V-Star, 81.9% on HR-Bench-4K, and 80.5% on HR-Bench-8K, substantially outperforming its corresponding backbone. Further experiments show that the benefits of P2R extend beyond high-resolution benchmarks to broader multimodal reasoning tasks. These results suggest that explicitly decoupling perception from reasoning provides an effective framework for fine-grained visual reasoning.

SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection 2026-11-16
📝 An earlier version was accepted at the Third Workshop on Agents in the Wild: Safety, Security, and Beyond (NeurIPS 2026). 16 pages. v2 updates the AgentDojo grid and stress-test results with the released SDK, spillway-guard 1.0.0 (PyPI). Project page: https://saintmachines.github.io/spillway/ Code and per-cell results: https://github.com/saintmachines/spillway
Show abstract

Tool-using agents act on email, files, and payments, so an instruction hidden in a web page or a shared document can make an agent send data or money to an attacker. Many runtime defenses put a language model on the decision that lets an action through, so the decision depends on a model that may read the attacker's text and adds model calls to every step. Provenance defenses keep the model off the final check but need a model to plan or route each task first. Where an action goes can instead be read from the tools' typed returns: an email's sender or a payment's account arrives in a typed field, while injected instructions arrive as free text. SPILLWAY turns this observation into a deterministic floor with no per-task plan. The check admits a call when each target value that appears in tool output but not in the user's task was returned by a read-only tool in a typed field or is covered by a fixed delegation rule. A deterministic sanitizer removes instruction-like regions its fixed rules match before the model reads them and retains the removed spans as evidence, and a local judge may restore a refused call, never one whose target lies in the removed text. Across three backbones, and seven task suites on one of them, no standard templated injection redirects an action to an attacker's target under SPILLWAY, at the highest observed mean utility and composite scores (NRP, ERS) of the defenses we run under that attack. A forged chat template also produces a raw zero, and mean in-scope attack success under a released reinforcement-learning attacker is .005. SPILLWAY reaches this floor with no defense-triggered backbone re-run and almost no added model call. As personal AI agents act on users' email, files, and payments, SPILLWAY gives them an auditable refusal floor on where their actions go that keeps them useful and holds across backbones.

Optimistic Verifiers Get Hacked: Reward Hacking Under an Exact Process Oracle 2026-11-16
📝 30 pages. A shorter version, "The Verifier Gap", appears at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Meta-Agents (non-archival)
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards inherits whatever its verifier gets wrong. Outside mathematics and code the verifier is a proxy, a rubric, a learned judge or a hand-written checker, and its distance from the true objective is never measured, so a rising reward cannot be told apart from a hack. We build a setting where the true reward is exactly computable at the process level: executable authorization policies over 124,416 contexts admit an oracle that lists every minimal sufficient reason for every decision. We compile these policies into exact process rewards, degrade the verifier along seven realistic axes (outcome-only, sufficiency, recall-only, extraction noise, coarse taxonomy, stale policy, LLM judge), and train against each while the exact verifier scores every rollout. Four phenotypes appear. Optimistic verifiers are hacked by padding: the sufficiency proxy ends at 0.940 while true faithfulness falls from 0.731 to 0.667 on 96 held-out episodes. Outcome-only verifiers saturate and stop teaching, noise dilutes, and ranking-preserving gaps do no harm. A model-free audit of the verifier, its selection error and gap sign, together with a short saturation pilot, anticipates which phenotype a run will produce. Across nine configurations spanning two frameworks, two model vendors, two adapter sizes, three precisions, two initialisations and two seeds, the phenotypes recur, sufficiency damage is positive in all 10 replication cells and beyond two standard errors in 7, and optimistic gaps remain the steeper branch. The damage magnitude does not replicate, and five pre-registered attempts to explain it are reported as they fell. We release the testbed, the training instrumentation, and 14 real public policies (Cedar, OPA, Gatekeeper) ported to executable oracles.

Procedure without Judgment: What Small Language Models Learn under an Exact Verifier 2026-11-16
📝 17 pages
Show abstract

When an authorization policy blocks an agent's tool call, the audit log should name the facts that forced the decision and nothing more. For an executable policy over an enumerable context space this minimal sufficient reason can be computed exactly, so an explanation is scored against ground truth rather than a proxy for it. We use that exactness to ask what a model can learn from a verifier that has no error in it. The explainer sees one request and one decision, never the policy, and has thirty turns in which it may call the policy on altered requests and ask whether a candidate set of facts is sufficient. Frontier models find the exact reason about half the time with the tools alone, and one sentence telling them to shrink the answer lifts Haiku 4.5 from 58% to 75%. A 4B model fine-tuned on 2000 traces of the deletion algorithm reaches 92% on the policy it was trained on. Teacher kind is what moves this: at matched data size the algorithm-taught student leads the frontier-taught one in all three training seeds, and the two arms never overlap. What the student learns is a fixed schedule of sufficiency checks, and the schedule does not travel. On three public policies it never saw, its answers stay sufficient (94%) and stop being minimal (48%), while Haiku under the same sentence is exact on all 47 episodes. Reinforcement learning from the exact reward leaves held-out accuracy where it was and inverts the strategy, from probing the policy to spending the entire budget on sufficiency checks. The oracle made the effect of training legible, but it did not make training better.

Algorithm Distillation for Safe In-Context Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Safe in-context reinforcement learning (Safe ICRL) asks a policy with fixed parameters to improve on an unseen constrained task from its interaction history while aiming to keep its cost within a budget. Prior work studied Safe ICRL on five benchmarks in three families: a grid world with a hidden goal and obstacles (DarkRoom), continuous navigation where goals and hazards stay invisible until reached (PointGoal, CarGoal), and locomotion with a hidden velocity limit (HalfCheetah, Ant). Safe Algorithm Distillation (Safe AD), trained on the learning histories of a safe RL algorithm, has shown adaptation failures in prior evaluations. For example, in the locomotion tasks its return stays below zero, and it rarely reaches the goal in CarGoal. We revisit two parts of Safe AD. First, we distill a budget-aware teacher with two phases. It searches for the hidden task, exploring more when more budget remains. After its first reward or cost, an expert policy exploits the task within the remaining budget. We collect the teacher's histories under fixed or dynamic budgets. Dynamic budgets increase the occupancy entropy of the return and budget inputs in every environment, and at test time they improve return and lower cost in DarkRoom and HalfCheetah. Second, we propose A-Lag, a test-time adjustment that reweights the distilled policy's actions by their predicted cost, with an adaptive Lagrange multiplier that is updated from the costs observed. In our experiments, A-Lag had lower mean episode cost than Safe AD in all five environments, with lower mean final return in four.

Searching for New Physics with Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-16
📝 5 pages, 1 figure, this version is prepared for the journal submission
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Finding new physics (NP) is the fundamental problem in particle physics today. Studying anomalies, i.e., measurements of low-energy observables whose values disagree with the predictions of the Standard Model (SM), is a powerful search strategy. The SM Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) provides a general model-independent framework for parametrizing NP; given some anomalies, it is natural to try to find the SMEFT operator(s) that can explain them. This is a challenging task because (i) the number of SMEFT operators is enormous, and (ii) at loop level there are very complicated correlations among the operators. Analyses by humans typically rely on phenomenological intuition to decide which operators are relevant. This is often biased and does not explore the complete SMEFT operator space. Interestingly, reinforcement learning (RL) techniques excel at tasks that require decision making to achieve their goals. In this paper, we introduce an RL method that can be used to find the SMEFT operators that explain any anomalies. Using some explicit examples, we demonstrate that our RL method can be used to efficiently search for NP at the level of SMEFT. The RL training can be performed efficiently using standard CPU resources, without requiring specialized computing hardware.

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) leverages a model of the environment, used for planning and generating samples without interacting with the real world. Motivated by evidence that informed initialization significantly improves the efficiency of MBRL, we propose TDec-Init, an algorithm to warm-start neural dynamics models using transition Jacobians. A Reinforcement Learning Method to Navigate SMEFT Operator Space 2026-11-16
📝 15 pages, 3 figures, GitHub Link: https://github.com/jackypheno/ReinforceSMEFT
Show abstract

In particle physics, discrepancies between experimental measurements and Standard Model (SM) predictions are commonly interpreted within new theoretical models. In this context, the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) provides a general framework encompassing a broad class of models. However, exploring the enormous space of possible SMEFT operators rapidly becomes a challenging combinatorial problem: many operators can contribute simultaneously to different observables, their effects are correlated, and distinct operator combinations can provide comparably good descriptions of the data. In this work, we formulate the exploration of the SMEFT operator space as a sequential decision-making problem using reinforcement learning (RL). Our RL agent employs a lightweight actor-critic architecture with approximately 40K trainable parameters in total. Rather than searching for a single best-fit theory, our method is designed to discover a diverse set of high-quality theory hypotheses. We test the method in a complex phenomenological environment comprising 132 observables across several sectors of particle physics, including 24 measurements that deviate from their SM predictions by more than $1.5σ$. We show that the learned RL policy efficiently constructs multi-operator theories that substantially improve the global fit. Comparisons with independent random-subset and random-sequential searches demonstrate the ability of the RL policy to navigate this large combinatorial space. Our results establish RL as a systematic framework for exploring diverse patterns of new physics (NP) interactions within SMEFT.

AuXSpec: Learning to Autoformalize Program Specifications via Executable Postconditions 2026-11-16

Show abstract

Formal specifications define intended program behavior and underpin verification, but writing them in theorem provers such as Lean is slow and expertise-intensive, making specification a major bottleneck to scaling formally correct code. Autoformalization from natural-language problems is promising, yet training specification models requires scalable feedback on whether generated specifications capture intended behavior. Prior approaches obtain this feedback through explicit proofs or separately implemented code with formal correspondence proofs, both expensive. We introduce AuXSpec, a Lean framework that pairs declarative postconditions with executable counterparts, obtained through type class resolution when possible and otherwise defined separately with an equivalence proof. Test cases can then evaluate declarative specifications by execution, letting us use agreement as a reinforcement-learning reward. Using AuXSpec, we synthesize 3,037 LeetCode problems and expand training to 19.5K samples through expert iteration on Codeforces. Experiments show that training Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.8-27B with the execution-based reward improves SpecGen pass@4 on AuXSpec-Test 0.7% $\to$ 82.7% and 14.3% $\to$ 84.3%, and the 27B model outperforms all frontier models. On the out-of-domain Verina benchmark with its original prompts, both models improve 7.0% $\to$ 90.5% and 21.9% $\to$ 90.5% and surpass VeriSpecGen-30B-A3B, the prior state of the art among open-source specification models, on pass@1 (theirs 47.6% vs. ours 73.7% and 84.5%) and pass@4 (theirs 63.9% at pass@10 vs. ours 90.5%). We release the weights of the trained 9B and 27B models and the 3,037 problems with test cases, including the 300-problem AuXSpec-Test benchmark with expert review and correctness proofs for future evaluation. WeVisForensics: Self-Evolving Agent for Omni AI-Generated Video Detection 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Recent advances in video generation call for reliable detection grounded in explicit forensic evidence. However, current methods primarily depend on single-pass chain-of-thought reasoning that overlooks brief or localized artifacts, or on predefined multi-agent workflows that offer limited flexibility in selecting relevant inspections. Even with adaptive investigation, optimizing the investigation policy alone does not directly improve the forensic capabilities of fixed experts. We introduce WeVisForensics, an agentic framework in which a main investigator agent coordinates a self-evolving library of forensic inspector sub-agents spanning visual and audio cues. The investigator learns which artifact type to examine, over which window, and when to conclude, and these interactions provide shared feedback for both levels of agents. Federated TD Learning in Heterogeneous Environments with Average Rewards: A Two-timescale Approach with Polyak-Ruppert Averaging 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Federated Reinforcement Learning (FRL) provides a promising way to speedup training in reinforcement learning when one has access to multiple edge devices that can operate in parallel. Recently, it has been shown that even when these edge devices have access to different dynamic models---such as cars or robots with differing system configurations---an optimal convergence rate that has a linear speedup proportional to the number of devices is achievable. However, this result requires that the algorithm's stepsize be chosen in a manner that depends on the unknown model parameters. Also, it applies only to a discounted setting, which has been argued to fit episodic tasks better than continuing control tasks. In this paper, we obtain finite-time bounds for heterogeneous FRL with average rewards. We show that optimal convergence rate and linear speedup are achievable even with a universal stepsize choice, independent of the underlying dynamics. To achieve our result, we modify the existing one-timescale FRL method to a novel two-timescale variant that additionally incorporates iterate averaging. We remark that this idea also extends to other settings already studied in the literature, such as the homogeneous FRL or the heterogeneous case with a discounted cost function.

Heteroscedastic Gaussian Process Q-learning for Sample Efficient Experimentation 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Self-driving laboratories must learn to control a process from tens to hundreds of expensive experiments, too few for deep reinforcement learning (RL). We learn the Q-function directly with a Gaussian process (GP), which avoids learning the dynamics, and show that the GP posterior supplies the learning rate of Q-learning. If each Bellman target is treated as a noisy measurement of the Q-function, the learning rate that minimises the mean squared error is the Kalman gain, and applying it to every observed transition is equivalent to GP regression. Heteroscedastic Gaussian Process Q-learning (HGPQ) sets the noise variance of each target with a heteroscedastic GP, which learns how unreliable the targets are across the states and inputs. Targets from noisy operating conditions, or from regions where the value function has large gradients, then move the estimate less than targets from low-noise regions, so HGPQ learns quickly where the data are reliable without being misled where they are not. For kernel value iteration with per-target variances that are assigned when a target is collected, we prove a high-probability bound on the error of the value estimate, uniform over the explored region. Pooled over five simulated benchmarks, which include a chemical reactor and a bioprocess, HGPQ approaches the best return found by any method in fewer episodes than GP, kernel and deep RL baselines.

Stacked intelligent metasurfaces (SIMs) have recently emerged as a powerful wave-domain technology that enables multi-stage manipulation of electromagnetic signals through multilayer programmable architectures. While SIMs offer unprecedented degrees of freedom for enhancing physical-layer security, their extremely large number of meta-atoms leads to a high-dimensional and strongly coupled optimization space, making conventional design approaches inefficient and difficult to scale. Moreover, existing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques suffer from slow convergence and performance degradation in dynamic wireless environments with imperfect knowledge of passive eavesdroppers. To address these challenges, we propose a hybrid quantum proximal policy optimization (QPPO) framework for SIM-assisted secure communications that jointly optimizes transmit power allocation and SIM phase shifts to maximize the average secrecy rate under power and quality-of-service constraints. Extensive simulations demonstrate that the proposed Q-PPO scheme consistently outperforms DRL baselines, achieving approximately 15% higher secrecy rates and 30% faster convergence under imperfect eavesdropper channel state information. These results establish Q-PPO as a powerful optimization paradigm for SIM-enabled secure wireless networks.

Skill Adapter: Personalized Modification and Online Routing of General-Purpose Skills 2026-11-16
📝 9 pages, 3 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents use external skills to reuse task procedures and experience with tools. General-purpose skills describe shared execution procedures, while specific tool choices, operating practices, and output requirements vary across users. In long-horizon tasks, subgoals, available inputs, and environmental states change as execution progresses. Agents must select currently applicable skills from an expanding skill pool and prepare candidates for subsequent steps. We propose Skill Adapter, which enables personalized modification and online routing of general-purpose skills. The system distills local adjustments from user requirements and execution feedback and stores them as Adapters linked to general-purpose skills. The routing agent selects general-purpose skills and applicable Adapters based on the current subtask, execution progress, and environmental feedback, incrementally maintaining a task-level online skill library that the main LLM reads on demand. The system preloads candidates for subsequent nodes and rechecks their applicability once actual inputs are determined. Reinforcement learning optimizes the two types of decisions using modification gains and routing outcomes, respectively. We evaluate Skill Adapter on ALFWorld, WebShop, and SkillsBench. Compared with SkillRL, it improves task success rates by an average of 11.43 percentage points and reduces mean execution time on all three benchmarks. Skill Adapter retains its performance advantage when the skill pool grows to 1,000 skills.

Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Aligned Action Spaces for Cross-Game Generalization 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Zero-shot cross-game generalization aims to consolidate accessible source-game experts into a single vision-language model (VLM) that controls unseen games from raw pixels without target-game parameter updates. While multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) mitigates offline exposure bias and single-game reinforcement learning (RL) overfitting, scaling it to autoregressive VLMs faces two bottlenecks. First, token-to-action misalignment from subword length bias distorts discrete action probabilities and uncertainty margins. Second, multi-teacher policy conflict arises as source teachers under incompatible dynamics issue contradictory closed-loop corrections, causing negative transfer. We propose CAMOPD (Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation), a post-training framework that resolves multi-teacher policy conflict within an aligned semantic action space. CAMOPD first uses complete-action alignment to normalize command completion likelihoods over each game's legal action set, forming a calibrated categorical policy that provides reliable uncertainty margins for selective on-policy retention and visual specialist probes that can be evaluated across source games. To resolve policy conflict without target-game data, conflict-aware multi-teacher consolidation cross-evaluates these specialists across source games to capture both visual identifiability and inter-teacher compatibility, upweighting compatible teachers while suppressing conflicting corrections. On GameWorld's temporal and directional transfer suites with Qwen3.5-4B, CAMOPD achieves 26/30 (86.7%) and 24/30 (80.0%) Task-1 zero-shot success on unseen GeoDash and Doodle Jump, outperforming prompting, weight-merging, and multi-task baselines.

CoopDistill: Equilibrium-Guided Multi-Agent Policy Distillation for Cooperative Driving 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Cooperative driving extends autonomous driving from individual vehicle decisions to joint maneuvers that improve traffic safety and efficiency. Optimization-based methods impose safety constraints but often fix paths to simplify multi-vehicle planning, limiting spatial flexibility. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) explores alternatives through interaction, but coordinated learning and safe execution remain challenging. Their integration nevertheless faces challenges in exploration efficiency, experience allocation, and execution reliability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes CoopDistill, an equilibrium-guided multi-agent policy distillation framework. A potential-game (PG) formulation provides a fixed-path longitudinal reference, around which a residual MARL policy learns longitudinal acceleration, conflict-approach timing, and lateral path corrections. A hard-case curriculum concentrates experience on interactions that PG leaves unresolved or substantially delayed.
