Value as Memory: Online Value-Guided Post-training for General Robot Policies 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Real-world post-training of generalist robot policies faces two challenges: partial observability makes action selection ambiguous, while offline post-training methods delay feedback from deployment experience. We present Value as Memory (VaM), a framework that addresses these challenges through value caching and online real-world reinforcement learning (RL). Value caching conditions actions on recent value predictions as task-progress memory, while online updates enable timely policy improvement by combining offline demonstrations, off-policy interventions, and on-policy rollouts for sample-efficient adaptation. As a result, VaM transitions from offline RL warmup to online autonomous learning with sparse rewards. Across long-horizon tasks involving deformable objects and contact-rich manipulation, VaM yields a mean absolute success-rate gain of $29.4\%$ with value caching, while achieving a $32.8\%$ gain with online autonomous learning over matched offline training. The evaluated online schedule performs $8\times$ as many policy iterations under the same gradient-step budget, with more than $2\times$ lower recorded time per gradient step with concurrent data collection and training.

GRASP: Plan-Guided Graph Retrieval with Adaptive Fusion and Reranking on Semi-Structured Knowledge Bases 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Retrieval over semi-structured knowledge bases (SKBs) must handle both textual descriptions and relational constraints. Text-based retrievers capture the overall semantics of a query but struggle with multi-hop relations, whereas graph-based retrievers follow relational paths precisely but miss the broader meaning. We propose GRASP, a hybrid retriever that learns to fuse the two. A small LLM planner translates the query into an executable graph plan in a single call, its graph ranking is fused with a text ranking, and a fine-tuned reranker reorders the fused candidates. Instead of training the planner to be accurate on its own, we optimize it with reinforcement learning to maximize the gain its graph ranking brings to the fused ranking. On three STaRK benchmarks, GRASP achieves the best results on all metrics, improving the average Hit@1 by 18.6 points over the strongest baseline, while taking only 2.47 seconds per query.

Generative visual verifiers play a crucial role in advancing multimodal intelligence. To enhance the robustness of visual verifiers in complex multimodal scenarios involving compositional requirements, we move beyond conventional binary verifiers that produce only True/False discrete judgments and introduce a verifier that automatically decomposes complex prompts or environmental rules into fine-grained components and assigns pointwise scores to provide a continuous and more informative critique signal. Our experiments further demonstrate that these structured and interpretable outputs improve the verifier's generalization to out-of-domain multimodal critic tasks. To train this verifier, we introduce a Decoupled RL training strategy that jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring, using precise local feedback to develop fine-grained verification capabilities that provide a foundation for reliable continuous scoring at the global level. From a theoretical standpoint, we further show that a pairwise verification paradigm yields more accurate critiques than the pointwise formulation when ground-truth images are available, with empirical results on diverse structured world-modeling tasks such as Maze and Sudoku further validating the effectiveness of our approach. Overall, our findings suggest a key insight: the transition from generative models to structured world-modeling agents critically hinges on the availability of accurate pairwise graded visual verifiers.

Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-15
Show abstract

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a multifingered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world experiment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-specific demonstrations or sim-to-real transfer.

Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning for Path-Safe Target Reaching in Robotic Harvesting of Hydroponic Lettuce 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Robotic harvesting requires precise manipulation to ensure accurate positioning of the end-effector, especially in a constraint workspace. This study introduces the Path-Safe Approach Proximal Policy Optimization (PSA-PPO), a target-conditioned local reaching framework designed for robotic harvesting of nutrient film technique (NFT)-hydroponic lettuce in greenhouses. The proposed PSA-PPO preserves the standard clipped PPO objective while introducing a harvesting-oriented policy formulation that integrates target-conditioned observations, corridor-aware spatial constraints, trajectory-history-based safety encoding, and task-specific success criteria for constrained robotic manipulation. PSAPPO was trained entirely in Isaac Lab and successfully transferred to a physical UR10e robotic arm without any real-world policy fine-tuning. In a controlled physical benchmark against 150 scheduled targets, PSA-PPO achieved the highest reaching rate of 94.7%, outperforming Cartesian+TOTG (86.0%), RRTConnect (88.0%), and RRT* (80.0%), while reducing median RMS jerk by 39.8 to 73.0% relative to the classical planners during the final approach segment. A formulation ablation evaluated over 200 simulation targets demonstrates that path-safety and side-corridor shaping are the principal drivers of harvesting-compatible performance: removing either term substantially degrades strict success even when terminal endpoint accuracy is preserved or improved, confirming that optimization toward the endpoint alone is an insufficient objective for constrained crop-proximal manipulation. Finally, PSA-PPO was integrated with an RGB-D vision module and an end-effector and evaluated as a complete greenhouse harvesting system across 144 lettuce harvesting attempts, achieving 94.4% vision-guided target reaching and 86.1% complete harvesting success.

We develop a robust reinforcement learning (RL) framework for learning adaptive treatment policies that generalize across patients and remain reliable under errors in the measured biomarker and shifts in the disease dynamics. Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Training language agents is bottlenecked by the high cost of long-horizon rollouts, yet standard agentic reinforcement learning (RL) learns only from a terminal scalar reward, leaving the supervision in intermediate steps largely unused. Prior work exploits this supervision through value targets that estimate per-step success probabilities or observation targets that fit environment response. Both are hard to construct well: (1) value targets come from the same single scalar signal for each step in the trajectory, and (2) observation targets, such as web pages and terminal logs, are long and noisy and reach only one step ahead. We propose CoPE, which adds a third kind of multi-step action targets. On successful on-policy trajectories, CoPE trains the policy backbone to generate the action sequences that follows each history. Each action is also supervised when forecast from the preceding histories, forming a temporal ensemble that the shared backbone absorbs during training, and this enables acting and forecasting to co-evolve. The policy still acts one step at a time at inference, with no overhead. Across five benchmarks (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, WebShop, $τ^2$-bench, and AppWorld), CoPE (1) achieves stronger aggregate performance than GRPO and RL baselines with predictive auxiliary targets across simulated environments, while improving overall performance on both realistic applications; (2) delivers up to $1.9\times$ and $2.0\times$ higher sample efficiency than ECHO and GRPO, respectively, on ScienceWorld; and (3) achieves these gains with a single policy backbone, without additional models, separate pretraining, or inference overhead. The codebase is available at https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/CoPE.

KnowGPO: Knowledge-Grouped Policy Optimization for Search Agents 2026-10-15
📝 Code available at https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/KnowGPO
Show abstract

Deep search agents solve knowledge-intensive tasks through long-horizon, multi-turn interactions, where assigning credit to individual search decisions is fundamentally difficult. Outcome-based reinforcement learning provides only a single terminal reward shared across an entire trajectory. Recent process-supervision methods aim to assign credit at finer granularity, but they typically anchor credit to either surface-level trajectory states or model-internal signals, both of which become unreliable in deep search where observations are heterogeneous and retrieved contexts are long and noisy. We propose KnowGPO, a reinforcement learning framework that aligns trajectories by the task-relevant knowledge they have acquired rather than by their surface observations. For each query, KnowGPO decomposes the information required to derive the answer into atomic knowledge conditions, and represents the state of a trajectory at each step by the subset of conditions it has satisfied. Trajectories sharing the same knowledge state are grouped for within-group comparison, and the incremental knowledge gain between consecutive steps serves as an environment-grounded process reward. Experiments on Xbench, BrowseComp-ZH, and GAIA show KnowGPO consistently outperforms outcome-only (GRPO) and process-supervision baselines (GiGPO, IGPO) by 3.8 to 4.2 points, with our 8B model rivaling or exceeding most 32B open-source systems.

Training Reasoning Models to Generate Diverse Scientific Ideas 2026-10-15
📝 Code and Data at:https://github.com/ManyaWadhwa/diverge_ideation | Under Review
Show abstract

One of the first steps of a research pipeline is generating ideas. Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate this step, but most of this work optimizes the quality of individual ideas, while overlooking diversity. In our work, we study document-grounded ideation, focusing on increasing the diversity of LLM generated ideas and ensuring they are high quality. Given prior papers, a model must propose a set of ideas that are each high-quality and clearly distinct from one another, while also being grounded in the input documents. To optimize for this, we propose training with a set-level reinforcement learning objective. In our setup, in a single generation, the model writes K structured idea cards grounded in the source papers, and the set is scored jointly, where each idea's quality is weighted by its distance from the ideas written before it. This teaches the model to steer each new idea away from its predecessors. We show that our trained 8B model achieves higher creative utility (a measure combining quality and diversity) than Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-6 Astra on our contamination-free benchmark. In a pilot human study with four experts, our sets are judged as promising as Claude Opus 4.8's and contain marginally more unique ideas. When a coding agent runs proof-of-concept experiments on the ideas, our model's success@k is comparable to Claude Opus 4.8's, so we see that the added diversity does not cost any executability.

AVC-Reward: Event-Centric Audio-Video Consistency Evaluation 2026-10-15

As joint audio-video generation models become more capable, they can generate audio-visual scenes exhibiting three forms of complexity: multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixture, and asymmetric cross-modal relations. However, current evaluations largely reuse metrics from earlier simple and unidirectional tasks. They typically evaluate an audio-video clip as a whole and rely on simple predefined relations, making them unsuitable for these increasingly complex scenes. We propose an event-centric evaluation framework that disentangles audio and visual events at the event level, analyzes each event independently, cross-validates relations across modalities, and aggregates the judgments into a coarse-to-fine, three-level consistency metric. AVC-Reward achieves state-of-the-art alignment with human judgment among automatic methods, provides interpretable rationales that localize failures, and improves the audio-video consistency of a joint generation model when used as an online reinforcement-learning signal. Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems 2026-10-15

Show abstract

Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration. However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift. In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs. The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path. Pixel-based language models are gaining momentum as alternatives to traditional token-based approaches, promising to circumvent tokenization challenges; however, existing models remain English-centric and are hard to train as generators. Towards Continual Learning: Mitigating Forgetting With Subspace Tuning 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) acquire new skills through fine-tuning, but it can destroy capabilities they already have: catastrophic forgetting remains a central obstacle to continual learning in LLMs. Recent works connect forgetting to how far, and along which directions, fine-tuning moves the weights. On-policy methods such as reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation avoid updating the principal singular directions of weight matrices and thus forget less. In contrast, SFT concentrates updates along these directions and is susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. In this work, we introduce Subspace Tuning (SST), which restricts each weight update to the bottom singular subspace, eliminating changes along the principal directions. SST is optimizer-agnostic and composes well with fine-tuning recipes including SFT and self-distillation. SST combines tight subspace protection, dense gradient estimation, and a simple refresh mechanism, enabling stronger learning than prior orthogonal fine-tuning methods. With these mechanisms, protecting as much as 99\% of the weight subspace substantially improves retention while preserving strong target-task performance. This behavior strengthens with model and data size and nearly eliminates forgetting in the large-model regime. SST also keeps the model close to its base distribution, inducing $2$--$4\times$ less KL drift than the baseline recipes. Overall, SST advances the learning--forgetting Pareto frontier, making it a practical default for fine-tuning pre-trained language models.

EliteReplay: Preserving Diverse Solutions in RL Post-Training via Quality-Diversity Search 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning has become a common post-training technique to improve the accuracy of LLMs on verifiable tasks. However, such training can narrow the set of solutions the model can produce. Several approaches have attempted to address this behavior-sharpening effect, but they often optimize metrics that do not reflect the diversity of correct solutions. Ideally, we want a model that can produce multiple good solutions, not only a single reliable one. We propose EliteReplay, a post-training recipe that pairs GRPO with an "elite" archive built by quality-diversity search. Before training, we use the frozen base model to collect solutions that span distinct approaches; during training, GRPO is combined with a replay loss on this archive. The result is a post-trained model that improves accuracy while preserving more of the base model's solution diversity. On multiple text-to-SQL tasks, EliteReplay achieves a higher pass@128 than all baselines, producing up to 77% more distinct correct strategies than the strongest baselines. It preserves most of the base model's correct strategies, outperforming existing methods in strategy retention. This diversity translates into practical benefits on ambiguous questions, where EliteReplay covers both interpretations nearly twice as often as the strongest baseline on AmbiQT.

Learning Rational Strategies for Temporal Objectives in Multi-Agent Systems 2026-10-15
Show abstract

We study rational synthesis for stochastic multi-agent systems with unknown dynamics, where each agent is equipped with its own probabilistic temporal objective specified using Probabilistic Linear Temporal Logic on Finite Traces ($\mathsf{PLTL}_f}$). We impose solution concepts (e.g., Nash equilibrium, subgame-perfect equilibrium) as constraints on the joint strategy profile, using payoffs derived from each agent's probability of satisfying its temporal objective. In particular, we formulate per-agent rational synthesis as finding a strategy profile -- if one exists -- such that (i) the designated agent's probabilistic temporal constraint is satisfied, and (ii) the profile represents a stable solution under a chosen solution concept. We give conditional convergence guarantees for tabular Nash-Q learning, certify satisfaction with simultaneous finite-sample confidence bounds, and assess strategic stability with approximate best-response checking. Our approach operates in a stochastic \emph{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning} (MARL) environment, where model-free learning algorithms attempt to attain extremal satisfaction probabilities for temporal objectives automatically compiled into reward automata. Our approach is empirically validated using 8 general-sum concurrent stochastic games.

Routing Policy Optimization for Online Diffusion Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning can align visual generators with reward signals beyond their pretraining objectives, but existing score-function methods typically introduce stochasticity by perturbing the reverse denoising trajectory, leading to costly rollout-based optimization. We instead ask whether stochastic exploration can be introduced inside the generator while preserving its original sampler. Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) answers this question by turning attention routing into a stochastic exploration action. At a few selected denoising levels, each query block keeps its own block and samples further key blocks in proportion to the generator's native attention mass, or keeps dense attention. The sampled route perturbs the velocity of that transition, and a Gaussian likelihood around the dense velocity scores the perturbation with a score-function objective. RPO further uses a controlled two-rollout estimator: two rollouts share the same prompt, initial latent, routed denoising levels, and dense anchor layers, differing only in their sampled attention routes. Their reward difference provides an advantage signal for the routed transitions. Because the likelihood differentiates the dense velocity itself, the learned updates remain effective after routing is removed. At inference, RPO restores dense attention and retains the original deterministic sampler. Experiments on text-to-image and text-to-video generation show that RPO improves generation quality while achieving nearly 4x higher training efficiency than Flow-GRPO-style methods. Please see our project page at https://velox-world.com/rpo for visual results.

Librarian Agents: Training LLMs to Curate Long-Context Workspaces that Anticipate Future Queries 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Reasoning over long document contexts remains a challenge for large language models (LLMs). Standard retrieval methods are inherently reactive, bottlenecking agents with complex, cross-document reasoning at inference time, while precomputed structures like knowledge graphs often fall short because they rely on rigid heuristics. Instead, can we allow agents to perform anticipatory directed curation: learning to proactively reorganize unstructured corpora prior to inference to efficiently serve diverse future queries. We build on a key asymmetry in modern agentic harnesses (e.g., Claude Code): while LLM agents are adept at navigating existing filesystems to solve an immediate task, they are rarely trained to construct reusable workspaces for future use. To instantiate this capability, we introduce Librarian Agents, a framework in which a builder agent transforms raw documents into a hierarchical filesystem of synthetic files and reference links, which forms a workspace tailored for a downstream answering agent. We formulate workspace construction as a Markov decision process and optimize the builder policy via reinforcement learning to directly maximize downstream answering accuracy. Empirically, Librarian Agents outperforms the strongest retrieval and precomputation baselines by 33 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus, amortizes upfront construction costs across repeated queries, and generalizes zero-shot to unseen domains on BRIGHT with a 31-point average accuracy improvement.

Minimizing Age of Incorrect Information in Pull-Based Remote Estimation of a Markov Source 2026-10-15
Show abstract

In this paper, we consider a discrete-time pull-based remote estimation system involving an information source and a remote monitor where the information source is a finite-state irreducible discrete-time Markov chain (DTMC) process. In this system, the remote monitor sends a pull request towards the source which responds by sampling its process so as to transmit the sampled values towards the monitor through a delay channel, encapsulated in information packets. We consider i) zero delay (ZD) and ii) unit delay (UD) channels, where the packet transmission times are zero and one slot, respectively. For remote estimation, we use the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimator, and for quantifying its performance, we use the mean age of incorrect information (MAoII) which is a recently proposed freshness and mismatch metric that penalizes an incorrect estimation along with its duration. Our goal is to obtain pull policies so as to minimize MAoII under a long-run sampling rate constraint. For the ZD model, we propose a semi-Markov decision process (SMDP) formulation of the problem, which allows us to find the optimum observation-dependent pull policy. On the other hand, the UD channel prevents the monitor from possessing perfect information on the actual AoII process at any given time, and the SMDP formulation of the ZD channel cannot readily be used for the UD model. For the system with the UD channel, we first provide a numerical algorithm to calculate the MAoII and the average sampling rate for a given observation-dependent pull policy. For the optimization of the underlying remote estimation system, we use the joint distribution of the age and source processes to be obtained from the history of all observations, termed as belief. Subsequently, we propose a belief-dependent policy obtained via the belief-MDP formulation and a constrained deep reinforcement learning (CDRL) algorithm.

Baselines and Score Centering in Policy Gradient Estimation 2026-10-15
📝 17 pages
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning has become a widely used approach to post-training large language models. In a typical implementation, learning is off-policy: The policy generating the responses differs from the policy being trained because of training-inference mismatch or policy staleness in asynchronous training. This discrepancy is commonly addressed by importance sampling, whose ratio is usually regularized in practice through truncation, masking, or clipping. Under regularized importance sampling, the importance-weighted policy gradient is no longer invariant to constant shifts in the reward. This invariance is recovered by using the covariance between the reward and the importance-weighted score as the estimand, which coincides with the policy gradient when the ratio is not regularized. This note develops this covariance perspective for both sequence-level and token-level importance sampling. The covariance perspective reveals the roles of the group-mean baseline and score centering in policy gradient estimation. Both perform centering in covariance estimation, but they differ in the mean being subtracted: The group-mean baseline uses a sample mean, whereas score centering uses a population mean. The two lead to distinct unbiased covariance estimators with different variances. Whether both forms of centering should be used simultaneously depends on whether importance sampling is applied at the sequence level or at the token level. With sequence-level importance sampling, score centering adds no benefit once the group-mean baseline is used; with token-level importance sampling, using the group-mean baseline without score centering generally introduces bias when the importance ratio is regularized.

BRIDGE: Bridging Reasoning In Distillation Gap Elimination via Structure-Aware Masking 2026-10-15
📝 15 pages, 13 figures. Accepted at SDM 2026
Show abstract

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has significantly improved LLMs' mathematical problem-solving capabilities, but distilling such capabilities into smaller models remains challenging due to the capacity mismatch between verbose teachers and compact students. Directly copying teachers' lengthy reasoning chains causes capacity overload, resulting in truncated outputs or repetitive failure. Existing remedies each sacrifice a critical property of CoT: implicit reasoning methods (e.g., compressing reasoning into hidden states) trade away interpretability and verifiability, while heuristic compression strategies (e.g., random step pruning) destroy logical integrity. On GSM8K, BRIDGE enables Qwen2.5-3B to achieve 11.29% accuracy improvement and 27.4% token reduction over the original model, outperforming instruction-tuned variants and distillation baselines.
