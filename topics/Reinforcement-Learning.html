Tenlai-TTS 2.0: Continuous-Representation LLM-Based TTS System with End-to-End Training 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) with continuous speech latents avoids quantization-induced information loss, but typically relies on a latent space that is learned separately and then frozen before language-model training. Such a space must satisfy two competing requirements: it must retain enough acoustic detail for high-fidelity reconstruction, yet be sufficiently structured and regularized to support autoregressive prediction by a language model. We present Tenlai-TTS 2.0, which learns continuous speech representations through end-to-end training to balance acoustic detail with autoregressive predictability. The model comprises a speech encoder with a capacity-controlled Gaussian bottleneck, a large language model (LLM) with an autoregressive diffusion transformer (AR-DiT) head, a flow-matching (FM) mel-spectrogram decoder, and a multi-task recognition model (RM). With energy normalization and fixed-variance Gaussian noise, the bottleneck has a closed-form upper bound on its information rate. Through joint optimization with reconstruction, recognition, and next-patch prediction objectives, we learn continuous speech representations that suit all three tasks and train the LLM and AR-DiT for autoregressive generation. The LLM and AR-DiT are further optimized using differentiable reconstruction and recognition feedback on their predicted latents through the FM decoder and RM. On Seed-TTS-eval, this feedback-based optimization reduces character error rate (CER) from 0.82% to 0.74% on test-zh and from 8.32% to 7.08% on test-hard-zh, and word error rate (WER) from 1.32% to 1.27% on test-en, compared with joint training alone. Subsequent reinforcement learning further reduces CER to 6.29% on test-hard-zh and WER to 1.08% on test-en, with speaker similarity scores of 0.811, 0.766, and 0.784 on test-zh, test-en, and test-hard-zh, respectively.

PartPO: Part-Level Policy Optimization for Shape-to-Program Generation 2026-11-22
Show abstract

We present PartPO, a reinforcement learning method for generating structured shape programs from 3D point clouds. Existing shape-to-program models are trained with supervised learning on procedurally generated data and generalize poorly to more diverse shapes, while reinforcement learning from execution feedback assigns a single scalar reward to a long program that builds many parts, making credit assignment difficult. PartPO instead builds on a core property of the task: shape programs are self-describing, with each semantically named part produced by its own code block. It segments each generated program by its parts and applies part-level rewards only to the code of the corresponding part, a category reward only to the object name, and a shape-level reward to the whole program. The required part supervision is obtained without manual annotation, by grounding part names on target meshes with an open-vocabulary segmentation model prompt-tuned on the model's own outputs. Applied to MeshCoder, PartPO improves reconstruction accuracy and execution success on ShapeNetCore and ABO over the base model, rejection-sampling fine-tuning and trajectory-level RL, and generalizes to the unseen HSSD dataset. Code will be released at https://github.com/HannuoZ/PartPO.

Communication-Aware Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Decentralized Cooperative UAV Deployment 2026-11-22
📝 This paper is accepted to IEEE GLOBECOM 2026
Show abstract

Autonomous Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) swarms are increasingly used as rapidly deployable aerial relays and sensing platforms, yet practical deployments must operate under partial observability and intermittent peer-to-peer connectivity. We present a graph-based multi-agent reinforcement learning framework trained under centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE): a centralized critic and global state are available only during training, while each UAV executes a shared policy using local observations and messages from nearby neighbors. Under restricted communication, neighbor relations are induced by an SNR-threshold connectivity graph. Our architecture encodes local agent state and nearby entities with an agent-entity attention module and aggregates inter-UAV messages with neighbor self-attention over a signal-quality-limited communication graph defined by a channel model. We evaluate the framework on a cooperative relay-deployment task, DroneConnect. Experimental results show that the proposed method achieves an approximately 12% increase in target coverage over MAPPO under restricted communication and partial observability, while remaining competitive with a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP)-based offline upper bound with full node observability.

FunGrasp-Zero: Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Functional Grasping 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Dexterous functional grasping is critical for robotic tool use, yet current dexterous hands exhibit substantial morphological diversity. This hardware heterogeneity makes cross-embodiment functional grasping a fundamental but largely unexplored problem. Existing functional grasping methods use retargeted hand poses as guidance, tying task representation to a specific embodiment and failing to explicitly capture object-centric functional contact intent, limiting generalization across hands. To address this, we propose FunGrasp-Zero, a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping. Instead of relying on embodiment-specific joint configuration, FunGrasp-Zero captures functional intent via an object-centric, cross-embodiment-consistent contact map, adapted to heterogeneous hands through morphological mapping. We construct a hand-object potential field from mapped contact regions, yielding attractive and repulsive guidance vectors. A Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Network (FC-GAT) leverages these vectors to coordinate multi-finger motion across hands, while a potential field reward guides fingers toward target regions and penalizes non-target collisions to enforce functional alignment and stability. We further establish X-FunBench for cross-embodiment functional grasping, evaluating both physical grasp success and functional intent preservation across hands. Evaluated on X-FunBench, our unified policy, co-trained on four embodiments, achieves 86% average grasp success rate across five unseen hands on novel objects. Deployed on four unseen physical hands (Leap, Inspire, Sharpa, Wuji) for novel object grasping, the policy achieves 80% average success rate without fine-tuning. To our knowledge, FunGrasp-Zero is the first RL-based framework to achieve zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping across unseen hands in both simulation and the real world.

Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning via Epigraph-Form Guided Exploration 2026-11-22
📝 NeurIPS 2026. Updated acknowledgments only
Show abstract

Discovering cooperative behavior in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is challenging due to the combinatorial complexity of joint state-action spaces, which hinders the emergence of coordinated behaviors from trial-and-error alone. Intrinsic rewards are often used to aid discovery, but naively combining them with team objectives can distort the learning signal, compromising task performance. In this paper, we propose EFXPLORER, a constrained exploration framework that maximizes exploration objectives subject to a constraint that preserves established task performance. We solve the resulting problem using an epigraph reformulation that introduces adaptive exploration budgets. This approach separates intrinsic rewards from task objectives and regulates exploration through task feasibility. To further encourage diverse and temporally extended exploration, we incorporate a successor distance-based intrinsic reward that captures long-horizon dependencies. Empirically, our method outperforms strong baselines and induces novel cooperative strategies across SMAX, VMAS, and MPE benchmark suites.

On Adaptive Step Sizes for Eligibility Traces in Deep Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-22
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/esraaelelimy/ada_traces
Show abstract

Many deep reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms store agent-environment interactions in a replay buffer and later learn by sampling transitions from it. However, this approach can be memory-inefficient and can delay learning because the agent can learn from a transition only when the buffer samples it. Additionally, most of these methods use one-step or short multi-step temporal-difference (TD) updates, which are slow at credit assignment. An alternative approach is to learn from the stream of observations as they arrive without relying on a buffer. Eligibility-trace algorithms allow these incremental updates and can also assign credit over many steps. These algorithms were common with linear function approximation but have only recently been used in deep RL. Basic questions, such as how to use vector step sizes with eligibility traces in the deep RL setting, remain open. In this work, we first show that adaptive vector step sizes must be part of the eligibility trace to keep the equivalence between the forward and backward views. We then show that the step sizes of common optimizers, such as Adam, do not have an online backward view. We introduce a new optimizer, Function-Space Distance (FSD), that maintains the forward-backward equivalence. We test four eligibility-trace algorithms covering semi-gradient and gradient TD updates, as well as value-based and actor-critic methods. We show that FSD performs better than SGD or naively applied Adam. The resulting algorithms outperform or match other streaming methods and are comparable to or better than PPO, which uses a buffer, in most environments. Code available at https://github.com/esraaelelimy/ada_traces

Decoupling Perception from Generation for Agentic 3D CT Report Generation 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Chest CT reports repeatedly assess the same anatomical regions for findings and their attributes, such as whether a lung nodule is present and where it is located. These assessments suggest learning to answer visual questions directly, rather than coupling perception to open-ended text generation. Yet current CT report generation models largely follow generic vision-language modeling, which may miss subtle abnormalities when training data are limited. We therefore formulate report generation as a set of visual question-answering decisions coordinated by a large language model (LLM) agent. This decouples visual perception from reasoning and report writing: perception can be learned as a discriminative, non-generative task, while the LLM focuses on reasoning over patient context and posing relevant visual queries. We introduce Rad-VL-JEPA, which unifies report retrieval, finding recognition, and fine-grained attribute characterization through vision-language joint-embedding prediction. Building on this perception module, an agent trained with reinforcement learning (RL) acquires patient-specific visual evidence, while a separate editor incorporates the evidence into the final report, further separating evidence acquisition from report realization. Experiments show that Rad-VL-JEPA achieves strong performance on fine-grained CT understanding tasks with lower amortized per-query inference time than a generative VLM baseline trained with the same CT encoder and VQA data. The resulting reporting system outperforms strong VLM-based and agentic baselines.

ARC: Adaptive Rollout Control for Efficient Synchronous LLM Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Synchronous reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models proceeds through a repeated rollout--barrier--update loop. Each update must wait for a fixed number of complete, group-relative training samples, yet individual response lengths and reward outcomes vary widely and only emerge as generation progresses. Static admission and unconditional completion therefore leave accelerators idle in some steps, and waste computation generating samples that will never contribute to the current update. We present ARC, a closed-loop rollout architecture that governs synchronous RL rollout at group granularity. ARC uses \textit{Calibrated Streaming Backfill} to judge from partial observations whether a group is still worth completing, backfilling a fresh candidate immediately when it is not; \textit{Deferred Group Completion} to park an uncertain early stop and resume it later instead of discarding it outright, bounded by a resume-cycle limit that guarantees termination; and \textit{Adaptive Admission} and \textit{Pipelining} to order admitted samples by early-measured duration and to admit further candidates only while expected supply still falls short of the target number of accepted groups. Under online evaluation, ARC reduces rollout time by $37.4\%$, a $1.60\times$ speedup, and improves effective GRPO throughput by $50.1\%$ over existing baselines, while directly recovering 98.9\% of the training groups that would ultimately have been kept.

VeriLogic: Reinforcement Learning over Verifiable Logical Reasoning Traces 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Logical reasoning derives conclusions from premises through explicit intermediate inference steps, providing a natural basis for process-level verification and supervision. However, existing learning methods often rely on response-level outcome signals, which overlook heterogeneous correctness within the reasoning trace: a correct answer may contain invalid reasoning, while valid intermediate steps may also appear in an incorrect response. To address this issue, we introduce Reinforcement Learning on Verifiable Logical Reasoning (VeriLogic), which turns internal reasoning traces into explicit and verifiable learning objects. Rather than treating a response as an indivisible learning unit, VeriLogic decomposes logical reasoning into explicit intermediate judgments, proofs, and final decisions, and organizes them into a unified "Grounding - Optimization - Refinement" pipeline: (1) Verification-Grounded Reasoning Environment Construction grounds logical reasoning into explicit intermediate states and formally checkable proof obligations, making the internal reasoning process accessible to verification and learning; (2) Logical Structured Policy Optimization learns from verifier feedback through complementary global and local rewards, jointly optimizing the overall reasoning outcome while providing targeted optimization signals to different intermediate reasoning paths; and (3) Verifier-Guided Iterative Refinement acts on verifier feedback by leveraging proof states and error messages to iteratively diagnose and repair failed reasoning. Experiments on six logical reasoning benchmarks show consistent gains over strong baselines, with average improvements of 6.39% and 5.00% on Qwen3 and Llama-3.1. These results highlight the value of making logical reasoning traces verifiable, learnable, and refinable beyond final-answer supervision.

Variance Reduction Based Experience Replay for Policy Optimization 2026-11-22
📝 47 pages, 6 figures. arXiv admin note: text overlap with arXiv:2208.12341
Show abstract

Effective reinforcement learning (RL) for complex stochastic systems requires leveraging historical data to improve sample efficiency and accelerate policy optimization. However, classical experience replay treats all past observations uniformly and fails to account for their varying contributions to learning. To address this limitation, we propose Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER), a principled framework that selectively reuses informative samples to reduce the variance of policy gradient estimates. VRER is algorithm-agnostic and can be integrated with existing policy optimization methods, yielding the sample-efficient off-policy algorithm, Policy Gradient with VRER (PG-VRER). To provide rigorous theoretical guarantees, we develop a novel analysis framework for experience replay that explicitly accounts for dependencies induced by Markovian dynamics and behavior-policy interactions. Using this framework, we establish finite-time convergence guarantees for PG-VRER and characterize a fundamental bias-variance trade-off: reusing older samples reduces gradient variance but may introduce greater estimation bias. Extensive experiments show that VRER consistently accelerates learning and outperforms state-of-the-art policy optimization algorithms

RAVEC: Reward-Aligned Visual Evidence Correction 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards improves multimodal reasoning, but response-level feedback provides limited guidance on which intermediate decisions to correct. Visual interventions reveal sensitivity to image content, yet this sensitivity alone does not establish reward alignment or support across questions. We introduce RAVEC (Reward-Aligned Visual Evidence Correction), which selects visual corrections using local and cross-question reward support and jointly determines their strengths within each response. Paired Visual Evidence Contrast (PVEC) compares predictions from an exponential moving average teacher at the same sampled response prefix under the original image and a version with its annotated evidence region suppressed, yielding signed correction directions. Credit-Risk Allocation (CRA) screens these directions using local reward alignment and reward support from other questions, excluding all responses to the source question. It then solves a response-level budgeted allocation problem, assigning additional cost to directions that locally reduce predictive entropy. The resulting signed visual loss augments Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) during training. Inference uses only the original image and question. Across ten multimodal benchmarks, RAVEC achieves the highest average accuracy among the compared methods with both 3B and 7B Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct models, outperforming GRPO by 2.67 and 1.39 percentage points, respectively. Component ablations support the combined design of evidence alignment, credit qualification, and risk-aware allocation. The project is available at https://yangjj007.github.io/ravec/.

Handover Cost Optimization in LEO Satellite Networks using an RL-based Multiple-Column Selection Strategy via Branch-and-Price 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Next-generation wireless networks increasingly rely on low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites for broad coverage and low latency, making efficient handover management essential. We formulate satellite handover as a combinatorial optimization problem, assigning each ground user to exactly one capacity-limited, visible satellite while minimizing network handover costs. We propose a reinforcement learning (RL)-based branch-and-price (B&P) framework with learned multiple-column selection (MCS) to accelerate column generation while retaining exact dynamic programming pricing. We also develop two lightweight heuristic policies: handover-aware visibility duration (HAVD) for online decisions and congestion-aware predictive (CAP) for offline planning. Additionally, a local-search repair algorithm adapts precomputed B&P or MCS-B&P solutions to demand perturbations through single-flip and per-user trajectory re-optimization, avoiding complete re-optimization. Simulations using realistic large scale constellations, including Starlink Gen-1, demonstrate that MCS-B&P achieves solution quality comparable to exact B&P with fewer column-generation iterations, fewer branch-and-bound nodes, and reduced computation time. HAVD and CAP offer effective online and offline cost-latency trade-offs, while local-search repair recovers most performance losses due to demand perturbations at a fraction of the computational cost of full re-optimization.

Your Diffusion-Based Driving Planner Only Needs a Nudge 2026-11-22
📝 Autonomous driving, Trajectory Planning, Diffusion-based Planners, Reinforcement Learning, RL Post-training, Off-policy RL
Show abstract

Diffusion-based planners capture the multimodality of human driving behavior well, yet covariate shift in closed-loop driving can lead them to generate trajectories that result in collisions or road departures. Reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning offers a promising way to tackle this issue, but applying it to diffusion-based planners is complicated by their iterative denoising process and receding-horizon execution. We find that small adjustments to the planned trajectories can prevent many of these unsafe outcomes. Motivated by this observation, we propose PlanNudge, which preserves the learned planning capabilities of a pretrained diffusion-based planner by freezing it and trains a lightweight policy that we call the nudge policy. At each planning step, the nudge policy outputs a nudge, a two-dimensional adjustment of the speed and curvature of the plan from the frozen planner. The nudge policy is a multilayer perceptron that predicts the nudge in a single forward pass, and both dimensions directly change the motion of the vehicle, so standard off-policy RL can train it in closed-loop simulation. We evaluate PlanNudge on nuPlan, a large-scale autonomous driving benchmark based on real-world driving data. In all six settings, PlanNudge matches or exceeds the final scores of the previous state-of-the-art RL finetuning method while using about one sixth as many environment steps and less than one fifth as much model compute. We also provide guidelines for reward shaping, value learning, and exploration when using off-policy RL to post-train autonomous driving planners. Our results are reported with means and standard deviations over six training runs. Project page: https://zerlinwang.github.io/plannudge/

Calibrating LLMs with Semantic-level Reward 2026-11-22
Show abstract

As large language models (LLMs) are deployed in consequential settings such as medical question answering and legal reasoning, the ability to estimate when their outputs are likely to be correct is essential for safe and reliable use, requiring well-calibrated uncertainty. Standard reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains models with a binary correctness reward that is indifferent to confidence, providing no penalty for confident but wrong predictions and thereby degrading calibration. Recent work addresses this by training models to produce verbalized confidence scores alongside answers and rewarding agreement with correctness. However, verbalized confidence is calibrated at the token level and thus exhibits inconsistency across textual variations with same semantic meaning. We propose \textbf{Calibration with Semantic Reward (CSR)}, a framework that calibrates language models directly in semantic space without a verbalized confidence interface. CSR combines the correctness reward with a novel semantic calibration reward that encourages exploitation among correct rollouts by promoting semantic agreement, and exploration among incorrect ones by discouraging spurious consistency. Experiments across three model families on HotpotQA (in-distribution) and TriviaQA, MSMARCO, and NQ-Open (out-of-distribution) show that CSR consistently achieves lower ECE and higher AUROC than verbalized-confidence baselines across nearly all settings, reducing ECE by up to $40\%$ and improving AUROC by up to $31\%$ over verbalized-confidence baselines, with calibration behavior generalizing robustly across all four evaluation settings.

This paper applies the authors' recent results on asynchronous stochastic approximation (SA) in the Borkar-Meyn framework to reinforcement learning in average-reward semi-Markov decision processes (SMDPs). We establish the convergence of an asynchronous SA analogue of Schweitzer's classical relative value iteration algorithm, RVI Q-learning, for finite-space, weakly communicating SMDPs. Population-Based Post-Training for Large Language Models 2026-11-21
📝 31 pages, 10 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is one of the most popular post-training paradigms for large language models. RLVR typically trains a single model under hyperparameters chosen before training begins, while researchers tune hyperparameters across consecutive runs. We introduce Population-Based Post-Training (PBPT), which trains several models in parallel and lets the training process do the tuning through population-based training. PBPT periodically evaluates models on a validation set. High-performers are duplicated and their hyperparameters perturbed to explore new configurations. Low-performers stop training and are replaced by these copies. On reasoning benchmarks (Enigmata, Nemotron) and interactive tool-use environments (Gaia2, AppWorld), PBPT produces stronger agents than compute-matched groups of independent RLVR learners. Furthermore, PBPT is able to recover from poor initial hyperparameters, automatically discovering better settings over the course of training.

Learning optimal gaits and styles for terrain-adaptive quadruped locomotion 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Animals use many different gaits to locomote at different speeds across varying terrains. A longstanding question in biology and robotics is: which gait is best? This question is most often addressed from an energy-efficiency perspective on flat terrain, leaving open how the answer depends on terrain and on the criterion optimized. Here we show that gait optimality is determined jointly by terrain, performance metric, and velocity. We use deep reinforcement learning and networks of abstract oscillators, modeling spinal central pattern generators, to learn 11 quadrupedal gaits across diverse challenging terrains, together with the locomotion style (base height, foot ground clearance, foot offsets) that each requires. A second policy then selects among them to optimize a user-specified criterion such as cost of transport, base stability, or joint smoothness. Across these performance metrics and within this repertoire, every gait is optimal under at least one terrain-metric-velocity combination. The framework adapts zero-shot to gait couplings and styles never seen during training, to terrains far beyond the training distribution, and to larger quadrupeds five times the training mass. With a single additional pitch-tracking reward term, the same gait couplings also support bipedal-mode locomotion. Our method provides a general and systematic way to produce expressive locomotion and to select the gait best suited to a given terrain and criterion. Skill-augmented reinforcement learning enables LLM agents to reuse task-solving knowledge across episodes, but existing systems typically expose agents to a flat skill pool and update skills only from coarse, episode-level outcomes. Memory-augmented LLM agents typically answer queries by retrieving relevant memories and feeding them directly to an answer model.
