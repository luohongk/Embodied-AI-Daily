ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Safety alignment has enabled large language models to reliably refuse harmful requests directly, yet the same request distributed across a multi-turn conversation still succeeds far too often. This brittleness has a mechanistic root: Alignment is shallow, with safety behavior concentrated in a model's first few output tokens, and existing defenses inherit the weakness, supervising the surface form of the response without ever giving the model an explicit safety decision to learn. We take the opposite view of shallowness: if a short span determines the trajectory of an entire response, that span is the highest-leverage place to intervene, and the defender can occupy it first. We propose Assess $\to$ Decide $\to$ Respond, a framework in which the defender assesses the user's intent, commits to an explicit, parseable safety decision, and only then generates a reply conditioned on it, with the commitment trained by reinforcement learning at the payload turn. Because the decision is a token span, the reward can be a rule: a self-verified reward checks the committed label against the conversation's known nature, requiring no judge and no annotation, while the same format also supports an LLM-as-judge reward over a graded decision space with safe redirection. Across two model families and fourteen benchmarks spanning safety, utility, and helpfulness, our defenders achieve the strongest multi-turn safety in a controlled comparison, maintaining their lead in safety while preserving utility at a modest cost to helpfulness. A per-token KL analysis and interventional probes confirm why this works: training concentrates its effect on the brief deliberation that sets a response's trajectory, and the decision committed there is a causal control point. The very shallowness attackers exploit becomes the mechanism of defense.

One to More, More to One: Category-Aware Iterative Expert Training for Software Engineering Agents 2026-10-29
📝 47 pages, 12 figures. v2: added evaluations on SWE-bench Pro (731 tasks), SWE-bench Pro V2/Hard-51, and LiveCodeBench v6 (175 tasks); updated SWE-bench Multilingual results; added 35B-A3B student experiments. Code: https://github.com/alibaba/AgenticBigBang
Show abstract

Repository-level software engineering (SWE) comprises heterogeneous task categories, whose progress under pooled agentic reinforcement learning can be uneven: gains in some categories coincide with regressions in others, while aggregate resolution obscures these changes. Motivated by this category see-saw, we develop a category-aware expert-training and policy-integration framework. Executable task construction and SWE Labeler, an evidence-grounded multi-axis labeling system, organize the training pools. Initial category-specific RL improves average training success while leaving uneven instance-level progress, motivating explicit consolidation of successful behavior and policy-adaptive task selection. Same-origin category experts alternate long-horizon Agentic-miniRL with Refresh-Repair-Expand (RRE): the updated policy refreshes instance mastery, reuses its own verified successful trajectories for Repair SFT, and reselects tasks for further RL. Label-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates the experts into one deployable student, with ReLU-gated reward extrapolation keeping only each teacher's improving direction over the reference. Expert training and policy integration require no external model to provide solution trajectories or action targets. On a fixed, audit-filtered Pro-618 subset, we connect Pooled RL and Balanced RL, expert development, and single-model integration through overall and per-category resolution and expert-gain recovery. In final-model evaluations, MOPD achieves mean resolution of 86.19% on SWE-bench Pro V2 and 71.78% on SWE-bench Multilingual, exceeding the base model by 4.57 and 3.89 percentage points, respectively. The same three 27B experts also improve a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B student across the evaluated task sets.

VeriCUA: Verification as Agentic Exploration for Computer-Using Agents 2026-10-29
📝 Work in progress
Show abstract

Building and scaling computer-using agents (CUAs) across desktop, mobile, and web environments requires reliable task-completion verification for data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning. Typical automated approaches prompt vision-language models (VLMs) to judge trajectories or generate programs to inspect application state. However, both rely on partial views of complex digital environments. VLM judges are restricted to supplied observations, while generated programs depend on accessible state. These views can omit decisive evidence or leave completion judgments unsupported. Tailoring separate verifiers to different platforms and access settings adds further engineering cost. We present VeriCUA, a platform-agnostic framework for verification through agentic exploration. It treats verification as dynamic evidence gathering and assessment within a shared procedure for online and offline settings across platforms and observation modalities. After inspecting the available context to construct a task rubric, the verifier selects GUI or code checks for unresolved requirements and combines their findings under explicit evidence rules. Experiments show improved agreement with reference labels over the compared baselines across these settings, with the largest offline gains on hard cases and stronger rejection of false completion claims online. Further analyses connect these gains to complementary evidence and test-time exploration.

PIVOT: Progressive Intervention via Online Teacher Correction 2026-10-29
📝 19 pages, 9 figures, 6 tables. Project page: https://zhihaoyang30.github.io/PIVOT/
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) is limited by sparse outcome-level feedback on hard reasoning tasks: when all rollouts in a group fail, rewards provide no learning signal. Stronger teachers can provide additional guidance, but this raises a central question: how can such signals be incorporated into RLVR to support successful rollouts while remaining learnable for the student? We distinguish two complementary mechanisms: trajectory-level intervention, which improves access to reward-bearing trajectories, and token-level supervision, which provides dense targets to guide student learning. Our controlled experiments show that either mechanism alone can remain insufficient, motivating their combination. We therefore introduce PIVOT, which inserts teacher-generated corrections into student rollouts and applies token-level KL supervision to these segments alongside the RLVR objective. Beyond their combination, we systematically analyze where to intervene and how to supervise, yielding a recipe for effective guidance. Across hard logic games and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, PIVOT consistently outperforms existing methods, nearly doubling mean avg@8 on logic games from 19.5% for the best-performing baseline to 35.6%.

Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm 2026-10-29
📝 67 pages, 31 figures, 22 tables
Show abstract

Foundation models are typically trained in stages: pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), with changes in both data and learning algorithms. We show that this algorithmic separation is not fundamental. By deriving suitable RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions, we place all of these stages under a single likelihood-based training algorithm, unified training. This perspective turns the foundation-model training lifecycle into a problem of choosing which data to train on, in what proportions, and when, with conventional staged training as a special case. We instantiate this framework in a unified training system that mixes static data and RL generations within a single trainer. Across controlled and real-world experiments, unified training improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities, and provides a stronger starting point for continual learning. This work lays the groundwork for a unified science of foundation-model training, connecting mathematical principles, training infrastructure, and the development of model capabilities.

Understanding and Enhancing Backdoor Persistency in LLM Agent Post-Training 2026-10-29
📝 EMNLP 2026 Findings
Show abstract

Developers can build LLM agents by adapting third-party models through benign post-training. We study a supply-chain threat in which an attacker supplies a model with a backdoor: hidden behavior that produces malicious outputs when a particular input pattern appears. Focusing on software-engineering agents, we ask whether such backdoors survive the developer's supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and subsequent task-level reinforcement learning (RL). We observe that benign SFT substantially reduces attack success, but subsequent RL often preserves the residual behavior and sometimes even increases attack success. Our analysis of backdoor erosion during SFT identifies two factors that may favor survival: initial backdoor strength and gradient compatibility with benign training. These factors motivate PersistBD, which refines an already-backdoored model before release to improve its persistency through the benign post-training process. On Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, PersistBD raises attack success from 20% to 74% after SFT and from 20% to 76% after SFT-RL, while maintaining comparable benign task performance. Together, our results show that backdoors can remain active through benign post-training and that adversaries can deliberately increase their persistence. This highlights a supply-chain risk for AI developers and motivates stronger techniques for detecting and mitigating inherited backdoors when adapting third-party models into agents. Our code is available at https://github.com/uiuc-kang-lab/PersistBD.

Towards Batch-to-Streaming Deep Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Control 2026-10-29
Show abstract

State-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning (RL) methods have achieved remarkable performance in continuous control tasks, yet their computational complexity is often incompatible with the constraints of resource-limited hardware, due to their reliance on replay buffers, batch updates, and target networks. The emerging paradigm of streaming deep RL addresses this limitation through purely online updates, achieving strong empirical performance on standard benchmarks. In this work, we propose two novel streaming deep RL algorithms, Streaming Soft Actor-Critic (S2AC) and Streaming Deterministic Actor-Critic (SDAC), explicitly designed to be compatible with state-of-the-art batch RL methods, making them particularly suitable for on-device finetuning applications such as Sim2Real transfer. Both algorithms achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art streaming baselines on standard benchmarks without requiring tedious per-environment hyperparameter tuning. We further investigate the batch-to-streaming transition, showing that a naive transition does not guarantee preservation of pre-trained policy performance, and propose a principled approach to address this challenge.

Training language models in simulation for psychiatric intake 2026-10-29
📝 76 pages, 8 figures, 15 tables (27 pages main text, 7 main figures, 2 main tables)
Show abstract

In psychiatric practice, the clinical intake interview remains a primary diagnostic instrument, requiring the clinician to establish rapport while disentangling complex, overlapping presentations. Here, we show that multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation environments improves language model performance on structured text-based psychiatric intake, spanning history-taking, differential diagnosis, and initial management planning. In a randomized, double-blind crossover study ($N = 60$ matched scenario pairs, 120 encounters) comparing the RL-trained agent against its identically prompted base model, 32 board-certified psychiatrists role-played standardized patients while a separate panel of 15 independent, board-certified psychiatrists scored the de-identified transcripts. The trained agent outperformed the base model in 76.7% of matched scenario pairs ($p < 0.0001$), with gains on an adapted clinical-examination rubric ($d_z = 0.77$, $p < 0.0001$), patient-centered communication (82.0% vs. 65.2%, $p < 0.001$), and differential diagnosis appropriateness (68.3% vs. 33.3%, $p < 0.001$). Across 127 psychiatrist-validated simulated intake scenarios, the trained agent nearly doubled its base model's clinical rubric score (0.706 vs. 0.379; composite reward 0.564 vs. 0.294, $p < 0.0001$). To systematically probe conversational safety, we established two Delphi-consensus taxonomies, of clinical safety risks and relational competencies, from which 256 challenging adversarial presentations were derived; we find that training in simulation environments more than halved under-triage of acute safety risks (12.9% vs. 30.5%, $p < 0.0001$). Simulation environments thus provide a controlled testbed for training and stress-testing psychiatric conversational agents, with gains that transferred to psychiatrist-enacted encounters, though residual gaps require safeguards and clinician oversight.

Reinforcement learning for control of partial differential equations 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Controlling dynamical systems governed by partial differential equations (PDEs) remains a challenging task in scientific computing, owing to high-dimensional state spaces, distributed actuation, nonlinear dynamics, and stringent real-time constraints. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for synthesizing feedback controllers that adapt to varying operating conditions and can be deployed efficiently in real-time. This review provides a unified perspective on RL and PDE-constrained optimization, and surveys model-free, model-based, and multi-agent RL approaches to PDE control, with particular emphasis on sample efficiency, generalization across parametric variations, and scalability to high-dimensional problems. Two demonstrative examples, the stabilization of a chaotic system and a flow control problem, illustrate the performance and pitfalls of RL agents in practice. Finally, we discuss evaluation and benchmarking practices, current limitations, and open research directions toward reliable, data-efficient, and scalable RL for scientific computing.

Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis: integrating reinforcement learning and conformal prediction for reliable wafer defect classification 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Semiconductor manufacturing is a complex, multi-step process that requires both high precision and low operating costs. Therefore, detecting defects in a high-volume setting is an important component of wafer map analysis for reliable production. However, most state-of-the-art deep learning approaches require a full scan of the surface, which hinders the development of high-throughput inspection systems. To address this problem, we propose Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis (UFLD), a novel algorithm that employs reinforcement learning for adaptive measurement acquisition and provides a prediction uncertainty level after each step, stopping the process once sufficient information is obtained. BR-MoE: Budgeted Semantic Release for Privacy-Preserving Traffic Analytics 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Traffic cameras support useful analysis, but their outputs can reveal private information. Replacing images with text reduces direct visual exposure. It does not place a formal bound on disclosure. The published reinforcement learning and mixture-of-experts framework refines scene descriptions through relevance, coverage, and brevity rewards. It does not account for repeated releases under a fixed privacy budget. This paper proposes budgeted semantic release as an extension of that framework. Four scene experts first process isolated video chunks. Their outputs are mapped to a fixed, bounded semantic schema. A reinforcement learning controller selects which expert summaries to release and how much privacy budget to spend. The controller uses public task requests and previous private releases. A separate privacy filter enforces the total budget. Calibrated noise is added before the summaries are converted to text. Under explicit chunk isolation and bounded-event assumptions, the complete release transcript satisfies an event-level differential privacy bound. The design also supports reuse of previous summaries without new disclosure. An evaluation protocol covers traffic utility, privacy attacks, release freshness, and computational cost. Illustrative tables show the intended reporting format. They do not establish empirical gains. The proposed contribution is a task-aware release interface with auditable privacy accounting for repeated traffic descriptions.

CALM: Decentralized Control of Cooperative Aerial Manipulation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-29
📝 8 pages
Show abstract

Cooperative aerial manipulation is difficult under wind, payload motion, and noisy goal broadcasts because multiple Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are coupled through a shared payload. We present CALM (Cooperative Aerial Learned Manipulation), a decentralized controller for aerial manipulators carrying a basket while tracking a moving ground robot. CALM combines a parameter-shared Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) policy, trained using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), with goal estimation, per-UAV Extended State Observers (ESOs), basket-mass estimation, and anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward. A disturbance-conditioned critic supports centralized training while execution remains decentralized. SCARL: Sequence Confidence-Aligned Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) improves language-model reasoning by scoring sampled responses with a rule-based verifier and reinforcing those that pass. Where a classifier's training couples predicted probability to labels, nothing ties an RLVR policy's confidence in a response to its reward: aligned training would drive responses with the same advantage toward equal confidence, yet the advantage is blind to confidence. Under this sequence-confidence misalignment, a success the policy barely found earns the same update as one it produces with near certainty, and a confident error is corrected no harder than an exploratory one. We propose SCARL (Sequence Confidence-Aligned Reinforcement Learning), which reshapes, but never re-signs, each advantage with the alignment gap between the response's group-relative confidence and the outcome its advantage certifies, normalized into shares of each class's advantage budget. Misaligned samples absorb the force, responses with the same advantage are driven toward equal confidence, and repairing the misalignment doubles as a remedy for the entropy collapse that erodes exploration. SCARL provably reduces to its base estimator when a class's confidences agree, adds no extra passes and one hyperparameter. On Qwen3-4B-Base it lifts average Pass@32 by 2.4 points over DAPO and is best among nine compute-matched methods on both coverage and per-draw accuracy; applied unchanged across four estimators and four backbones, it improves 14 of 16 settings while preserving policy entropy and widening the confidence-correctness separation. Our code is available at https://github.com/kezhh163/scarl.

Low-Rank Adaptation for Critic Learning in Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-29
📝 2026 Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)
Show abstract

Scaling critic capacity is a promising direction for improving off-policy reinforcement learning (RL). However, recent work shows that larger critics are prone to overfitting and instability in replay-based bootstrapped training. In this paper, we propose using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) as a structural regularizer for critic learning from scratch. Our approach freezes randomly initialized base matrices and optimizes only the corresponding low-rank adapters, thereby constraining the learned weight changes to low-rank residuals. We evaluate our method on state-based continuous-control tasks, using SAC with SimbaV2 and BRC on DMC-Hard, and FastTD3 with its SimbaV2 variant on IsaacLab. Empirically, LoRA reduces critic loss during training and improves overall policy performance, achieving the best or competitive results on most tasks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our low-rank updates provide a simple and effective form of structural regularization for critic learning in off-policy RL.

Robust Instruction Compliance in Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-29
📝 NeurIPS 2026 Atlanta Poster
Show abstract

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in real-world use cases may need to adapt to external natural language instructions that interrupt ongoing behavior and conflict with long-horizon objectives. However, conditioning rewards on instructions introduces a fundamental failure mode as Bellman updates couple value estimates across instruction contexts, leading to inconsistent values when instructions interrupt macro-actions. We propose Macro-Action Value Correction for Instruction Compliance (MAVIC), which corrects Bellman backups at instruction boundaries by correcting the incoming instruction objective and restoring the continuation value under the current objective. Unlike reward shaping, MAVIC modifies the bootstrapping target itself, enabling consistent value estimation under stochastic instruction switching within a unified policy. We provide theoretical analysis and an actor-critic implementation, and show that MAVIC achieves high instruction compliance while preserving base task performance in increasingly complex cooperative multi-agent environments.

Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning with General Utilities via Neural Dual Critics 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with general utilities optimizes nonlinear functionals of policy-induced occupancy measures. We develop a natural actor-critic method for concave RLGU objectives using an exact variational dual representation, whose existence follows from concavity and differentiability, with explicit constructions for a broad class of separable occupancy utilities. Rather than estimating the occupancy measure and then forming the policy-gradient pseudo-reward, our method learns this pseudo-reward directly as a function of the state-action pair using a neural critic. The critic is trained by projected stochastic updates, while multilevel Monte Carlo combines outputs across training horizons to control its bias at logarithmic expected cost. Our analysis separates conditional actor bias from estimator second moments and accounts explicitly for dual optimization, critic approximation, and neural linearization errors. Under policy-transfer, dual-stability, and local critic-regularity conditions, the method achieves $\widetilde{O}(T^{-1/2})$ global convergence above policy, critic-class, and finite-width approximation errors using $\widetilde{O}(T)$ expected environment transitions and reference samples. For a fixed critic representation, this yields $\widetilde{O}(ε^{-2})$ sampling complexity above the approximation floor, covering infinite state-action spaces and occupancy utilities beyond fixed finite vectors of returns.

From MS/MS evidence to adaptive spectroscopic characterization: Reinforcement learning guided by candidate molecular structures 2026-10-29
📝 17 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables. Supporting information: 10 pages, 3 figures and 7 tables, provided as an ancillary PDF
Show abstract

Small-molecule identification from tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) may require complementary measurements. Herein, we propose a structure-informed deep Q-network (SI-DQN) that uses existing MS/MS evidence to decide whether further characterization is needed, which measurement to request, and when to stop. The policy combines spectral representations and candidate rankings with score-weighted descriptors of the candidates' chemical features and structural differences. The policy was evaluated on 79,462 held-out samples with simulated MS/MS, infrared (IR), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra from the Multimodal Spectroscopic Dataset. Evaluation used fixed hard candidate sets containing the correct structure and equal normalized acquisition costs. Averaged across three training runs, SI-DQN increased Top-1 accuracy from 80.10% to 93.78% for positive-ion MS/MS starts and from 70.44% to 93.31% for negative-ion starts. The corresponding mean additional measurement counts were only 0.227 and 0.340 per sample, respectively. Complete acquisition achieved 97.72% accuracy with four additions, whereas SI-DQN reduced the additional measurement count by 94.3% and 91.5%. Relative to a rule-based policy, SI-DQN reduced the composite loss combining terminal ranking error and acquisition cost by 19.3% and 25.5%. Adding structural information to DQN further improved accuracy while reducing requests across all three training runs. At comparable ranking uncertainty, SI-DQN increased request rates relative to DQN more strongly in groups with high structural disagreement, where larger reductions in composite loss were also observed. Together, these results support using the ambiguity among candidate structures after MS/MS analysis to guide further characterization, providing a quantitative basis for balancing identification performance against subsequent measurement effort.

The greater mass, dimensions and intricate dynamics increase the requirements for cooperation from surrounding traffic in critical maneuvers, requiring a careful balance between safety constraints and efficient long-horizon decision making. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides rigorous constraint satisfaction but relies on terminal cost which can limit its effectiveness at short, computationally affordable horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) captures long-term driving behavior but offers no constraint guarantees and tends toward overly conservative policies when trained under sparse collision penalties. Show abstract

Computer-use agents built on large language models (LLMs) work in software that can change while they act: an appointment moves, a policy is revised, a record is updated. Testing agents under such changes is harder than it looks. When an agent fails, the cause may be the agent, but it may also be a change that did not fire as intended, an update the agent never received, or a grader that scored the episode wrongly. We introduce EnvOS, a framework for building and certifying environments that change mid-task. Evaluation Metrics for Safe Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Safe reinforcement learning (RL) is commonly formalized as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), in which an agent maximizes expected reward while keeping its expected cumulative cost below a specified safety bound. Existing safe RL benchmarks predominantly report whether an algorithm is safe on average, following this expectation-based guarantee. We argue that this convention is insufficient to reliably characterize an algorithm's true safety: it fails to capture how often and how severely the safety bound is violated, whether this holds consistently across tasks and safety bounds, and whether training-time behavior is representative of behavior of the final converged policy. Therefore, we introduce (i) evaluation metrics for safe RL that address each of these concerns and in addition allow for aggregation across tasks and safety bounds. We furthermore define (ii) a safety tier system to systematically categorize and compare algorithms in terms of safety and reliability at both training and for a final policy. Using this framework, we provide (iii) an empirical safety evaluation across multiple safety navigation tasks.
