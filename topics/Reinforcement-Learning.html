Discovery of Diverse and Realistic Financial Tail-Risk Using Generative Flow Networks 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Accurate modeling of rare, high-impact financial events is essential for robust risk management. Sampling-based techniques such as Sequential Markov Chain Monte Carlo (SMCMC) generate diverse scenarios but inefficiently explore high-risk regions, while Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) converges on narrow sets of scenarios, limiting diversity. We present GRID, which uses Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) -- samplers that draw objects in proportion to a reward -- to systematically generate diverse and realistic financial tail-risk scenarios. Building on continuous GFlowNets, GRID introduces a flexible mixture-policy architecture with variable-specific distribution families for high-dimensional, sequential macro-financial data, together with plausibility constraints that keep generated trajectories realistic. A central practical advantage is robustness to reward-proxy imperfection: because GFlowNets sample proportionally rather than maximize, they remain broadly exploratory even when the oracle's out-of-sample accuracy is low -- an unavoidable reality in financial forecasting. On three real-world crises, GRID improves scenario diversity, realism, and tail-risk coverage over RL and MCMC baselines, and ablations over oracle architectures and distribution families yield practical insights into continuous GFlowNet behavior under noisy rewards.

Discovering Jailbreaks via Curriculum of Models 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Automated jailbreak discovery aims to find prompts that elicit prohibited behaviors from safety-trained language models, helping developers identify vulnerabilities before deployment. One class of approaches uses reinforcement learning to train an investigator model to generate prompts that elicit a specified behavior from a target model. However, training directly against a safety-trained target is difficult because nearly all prompts are refused, leaving the investigator with a sparse reward signal. Prior work addresses this with the propensity bound (PRBO), a proxy reward estimated by sampling responses from a more compliant target, and then adjusting for how likely those responses would have been under the original target. We introduce Curriculum of Models (CoM), an alternative method that trains the investigator against a sequence of modified versions of the target, progressing gradually from a version that readily exhibits the specified behavior to the original target. For jailbreak discovery, training begins against a target version that rarely refuses harmful requests, and advances to the next, harder version once the investigator reliably elicits the behavior from the current one. We evaluate CoM on elicitation tasks involving CBRN materials, explosives, and illicit drugs, using two open-weight targets, gemma-4-31b-it and gpt-oss-120b. We compare against PRBO and direct training on the original target without a curriculum. When the investigator is trained to elicit a single behavior, CoM finds successful jailbreaks more reliably than either baseline. When trained on multiple behaviors simultaneously, the CoM-trained investigator can elicit held-out behaviors, though PRBO-trained investigators achieve higher success rates. Our results suggest that a simple curriculum over target models offers a promising approach for addressing sparse rewards in rare-behavior elicitation.

DUET: Optimize Token-Budget Allocation for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards 2026-10-28
📝 The Fortieth Annual Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) generates hundreds of thousands of tokens per training step, with rollout generation dominating the computational cost. The overall token budget can be controlled along two main dimensions: (i) deciding which prompts to allocate rollouts to, and (ii) deciding how long each rollout should be. Prior work has generally controlled only one of these dimensions at a time. We show that jointly tuning both decisions under a shared compute budget improves both reasoning quality and wall-clock training time. We instantiate this view as \textbf{DU}al-controlled tok\textbf{E}n alloca\textbf{T}ion (DUET), a computationally efficient layer over GRPO that uses a lightweight pre-rollout surrogate of prompt informativeness to set how many rollouts each prompt receives, and a marker-gated abort rule with importance reweighting to set when to stop them. On Qwen3-1.7B trained on MATH, DUET outperforms full-budget GRPO and the other three budget-aware baseline methods. DUET's advantage further generalizes to other benchmarks across math and coding, and is on par with the best baseline on the scientific Q\&A domain, while also achieving a $1.62\times$ wall-clock speedup. More notably, using only 50\% of the token budget, DUET still outperforms all baseline methods at their full budget, achieving an even higher $2.51\times$ speedup over full-budget GRPO. We verify the high performance of DUET on other backbone LLMs, including Qwen3-4B to 14B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct. Notably, the gap between DUET and the strongest baseline \emph{widens} as the budget tightens, contrary to the usual pattern in which efficient methods trade off quality as compute decreases. More broadly, these results suggest that DUET budget-aware control strategies are valuable not only for accelerating training, but also for improving the quality of the learning signal.

Skillful and realistic precipitation forecasting with reinforced diffusion 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Accurate precipitation forecasting remains elusive as physics-based models struggle to resolve mesoscale convection and machine learning often yields physically inconsistent, blurred results that consistently underestimate extreme events. We present FitzRoy, the first system that simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art predictive skill and physical realism over the continental United States. Through a reinforced diffusion framework, where a conditional discrete diffusion model is refined via reinforcement learning, FitzRoy effectively resolves the long-standing trade-off between pointwise accuracy and structural fidelity. Across lead times up to 24 hours, FitzRoy consistently matches or exceeds state-of-the-art models while preserving structural realism and capturing extreme events. Its low-latency inference further enables deployment in real-time operational settings. In case studies such as the 2025 Texas Hill Country floods and the 2026 Missouri floods, FitzRoy signals record-breaking accumulations that existing models largely missed. By bridging the gap between realism and skill, FitzRoy establishes a new frontier for high-fidelity precipitation forecasting.

Where to Spend Rollouts: Hit-Utility Optimal Rollout Allocation for Group-Based RLVR 2026-10-28
📝 25 pages, 10 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a central paradigm for improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models. Group-based policy optimization methods, such as GRPO, typically allocate a fixed number of rollouts to every prompt. This uniform allocation can be inefficient: it over-allocates compute to prompts whose sampled groups are already saturated while under-exploring prompts for which additional samples may reveal useful correct trajectories. To address this limitation, we introduce hit utility, the posterior probability that at least one rollout in a proposed additional allocation for a prompt will be correct. Building on this notion, we propose Hit-Utility Optimal Rollout Allocation (HORA), a learning-free rollout allocation policy that maximizes total posterior hit utility within each allocation batch. HORA adaptively reallocates rollout budgets while leaving the downstream reward evaluation and group-based advantage estimator unchanged. Across two training sets, four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, and three model families at multiple scales, HORA preserves comparable Pass@1 and improves Pass@K over compute-matched GRPO, and it outperforms related allocation baselines at large K. It is also drop-in compatible with other group-based estimators such as RLOO. Ablation studies indicate that the uniform prior used by HORA is competitive with five prompt-conditioned learned-prior alternatives.

Search-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Component Query Understanding in Roblox Game Search 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Query understanding (QU) in production search systems has typically relied on separate models for each subtask, such as search intent classification, attribute extraction, and query rewriting. Large language models (LLMs) now make it possible to consolidate these subtasks into a single model that emits a unified QU output. This consolidation, however, introduces a new optimization challenge. A single model serves multiple components, each with a distinct downstream role within the search pipeline, and the utility of each depends on how the underlying search engine acts on it rather than on agreement with static labels. We present a search-aware reinforcement learning (RL) framework for multi-component QU based on a distill-then-RL paradigm. Teacher-student supervised fine-tuning (SFT) first yields a schema-compliant small policy. The RL stage then starts from this policy, executes each QU plan on the live production search engine, and optimizes every component with a reward tailored to its operational role, rather than relying on a single reward tied to the final search outcome. On a large-scale user-generated game platform, this component-specific optimization raises NDCG@20 by 8.9 points over the SFT policy and by 3.5 points over a single end-to-end reward. The resulting small, low-latency student models also surpass their much larger teacher, and our final model is being incrementally deployed in production.

RuleForge: Inferring and Instantiating Latent Visual Rules as Executable Programs 2026-10-28
📝 13 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Visual rule reasoning asks whether multimodal models can recover an abstract concept from a few examples. Existing studies either infer rules from visual examples but evaluate only classification or verbalization, or supervise executable generation with rules provided as input. Consequently, the relationship between visual rule understanding and reliable rule instantiation remains insufficiently explored. We present Bongard-Forge100k, a controlled benchmark, together with RuleForge, a backbone-agnostic framework that jointly learns visual rule understanding and rule-conditioned executable generation. The benchmark contains 108,797 problems: 86,000 for training, 19,297 for testing familiar rules, and 3,500 for testing held-out rule compositions. The framework progresses through rule-understanding training, joint understanding and scene-program supervision, and refinement via execution-grounded reinforcement learning with Understanding Replay. Executing generated programs under multiple random seeds provides verifiable feedback on executability, rule satisfaction, diversity, and robustness. With UniLIP as the primary backbone, RuleForge improves multiple-choice accuracy over the base model by 20.93 percentage points on familiar rules and 28.42 percentage points on held-out rule compositions, while increasing scene-level rule satisfaction on familiar rules from 2.91% to 70.99%. Additional experiments on InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL, and Show-o2 demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed training data and framework across diverse multimodal architectures.

Value Function-Guided MPPI for Hierarchical Planning and Control in Off-Road Autonomous Navigation 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Autonomous navigation in off-road terrain is commonly achieved using hierarchical control and planning architectures that rely on explicit cost maps and one-way information flow from a high-level planner to a low-level controller, decoupling planning from the true execution cost experienced during control. We propose a hierarchical planning and control framework in which information flows bidirectionally, allowing the low-level controller to guide trajectory selection at the planning level. The highest level of the hierarchy is a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control-style planner that determines optimal trajectories and passes these waypoints to a pretrained low-level reinforcement learning (RL) controller. Rather than drawing from an explicit cost map, our MPPI planner leverages the value function of the RL controller as an implicit cost representation. In this formulation, MPPI serves as a trajectory exploration mechanism, while the low-level controller's value function guides the search. To account for the distribution shift seen by the value function between training and evaluation, we propose an online policy evaluation process that adapts the value function to unseen driving experiences, and we demonstrate this adaptation in unseen off-road simulation environments. We evaluate our approach in a high-fidelity off-road driving simulator across a range of challenging terrain conditions and compare its performance against a baseline MPPI controller. The results demonstrate that our method achieves safer and more effective navigation to goal locations while eliminating the need for explicit cost maps and enabling tighter coupling between planning and control.

Latent Decision Flow Rewards for Clinical Guideline Reasoning 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Clinical practice guidelines encode evidence-based recommendations as conditional decision flows, which makes them a natural source of process supervision for training reasoning models. Two properties of clinical reasoning make this supervision difficult to use. A decision flow consistent with a guideline does not by itself determine the correct answer for a given patient, which we call the path--answer gap, and several valid decision flows can support the same clinical decision, which we call path multiplicity. Current training approaches address parts of this problem. Answer rewards supervise the final decision, and process rewards computed against a reference flow supervise one valid decision flow per case. Supervised fine-tuning on annotated flows teaches guideline-consistent reasoning but constrains the model to one trajectory per case and can reduce answer accuracy. We introduce GUIDER, a method that jointly optimizes a decision flow controller and a reasoning policy. The controller learns to generate plausible decision flows from guideline-derived supervision, and the policy learns to generate a decision flow and a final answer for each patient case. GUIDER computes the process reward for the policy over several controller-generated flows, which accounts for path multiplicity, and gates this reward on answer correctness, which addresses the path--answer gap. Joint training allows the process supervision to evolve with the policy. On MedGUIDE, GUIDER with Qwen3-14B achieves 82.5 weighted accuracy and 73.5 guideline adherence, 3.3 and 3.1 points above the strongest clinical reinforcement learning baseline. With Qwen3-14B and Gemma4-12B backbones, GUIDER also improves over CRPO, MRPO, and FaithMed on the out-of-domain benchmarks AMEGA-LLM, RealMedQA, and CancerGUIDE.

Learning unified control of intrinsic nonlinear spin dynamics in atomic qudits for magnetometry 2026-10-28
📝 (6.5+3.5+4) pages, (4+4) figures
Show abstract

Generating and preserving metrologically useful quantum states is a central challenge in quantum-enhanced metrology. In low-field atomic magnetometry with multilevel atoms, the nonlinear Zeeman (NLZ) effect is both a resource and a limitation. It can generate internal spin squeezing within a single atomic qudit, but under fixed readout it also rotates and distorts the measurement-relevant quadrature, limiting the usable metrological gain. The problem is further complicated by the time dependence of both the squeezing axis and the nonlinear evolution itself. Here we show that reinforcement learning can transform NLZ dynamics from a source of readout degradation into a sustained metrological resource. Using only experimentally accessible low-order spin moments, a trained agent identifies a unified control policy for this class of intrinsically nonlinear sensing dynamics. We illustrate the approach in the \(f=21/2\) manifold of \(^{161}\mathrm{Dy}\), where the learned policy rapidly prepares strongly squeezed internal states and stabilizes more than \(4\,\mathrm{dB}\) of fixed-axis spin squeezing under continuous NLZ evolution. Including state-preparation overhead, the learned protocol yields a single-atom magnetic-field sensitivity of \(13.9\,\mathrm{pT}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}\), approximately \(3\,\mathrm{dB}\) beyond the standard quantum limit. Our results establish learning-based control as an experimentally feasible route for converting unavoidable intrinsic nonlinear dynamics in multilevel atomic sensors into operational metrological advantage.

Modern portfolio management increasingly demands a balance between traditional risk-adjusted returns and strict Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) mandates. Train at Moving Edge: Online-Verified Prompt Selection for Efficient RL Training of Large Reasoning Model 2026-10-28
📝 NeurIPS 2026 Camera Ready
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become essential for post-training large language models (LLMs) in reasoning tasks. While scaling rollouts can stabilize training and enhance performance, the computational overhead is a critical issue. In algorithms like GRPO, multiple rollouts per prompt incur prohibitive costs, as a large portion of prompts provide negligible gradients and are thus of low utility. To address this problem, we investigate how to select high-utility prompts before the rollout phase. Our experimental analysis reveals that sample utility is non-uniform and evolving: the strongest learning signals concentrate at the ``learning edge", the intersection of intermediate difficulty and high uncertainty, which shifts as training proceeds. Motivated by this, we propose HIVE (History-Informed and online-VErified prompt selection), a dual-stage framework for data-efficient RL. HIVE utilizes historical reward trajectories for coarse selection and employs prompt entropy as a real-time proxy to prune instances with stale utility. By evaluating HIVE across multiple math reasoning benchmarks and models, we show that HIVE yields significant rollout efficiency without compromising performance.

Curriculum-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robust Autonomous Rocket Landing under Atmospheric Uncertainty 2026-10-28
📝 21 pages, 12 figures
Show abstract

Autonomous far-range rocket landing is a challenging control problem because navigation, descent-rate regulation, attitude management, and fuel use must be coordinated in the presence of uncertain atmospheric and vehicle dynamics. This paper presents a curriculum-driven proximal policy optimization (PPO) framework for three-dimensional rocket landing in a stochastic environment incorporating altitude-dependent aerodynamic drag, wind shear, temporally correlated turbulence, discrete gusts, uncertain propulsion and aerodynamic parameters, thrust-vector misalignment, engine lag, actuator-gain variation, noisy observations, and command latency. Learning is organised into far-range approach, soft descent, and integrated landing stages. Policy transfer, critic warm-up after reward changes, rehearsal of earlier tasks, and progressive expansion of weather severity are used to support learning across these coupled phases. Across 100,000 evaluation episodes, the policy achieves 96,656 successful landings, corresponding to a success rate of 96.656\% and a 95\% Wilson confidence interval of 96.543--96.766\%. Success decreases from 98.84\% under zero-disturbance conditions to 88.07\% in the highest weather-severity band. Successful touchdowns exhibit small miss distances and low residual horizontal and vertical velocities. An exploratory logistic-regression analysis identifies mean wind speed as the strongest measured correlate of success, with initial distance, initial altitude, initial vertical velocity, and wind-shear exponent also associated with outcomes. The results quantify both the effectiveness of the learned policy over the evaluated disturbance distribution and the degradation that occurs under severe compound atmospheric conditions.

Episodic looking - using reinforcement learning to bias perception enhances few-shot and continual learning with protection from forgetting 2026-10-28
📝 21 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

There is a fundamental contradiction between the desire for models that can generalize, and models that can continuously adapt to recent input with changing statistics. These conditions, captured in few-shot and continual learning settings, can bias or disrupt learned representations. This harms generalization to a broader context, leaving the model unable to overcome input variance. This paper proposes a solution based on the Complementary Learning System architecture (CLS), in which a Short-Term Memory (STM) delegates generalization to a slow, statistical Long-Term Memory (LTM). Delegation is achieved by working in a space of robust, generalized perceptions produced by the LTM. With this separation, the STM can focus on memorizing recent input and avoiding interference between memories. We show that on an image classification task, the resulting CLS model is more resistant to forgetting in a domain-incremental continual learning setting than a baseline ResNet LTM, and generalizes more effectively in a few-shot setting than other continual learners. In the continual setting, overall accuracy is slightly worse than existing continual learning models, but with a different mix of performance characteristics. Results analysis suggests that the model is limited by the representations and representational plasticity of the LTM. In future work, we will seek to address this by replaying STM memories to the LTM.

Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation 2026-10-28
📝 30 pages
Show abstract

Rubric-based reinforcement learning (RL) offers interpretable, fine-grained supervision for aligning large language models (LLMs) on open-ended generation tasks. However, static rubric criteria may become saturated or remain beyond the policy's exploration, rendering the reward signals insufficiently discriminative. While recent efforts seek to guide sampling exploration or evolve rubrics, they fail to explicitly factor in how rubric difficulty should track the evolving policy's capabilities, resulting in a persistent mismatch between the optimization objective and the policy's current capability frontier. In this paper, we propose \textbf{Co}-Evolving Policy and Rubric via \textbf{C}apability \textbf{A}wareness (\textbf{CoCA}), a closed-loop framework that jointly adapts the rubric and policy through criterion-level diagnosis. Specifically, CoCA contrasts query-only and rubric-guided rollouts to characterize each criterion relative to the current policy, recalibrates criteria with mismatched difficulty, and identifies guidance-revealed capability gaps for frontier-based learning. Based on this insight, CoCA either expands rubric-conditioned frontiers through guided optimization or transfers qualified gains through on-policy self-distillation. As the policy updates, CoCA improves the capability diagnosis and enables more reliable distillation, thereby completing the co-evolution loop. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and LLMs demonstrate that CoCA consistently improves performance over rubric-based RL methods. Moreover, CoCA promotes the internalization of guidance-revealed capabilities into query-only behavior while mitigating capability degradation.

A Minimally Informed Black-Box Fake-Report Attack on DRL Traffic-Signal Control 2026-10-28
📝 7 pages, 3 figures, 5 tables. Submitted to IEEE VTC2027-Spring (under review)
Show abstract

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) traffic-signal controllers may use vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) reports, exposing their perception layer to false-data injection. Existing demonstrations typically assume either white-box access to the controller's weights or full observability of the traffic state. We study the impact of a weaker and more realistic adversary who has no access to the RL model and whose traffic observations are limited to her own approach. This black-box, minimally informed attacker trains an imitation surrogate of the controller. She then crafts bounded fake reports, restricted to inflating vehicle counts on her own approach, that transfer to the real controller. We demonstrate that despite the loss of accuracy relative to an attacker who can observe the entire state and action space, this limited attacker can significantly increase the average waiting time for other approaches while barely changing the waiting time on her own approach, i.e. externalizing the added delay onto the other approaches. Because the only realizable move is to inflate one's own reported queue, we find that a zero-knowledge attacker that simply reports the maximum feasible queue is as damaging as the surrogate-based one, while an unstructured random inflation is not: the surrogate is not required to reproduce the degradation, and the exposed vulnerability is the V2I sensing channel itself. The surrogate's role is directional control, selecting a disruptive or a selfish objective at the same budget. The attack persists under +/-20% uniform demand variation. Finally, on a common per-lane budget we place the attacker on a knowledge ladder against white-box adversaries, and find the black-box surrogate matches a realizable white-box attacker that uses the victim's own gradients; only abandoning realizability raises the damage further.

Noise Steering: Exploiting Wake Steering and Acoustic Directivity for Noise-Constrained Wind Farm Control via Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-28
📝 17 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

Existing noise mitigation strategies for wind farms usually consist in reducing turbines power output, without accounting for the wake interactions between them and the noise emission directivity. This leads to over-conservative policies and unexploited wind power. Yet, turbine yaw can simultaneously affect wake interactions and the direction of noise emission, offering an additional degree of freedom for farm-level noise control. We therefore propose Noise Steering, a model-free reinforcement learning (RL) strategy that jointly controls turbine yaw angles and power setpoints to maximise farm power while satisfying a local noise constraint in a defined region. To enable training at a reduced computational cost, a low-fidelity acoustic model is coupled with the steady-state FLORIS simulator. The RL agent effectively learns how to redirect wakes and noise directivity away from the protected region while redistributing power across the farm. On a six-turbine test farm, the policy satisfies the 40 dB noise constraint throughout the full range of turbulent winds, with an average power deficit of only 2% relative to greedy unimpeded control, and up to 7.7% at high wind speeds. In comparison, the industry-standard noise-reduced operation (NRO) baseline here generates an average power loss of 17%. When deployed in the higher-fidelity FASTFarm simulator, the policy largely retains its performance under steady and turbulent inflow, despite differences in simulation fidelity. These results demonstrate the potential of combining yaw control and power derating through RL to improve the power-noise trade-off in wind farm operation.

AEM: Adaptive Entropy Modulation for Multi-Turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-28
📝 30 pages
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has substantially improved the ability of large language model (LLM) agents to interact with environments and solve multi-turn tasks. However, effective agentic RL remains challenging: sparse outcome-only rewards provide limited guidance for assigning credit to individual steps within long interaction trajectories. Existing approaches often introduce dense intermediate supervision, such as process reward models or auxiliary self-supervised signals, which increases supervision and tuning complexity and may limit generalization across tasks and domains. We present AEM, a supervision-free credit assignment method that adaptively modulates entropy dynamics during RL training to improve the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Since in agentic RL the environment is typically affected by a complete response, rather than an individual token, our analysis lifts entropy dynamics from the token level to the response level, aligning uncertainty estimation with the effective action granularity of LLM agents and reducing sensitivity to token-level sampling noise. We further show that entropy drift under natural-gradient updates is governed by the interaction between the sampled-response advantage and its relative surprisal. Motivated by this result, AEM derives a practical response-level uncertainty proxy and uses it to rescale advantages, leveraging the evolving balance between positive and negative samples to naturally transition from exploration to exploitation. Extensive experiments on ALFWorld, WebShop, and SWE-bench-Verified with models ranging from 1.5B to 32B demonstrate that AEM consistently improves strong RL baselines, including a +1.4\% gain when integrated into a state-of-the-art software-engineering RL training framework.

Stable but Wrong: When Learning Stabilizes Away from the Truth 2026-10-28
📝 33 pages, 4 figures; major revision. Added two controlled challenge-correction experiments with external ground truth, reorganized the theoretical analysis and appendices, and updated the author list to reflect contributions to the revised work. The core SBW framing and title are retained
Show abstract

Stable training is often treated as evidence that learning is succeeding, but stability characterizes optimization behavior rather than correctness relative to an external objective. We study what happens when the signal being optimized remains persistently biased. We define Stable but Wrong (SBW) as a learning state in which optimization remains active or stabilizes while the learned outcome stays systematically displaced from an independently defined objective. A minimal theoretical model shows that persistent systematic bias can separate a stable convergence point from the true objective even under standard conditions for stable convergence. We then introduce static and dynamic bias in reinforcement learning, supervised learning, and continual fine-tuning of large language models. Across these settings, optimization of the current learning signal continues or stabilizes while correctness relative to the external objective declines or remains displaced. Finally, two controlled challenge-correction experiments with external ground truth test whether a prespecified error can be exposed and changed. In controlled scientific discovery and CIFAR-10, targeted evidence corrects or reduces established errors more effectively than equal-budget general evidence, with no evident harm to paired correct states under the tested protocols. These experiments do not provide a general method for automatically discovering unknown errors. Overall, SBW identifies a basic limit of convergence and other internal optimization metrics as evidence of correctness in systems that learn through persistent bias and feedback.

SCOPE: Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-28
📝 18 pages, 8 figures, 5 tables
