DecArena: Evaluating Large-Scale Decentralized Multi-Agent Collaboration 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Emerging personal AI agents raise fundamental scalability and privacy concerns for centralized multi-agent systems (MASs), which rely on a global orchestrator with broad access to agents, resources, and communication. In decentralized environments, global visibility is unavailable. Agents need to proactively discover non-local resources (e.g. tools, skills), and collaborate with other agents to solve tasks. We study decentralized multi-agent collaboration under graph-constrained communication, a natural reflection of human structures. Each agent observes only its local state and can interact only with neighboring agents. We introduce a benchmark with synthetic scalable distributed tool calling with 100K+ tools. Our experiments reveal that recent LLM agents struggle to discover non-local relevant resources and collaborate in decentralized setting. Moreover, standard reinforcement learning fails to reliably overcome this limitation, suggesting that decentralized MAS needs more explicit harness mechanisms for effective collaboration, even using SOTA LLMs. We then present a strong baseline \textbf{GraphHarness}, combining topology-aware warmup with self-improving LLM-based routing. Results show that GraphHarness substantially improves task success (+48.5) and produces higher quality collaboration (+30.6), while using fewer costs (+34.0) than baselines.

ReShap: Interpretable Reinforcement Learning for High-Dimensional Multi-Action Settings 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) plays a crucial role in tackling complex tasks, yet its reliance on deep neural networks makes the learned policies opaque. To enhance the trustworthiness of RL policies, existing work (e.g., the SILVER framework [Li et al., 2025]) has focused on Shapley-based interpretability, but is typically restricted to low-dimensional state spaces, binary-action settings, and a single class of linear surrogate. We propose ReShap, a policy-distillation framework that extends Shapley-based interpretability to high-dimensional, multi-action environments. ReShap extracts compact feature representations from raw image observations, performs SHAP-based feature attribution to characterize how each feature contributes to the policy's decisions, and distills the deep RL policy into an interpretable surrogate trained on the full set of (state, policy-action) pairs along the policy's visited distribution. We instantiate the surrogate with three interpretable model families (decision trees, linear regression, and logistic regression) and compare their fidelity and task return. We evaluate ReShap on Atari and Box2D environments using three deep RL algorithms and conduct a human-subject study to assess the clarity and trustworthiness of the resulting policies. Results show that ReShap preserves competitive task performance, that decision trees generally outperform linear and logistic regression, and that linear regression is weakest in most settings.

The Illusion of Diversity: Aligning LLM Exploration via Effective Entropy 2026-11-11
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has shifted the alignment paradigm of large language models from subjective preference to objective correctness, yet a fundamental disconnect persists between exploration diversity and reasoning validity. Existing strategies predominantly rely on "blind" entropy metrics, e.g., token-level or global sequence-level entropy, which structurally fail to distinguish between effective reasoning and potential hallucinations. This misalignment creates an illusion of diversity, where high entropy tends to signal chaotic degeneration rather than valuable exploration. To bridge this gap, we introduce Effective Entropy, a validity-aware entropy concept that quantifies diversity exclusively within the valid subspace and systematically filters out the noise of incorrect trajectories. Built on this concept, we instantiate VEPO, a simple Validity-aware Entropy Policy Optimization method that directly incorporates effective entropy into RLVR training. VEPO induces an adaptive attraction dynamic that drives up the probability of under-explored valid trajectories, a mechanism that matches the practical regime of large-scale autoregressive models where sequence probabilities remain extremely small. Experiments on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks with 1.5B and 7B DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen models show consistent aggregate accuracy gains over GRPO and competitive repeated-sampling success. A direct evaluation with matched correct counts finds more distinct strategies on commonly solved MATH500 problems, supported by paired statistical tests and three-seed training comparisons.

What Makes a VLA Reactive? A Study of Execution Strategies for Dynamic Manipulation 2026-11-11
📝 13 pages, 7 figures, Efficient Foundation Models for Real-Time Embodied AI, CoRL 2026 Workshop
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models predict chunks of future actions, and when objects move, those chunks go stale before they are executed. We study what makes a pretrained VLA reactive, using \pihalf{} on two dynamic simulation benchmarks and three real-robot tasks. We vary the prediction and execution horizons, compare synchronous and asynchronous inference, test methods that deliver actions sooner or keep successive chunks continuous, and adapt the policy with motion cues and latent-space reinforcement learning. The best prediction horizon is about one second, and the best execution horizon depends on the checkpoint. Short horizons help more with asynchronous execution, and methods that keep the trajectory continuous outperform those that only reduce latency. Adapting the policy repairs targeted failures, but on the conveyor task changing the execution horizon gained as much. Reactivity depends on the execution pipeline as much as on the policy, so both should be evaluated together.

DeALR: Decoupled Audio-Latent Optimization with Acoustic Backtracking for Test-Time Reasoning 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Large audio-language models (LALMs) typically encode audio once and conduct subsequent multi-step reasoning in a language-dominated space, weakening access to fine-grained acoustic evidence. Although recent multimodal approaches have explored re-engaging perceptual inputs during reasoning, whether a frozen LALM can actively revisit crucial acoustic evidence in the latent space at test time remains underexplored. To bridge this gap, we introduce Decoupled Audio Latent Reasoning (DeALR), a novel test-time latent reasoning framework that shifts the paradigm from text-dominated deliberation to active audio-latent interaction. Inspired by the human cognitive process of "backtracking" to specific acoustic cues during complex listening tasks, DeALR establishes a dynamic interaction between learnable thought tokens and acoustic segments. Specifically, we introduce Span-Level Acoustic Backtracking, which traces initial attention distributions to dynamically isolate contiguous, key acoustic spans. Furthermore, we propose Decoupled Audio-Latent Optimization (DALO), a test-time reinforcement learning algorithm that jointly refines thought vectors and acoustic patches via confidence-guided policy gradients. Crucially, DALO explicitly decouples trajectory search from response generation by reinstating uncorrupted, high-fidelity audio embeddings prior to text synthesis, entirely eliminating optimization-induced distribution drift while retaining the navigation of the refined thoughts. Comprehensive experiments on benchmarks demonstrate that DeALR consistently outperforms prior test-time optimization approaches, delivering substantial improvements in both confidence calibration and reasoning accuracy.

SimRec: Learning to Search, Ask, and Recommend from Simulated Interaction 2026-11-11
📝 19 pages,5 figures,16 tables. Code: https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/SimRec
Show abstract

LLM-based recommendation agents can search catalogs, inspect products, ask users, and revise rejected recommendations. However, these behaviors are often governed by hand-designed workflows, while existing training methods optimize isolated components such as query rewriting or list ranking rather than the interaction policy as a whole. We present SimRec, a reinforcement learning framework that trains the full interaction policy of a tool-integrated recommendation agent. SimRec introduces two components. A task-anchored user simulator provides feedback on every recommendation: conditioned on the current task need rather than on user profile, and it can be an external LLM or the recommender itself under self-play. On ESCI and Amazon C4, SimRec improves the NDCG@100 of a Qwen3-4B agent from 32.23% to 35.42% and from 22.94% to 24.72%}, outperforming training-free agents on far larger backbones and task-aligned baselines on NDCG. Analysis shows that the trained agent verifies candidates, uses preferences, and recovers from rejected recommendations far more often than its untrained counterpart. Delayed homomorphic reinforcement learning for environments with delayed feedback 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning in real-world systems often involves delayed feedback, which violates the Markov assumption and impedes both learning and control. Canonical augmentation-based approaches address this issue by augmenting the state with action histories, but suffer from state-space explosion and the resulting sample-complexity burden. Despite recent progress, state-of-the-art augmentation-based methods either primarily alleviate this burden for the critic or employ separate mechanisms for the actor and critic. To address this limitation, we propose delayed homomorphic reinforcement learning (DHRL), a framework grounded in MDP homomorphisms that introduces a belief-equivalence relation over the augmented state space to collapse control-redundant augmented states into a compact abstract state space. This framework yields exact abstraction under deterministic dynamics and approximate abstraction under stochastic dynamics, providing a unified abstraction mechanism that benefits both the actor and critic. In finite domains, exact abstraction preserves optimality and recovers the delay-free sample-complexity order, while approximate abstraction admits a bound on the value loss of the resulting policy. For continuous domains, we introduce deep delayed homomorphic policy gradient (D$^2$HPG), a deep actor-critic instantiation of the DHRL framework. Experiments on MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks demonstrate that D$^2$HPG outperforms strong augmentation-based baselines, especially in long-delay regimes. Code is available at https://github.com/JongsooLee/DHRL.

Is the Importance Ratio Necessary for Stable Reinforcement Learning in LLMs? 2026-11-11
📝 21 pages, 11 figures, 5 tables. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become central to post-training large language models (LLMs). However, popular RL methods like GRPO incur non-negligible overhead by computing both old-policy and current-policy likelihoods to form importance sampling ratios. In this work, we propose Likelihood-Gated Policy Optimization (LGPO), which enforces a soft trust region constraint via likelihood-based gating, eliminating the need to compute old-policy likelihoods. Empirically, we show that removing the importance sampling correction term does not harm training stability, whereas removing the trust region mechanism can lead to collapse. Moreover, ratio-based clipping can fail in fully on-policy training: the importance ratio stays at 1, so the ratio-based trust region constraint never activates. Under standard training settings where GRPO is stable, LGPO achieves comparable training stability and peak performance while reducing training time by ~18% on average relative to a standard implementation that recomputes old-policy likelihoods. In fully on-policy training, where GRPO fails, LGPO remains stable, enabling more efficient and robust LLM RL post-training across training regimes.

A physics-constrained deep reinforcement learning method for autonomous mission planning of Titan deep space probes 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Titan is a compelling target for future deep space exploration, while its extreme Earth-Titan distance and complex environment make continuous ground-in-the-loop planning impractical. Existing planning methods, primarily developed for Earth-observation scenarios, have limited generalizability to uncertain deep space conditions and adaptability to time-varying mission states. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a physics-constrained maskable proximal policy optimization (PCM-PPO) method for Titan autonomous mission planning. A Titan-specific discrete-time Markov decision process is established to characterize mission feasibility and resource constraints. A system dynamics and geometric model is incorporated to enhance physical representation, including central gravity, J2 perturbation, third-body attraction, and downlink occultation. To accommodate opportunity targets under a bounded action dimension, PCM-PPO organizes predefined and opportunity targets through a fixed-capacity target pool and represents the mission state with a target-wise state matrix and a global state vector. A physics-constrained action mask constructs an admissible decision domain from current mission states, guiding policy learning toward physically executable decisions and avoiding infeasible exploration. An integrated training strategy facilitates policy optimization under coupled constraints. Comparative, ablation, and uncertainty experiments validate the effectiveness of PCM-PPO. PCM-PPO provides a feasible and interpretable scheme for autonomous mission planning of Titan deep space probes.

In the legal domain, LLMs can improve reliability and interpretability by generating explicit reasoning traces. However, training LLMs to produce high-quality reasoning traces remains challenging. Existing supervised fine-tuning methods struggle when reasoning traces are significantly longer than final answers, as the learning signal for the answer becomes diluted. Meanwhile, reinforcement learning methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization also have several limitations, including costly reward design, performance plateaus, and reward hacking. APG-Planner: Learning Vision-Based High-Speed Trajectory Planning via Analytic Policy Gradient 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Vision-based high-speed navigation requires a trajectory planner that can reactively generate smooth, collision-free trajectories from onboard visual observations. Deep reinforcement learning offers a promising approach to vision-based trajectory planning, as it enables the optimization of sequential decision-making policies in complex environments. However, model-free policy gradient methods rely on sample-based policy gradient estimation, which yields noisy gradient estimates and inefficient training. To this end, we propose a framework for learning trajectory planning policies using the analytic policy gradient (APG) method and use it to train APG-Planner, a vision-based high-speed trajectory planner. The proposed framework leverages differential flatness to analytically reconstruct vehicle states from planned trajectories, thereby enabling differentiable trajectory-level state transitions without requiring an explicit low-level dynamics model. By iteratively performing trajectory planning and differentiable state transitions, we construct a differentiable episode rollout and analytically compute policy gradients from episode-level losses, improving both training efficiency and policy performance. After approximately 30 minutes of training on a single GPU, APG-Planner demonstrates comparable or superior performance to existing baseline methods in simulation and achieves speeds of up to 11\,m/s in real-world flight experiments. We further validate the proposed framework on a skid-steer ground robot, demonstrating its applicability to differentially flat robotic systems beyond quadrotors.

Global Convergence of Wasserstein Policy Gradient for Entropy-Regularized Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Wasserstein policy gradient (WPG) optimizes stochastic policies in the optimal-transport geometry of action distributions. In entropy-regularized reinforcement learning, WPG combines transport driven by gradients of the soft Q-function with entropy-induced diffusion. The resulting dynamics are coupled through Bellman policy evaluation, and the policy optimization objective is generally nonconvex. We establish a unified global convergence theory of WPG for infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision processes with general state spaces and continuous action spaces. We identify a statewise gradient-dominance inequality by exploiting the Bellman structure together with log-Sobolev inequalities, which we show to hold uniformly along the trajectory. This inequality relates the squared norm of the Wasserstein policy gradient to the value gap in the sup norm. In continuous time, we prove well-posedness of the flow and exponential convergence of the value gap in the sup norm and of the policy in statewise KL divergence. In discrete time, by additionally controlling the regularity and the discretization error, we obtain geometric convergence to a neighborhood of the optimum with constant step sizes and an $O(\varepsilon^{-1})$ iteration complexity with diminishing step sizes for fixed problem parameters. Finally, we establish faster local convergence rates for both dynamics, governed by the geometry of the optimal policy.

MOON-RL: Multi-Objective Optimization via Objective Space Navigation with Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-11
📝 66 pages, including appendices. Code, data and supplementary video links are provided in the manuscript
Show abstract

Many engineering design problems involve multiple conflicting objectives and strict feasibility constraints. They are typically solved with population-based optimizers that require many evaluations and offer limited online control over where the search is focused. We present MOON-RL, a reinforcement-learning-based optimization framework that formulates constrained multi-objective search as preference-directed navigation in the objective space. In MOON-RL, a lightweight PPO policy receives objective-space and constraint-related observations and acts as an instance-specific adaptive search process. It learns how to update the design so that the current solution advances along a user-specified trade-off direction, while a simple meta-algorithm periodically resets the policy, resamples starting designs from a continuously updated archive of non-dominated solutions, and adjusts step-size and penalty schedules. We evaluate MOON-RL on real-world actuator-design problems and additional convergence-hard constrained benchmarks, comparing against established evolutionary algorithms under matched evaluation budgets. In focused search scenarios, MOON-RL achieves competitive performance in the region of interest, and ablation studies show that both the RL-driven adaptive sampling process and the meta-algorithm contribute to this behaviour, suggesting that repurposing RL training as objective-space navigation is a viable building block for interactive, preference-guided design tools. Additional experiments show that the framework also accommodates adaptive non-RL samplers, while PPO remains a strong choice for fixed-target, preference-directed objective-space navigation.

Reinforcement learning sharpens LLM reasoning, but parameter updates are expensive, forgetful, and opaque; training-free libraries swap gradients for a textual store but still use trajectory-level, correlation-based admission and flat structure. Silent Neuron Theory and Plasticity Preservation for Deep Reinforcement Learning in Adaptive Video Streaming 2026-11-11

Show abstract

Adaptive video streaming optimizes Quality of Experience (QoE) metrics by selecting appropriate bitrates according to varying network bandwidth and user demands. In practice, however, real-world network bandwidth often exhibits heterogeneity relative to training environments. Current methods predominantly tackle this problem through learning-based approaches designed to improve generalization performance. Our systematic investigation reveals a critical limitation: neural networks suffer from plasticity loss, significantly impeding their ability to adapt to heterogeneous network conditions. Through theoretical analysis of neural propagation mechanisms, we demonstrate that existing dormant neuron metrics inadequately characterize neural plasticity loss. To address this limitation, we have developed the Silent Neuron theory, which provides a more comprehensive framework for understanding plasticity degradation. BunshinRL: Whole-Workspace Forking for Agentic Reinforcement Learning Rollouts 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) rollouts interleave model reasoning with tool calls in persistent workspaces. Scaling these rollouts incurs substantial memory costs for concurrent environments, while slow tool calls delay entire rollout batches. We present BunshinRL, an agentic RL rollout system that exploits shared environment state and recurring tool calls through three techniques: memory sharing across environments, tool-prefix caching, and speculative tool execution. These techniques build on BunshinFork, a novel live whole-workspace forking mechanism for containers. We implement BunshinRL and BunshinFork through Linux kernel module and CRIU modifications. BunshinRL reduces peak memory by up to 43%, total memory consumption by up to 51%, and end-to-end rollout latency by 23%.

Recently, researchers have devised a mixed integer linear problem (MILP) to jointly determine the traffic signal and road width designs. However, this joint optimization problem must be solved repeatedly whenever the traffic flow pattern varies. Solving the MILP may also take impractical computing time as the problem size gets larger. We present a robust methodology for solving the joint optimization problem using reinforcement learning (RL) on a real-time basis. Abrupt changes in lane markings with an incompatible signal phase can jeopardize drivers. To sidestep such a dangerous action change, we train a model with human feedback to filter infeasible action transitions in a rule-based manner. We devised a deep Q-network that can accommodate joint actions for traffic signal and lane marking plans. To ensure the implementation of the proposed operation, a variable sign for lane markings is necessary at each stop line of an intersection. SplitCom: Communication-efficient Split Federated Fine-tuning of LLMs via Temporal Compression 2026-11-10

Federated fine-tuning of on-device large language models (LLMs) mitigates privacy concerns by preventing raw data sharing. However, the intensive computational and memory demands pose significant challenges for resource-constrained edge devices. To overcome these limitations, split federated learning (SFL) emerges as a promising solution that partitions the model into lightweight client-side and compute-intensive server-side sub-models, thus offloading the primary training workload to a powerful server. Nevertheless, the high-dimensional activation exchanges in SFL lead to excessive communication overhead. To overcome this, we propose SplitCom, a communication-efficient SFL framework for LLMs that exploits temporal redundancy in activations across consecutive training epochs. Inspired by video compression, the core innovation of our framework lies in selective activation uploading only when a noticeable deviation from previous epochs occurs. To balance communication efficiency and learning performance, we introduce two adaptive threshold control schemes based on 1) bang-bang control or 2) deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG)-based reinforcement learning. Moreover, we implement dimensionality reduction techniques to alleviate client-side memory requirements. Furthermore, we extend SplitCom to the U-shape architecture, ensuring the server never accesses clients' labels. Extensive simulations and laboratory experiments demonstrate that SplitCom reduces uplink communication costs by up to 98.8\,\% in its standard configuration and total communication costs by up to 95.8\,\% in its U-shape variant without noticeably compromising model performance.
