STVLA: Spatial-aware and Temporal-memory forVision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Robotic manipulation often requires both spatial scene understanding and task history. However, many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely primarily on current observations, without explicitly integrating spatial structure and task history. Appearance-based memories can be sensitive to occlusion and viewpoint changes, while spatial representations of the current scene alone do not preserve how the scene has changed during task execution. We introduce STVLA, a framework that incorporates features from a pretrained visual geometry foundation model into temporal memory alongside visual representations. This allows the policy to recall past scene structure as context for current action decisions. To select informative history, STVLA uses a mathematically grounded scoring rule that balances relevance to the current observation with complementary evidence from memory. Across evaluations on LIBERO, LIBERO-Mem, MIKASA-Robo, and SimplerEnv Bridge, STVLA achieves the highest average success rate among the compared methods on each benchmark. On real-world block stacking, object organization, and phone packaging, it improves success over $π_{0.5}$ by $58.33$, $30.00$, and $35.00$ percentage points, respectively. These results support integrating scene geometry into temporal memory to improve manipulation requiring both spatial precision and task history. Project website: \href{https://stvla.github.io/}{https://stvla.github.io/}

I See What You Want: Decoding Hierarchical Operator Intent from Visual Teleoperation History 2026-11-12
📝 8 pages, 6 figures. Project page: https://see-want.github.io/
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have become a powerful foundation for robotic scene understanding, task planning, and language-conditioned control. A natural question follows: can these models also decode what a human operator intends while teleoperating a robot, without any explicit instruction? We formulate this as hierarchical intent prediction, jointly inferring the ongoing atomic action and the broader mission it serves from a short visual teleoperation history. To study this, we introduce VI AMO (Vision Intention Action and Mission Ongoing), a dataset of 6,600 episodes from eleven robot sources with 13,653 temporally aligned action annotations and 9,702 mission annotations. Large proprietary models struggle with this problem: on held-out episodes, GPT-4o reaches Sentence-BERT similarities of only 0.563 and 0.470 for action and mission, and GPT-5.6 Luna achieves only 0.581 and 0.554. Fine-tuning a VLM on VI AMO yields our Vision-Intention Model (VIM), which raises these scores to 0.825 and 0.821 and still reaches 0.726 and 0.717 on the 35.4% of test actions with both labels absent from training. Finally, as a downstream application, VIM's predicted mission enables a vision-language-action (VLA) policy to take over from the operator mid-task in a real-world environment unseen during VIM fine-tuning, reducing human teleoperation time by 34.6% relative to GPT-4o and 39.8% relative to GPT-5.6 Luna, while cutting intent-prediction latency by roughly 70% on average.

MOSAIC: Modular Obstacle and Spatial-Aware Framework for Manipulation In Cluttered Scenes 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated strong performance in robotic manipulation, yet most rely primarily on 2D visual observations and language instructions, leaving scene geometry and obstacle relationships to be inferred implicitly. This limitation is particularly problematic in cluttered environments, where small spatial errors can lead to unintended contacts or collisions. We propose MOSAIC, a modular spatial- and obstacle-aware framework that can be integrated with pretrained VLA policies to improve object-level scene understanding and action generation. Our method augments the VLA backbone with an auxiliary semantic grounding task that predicts role-specific masks for the target object, destination region, and surrounding obstacles. We further combine the target and destination masks with depth estimated from 3D scene reconstruction to construct role-specific geometric tokens that are injected directly into the action expert. In addition, we propose obstacle-aware attention supervision that emphasizes obstacles near the robot body during task execution. The resulting attention features are subsequently injected into later action-expert layers to further guide action generation. Together, these components provide explicit semantic, geometric, and obstacle-aware cues while preserving the underlying pretrained VLA architecture. We evaluate our framework on pretrained VLA model using the RoboPRO benchmark, including visual perturbation tests, and further validate it on real-world manipulation tasks. MOSAIC improves the success rate by 5.1% in cluttered scenes, and by 9.7% under visual perturbations, with gains of up to 25.0% in the most challenging environment. We will release our code and dataset upon acceptance at the following Github page: https://mmahdavian.github.io/MOSAIC/

DisasterVLG: Vision-Language Grounding and Evidence-Based Reasoning for Disaster Scene Understanding 2026-11-11
Show abstract

General-purpose vision-language models struggle with crisis imagery due to its inherent noise, ambiguity, and high variability across disaster events - yet timely, accurate scene understanding is critical for humanitarian response. To address this, we introduce DisasterVLG, a large-scale multi-task dataset containing 1.24 million disaster images from 142 countries paired with 33 million structured annotations spanning dense captioning with localization, incident assessment, relational scene descriptions, and crisis-grounded visual question answering (VQA) across diverse hazard types and scene contexts worldwide. Unlike prior disaster benchmarks that focus narrowly on classification, DisasterVLG unifies recognition, grounded reasoning, and uncertainty-aware interpretation. A distinguishing feature is its evidence-grounded annotation design: each VQA instance includes supporting evidence text that links visual observations to answers, alongside positive and negative question pairs, multiple answer formats, and difficulty labels. Visual grounding layer links caption phrases to image regions and relationship statements to subject-object boxes, with explicit labels for visual supports and clues. To support rigorous evaluation, we construct a human-verified test set of 38K images (1.15M annotations) spanning broad geographic regions and disaster types. Experiments with domain-adapted vision-language models demonstrate strong performance across disaster captioning, incident recognition, and grounded VQA, while also exposing the limitations of general-purpose models in crisis-specific reasoning. DisasterVLG establishes a scalable, reproducible benchmark for vision-language models that must reason reliably under real-world uncertainty. We will publicly release the dataset, trained models, and code.

Autonomous Trail Racing with a Bicycle Robot 2026-11-11
📝 35 pages, 10 figures, 3 tables (incl. appendix with supplementary figures and tables). Submitted to The International Journal of Robotics Research. Project page: https://pages.rai-inst.com/autonomous-trail-racing/. Code: https://github.com/rai-opensource/sem_map
Show abstract

Navigating off-road trails on a mountain bike requires diverse locomotion skills, including aerial maneuvers, cornering, and slip recovery, paired with sophisticated perception for scene understanding. Translating these capabilities to autonomous, two-wheeled robots remains a formidable challenge. We show that autonomous, high-speed trail navigation can be achieved through a single idea: encoding the trail as a semantic layer of the terrain. In simulation, this layer embeds the navigation task into the training environment; during deployment, it is reconstructed onboard from camera and LiDAR data. First, we introduce a unified learning framework that treats local navigation and locomotion as a joint optimization problem, bypassing the twist or pose command interface of classical control hierarchies. With fixed reward weights, the same framework produces both trail racing and agile parkour, shaped only through terrain semantics and episode length. Second, our perception pipeline combines a frozen vision foundation model with a lightweight dual-head decoder that predicts per-pixel terrain semantics and confidence scores, which are fused with LiDAR range measurements into an egocentric semantic elevation map at 30 Hz onboard an NVIDIA AGX Orin. We validate our framework on the Ultra Mobility Vehicle (UMV), a bicycle robot that autonomously raced across all sections of a kilometer-long, unstructured mountain bike trail at speeds exceeding 5 m/s and averaging 3.26 m/s. In simulation, our policy fails up to an order of magnitude less often than hierarchical baselines while driving faster.

UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 31 pages, 7 figures, 6 tables. Project page: https://ugg-model.github.io/
Show abstract

Understanding 4D scenes is a core challenge in visual intelligence, requiring models to jointly capture scene geometry, appearance, and dynamics from partial observations. However, existing approaches are subdivided into feedforward models for direct geometric prediction, and generative models for visual synthesis. We introduce UGG, a unified framework for 4D scene understanding that jointly models RGB video and geometric representations such as depth and camera pose. By assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, UGG formulates visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion within a single generative model. This flexible formulation enables the model to learn from diverse, heterogeneous datasets with different subsets of geometric and visual annotations. Furthermore, our generative formulation enables inference-time search over multiple plausible scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence extracted from observed RGB frames to improve geometric prediction without ground truth geometry. We demonstrate UGG's effectiveness over non-unified baselines across camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, including in challenging sparse observation settings. Code and visualizations are available at https://ugg-model.github.io/

Where to Approximate in Neurosymbolic Inference? 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Probabilistic neurosymbolic methods rely on weighted model counting (WMC) to combine neural predictors with symbolic constraints. Exact WMC is #P-hard and typically scales poorly, so many methods resort to approximations. By unifying existing approaches under a three-step bottom-up approximate compilation pipeline, we study where approximation budget is best allocated. Inspired by tensor networks, we instantiate two steps with tensor train decompositions and derive new pipelines that exactly multiply approximated factors and recompress their products via SVD- or interpolation-based schemes. Across six tasks in Sudoku, arithmetic reasoning, and scene understanding, and across increasing problem sizes, our pipelines produce WMC estimates orders-of-magnitude more accurate than prior methods. This translates into stronger downstream learning on tasks where reasoning relies on tight interactions between symbolic factors, where cruder approximations ignore these dependencies. Our pipelines open novel design space for effective approximate compilation of challenging probabilistic reasoning tasks.

Towards Text-Only Remote Sensing Scene Classification via Semantic Proxy 2026-11-10
📝 13 pages, 4 figures, 14 tables, including appendices
Show abstract

Remote sensing scene classification (RSSC) is traditionally formulated as a vision-centric problem, where inference relies on rich visual representations extracted from images. In this work, we revisit this assumption by introducing text-only RSSC, a new problem setting that investigates whether remote sensing scenes can be recognized using only textual semantic representations. To enable this study, we propose SeProRS, which reformulates RSSC as a text-only classification task through semantic proxy generation. Specifically, a vision-language model (VLM) is first employed to convert remote sensing images into textual descriptions, termed semantic proxies, which are then encoded by a text embedding model for scene classification. This decoupled pipeline provides a unified and reproducible benchmark for evaluating text-only scene understanding. Furthermore, we investigate representative prompting strategies and proxy generators, and analyze their impact on classification performance, text embedding behavior, cross-generator transferability, and efficiency. Extensive experiments on three well-known RSSC datasets show that semantic proxies can preserve substantial discriminative information, while also revealing inherent limitations of purely text-based scene understanding. This paper does not aim to replace image-based RSSC, but instead establishes a new research direction by quantifying the feasibility and boundaries of semantic proxy-based scene recognition. The proposed benchmark and analysis provide a foundation for future research on efficient and modality-reduced remote sensing scene understanding. AgenticConflictLens: From Long Traffic Videos to Verifiable Conflict Evidence 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Traffic cameras continuously record urban road-user behavior, providing proactive safety evidence beyond crash reports. Yet safety-critical events - conflicts, near misses, unsafe turns, sudden braking, and crosswalk interactions - are rare and brief, buried in hours of routine footage. Existing traffic-video pipelines extract detections, trajectories, and risk scores but process footage uniformly, without adapting to an analyst's query. Vision-language methods improve scene understanding but often lack traffic-specific verification of participant-level conflicts. We introduce AgenticConflictLens, an agentic, query-conditioned framework for efficient, auditable conflict extraction from long fixed-camera traffic videos. Given a natural-language safety query, a Traffic-Aware Controller combines structured traffic memory with specialized conflict-analysis tools to choose where to search, which road users to examine, and which evidence to verify or reject. Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved strong image and video understanding, yet their visual-spatial representations remain geometrically fragile, leading to failures in spatial reasoning needed for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous driving. Prior approaches to geometry grounding either fine-tune VLMs on spatial question answering, which can perpetuate spurious visual representations, or fuse features from large geometry-grounded vision models, which substantially increases model size at inference. Knowledge distillation from geometry-grounded vision models offers an alternative, but directly matching multi-view teacher features can disrupt the pretrained alignment between visual and textual representations, degrading object- and language-semantic capabilities. We propose multi-view relational distillation (MVRD), which distills patch-wise cosine similarities across views instead of the teacher features themselves. These relations encode geometric correspondences adequate for spatial understanding, while leaving the student representation underdetermined, allowing it to remain close to its pretrained vision- language space. Across representative VLMs, MVRD improves visual-spatial reasoning, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and feature distillation while approaching feature fusion methods with considerably fewer added parameters and lower latency. We show that MVRD makes visual representations more geometric while retaining language alignment, and generalizes to 3D scene understanding tasks such as object grounding, dense captioning, and question answering. Code is available at https://github.com/kietngt00/MVRD

A forensic evaluation framework for egocentric foundation models: an EFM3D case study for crime scene reconstruction 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Foundation models increasingly combine geometry, semantics, and spatial context for three-dimensional scene understanding, but their suitability for forensic crime scene reconstruction remains unclear. This study presents a structured forensic evaluation framework for egocentric foundation models and demonstrates it using the Egocentric Foundation Model for 3D (EFM3D). Four simulated indoor crime scenes were recorded with Meta Project Aria Gen~1 at Robert Gordon University's East Lodge House forensic training facility. The framework evaluates room-scale reconstruction, semantic scene understanding, computational performance, and forensic suitability. Across the four scenes, EFM3D generated meshes averaging $12{,}668\pm2{,}430$ vertices and $24{,}838\pm4{,}903$ faces, with 39,529 recorded semantic predictions spanning 21--24 categories at a mean confidence of 0.40. HIST3R: Decomposing and Rectifying State Updates with History for Streaming 3D Reconstruction 2026-11-09
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://kennethyangg.github.io/HIST3R/
Show abstract

Streaming 3D reconstruction with recurrent states enables long-video scene understanding under bounded memory, but turns reconstruction into a state-evolution problem: each incoming frame updates a compact scene memory on which all subsequent predictions depend, so unreliable updates overwrite historical context and accumulate into long-horizon geometric drift. We analyze CUT3R-style recurrent updates through a direction--magnitude decomposition, in which the magnitude sets the update gain, i.e., how strongly an observation is written into the state, and the direction encodes the geometric intent, i.e., where the update drives the state. This decomposition provides a unified view of CUT3R and its variants, revealing that existing training-free extensions perform magnitude-only control: they can attenuate an unreliable update but cannot correct its direction. We propose HIST3R, a training-free framework built on CUT3R that controls both components using history. HIST3R rectifies the update direction using locally consistent historical states, and modulates the update magnitude according to the visual discrepancy between the current observation and its history, suppressing redundant updates while preserving complementary evidence. Across camera pose estimation, 3D reconstruction, and video depth estimation, HIST3R consistently improves long-horizon performance while retaining the memory footprint and inference speed of CUT3R. With 1000 input frames, it reduces ATE by 23.87% on ScanNet and 33.65% on TUM-Dynamics relative to the strongest competing method; with 500 frames, it improves median reconstruction accuracy over the strongest CUT3R-based variant by 56.52% on 7-Scenes and 33.96% on NRGBD.

ScanDreamer: A Map-Grounded World Model for Quadrotor Navigation 2026-11-08
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Submitted to AICAP 2027; decision pending
Show abstract

Quadrotors inspecting buildings and tunnels must navigate unknown clutter with a forward-facing depth camera whose limited field of view constrains scene understanding and collision-avoiding navigation. Recent world-model agents learn navigation through imagined experience (e.g., AirDreamer), but remain data-intensive and often lack explicit geometric memory and awareness of what the camera can observe. We propose ScanDreamer, a map-grounded world model that constructs an online 2D scan-memory map from depth images and vehicle pose. Our world model, actor, and critic are conditioned on this map, which also defines a geodesic progress reward. Moreover, a forward-cone penalty discourages horizontal motion outside the camera's viewing direction. During imagination, ScanDreamer propagates the quadrotor pose over a frozen map and derives progress and wall-contact signals from the mapped geometry. We train ScanDreamer for 0.8 million environment steps (5.5 h on one GPU) and evaluate it in NVIDIA Isaac Sim over 1560 episodes spanning unseen layouts and parameter ranges. Across three seeds (mean $\pm$ SD), ScanDreamer achieves 96.3 $\pm$ 0.3% success with 3.6 $\pm$ 0.3% collision, compared with 59.6 $\pm$ 3.0% success and 28.8 $\pm$ 2.8% collision for AirDreamer at the same online-interaction budget. Removing the forward-cone penalty increases collisions to 9.9 $\pm$ 3.1%. These results show that combining explicit map memory, map-grounded imagination, and perception-aware motion substantially improves navigation performance and data efficiency. The source code is available at https://github.com/WANGSHUAIzzzz/ScanDreamer.

MOSU2: Long-range Navigation System with Multi-modal Scene Understanding 2026-11-08
Show abstract

Robots that travel long distances through public outdoor spaces must do more than avoid obstacles: they must remain on terrain suited to their mobility and decide when to proceed, yield, or detour around pedestrians, cyclists, vehicles, traffic signals, and temporary restrictions. Multi-task scene understanding models typically rely on modified vision encoders or complex decoders for cross-task interaction, which limits their flexibility and compatibility with off-the-shelf vision foundation models (VFMs). From Detection to Understanding: TAR and TAR-Bench for Multi-Task Traffic Anomaly Reasoning 2026-11-07

Detecting a traffic anomaly does not establish whether a video-language model can explain what happened, localize it in time, or identify its causes.
