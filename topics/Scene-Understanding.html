Physicl Scenes: An Artist-created 3D Dataset for Learning Perception and Action 2026-11-24
📝 Project: https://liu115.github.io/physicl video: https://youtu.be/UgyQFQSU-oc
Show abstract

We present Physicl Scenes, a high-quality artist-created and curated dataset of 6,499 3D scenes spanning household rooms, public interiors, and outdoor spaces. The dataset combines detailed geometry, physically based materials, and carefully composed environments with object- and part-level semantic annotations, providing rich, joint supervision for visual and embodied learning. We demonstrate its value as training data across inverse rendering, 3D reconstruction, scene understanding, and mobile manipulation. Across these tasks, training state-of-the-art methods with our scenes improves performance over alternative synthetic datasets. In addition, we introduce a mobile manipulation benchmark, evaluating vision-language-action models performing physical pick and place in diverse 3D scenes with realistic layouts, and using our part annotations to also score where each object is grasped. Our dataset will be released publicly to facilitate research in visual and embodied AI.

BARISTA: A Multi-Task Egocentric Benchmark for Compositional Visual Understanding 2026-11-24
📝 Accepted CTB Workshop at ICML 2026
Show abstract

Scene understanding is central to general physical intelligence, and video is a primary modality for capturing both state and temporal dynamics of a scene. Yet understanding physical processes remains difficult, as models must combine object localization, hand-object interactions, relational parsing, temporal reasoning, and step-level procedural inference. Existing benchmarks usually evaluate these capabilities separately, limiting diagnosis of why models fail on procedural tasks. We introduce BARISTA, a densely annotated egocentric dataset and benchmark of 185 real-world coffee-preparation videos covering fully automatic, portafilter-based, and capsule-based workflows. BARISTA provides verified per-frame scene graphs linking persistent object identities to masks, tracks, boxes, attributes, typed relations, hand-object interactions, activities, and process steps. From these graphs, we derive zero-shot language-based tasks spanning phrase grounding, hand-object interaction recognition, referring, activity recognition, relation extraction, and temporal visual question answering. Experiments reveal strong variation across task families and no consistently dominant model family, positioning BARISTA as a challenging diagnostic benchmark for procedural video understanding. Code and dataset available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/ramblr/BARISTA.

GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models typically predict actions from current 2D observations, whereas existing 3D policies infer geometry per step without retaining it across an episode. Action generation under occlusion and across multiple stages requires a compact spatial abstraction that persists over time without reconstructing a full 3D scene at each step. Based on this insight, we introduce GeoMem-VLA, a framework that internalizes explicit 3D scene and motion supervision into learnable latent GeoPlantokens, leaving the vision-language backbone's native visual embeddings unaligned. During training, a frozen geometry teacher and demonstration trajectories supervise these tokens via an auxiliary flow-matching head over current scene point maps and future end-effector tracks. Across steps, the policy stores observation and GeoPlan representations in separate memory banks, merges similar adjacent states into event memories, and fuses retrieved history with current features to predict actions. At inference, the 3D teacher and auxiliary head are removed, and action generation relies solely on the latent GeoPlan tokens and episode memory without explicit scene reconstruction. GeoMem-VLA achieves average success rates of 98.9% on LIBERO, 91.10% and 91.24% on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean and Randomized settings, and 78.0% across five real-world manipulation tasks.

RoadState: A Domain-Prior-Enhanced Vision-Language System for Industrial-Grade Lane-Level Map Updating 2026-11-24
📝 12 pages, 5 figures, including appendices. Accepted to AAAI-IAAI 2027
Show abstract

Lane-level maps provide foundational structured road information for navigation, intelligent driving, and intelligent transportation systems. Understanding road-scene semantics from structural and contextual cues is essential for reliable lane-level map updating, yet remains challenging in production due to occlusions, ambiguous lane markings, temporary obstacles, and viewpoint changes, while downstream workflows require temporally consistent, physically valid, and auditable outputs rather than free-form descriptions. We present RoadState, a production-deployed vision-language system that transforms sequential road-scene observations into structured lane-level semantics for Baidu Maps' map-updating pipeline. Its core model, RoadState-VLM, formulates road-scene understanding as autoregressive state estimation, predicting the current structured road state from the previous observation, previous symbolic state, and current observation. To improve semantic consistency and reasoning fidelity across interdependent road-state dimensions, we introduce Geometric Chain-of-Thought (Geo-CoT), which encodes a directed acyclic graph of semantic dependencies as a domain prior. On a real-world dataset, RoadState-VLM achieves 94.33% mean accuracy across six road-state dimensions, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned VLM baseline, with ablations confirming the complementary gains of Geo-CoT and temporal transition context. In production, a cascaded routing pipeline directs routine cases to lightweight models and reserves complex cases for RoadState-VLM. Deployed since March 2025, RoadState supports lane-level map updating across 360+ cities, reduces overall production costs by 30%, and maintains robust performance under stress testing with human-in-the-loop auditing.

SparQ4D: Learning to Act through Sparse 4D Queries 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Successful manipulation requires a policy to understand where things are and how they will move, yet action demonstrations leave this spatiotemporal structure implicit. While explicit 4D supervision has proven effective, reducing the cost of dense 4D prediction remains challenging. This paper introduces SparQ4D, a lightweight yet expressive decoder that efficiently injects holistic 4D scene understanding into policies and improves success rates. At its core is a query-based decoder attached to a pretrained policy, which casts many spatialtemporal tasks into one unified interface (e.g. depth, 2D/3D point motion, multi-view correspondence, and hand-eye relations). This design enables efficient training and inference without sacrificing expressivity: the policy learns rich 4D structure from a small number of sampled queries during training, while the decoder can be removed at inference with zero additional overhead. Across multiple backbones, simulated environments, and real-world settings, SparQ4D achieves state-of-the-art manipulation performance, outperforming competing methods while training up to $5\times$ faster. Probing analyses further reveal that this 4D structure is recoverable from the policy.

PhysRF-WorldJEPA: Transforming Heterogeneous Large Language Models into Manifold-Decoupled Millimeter-Wave Radar World Models 2026-11-24
📝 11 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar enables privacy-preserving, all-weather physical scene understanding across darkness, smoke, and walls. However, standard instruction tuning treats continuous radar streams as static text-like tokens, causing near-random global radar-to-text retrieval and severe physical drift under sensor blackouts. Conversely, coupling pairwise contrastive losses (e.g., SigLIP) directly into the shared backbone improves retrieval but injects $O(B^2)$ repulsive gradients that fracture continuous coordinate manifolds ($R^2 < 0$). We present PhysRF-WorldJEPA, a manifold-decoupled Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) that separates continuous electromagnetic state prediction from cross-modal language alignment. An unattenuated 1.0x clock anchor and parallel dual-scale RF encoder feed a lean ~3% LoRA LLM trained with two-step state-space EMA-JEPA (Denoise -> Rollout) and sample-axis Batch-VICReg to predict +3.0 s physical states; a stop-gradient alignment head then maps frozen prompt states onto the text hypersphere with false-negative masking. On a 4,541-window tri-domain suite (77/60.25/60 GHz) against 10 baselines, freezing the physical backbone and training only the decoupled head lifts R->T R@10 from 5.95% to 15.01% and improves MedRank from 107.0 to 60.0 while preserving identical physical readout: 3.37 deg ($R^2=+0.961$) blackout +3.0 s torso tilt, 1.89 m ($R^2=+0.679$; 1.19 m/+0.863 at the RF front-end) cluttered UAV altitude, 2.04 m ($R^2=+0.461$) behind-wall NLOS range, 0.9976 virtual-RIS invariance, and 100% fall recall. Across five 0.5B-1.8B LLM families (15 zero-overhead models), World-JEPA improves retrieval and 75%-drop completion in 10/10 runs and blackout tilt in 4/5 (Span-Euc), 2/5 (Scatter), and 5/5 (per-family best) over SFT.

SurgGenesis: A Generative Surgical World Model for Future-Aware Surgical Understanding 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Surgical scene understanding is constrained by data scarcity and long-tail class imbalance, as annotating surgical videos demands scarce clinical expertise while rare procedural events remain underrepresented. We tackle this through data generation and propose SurgGenesis, a generative surgical world model that learns future surgical state transitions in a latent space from visual observations and textual procedural descriptions, serving as a scalable surgical data engine. To adapt a large-scale video diffusion model to the data-scarce surgical domain without catastrophic forgetting, we introduce a three-stage progressive LoRA strategy that incrementally injects endoscopic visual priors, procedural semantics, and spatial motion cues. Rather than judging generation by visual fidelity alone, we validate SurgGenesis along two axes that separate a world model from a generic generator: (i) future-awareness, by comparing predicted futures against the real future through a GT-latent prediction gap and temporal-horizon analysis, and (ii) action-faithful usefulness, by checking that generated clips contain the commanded surgical triplet and that using them to rebalance long-tail events and drive downstream understanding (segmentation, triplet recognition, 3D reconstruction) yields action-specific gains. Ground, Guide, Act: Improving VLA Spatial Generalization with Multimodal Foundation Models 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models effectively learn action policies from robot demonstrations, but struggle to generalize to changes in object positions and task goals. General multimodal foundation models (MFMs) offer complementary scene understanding and spatial reasoning, but remain slow and unreliable for direct fine-grained control. To understand how MFMs can improve VLA spatial generalization, we conduct a systematic capability analysis across multiple VLA architectures using atomic-stage interventions, revealing that many failures stem from poor target grounding and global spatial routing, while local manipulation skills remain largely usable. Based on these findings, we propose MFM-VLA, a selective collaboration framework in which an MFM provides target grounding and spatial guidance, controllers execute free-space motion, and the VLA retains control over fine-grained manipulation. Pruning Autonomous Driving VLAs: What to Preserve for Reasoning and Action 2026-11-23
📝 45 pages. Project page: https://cvlab-kaist.github.io/MoOP
Show abstract

Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) models for autonomous driving use chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to guide action generation, but incur substantial memory and computational costs. We study structured pruning for these models, focusing on preserving both scene understanding and trajectory prediction. Our analysis of Taylor importance in Alpamayo-1.5-10B shows that reasoning and trajectory objectives assign different importance to the same vision-language model (VLM) structures. In the action expert, MLP channels exhibit more stable importance rankings across denoising steps than query heads, and a small fraction of channels accounts for most of the measured importance. Based on these findings, we propose MoOP, which combines importance ranks from both objectives for VLM pruning and aggregates stepwise importance to select a fixed subset of expert MLP channels. At 24.0% total parameter reduction, VLM-only pruning with MoOP performs comparably to or slightly better than the strongest baselines in driving, while retaining 94.0% of the dense model's LingoQA score, far above all other methods. At a higher reduction of 30.0%, MoOP degrades much less than every other method, as it is the only one whose reasoning does not collapse. Removing 93.75% of expert MLP channels increases total parameter reduction to 39.4%, with nearly unchanged open-loop errors relative to VLM-only pruning and competitive closed-loop performance. The resulting model achieves a 1.37x end-to-end inference speedup and 36.6% lower peak memory than the dense model, without post-pruning fine-tuning.

RadioWorld: Transferring World Models to Radio-Frequency Radiance Fields 2026-11-22
Show abstract

Electromagnetic (EM) wave propagation in the physical world constitutes a Radio-Frequency (RF) radiance field, providing rich information for 6G communication and sensing. However, existing neural RF field methods suffer from three fundamental limitations: (1) they predominantly rely on per-scene optimization and cannot generalize to unseen scenes; (2) they require costly reference RF measurements in each target scene; and (3) they degrade severely when extrapolating beyond the vicinity of reference measurements. To overcome these limitations, we present RadioWorld, a general framework that adapts a world model to synthesize RF radiance fields directly from physical scene geometry and transceiver locations, without requiring any reference RF measurements at inference. Specifically, RadioWorld couples two specialized modules within a dual-stream Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture: a Scene-Understanding Transformer that encodes 3D scene geometry and transceiver locations to extract environment-aware global EM propagation features, and a Field-Generation Transformer that attends to these features to synthesize the RF radiance field with the local query conditions. We also establish UrbanRF-Bench, a large-scale ray-traced benchmark spanning 657 urban scenes and 32,850 RF radiance fields across seven global cities. Extensive experiments demonstrate that RadioWorld significantly outperforms specialized baselines and accurately synthesizes RF fields at arbitrary locations in unseen cities. Code, datasets, and checkpoints will be released upon acceptance.

Decision-making and motion planning constitute critical components for ensuring the safety and efficiency of autonomous vehicles (AVs). Existing methodologies typically adopt two paradigms: decision then planning or generation then scoring. However, the former architecture often suffers from decision-planning misalignment that incurs risky situations. Meanwhile, the latter struggles to balance short-term operational metrics (e.g., immediate motion smoothness) with long-term tactical goals (e.g., route efficiency), resulting in myopic or overly conservative behaviors. To address these issues, we introduce PRIMA, a preference-informed multimodal trajectory planning framework assisted by a Vision-Language Model (VLM). Comprehensive simulation-based closed-loop evaluations on nuPlan and InterPlan demonstrate competitive performance across common and long-tail benchmarks. ViewAgent3D: A Training-Free Multi-View Pipeline for 3D Scene Understanding 2026-11-21
Show abstract

3D scene understanding tasks are often approached by building explicit 3D scene representations or by training specialized multi-modal models over point clouds, voxels, or scene graphs. We take a complementary view: for many queries, the main bottleneck is not the absence of a stronger 3D representation but the selection of a small subset of multi-view RGB views that actually makes the query answerable. We present a training-free, query-conditioned multi-view pipeline that performs diverse tasks using only 2D images and a set of tools used purely for inference. Our method first decomposes each query into a set of concepts that identify unique attributes that assist with reasoning. This drives a coarse-to-fine evidence selection process: a coarse retrieval selects and scores all views, a diversity-aware selector reduces redundancy, and a reranking stage helps shortlist views by target visibility or answerability. The resulting evidence is optionally grounded for queries that benefit from explicit localization, and a self-verification step helps refine the final response, especially for open-ended cases. Despite using no task-specific training and no explicit 3D representations at inference time, our approach provides approximately 10% gain on 3D VQA and 5% gain on dense captioning and Embodied QA while being applicable across different scenarios. We further propose a unique judge-based evaluation system, motivating a judge who takes into account many semantically correct or partially correct answers, thereby improving holistic scene understanding.

Learning Continuous Scene Depth Representations via Cross-Modal Gaussian Rendering 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Depth super-resolution with color guidance is a fundamental problem in geometric scene understanding that aims to reconstruct high-resolution depth using structural cues from color images. In practical RGB-D systems, the resolution gap between depth and color images often varies, requiring flexible upsampling factors. However, many existing methods are designed for predefined integer scales. Although implicit methods support arbitrary-scale reconstruction, they typically rely on computationally demanding per-pixel neural decoding and may struggle to recover sharp depth boundaries and fine structures. To this end, we propose a cross-modal Gaussian rendering framework that learns continuous depth representations for arbitrary-scale depth super-resolution. Specifically, we first construct hierarchical Gaussian representations for progressive depth reconstruction, with explicit spatial support defined by learnable Gaussian primitives. Building on this representation, we design a Structure-Aware Gaussian Aggregation module that combines spatial weights with cross-modal feature affinities to reduce aggregation across depth discontinuities. We then develop a Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Rendering module that adapts Gaussian spatial kernels to the target pixel footprint. To further facilitate cross-modal learning, we propose a training strategy that gradually introduces higher-frequency guidance information during training. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method at both in-distribution and out-of-distribution upsampling scales.

3DVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models via 3D Spatial and Instance Understanding 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action models have achieved remarkable progress in robotic manipulation, yet they suffer from a critical limitation: a lack of 3D scene understanding. This deficiency manifests as three intertwined challenges: weak extraction of 3D spatial positions without enforcing multi-view consistency, inadequate 3D instance understanding, and fragile reasoning under occlusion. Although mature 3D perception methods exist, their direct integration into VLA pipelines is hindered by architectural incompatibility and by heavy reliance on costly instance-level annotations. To address these challenges, we propose 3DVLA, a framework compatible with existing VLA models, which introduces 3D understanding without requiring extra manual labels or discarding VLM priors. Results show consistent and significant gains in manipulation performance, validating both the effectiveness and plug-and-play compatibility of our approach.

An Autonomous Adaptive Learning System for Robotic Grasping in the Circular Factory 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Linear manufacturing typically operates in highly structured settings with standardized objects, allowing robots to execute pre-programmed motions based on precise models. In contrast, circular manufacturing involves dynamic and less controlled environments, where product components can vary due to appearance and physical changes across different product generations. In this context, uncertainty arises from both the unknown or partially observed states of components (e.g., damaged surfaces, altered shapes) and the unstructured disassembly environment, often characterized by heavy clutter, occlusions, and limited sensing viewpoints. This demands a flexible and adaptable robotic grasping system that can cope with unexpected object changes. To address these challenges, this paper proposes an autonomous grasp learning framework designed for highly uncertain and variable conditions. The framework explicitly distinguishes between uncertainty due to noisy or incomplete sensory observations (aleatoric uncertainty) and uncertainty due to limited knowledge and distribution shift (epistemic uncertainty). Moreover, the framework employs an active perception strategy to reduce visual ambiguity: semantic and spatial scene understanding is achieved using vision language models (VLMs), while view planning enables the robot to adjust its viewpoint to improve observability. In addition, the approach leverages large-scale human demonstration data to learn interaction patterns, which are then transferred to robotic end-effectors, enabling robust manipulation in these variable settings. Quantitative validation, including the utility of active vision on disassembled components in practice, is expected to form an important part of future work.

Backbone Matters: Benchmarking Encoder-Decoder Networks for Semantic Segmentation of Driving Scenes on BDD100K 2026-11-19
📝 6 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Semantic segmentation gives an autonomous vehicle a dense, pixel-level description of its surroundings and is a core part of scene understanding. Most segmentation networks consist of an encoder (the backbone) and a decoder, yet the backbone is often chosen without a systematic comparison. This work studies this choice on the BDD100K driving dataset. Four widely used decoders (U-Net, FPN, LinkNet and PSPNet) are combined with 13 backbones from six families (ResNet, VGG, DenseNet, Inception, MobileNet and EfficientNet), and all 52 models are trained with one common protocol. The results and the scope of this study are discussed.

Show abstract

Identifying a driver's gaze target provides object-level information for visual attention monitoring in intelligent vehicles. Gaze-zone classification and continuous attention localization provide limited information about the identity of the attended scene target. Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation requires layouts satisfying geometric, functional, and semantic constraints.
