SpaceCast-Bench: Evaluating Predictive Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models 2026-10-16
📝 Project Page: https://zjureal.com/SpaceCast-Bench Code: https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/SpaceCast-Bench
Show abstract

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks mainly test spatial perception: reading off relations already visible in the input. Yet real-world spatial intelligence demands predictive spatial reasoning: constructing a scene from observations, anticipating how an intervention changes it, and reasoning about the unseen outcome. We introduce SpaceCast-Bench, the first benchmark to directly and diagnostically evaluate this capability. Built around an observe-transform-infer framework, its 3,862 questions from 182 real-world scenes span 16 task types at three levels: static perception, local prediction, and global prediction, progressively requiring scene understanding, spatial state updating, and relational inference over unobserved outcomes. Evaluating 21 models exposes a stark gap: the strongest model reaches only 58.0% against 87.2% human performance, while spatially specialized models remain near random chance. Controlled analyses further reveal that bridge views are critical for integrating distributed observations, and that explicit 3D evidence benefits models more reliably than generated outcome images or videos. Fine-tuning on our programmatically generated data lifts Qwen3-VL-4B from 34.0% to 65.7% with macro-average gains across six out-of-domain benchmarks.

VISTA: A Multimodal Egocentric Benchmark for Goal-Oriented Perception and Assistance to Blind and Low-Vision Users 2026-10-16
📝 Code and dataset are available at https://harvard-ai-and-robotics-lab.github.io/VISTA
Show abstract

Blindness and low vision limit many people's ability to complete daily tasks, motivating AI assistants that provide useful guidance. Yet, existing benchmarks do not adequately test whether vision models can provide effective everyday assistance. In particular, i) existing benchmarks primarily rely on visual and textual inputs, with limited support for other modalities that are increasingly available in egocentric wearable devices such as gaze, inertial measurements, spatial trajectories, and others; and ii) existing evaluations emphasize scene understanding and question answering, with limited coverage of goal-conditioned guidance for blind and low-vision users across everyday tasks. To bridge this gap, we introduce VISTA (Visually Impaired Scene and Task Annotations), a multimodal egocentric benchmark for goal-oriented assistance for blind and low-vision users. VISTA contains $\sim$1,000 egocentric recordings captured with Project Aria glasses; each recording provides synchronized sensor streams, six of which serve as model inputs, and three different types of annotation, covering ten task categories (TC), from object localization to hazard detection. We evaluate 14 representative vision-language models zero-shot on VISTA and design a lightweight multimodal model baseline that integrates all six modalities. Experimental results show that, although existing models can often describe egocentric scenes, they remain limited in generating goal-directed assistive guidance. VISTA thus provides a benchmark for evaluating multimodal models that could improve everyday accessibility.

VesperMUSE: Multi-Modal Fusion Vision Transformer for Image Semantic Segmentation Under Diverse Light Conditions 2026-10-15
📝 A dissertation submitted to The University of Manchester for the degree of Bachelor of Science in Computer Science
Show abstract

Semantic segmentation is critical for the reliable navigation and scene understanding of autonomous systems, yet its performance degrades severely in low-light environments due to noise, artifacts, colour distortion, and the loss of structural details. To address this challenge, this work introduces VesperMUSE, a unified multi-modal framework designed to achieve robust image semantic segmentation across diverse illumination conditions. The architecture addresses the light deficiency problem on multiple levels: proposing a novel physics-informed synthetic low-light adaptation algorithm, approaching Low-Light Image Enhancement (LLIE) through a thermal-guided paradigm, and finally aiming to recover the intrinsic scene details for semantic segmentation through a multi-modal fusion approach. Following enhancement, the framework dynamically integrates the primary RGB features with auxiliary thermal representations and surface normal maps highlighting geometric discontinuities and material boundaries. At the core of this integration is a highly scalable, modality-agnostic self-attention mechanism that extracts both global context and fine local features from an arbitrary number of auxiliary inputs. By combining and projecting these hierarchical multi-modal representations into a unified component, the attention module allows the network to selectively attend to relevant cross-modal cues without requiring any architectural modifications when different sensor configurations are used. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that VesperMUSE successfully recovers missing visual cues, providing a flexible, state-of-the-art (SOTA) solution for machine perception in extreme and unstructured real-world environments.

Hear, Localize, and Reason: Spatially Aware Scene Understanding for Audio-Visual LLMs 2026-10-15
📝 NeurIPS 2026; Project page: https://hlrllm.github.io/
Show abstract

Audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) have made strong progress in multimodal understanding, yet most treat audio as monaural semantic content, limiting their ability to reason about where sounds originate and how they relate to the visual scene. Recent spatial audio-visual studies address parts of this problem, but often focus on specific abilities, such as spatial correspondence or direction and distance reasoning. In this work, we introduce HLR-AVSceneQA, a comprehensive benchmark with a hear-localize-reason hierarchy that evaluates whether models can recognize what is heard, localize sound sources in egocentric and allocentric views, and use these localized sources to reason about how they relate to the visual scene. We further introduce HLR-LLM, which augments a strong audio-visual foundation model with a binaural spatial audio branch alongside its monaural semantic audio pathway. We train HLR-LLM with a curriculum that incorporates final-stage chain-of-thought supervision for multi-step spatial reasoning. Experiments show that existing AV-LLMs remain limited in spatial grounding and relational reasoning, while HLR-LLM substantially improves spatial audio-visual scene understanding without compromising semantic audio-visual perception.

RoboAtlas: Contextual Active SLAM 2026-10-15
📝 Alexander Schperberg and Shivam K. Panda made equal contribution

Show abstract

We present RoboAtlas, a contextual Active SLAM framework that adaptively balances geometric exploration and semantic reasoning using a scalable 3D semantic mapping system, OpenRoboVox. RoboAtlas integrates frontier exploration, global semantic-map reasoning, and egocentric VLM-based reasoning through a contextual multi-armed bandit that transitions from exploration to semantically guided navigation as scene understanding improves. We evaluate the system in simulation and on a Unitree Go2 robot in large-scale real-world environments exceeding 1800 m2 with approx. 30k mapped semantic instances, achieving a 100% task success rate. On the GOAT-Bench "Val Unseen" benchmark, RoboAtlas achieves state-of-the-art performance with highest reported success rate (SR) of 90.6%, using GPT-4o, improving over the strongest prior baseline by 17.8 percentage points in SR. Using the much smaller Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, it still achieves 88.8% SR, outperforming all baselines using GPT-4o in SR, and revealing the importance of the information gained by our semantic mapping framework over simply replacing the underlying foundation model. The results demonstrate that grounding foundation models with large-scale 3D semantic maps enables robust and efficient contextual Active SLAM.

Lluna: Collaborative Unimodal Models for Missing-Modality Inference 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Pretrained unimodal models offer a flexible route to multimodal prediction through collaboration, but this collaboration can become less accurate than a surviving unimodal model when modalities are missing. We study this setting using fixed surviving-model anchors and find that collaborative gains can weaken or reverse as inputs disappear, even when the anchor's own input is unchanged. Our diagnostics further reveal reduced recovery of modality-specific information after aggregation and state-dependent collaborative representations. We propose Lluna, which combines a direct private prediction path from adapted unimodal features with a common graph-based collaboration branch that provides a residual correction. Progressive Collaboration Consistency organizes richer-to-poorer guidance along single-modality removals, aligning normalized common representations while task supervision adapts predictions to the available evidence. Across eight datasets spanning affective computing, scene understanding, and medical prediction, Lluna achieves the best evaluated average performance over non-full input states, improving Mean-Missing by 4.26\% on average relative to the strongest evaluated baseline. The fixed-anchor diagnostic also shows positive gains in mean collaboration under minimal inputs on all eight tasks. Code is available at https://github.com/AIFrontierLab/Lluna.

OLiVES: An Outdoor Low-Light Video Benchmark for Enhancement and Segmentation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Low-light video processing remains an underexplored problem in computer vision, primarily due to the scarcity of high-quality ground-truth data. Existing public datasets exhibit several limitations, including restricted motion diversity, limited scene coverage, weak degradations, reliance on auxiliary modalities such as event data, and misalignment between low-light inputs and ground truth. Furthermore, current datasets typically address either low-light enhancement or scene understanding, whereas these tasks should be considered jointly. We introduce \textbf{OLiVES}, a benchmark dataset for outdoor low-light videos with dynamic scenes, designed to support both low-light video enhancement (LLVE) and video object segmentation (VOS). The dataset addresses key content gaps by incorporating a diverse range of natural textures, animals, occlusions, dynamic activities, and close-up scenes. To the best of our knowledge, OLiVES is the first outdoor low-light video dataset captured using professional cinema-grade cameras. It provides over 25,000 aligned normal- and low-light frame pairs, along with over 46,000 VOS annotations. We benchmark state-of-the-art LLVE and VOS methods on OLiVES and show that training on it improves performance on both tasks. The OLiVES project page is available at: https://anonymous-OLiVES.github.io/.

HERO: Learning Humanoid End-Effector Control for Visual Whole-Body Open-Vocabulary Object Grasping 2026-10-14
📝 CoRL 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Visual loco-manipulation of arbitrary in-the-wild objects requires accurate end-effector (EE) control and a generalizable understanding of the scene from visual inputs (eg, RGB-D images). Existing imitation and sim2real methods jointly learn both these aspects via monolithic end-to-end learning and are thus hard to scale. In this work, we bring to bear the best tools for each of these problems -- large vision models for generalizable scene understanding and simulated training for accurate EE control -- leading to an overall modular loco-manipulation system that exhibits strong generalization. Our core technical innovation is HERO, an accurate residual-aware EE tracking policy made possible by combining classical robotics with machine learning. It uses a) inverse kinematics to convert residual end-effector targets into reference trajectories, b) a learned neural forward model for accurate forward kinematics, and c) goal adjustment and replanning. Together, these innovations reduce the end-effector tracking error to 2.44cm, outperforming the strongest prior method by 5.5x. Our overall system operates in diverse real-world environments, from offices to coffee shops, where the robot reliably grasps various everyday objects (eg, mugs, apples, toys) on surfaces ranging from 43cm to 92cm in height. Systematic modular and end-to-end tests demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed design. We believe our advances open up new ways of training humanoids to interact with daily objects. Project page: https://hero-humanoid.github.io/

LAMP: 3D Source-Aware Lighting Estimation at Multiple Points 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Existing illumination estimation methods primarily recover lighting distributions for rendering, but typically leave the contributions of individual physical light sources at specific scene locations implicit. We address this gap by leveraging the scene understanding capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to connect local illumination with its physical sources. We introduce LAMP, a framework for point-conditioned, source-aware illumination reasoning from a single RGB image with marked query points. LAMP constructs a shared inventory of active light sources, ranks contributing sources for each query, and predicts their 3D query-to-source directions. We supervise these relationships using procedurally controlled indoor scenes, reusing independently rendered source responses to derive contribution targets across lighting configurations and obtaining exact directions from source and query positions. On the Infinigen test set, fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B increases dominant-source 3D PCK@0.5m from 0% to 70.6%, with 94.5% of directions within 12.5 degrees of ground truth among correctly localized sources. Combined with a lightweight network predicting dense RGB irradiance, LAMP achieves probe-rendering quality comparable to prior lighting-estimation methods while additionally localizing individual physical sources in 3D and explicitly attributing local illumination to them.

Write Once, Plan Many: Building Reusable Visual Planning Systems from One Scene 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) can reason about visual planning tasks, but querying them for every instance incurs repeated inference cost and latency. Generating reusable planning programs offers an alternative, but constructing reliable planning systems for new problem domains without extensive supervision remains challenging. We present a training-free framework that constructs reusable perception and planning programs from a domain specification and a single practice scene without a paired solution. The central challenge is obtaining useful feedback from the limited input. Our framework addresses it by leveraging VLM's ability to provide a renderer and a dynamic model based on its scene understanding. Rendering detected states enables comparison with the practice scene, while checking simulated action outcomes against the domain specification helps expose errors in rule implementation. Together, these checks guide refinement of the grounding program and planner without requiring solution labels. After refinement and component selection, the programs are frozen and solve new instances without further VLM calls. The same framework constructs systems across domains without manual pipeline redesign. Across fifteen domain variants spanning 2D and 3D scenes with discrete and continuous state, our systems achieve 99.3% average planning success, compared with 80.9% for the strongest domain-level program-generation baseline and 90.7% for the strongest instance-level baseline. Cost analyses show a favorable success-cost trade-off, and ablations confirm the contributions of the executable domain model, execution feedback, and component selection.

PROSE: Training-Free Egocentric Scene Registration with Vision-Language Models 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Registering two captures of the same indoor space taken at different times underpins persistent spatial memory for robots and AR systems, yet the realistic version of this task is egocentric and its most scalable form is RGB-only. Head-mounted cameras yield blurry, fast-moving, partially overlapping views from which dense geometry is hard to recover. Classical registration leans on exactly the clean point clouds this setting lacks, while learned scene-graph methods require a pre-built or annotated graph and a trained matcher that we find brittle under egocentric data. We take a different route, using a pretrained vision-language model as the source of both scene understanding and cross-scan matching. Our method, PROSE (Prompted Scene rEgistration), lifts each RGB sequence into an object-level 3D scene graph using off-the-shelf foundation models for geometry, segmentation, and language, then prompts the same VLM to match object instances across the two RGB sequences. To make this matching tractable and reliable, we leverage object heights as a prior and verify each proposed match with a paired same/different query, then solve for the rigid transform by hypothesizing a candidate per matched object and selecting the one with the strongest geometric consensus. PROSE adds no learned parameters and requires no depth sensor, training, or annotated graph. On the egocentric Aria Digital Twin and Aria Everyday Activities benchmarks, it outperforms both geometric and learned scene-graph baselines in registration accuracy, on ground-truth and RGB-reconstructed point clouds alike, and the scene graph it produces transfers directly to downstream tasks.

Perception Beyond the Visible: Incompleteness-Aware Scene Understanding for Robot Autonomy 2026-10-13
📝 Research statement for RSS Pioneers 2026 (Robotics: Science and Systems). OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=wP5JjTHXnQ

Reliable robot autonomy requires reasoning about the world from partial and noisy observations. However, many perception systems model only visible scene regions, rely on fixed semantic categories, and assume reliable sensor measurements. These assumptions break under occlusion, novel object encounters, and sensor degradation. In this paper, I present a research vision for incompleteness-aware scene understanding. I organize this vision around three fundamental challenges: incomplete evidence, incomplete knowledge, and incomplete sensor reliability. I discuss my research contributions toward addressing these challenges through amodal perception, semantic occupancy forecasting, open-world scene understanding, label-efficient learning, and uncertainty-guided multi-modal fusion. I also discuss active sensing and safe adaptation strategies that allow robots to reduce uncertainty through interaction. The goal is to develop calibrated and actionable scene representations that support reliable autonomy in dynamic, open-world environments.

Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved strong image and video understanding, yet their visual-spatial representations remain geometrically fragile, leading to failures in spatial reasoning needed for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous driving. Prior approaches to geometry grounding either fine-tune VLMs on spatial question answering, which can perpetuate spurious visual representations, or fuse features from large geometry-grounded vision models, which substantially increases model size at inference. Knowledge distillation from geometry-grounded vision models offers an alternative, but directly matching multi-view teacher features can disrupt the pretrained alignment between visual and textual representations, degrading object- and language-semantic capabilities. We propose multi-view relational distillation (MVRD), which distills patch-wise cosine similarities across views instead of the teacher features themselves. These relations encode geometric correspondences adequate for spatial understanding, while leaving the student representation underdetermined, allowing it to remain close to its pretrained vision- language space. Across representative VLMs, MVRD improves visual-spatial reasoning, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and feature distillation while approaching feature fusion methods with considerably fewer added parameters and lower latency. We show that MVRD makes visual representations more geometric while retaining language alignment, and generalizes to 3D scene understanding tasks such as object grounding, dense captioning, and question answering.

AURORA-KITTI: Any-Weather Depth Completion and Denoising 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Robust depth completion is fundamental to real-world 3D scene understanding, yet existing RGB--LiDAR fusion methods degrade significantly under adverse weather, where both camera images and LiDAR measurements suffer from weather-induced corruption. In this paper, we introduce AURORA-KITTI, the first large-scale, physics-consistent synthetic RGB-LiDAR benchmark for any-weather depth completion and denoising. We further formulate Depth Completion and Denoising~(DCD) as a unified task that reconstructs clean dense metric depth from weather-corrupted RGB images and sparse LiDAR inputs while suppressing weather-induced noise. AURORA-KITTI contains over 82K weather-consistent RGB--LiDAR pairs with metric depth supervision, spanning diverse weather types, three severity levels, day/night scenes, paired clean references, lens occlusion conditions, and textual descriptions.Moreover, we provide DDCD as a strong and efficient distillation-based reference baseline. DDCD is trained on AURORA-KITTI using a frozen teacher applied to paired clean RGB images only during training; at inference, it uses weather-corrupted RGB and sparse LiDAR. Experiments on AURORA-KITTI and the real-world DENSE benchmark show that existing depth completion and foundation depth models remain vulnerable under severe weather, while DDCD improves over weather-aware retraining. Controlled experiments further show that physics-consistent adverse-weather data is the dominant driver of robustness, with clean-prior distillation providing complementary gains. Comparisons with off-the-shelf zero-shot methods use different training protocols; same-data retraining and component ablations separately quantify the contributions of weather-aware data and DDCD. Github: https://github.com/Warwick-Jocelyn/AURORA-KITTI.

Panoramic Out-of-Distribution Segmentation 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted to IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems for Video Technology (TCSVT). Code and datasets will be available at https://github.com/MengfeiD/PanOoS
Show abstract

Panoramic imaging enables capturing 360° images with an ultra-wide Field-of-View (FoV) for dense omnidirectional perception, which is critical to applications such as autonomous driving and augmented reality. However, current panoramic semantic segmentation methods fail to identify outliers, and pinhole out-of-distribution segmentation (pinhole-OoS) models perform unsatisfactorily in the panoramic domain due to pixel distortions and background clutter. To address these issues, we introduce a new task, Panoramic Out-of-Distribution Segmentation (PanOoS), with the aim of achieving comprehensive and safe scene understanding. Furthermore, we propose the first solution, POS, which adapts to the characteristics of panoramic images through text-guided prompt distribution learning. Specifically, POS integrates a disentanglement strategy designed to materialize the cross-domain generalization capability of CLIP. The proposed Prompt-based Restoration Attention (PRA) optimizes semantic decoding by prompt guidance and self-adaptive correction, while Bilevel Prompt Distribution Learning (BPDL) refines the manifold of per-pixel mask embeddings via semantic prototype supervision. Besides, to compensate for the scarcity of PanOoS datasets, we establish two benchmarks: DenseOoS, which features diverse outliers in complex environments, and QuadOoS, captured by a quadruped robot with a panoramic annular lens system. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of POS, with AuPRC increasing and FPR95 decreasing by 34.25 and 21.42 percentage points, respectively, compared with RbA on DenseOoS, outperforming state-of-the-art pinhole-OoS methods. Moreover, POS achieves leading closed-set segmentation capabilities and advances the development of panoramic understanding. Code and datasets will be available at https://github.com/MengfeiD/PanOoS.

Articulated 3D Scene Graphs for Open-World Mobile Manipulation 2026-10-12
📝 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026)
Show abstract

Semantic scene understanding has enabled robots to reason and perform affordance-driven object interaction. Despite this, real-world manipulation remains fundamentally limited by the inability to predict how objects move and are articulated. Long-horizon mobile manipulation requires closing the gap between semantics, geometry, and kinematics. In this work, we present MoMa-SG, a novel framework for building semantic-kinematic 3D scene graphs of articulated scenes containing a myriad of interactable objects. Given RGB-D sequences containing multiple object articulations, we temporally segment object interactions and infer object motion using occlusion-robust point tracking. We lift point trajectories to 3D and estimate articulation models using a novel unified twist estimation formulation that robustly estimates revolute and prismatic joint parameters in a single optimization pass. Next, we associate objects with articulations and detect contained objects by reasoning over parent-child relations at identified opening states. We also introduce the Arti4D-Semantic dataset, which provides parent-child relation annotations between container and contained objects together with object axis annotations across 62 in-the-wild RGB-D sequences containing 600 object interactions and three distinct observation paradigms. We extensively evaluate the performance of MoMa-SG on two datasets and ablate key design choices of our approach. In addition, real-world experiments on both a quadruped and a mobile manipulator demonstrate robust manipulation of articulated objects in everyday home environments. We provide code and data at: https://momasg.cs.uni-freiburg.de.

ConDenseDrive: Knowledge-Dense, Token-Sparse Visual Encoder for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-12
📝 17 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

End-to-end driving requires rich spatiotemporal representations to support scene understanding and trajectory planning under strict latency constraints. However, most systems initialize their visual encoders from generic image pretraining and densely process all patch tokens for each camera view and input frame, limiting both representation quality and inference efficiency. To address these challenges, we present ConDenseDrive, a knowledge-dense and token-sparse visual encoder for end-to-end autonomous driving. Our three-stage framework first makes the encoder knowledge-dense by distilling complementary visual, semantic, and geometric knowledge from three vision foundation models. It then adapts the distilled encoder to multi-frame end-to-end driving and integrates spatiotemporal context into register tokens for planning. Finally, it makes the encoder token-sparse by applying register-guided token compression, reducing redundant patch computation and yielding a 2.0$\times$ speedup while improving planning performance. ConDenseDrive achieves 94.5 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 90.8 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, and reaches 37.4 HD-Score in zero-shot evaluation on HUGSIM. Extensive experiments and qualitative analyses demonstrate that visual encoder design provides an effective route to reliable and efficient end-to-end driving.

Task-Aligned Stability Analysis of Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving Hazard Detection 2026-10-12
📝 8 pages (5 main body + 3 references / appendices). ICML 2026 Workshop on Combining Theory and Benchmarks (CTB)
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used for scene understanding in autonomous driving, but robustness analysis often relies on task-agnostic embedding stability alone. We study whether corruption-induced embedding drift predicts changes in a task-aligned hazard score derived from CLIP image-text similarities. Using controlled corruptions on BDD100K road scenes, we compare embedding drift against margin drift, defined as the change in hazard score under perturbation. The relationship is highly corruption-dependent: some families exhibit strong coupling between representation drift and decision drift, while others induce hazardous decision instability despite relatively modest embedding change. Furthermore, corruption families differ in failure direction: most suppress hazard detections via false negatives, while occlusion instead triggers false alarms, suggesting that benchmark design should account for asymmetric failure modes, not just overall instability rates. These results suggest that robustness benchmarks should include task-aligned stability measures in addition to embedding-level perturbation statistics.

From Failure Prediction to Component Selection in Semantic Segmentation 2026-10-12
📝 55 pages total: 29-page main paper with 9 figures and 2 tables, followed by 26-page supplementary material with 5 figures and 20 tables. Evaluation code, experimental specifications, and numerical results: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23276040
Show abstract

Reliable scene understanding requires deciding which predictions to trust, retain, or inspect. In semantic segmentation, component failure predictors support these decisions, but their predictive performance does not fully describe the value of the resulting selection. We develop a decision-centered evaluation framework that connects three questions: what an available score predicts, what can be learned from its existing fields, and what its ranking delivers. Across three segmentation systems on Cityscapes and ACDC at 90% retention, adapted MetaSeg reduces retained failure relative to forecast entropy in all twelve architecture-population comparisons, but does not establish a failure advantage over direct probability ranking. Image-nested source experiments reveal a different opportunity: reusing the full field set improves predictive and retained-loss performance under both learners in all three systems, while the raw OCRNet probe improves prediction with unresolved selective gains. Mapping a fixed prediction pair to conditional log-loss risk produces mixed selection effects, which we examine through the components exchanged by the two rankings. These findings distinguish the performance of a fitted score from the value obtainable from its inputs and the consequences of its use. The framework provides a practical basis for deciding whether to adopt an additional score, relearn existing fields, or revise a selection rule, and for explaining the gains and failures of component-level reliability methods.

SlotAVS: Object-Centric Audio-Visual Segmentation via Bi-Modal Slot Attention 2026-10-11
Show abstract

Audio-Visual Segmentation (AVS) requires predicting pixel-wise masks of sounding objects by grounding acoustic signals in visual space. In multi-source scenes, concurrent sound sources often remain entangled within shared visual representations, leading to merged masks or unstable specialization when using standard alignment-focused transformer architectures. To address this, we present SlotAVS, an object-centric framework driven by competitive bi-modal slot attention. While robust foundation models provide rich semantic priors, SlotAVS shifts the paradigm from global cross-modal fusion to explicit competitive binding. By initializing discrete object slots from temporal audio sequences and enforcing strict slot-wise competition over spatial visual tokens, our method structurally mitigates the blending of distinct acoustic entities. Further, to stabilize instance-level learning, we introduce a Hungarian matching strategy that optimally aligns slots with ground-truth objects using spatial and boundary overlap. Coupled with a numerically stable, confidence-aware mask fusion mechanism, SlotAVS robustly aggregates multi-source predictions. Extensive experiments on the AVSBench dataset demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, achieving 88.6% mIoU on the Single-Source (S4) split and 71.7% mIoU on the Multi-Source (MS3) split. These results show that explicit competitive binding is critical for complex audio-visual scene understanding and effectively leveraging foundation-model priors without relying solely on their capacity.

SpikeOcc: Towards Resilient Multimodal 3D Occupancy Perception via Spiking Experts 2026-10-11
Show abstract

Multimodal 3D occupancy prediction underpins safe autonomous driving through comprehensive scene understanding. However, existing methods have largely pursued higher accuracy through sophisticated fusion, leaving resilience to missing sensors underexplored. In this paper, we investigate spiking neural networks (SNNs) as a means of building more resilient multimodal perception. Still, the discrete nature of spiking neurons makes optimization challenging. To overcome this difficulty, SpikeOcc is introduced with two complementary components, a Spiking Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) and Sensor Availability Learning (SAL). Specifically, SMoE organizes RGB, LiDAR, and joint fusion into specialized pathways, enabling full-width unimodal representations while retaining the original multimodal features via a bounded residual. Beyond sensor dropout, SAL employs a staged curriculum with explicit routing supervision, progressively improving robustness under severe sensing conditions. Remarkably, SAL also yields notable gains for conventional models, highlighting its broader potential for resilient multimodal learning.
