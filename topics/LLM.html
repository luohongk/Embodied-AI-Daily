PixelMoE: Scaling Pixel-Space Diffusion with LLM-Style Mixture-of-Experts 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Pixel-space diffusion models provide an appealing alternative to latent-space diffusion models by directly modeling raw images without relying on a learned tokenizer. However, scaling pixel-space models has predominantly followed dense strategies, tightly coupling model capacity with active computation. In this work, we show that standard LLM-style Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures can be effectively transferred to pixel-space diffusion. Building on this finding, we introduce PixelMoE, a pixel-space generative framework that sparsely scales model capacity with standard LLM-style MoE, without diffusion-specific routing mechanisms or auxiliary routing objectives. Specifically, PixelMoE combines standard Token-Choice (TC) routing with fine-grained routed experts for input-adaptive sparse computation, a shared expert for capturing common representations, and a bias-based load-balancing strategy. To complement sparse capacity scaling, PixelMoE further introduces Pixel-Space Hierarchical Flow Matching, which varies spatial resolution along the denoising trajectory from global structure formation to fine-grained detail refinement. At comparable active parameter counts, PixelMoE consistently outperforms its dense counterparts across model scales, demonstrating effective sparse capacity scaling in pixel space. On class-conditional ImageNet-1K, PixelMoE achieves FID scores of 1.47 at 256x256 and 1.53 at 512x512 without representation alignment or perceptual losses, comparing favorably with leading pixel-space and latent-space generative models. Back-Translation Still Matters: Leveraging Monolingual Data for Low-Resource Machine Translation with LLMs 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Pre-trained Large Language Model (LLMs) are effective on many tasks in zero-shot and few-shot settings. For machine translation of low-resource languages, the obvious options are few-shot prompting and fine-tuning on a small parallel set. We ask: what if moderate amounts of low-resource monolingual data are also available? We argue that, rather than continued pretraining, a more effective use of this data is the pre-LLM era technique of back-translation (BT). The current fine-tuned LLM translates monolingual sentences in each language, and the resulting pairs train it in the reverse direction. Updates on the parallel set are interleaved for stable learning. With 1K parallel pairs on Odia and Swahili, BT beats continued pretraining into the low-resource language by 7.7 to 16.4 XCOMET-XXL points in domain. With beam-decoded intermediate translations, it also beats a model with 10 time more parallel data. In-context examples retrieved from the parallel data further improve BT. We also analyze the source, quality and amount of monolingual text. Across five low-resource languages, with Hindi as a high-resource control, and two 3B backbones, BT improves translation into the low-resource language by 16 to 173% XCOMET-XXL over parallel-only fine-tuning. Blind human evaluation on Odia and Sanskrit agrees with the automatic metrics.

Where Do LLM Ensemble Gains Come From? Decomposing Candidate Value, Diversity, and Aggregation 2026-10-13
📝 14 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

LLM ensemble gains are often measured against a single baseline, but the value attributed to additional candidates can change with the comparison used. We study an LLM aggregator under four matched conditions: Direct (no candidates), Single-slot (one candidate in a multi-slot interface), Duplicate (one candidate repeated), and Multi-candidate (separately generated candidates). Under either non-diverse control, overall candidate gain (CIV) splits algebraically into an additional-candidate contrast (DSV) and a candidate-context contrast (CCV); the split, but not CIV, depends on the chosen control. On MultiHopRAG model diversity (k=2), the repetition gap Rep=-8.33 pp [-13.25, -3.75] quantifies the difference between gains measured against Duplicate and Single-slot. On a matched FRAMES stronger-panel extension (n=200, k=3, aggregator output limit 4096), paired Delta Rep=+9.67 pp [+6.00, +13.50]; within panels, Rep changes sign (original -6.33; stronger +3.33). In FRAMES evidence aggregation, candidate-context contrasts remain strongly negative under both non-diverse controls. Our results show why ensemble evaluations should state the reference control before attributing gains to additional candidates.

📝 Dianjing Fan and Yao Li equally contributed to this work. 7 pages, 1 figure
Show abstract

Sequential user behavior modeling is widely adopted in industrial recommender systems; however, significant gaps remain in financial services, where pre-login web interactions and authenticated in-app experiences differ drastically. Specifically, pre-login web users typically explore new products, whereas logged-in app users focus on account servicing. Due to the challenge of cross-channel entity resolution (e.g., matching anonymous web sessions to authenticated mobile accounts), web-based intent signals remain underutilized for post-authentication personalization. Existing methods for capturing web-based intent are often ad-hoc and narrow, lacking the flexibility to support both quantitative downstream recommendations and qualitative understanding at scale. On the user conversion prediction task, the embedding outperforms the LLM labels by 4.3% on micro F1, while the distillation layer delivers interpretable labels at ultra-low latency with only a 7% performance drop.

📝 13 pages body and 4 pages appendix, 19 pages total. Accepted to the 2026 ACM SIGOPS Annual Technical Conference (SIGOPS ATC 2026)
Show abstract

As LLM inference becomes a major cloud workload, its growing energy footprint makes cluster-wide energy optimization increasingly important. Serverless LLM serving helps platforms absorb traffic volatility by elastically sharing GPU resources across models, but this sharing also makes energy optimization difficult. Multiple co-resident models run under one device-wide operating point, while their resource demands and latency slack change across execution phases and load conditions. As a result, minimizing energy requires coordinated scheduling across request placement, runtime resource adaptation, and workload consolidation. We present Festina, a profiling-guided, power-aware control plane to minimize cluster-wide energy for serverless LLM serving. Festina further performs energy-aware workload consolidation to reduce GPUs' static power consumption via SLO-aware migration. Financial analysis requires reasoning over heterogeneous signals, including company fundamentals from regulatory filings and trading signals derived from market prices. However, existing financial benchmarks focus almost exclusively on regulatory filings, neglecting market dynamics and cross-signal interactions. We introduce FinTradeBench, a benchmark of 1,400 questions covering NASDAQ-100 stocks over a ten-year window. To holistically evaluate financial reasoning, FinTradeBench includes questions in three reasoning categories: fundamentals-focused, trading-signal-focused, and hybrid questions that require cross-signal reasoning. Our benchmark starts with 150 expert-authored seed questions, which are then scaled to 1400. To ensure reliable evaluation, we create a Core400 subset in which every item is expert-reviewed. In zero-shot (No-RAG) response generation, we observe that all models perform substantially worse on trading-focused questions compared to fundamental-focused questions. LLMs' capabilities to do computation and evidence selection contribute most to reasoning failures. Additionally, retrieval significantly improves trading-signal reasoning but does not improve fundamental reasoning, where identifying relevant signals remains the dominant failure mode. Finally, rather than relying on a small, fine-tuned model for time-series reasoning, it is better to summarize the time-series data and leverage LLMs' reasoning capabilities.

LLM-as-Jev: LLMs Are Already Jev-Style Decision Models -- When and How to Fine-Tune Them 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Jev-style decision models have gained surging traction by making decision-making dramatically faster and more efficient, returning categorical probability distributions over predefined options without generating free-form text. However, because Jev's architecture and training methodologies remain proprietary, how to replicate these capabilities effectively remains an open question. In this work, we investigate the extent to which general-purpose LLMs already possess this capability out of the box, and when fine-tuning is actually necessary. We present LLM-as-Jev, an architecture-preserving framework offering both a training-free inference recipe and a fine-tuning objective with KL-divergence anchors. Across Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3-0.6B, we find that modern LLMs are inherently effective decision models: off the shelf, frozen models match or exceed existing training-free readouts on the same 4B backbone, scale to arbitrary option counts, and natively handle multimodal decisions. They also seamlessly inherit the backbone's thinking mode: while Jev was introduced as a System 1 model, "Thinking Jev" leverages budgeted test-time reasoning to boost accuracy on challenging tasks without training. Fine-tuning provides targeted gains for smaller models and complex routing, while our KL anchors prevent behavioral degradation in general text generation.

OracleProto: A Reproducible Framework for Benchmarking LLM Forecasting via Knowledge Cutoffs and Temporal Masking 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Forecasting is a composite capability encompassing information gathering, evidence integration, situational assessment, and decision-making. Large language models (LLMs) combine reasoning and tool use, offering new avenues for automated forecasting and decision support. Yet LLM forecasting evaluation faces a structural challenge: live benchmarks support prospective assessment, but limited question reuse and changing information conditions make results difficult to reproduce and compare; retrospective benchmarks offer reproducibility but risk information leakage. We introduce OracleProto, a reproducible framework for evaluating LLM forecasting in a controlled, temporally masked environment. Through model knowledge cutoff constraints and temporal masking, OracleProto constructs a temporal sandbox for real-world web retrieval, audits information boundaries through leakage detection, and reconstructs resolved events as forecasting instances. Discrete answer normalization and hierarchical scoring enable unified evaluation. We evaluate 27 model-configuration pairs on 300 FutureX-Past-derived questions over 24,300 prediction trials, assessing forecasting quality, sampling stability, and cost efficiency under controlled information boundaries. An audit finds only 1.37% residual leakage in retrieval results after temporal masking and leakage filtering. OracleProto retains resolved forecasting tasks as reusable evaluation resources under explicit temporal boundaries, supporting continual accumulation of forecasting datasets and systematic evaluation of forecasting capabilities. What Users See Is Not What Models Read: Split-View PDFs in Document-to-LLM Supply Chains 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted by IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy (S&P 2027)
Show abstract

Document-to-LLM applications typically read uploaded PDFs by first translating them into text through an extraction layer whose output is not ordinarily exposed to users. We show that this layer enables split-view PDFs: one document can have two semantic views before model reasoning. By mining specification-permitted or implementation-tolerated representation gaps at the PDF render/extract boundary, we instantiate 25 extraction gaps (EG) in which extractors return attacker-controlled or extractor-dependent text while the rendered page shows benign or different content. The gaps form four families: semantic overrides, hidden semantic injection, reading-order splits, and font-decoding splits, and 14 gaps have no exact path/mechanism-level match in prior PDF-to-LLM attacks. We evaluate these gaps on 16 PDF processing stacks and 7 commercial LLM services. Each gap causes render-extract divergence on at least one stack. Under a gap-level exposure criterion, every evaluated service exposes at least one gap, with 12/25 to 21/25 exposed gaps. Exposure is driven mainly by the ingestion stack -- not model identity alone. We further show that tested safety filters cover only selected hidden-text constructions. To support triage, we develop a static screening scanner whose rules trigger on all 25 benchmark gaps, and discuss dual-view consistency as a longer-term defense direction.

Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Controlling an acting LLM agent means deciding, at each step of a long-horizon task, whether to trust its next action, abstain, or hand off to a human. That decision needs a per-step confidence signal predicting whether the current rollout will eventually succeed. The agent's own self-reported verbalized confidence is the natural candidate, but existing reinforcement learning methods calibrate it against the correctness of a decision at the current step. We propose a method to calibrate it against the eventual outcome of a multi-step episode: under GRPO, the group of rollouts for a task supplies success labels, and a proper scoring rule scores each step's confidence against the realized outcome. In offline RL on recorded trajectories of a Qwen2.5-7B agent in ALFWorld, TextWorld, and WebShop environments, the AUROC of step confidence against episode outcome rises from near chance to 0.68-0.85, and the top-10% of steps the agent is most confident about succeed ~4.3x more often than average. In online RL on the live ALFWorld agent, four calibration rewards improve early-step AUROC, expected calibration error goes from 0.42 to below 0.12, and selective execution improves over both the untrained agent and a format-only RL control, without degrading task success. We also introduce an evaluation protocol that separates episode depth, task difficulty, and attempt-level foresight, which shows where the gain lies: the trained agent recognizes from its first step which tasks it will fail, while telling a succeeding attempt from a failing one at the same task is harder, with some early-window signal emerging on TextWorld.

Rethinking Projector Training in Multimodal LLMs 2026-10-13
📝 NeurIPS 2026 camera-ready
Show abstract

Multimodal LLMs are typically trained by first aligning a vision encoder with a language model via projector training on large image-caption datasets, followed by joint training of the full multimodal model. Despite its widespread use, the role of the projector training stage remains poorly understood. We find that (i) using only $10$ to $20\%$ of the data for training the projector already recovers most downstream performance gains, and (ii) projectors trained on different datasets can be linearly interpolated while largely preserving performance. These results suggest that projector training mainly performs a coarse alignment, which many different projectors achieve equally well. Motivated by these findings, we propose PORTAL, a training-free projector initialization that requires neither paired image-caption data nor gradient-based optimization, but only per-modality summary statistics. PORTAL computes an optimal-transport-based map between Gaussian approximations of the vision and language feature distributions on a shared principal subspace, so that the projector training stage can be skipped entirely. Without any paired data, PORTAL matches the standard pipeline trained on 558k image-caption pairs across six LLM backbones, two vision encoders, and 16 benchmarks. It outperforms that pipeline in $3$ of $7$ (vision encoder, LLM) settings and reaches an average $98\%$ of its score across the other four.

Compare to Correct: Pairwise-Guided Logit Adjustment for LLM Test-Time Scaling 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Sampling-based test-time scaling enhances LLM reasoning by aggregating multiple reasoning paths for the same query. Existing methods use answer frequencies or path confidence to compute candidate scores, which do not explicitly characterize comparisons between competing answers. Inspired by the Condorcet principle in social choice theory, we introduce pairwise confidence comparisons between paths supporting different answers to capture answer-level comparative advantage. We combine this comparative evidence with voting support through a KL-regularized objective. Theoretically, we show that this comparative-advantage is equivalent to the adjustment to the traditional voting logits. In this way, our proposed Pairwise-Guided Logit Adjustment (PGLA) framework allows comparative evidence to compensate for weaker voting support and correct voting-based ranking errors. Experiments on 10 challenging benchmarks including mathematical reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and code generation with 7 models ranging from 1.7B to 32B parameters speak to the effectiveness of this plug-and-play term. Additional evaluations with external reward scores and online sampling further demonstrate its applicability.

Show abstract

Existing automation either searches predefined recipe spaces that enable systematic optimization but remain fixed rather than adapting their available choices to the target tasks, or uses open-ended agents that discover task-specific choices but leave them implicit in trial-level edits, limiting their systematic reuse and recombination across trials. To fill this gap, we introduce optimization over a task-adapted recipe space, a novel paradigm that can adapt the recipe space itself to the target tasks while representing its choices explicitly for systematic reuse, recombination, and joint optimization. Revisiting Cauchy-Schwarz Norm Bounds for SDC Detection in LLM Inference 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) execute billions of floating-point operations per output token across many hardware accelerators. At this scale, hardware-induced faults can bypass system-level detection mechanisms and cause Silent Data Corruption (SDC). Algorithm-Based Fault Tolerance (ABFT) is the state-of-the-art technique for protecting matrix multiplication from such faults, but it incurs non-negligible computational overhead and requires thresholds to distinguish hardware-induced errors from floating-point roundoff. In this paper, we revisit Cauchy--Schwarz Norm Bounds (CSNB) as a low-overhead alternative for detecting SDC during LLM inference. We compare CSNB against ABFT through controlled fault injection and evaluate the runtime overhead of both verification and threshold determination across multiple model sizes, batch sizes, and generation lengths. Our results show that, on average, CSNB retains 99% of the fault detection capability of ABFT for the evaluated dense linear layers while reducing verification overhead by 87% and threshold determination overhead by 67%. For Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) router gate computations, however, CSNB retains only 55% of the fault detection capability of ABFT. These findings suggest that CSNB is a promising low-overhead alternative for protecting dense linear layers in LLM inference, while checksum-based verification remains preferable for the evaluated MoE router gate.

Lost in Implicit Routing: How LLM Agents Fail to Load Relevant Skills at Scale 2026-10-13
📝 22 pages, including references and appendices. Code: https://github.com/jinlinll/lost-in-implicit-routing
Show abstract

Reusable skills let LLM agents load task-specific instructions on demand, but they make execution depend on implicit routing over skill metadata. We test whether this design scales by holding SkillsBench tasks and their golden skills fixed while adding irrelevant skills. We find skill routing collapse: agents increasingly load fewer or no skills, even though the relevant skills remain available. With 1,000 irrelevant skills, loaded-skill recall falls from 73.6% to 27.4% and the zero-load rate rises from 9.3% to 51.6%, whereas explicit selection from the same library retains 73.9% recall. Our analysis informs skill protocols and evaluation for agents with growing skill libraries.

A Global View of How LLMs Value Human Time 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Human psychometric questionnaires are increasingly used to characterize large language models (LLMs), but it remains unclear what exactly their responses reflect. When "Must" Becomes "Maybe": Erosion of Workflow Constraints in LLM Agent Handoffs 2026-10-13
📝 16 pages, 4 figures. Revised title, abstract, and exposition; expanded discussion and reorganized appendix
Show abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents build on one another's work through summaries, plans, and other intermediate artifacts. These records let a downstream agent continue without revisiting the complete upstream context. We uncover a hidden loss in this process: handoffs can carry tasks forward while eroding judgments already established upstream. A requirement that must be satisfied before action becomes a caveat that the next agent may acknowledge and set aside. The issue remains visible, but its role has changed. We define operational state preservation as retaining an established judgment's role in constraining downstream action. Safety blockers provide a concrete instance: an unresolved prerequisite restricts the admissible actions until the condition is resolved. In our experiments, we confirm correct upstream detection, hold source permissions fixed, and vary the handoff supplied to a local-view executor. Direct-state controls preserve every blocker, whereas compression, plan assimilation, convergence, ownership deferral, and precedent substitution repeatedly weaken them. Ownership deferral produces 76.7% blocker deactivation; multihop compression produces 97.2%. Measuring the transmitted state separately from endpoint action exposes two routes to behavioral recovery. In fixed-artifact replays, restoring the full blocker description raises recognition from 6.2% to 99.2% and reduces forbidden action from 85.2% to 0.0%. An authorization-check prompt instead reduces forbidden action to 1.6% while leaving artifact deactivation at 95.3%. A successful endpoint can therefore conceal a damaged handoff. These findings make maintaining earlier judgments a core capability of reliable collaboration: each stage must carry forward both what earlier work established and what it requires of subsequent action. Semantic availability does not guarantee operational preservation.

LLM Alignment--Utility Asymmetry under Semantic-Preserving Transformations 2026-10-13
📝 58 pages. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Large Language Model (LLM) alignment is intended to ensure that models remain helpful and safe, but its stability under input distributional shift is not yet fully understood. Prior work shows that aligned models can fail under jailbreak prompts, alternative encodings, and cross-lingual transfer, yet these failures are usually studied as attacks rather than controlled probes of alignment generalization. Moreover, existing evidence is largely grounded in natural language variation already represented during pretraining, leaving unresolved whether alignment generalizes with semantic content or remains tied to superficial surface patterns. In this paper, we study this question using synthetic semantic-preserving transformations that are rule-based and invertible, preserving task-relevant meaning while shifting inputs beyond standard linguistic variation. Across four open-weight and four commercial models, under both fine-tuning and in-context learning, we use these transformations as a probe of alignment generalization and identify an empirical pattern we term Alignment--utility asymmetry: once models can operate effectively on transformed inputs, task utility is often substantially retained while alignment failure increases more sharply. For example, adapted GPT-4.1 mini shows only limited utility degradation under transformation while its harmful rate rises from 13.3 to 74.3; Gemini 3 Flash similarly retains near-original utility while its harmful rate increases from 2.3 to 43.0.
