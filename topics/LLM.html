Fine-tuning Multi-modal LLMs with ART: Art-based Reinforcement Training 2026-10-29
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to specific tasks usually involves fine-tuning: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) edits the weights, while soft prompting prepends trainable virtual tokens. Both change how an already-optimized model is served, so serving many task-specific adapters at high throughput requires adapter-aware infrastructure. We propose Art-Based Reinforcement Training (ART), which optimizes a single auxiliary input image to specialize a frozen Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) for a specific task. At inference time, ART simply includes this image in each standard multimodal prompt, requiring no changes to the serving engine. We compare ART with LoRA and soft prompting across five reasoning benchmarks for four small MLLMs from three architectures. While different methods perform best for different tasks, ART ranks first or second in 10 of 20 benchmark-MLLM pairs, with its best results on the two math word problem benchmarks (GSM8k and SVAMP). It is significantly ahead of the next best adapter in 2 cases, within the noise of the next best adapter in 6, and significantly above the text-only baseline in 18. ART therefore reaches accuracy close to LoRA and soft prompts while serving faster than LoRA. In the vLLM serving stack, ART reduces throughput by 6% to 14% relative to text-only requests, compared to LoRA's 13% to 93% slowdown. Beyond efficient serving, ART artifacts are plain images, making them easy to store, distribute, and share. This approach keep task-specific adaptation outside the model weights, so the same frozen model can support different tasks without separate model variants.

TextSeal2: Distortion-Free LLM Watermarking with Copula Mixing and Memoryless Races 2026-10-29
📝 Code: https://github.com/facebookresearch/textseal
Show abstract

We present TextSeal2, a distortion-free watermark for large language models that keeps its signal under speculative decoding: tokens that replace rejected drafts remain watermarked with the same keys, and the acceptance rate is unchanged. It also preserves output diversity and adds no measurable inference latency. Its predecessor, TextSeal, restores diversity in the Gumbel-max scheme by routing each token to one of two keys via a coin flip. This is suboptimal because the detector averages the scores from both keys without knowing which key was used. TextSeal2 instead merges two or more keys into a single virtual key and couples it to fresh noise through AutoMark's Gaussian copula. Any number of keys, including publicly shared keys, can be merged this way without diluting detection. Under speculative decoding, TextSeal2 verifies keyed drafts with one more exponential race, run on the leftovers of the draft races. By memorylessness, this race reproduces block verification, so the output follows the target model exactly. We evaluate TextSeal2 on three LLMs. At equal output token diversity, TextSeal2 detects 8 to 22 percentage points more 100-token answers than TextSeal at a false-positive rate of $10^{-3}$, and is comparable to textGrain while requiring no post-logits optimization. Under speculative decoding, it detects 18 and 22 percentage points more 200-token answers than TextSeal and textGrain, respectively, at the same acceptance rate.

Understanding and Enhancing Backdoor Persistency in LLM Agent Post-Training 2026-10-29
📝 EMNLP 2026 Findings
Show abstract

Developers can build LLM agents by adapting third-party models through benign post-training. We study a supply-chain threat in which an attacker supplies a model with a backdoor: hidden behavior that produces malicious outputs when a particular input pattern appears. Focusing on software-engineering agents, we ask whether such backdoors survive the developer's supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and subsequent task-level reinforcement learning (RL). We observe that benign SFT substantially reduces attack success, but subsequent RL often preserves the residual behavior and sometimes even increases attack success. Our analysis of backdoor erosion during SFT identifies two factors that may favor survival: initial backdoor strength and gradient compatibility with benign training. These factors motivate PersistBD, which refines an already-backdoored model before release to improve its persistency through the benign post-training process. On Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, PersistBD raises attack success from 20% to 74% after SFT and from 20% to 76% after SFT-RL, while maintaining comparable benign task performance. Together, our results show that backdoors can remain active through benign post-training and that adversaries can deliberately increase their persistence. This highlights a supply-chain risk for AI developers and motivates stronger techniques for detecting and mitigating inherited backdoors when adapting third-party models into agents. Our code is available at https://github.com/uiuc-kang-lab/PersistBD.

A Low-Rank Space Captures Linear Alignment Between LLM and Human Neural Activity During Naturalistic Language Listening 2026-10-29
📝 23 pages, 12 figures. Code: https://github.com/thefinnlab/low-rank-alignment
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as computational models of human language processing, yet it remains unclear which components of their representations drive alignment with neural activity. Here, we use ridge reduced-rank regression to identify the brain-predictive subspace of LLM representations: the representational directions that support linear prediction of neural responses. Across three naturalistic language-listening datasets spanning intracranial electrophysiology and fMRI, and six model conditions spanning autoregressive, bidirectional masked, and randomly initialized language models, we find that full linear encoding performance can be recovered using only 1-3% of the original embedding dimensionality. These low-dimensional subspaces generalize across held-out participants and, to a lesser extent, across datasets, suggesting that neural predictivity is concentrated in a compact and partially shared representational space. We next compare these brain-predictive directions with the principal axes of variation in the embeddings. Principal component analysis requires substantially more dimensions to recover neural prediction, demonstrating that the directions most useful for predicting neural activity are distinct from those capturing the most overall variance within LLM representations themselves. Finally, characterizing the learned subspaces allows us to surface interpretable features that are overrepresented in neural activity above and beyond the LLM representations. Together, these results show that LLM-brain alignment is supported by a small, structured subset of model representations and provides a framework for better understanding exactly why these representations align. Code can be found at: https://github.com/thefinnlab/low-rank-alignment

Beyond Surface Forms: A Comprehensive, Mechanism-Oriented Taxonomy of Indirect Linguistic Encoding for LLM-Based Coded Language Detection 2026-10-29
📝 Published in EMNLP 2026 Main: Beyond Surface Forms: A Comprehensive, Mechanism-Oriented Taxonomy of Indirect Linguistic Encoding for LLM-Based Coded Language Detection (Firoozfar et al., EMNLP 2026) | https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2444/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

To avoid moderation and surveillance on social media, some users routinely invent indirect linguistic expressions (ILE) that camouflage sensitive meanings. Such disguised expressions surface as algospeak, euphemisms, and adversarial obfuscation, depending on intent and context, and often involve recurring encoding mechanisms. We propose a comprehensive, mechanism-oriented taxonomy of ILE that abstracts away from communicative goals and instead categorizes the underlying operations through which meaning is encoded and recovered. We evaluate the taxonomy by incorporating it into large language model (LLM) prompts and comparing it with four existing taxonomies and a no-taxonomy baseline, using 2,000 manually annotated TikTok and Bluesky posts. The proposed taxonomy attains the strongest document- and span-level performance across three LLMs. Compared with the best-performing benchmark, its document-level gains are +3.8 accuracy/+4.3 macro-F1 percentage points for GPT-5.4, +0.9/+1.3 for Sonnet 4.6, and +2.1/+2.2 for DeepSeek V4. While the direction of improvement is consistent, the magnitude is model-dependent. The span-level gains are mainly driven by higher recall, sometimes at the cost of precision. In an additional cross-lingual evaluation of 800 Chinese codedlanguage reviews, the unadapted taxonomy also achieves the highest document- and span-level F1 across the three LLMs. The empirical results reveal the importance of a comprehensive, mechanism-oriented taxonomy as a stable scaffold for detecting emerging coded language and a useful input to content moderation. Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections 2026-10-29
📝 14 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Unlearning concerns the task of removing the influence of a sensitive portion of the training data, such as protected, copyrighted or private text, from a model without retraining it from scratch. While unlearning requests happen continuously during a model's lifetime, the vast majority of LLM unlearning methods are ill-suited for iterated removal, yielding steep decay in forgetting and utility upon sequential unlearning. This decay stems from interference between successive updates, as each new deletion can undo earlier ones or push the model further along directions already degraded by them. To address this, we introduce Continual Orthogonal Projection (COP), a gradient-projection scheme that restricts each update to the orthogonal complement of activation subspaces cached from prior forget sets. This approach provably guarantees first-order non-interference with earlier deletions. COP stores only a low-rank basis per layer, requiring neither past gradients nor previous forget sets, and plugs into unlearning methods that update the model through gradient steps on a differentiable loss without modifying its objective. Experiments on standard LLM unlearning benchmarks (TOFU and RWKU) show that COP preserves utility across sequential requests without weakening forgetting, preventing the collapse of standard objectives and forgetting more effectively than continual-specific baselines.

Understanding and Mitigating Role Spoofing in LLMs 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Despite growing capabilities, LLMs remain susceptible to prompt injections: malicious inputs that can manipulate or hijack models. In particular, it has recently been observed that LLMs can exhibit role confusion (Ye et al., 2026), where models attribute the source of text based on its style instead of its labeled role. Malicious text in tool outputs (for instance, a malicious webpage accessed by an agent) can leverage role confusion to impersonate the user, an attack we call role spoofing}. In this work, we seek to understand and mitigate role spoofing. We study how models track "who said what" in a conversation, showing that they use cues from both content (e.g., semantics and writing style) and structure (e.g., control tokens and formatting). We show that LLMs are highly susceptible to role spoofing attacks that combine both fake structure and content. Additionally, in a toy setting, we show that adversarial training which teaches the model to ignore content-based injections does mitigate content-based attacks but leaves the model susceptible to structure-based attacks. This can explain why frontier models, with increased resistance to many injection attacks, can still be susceptible to role spoofing. Finally, we propose a new method for detecting role spoofing using linear probes trained to predict roles in a conversation, and show that it is effective across different settings.

Don't Lose the Thread: Empowering Long-Horizon LLM Agents with Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted at COLM 2026. Code is available at https://github.com/JIA-Lab-research/CORAL
Show abstract

Agents powered by large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable progress in solving complex reasoning tasks. However, LLM agents often falter on long-horizon tasks due to cognitive overload, as their working memory becomes cluttered with expanding and irrelevant information, which dilutes their attention and hinders effective planning and reasoning. To mitigate this challenge, we introduce Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation (CORAL), a novel reasoning paradigm that empowers agents to proactively optimize their context. Implemented as an agent-callable working memory management toolset, CORAL allows an agent to checkpoint its task progress and verified facts, and adaptively initiate a new problem-solving episode by resetting cluttered context while preserving only the checkpointed information, enabling it to resume reasoning from a clean state. We further enhance the agent's checkpointing capabilities using a Multi-episode Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization algorithm. On the GAIA benchmark, CORAL with an 8B model achieves 42.8% average accuracy, with particularly strong gains on the most challenging long-horizon tasks (Level 2 and Level 3). Crucially, these gains come at a substantially lower inference cost: CORAL consumes 6-7$\times$ fewer input tokens than a ReAct agent. It also generalizes to multi-hop Knowledge QA benchmarks and to the considerably harder BrowseComp, where it improves a strong proprietary agent by 8.0 points.

Rimuru: Unified Memory Management for Modern LLM Serving via Elastic Expert Offloading 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Modern LLMs strain device memory through large model weights and attention states, particularly in resource-constrained scenarios. Existing systems separately provision memory for model weights and attention states, imposing fixed fixed boundaries that cannot adapt to the changing memory demands of workloads. Under heavy workloads, insufficient state capacity limits request concurrency and reduces throughput; under light workloads, idle memory in one region cannot be reallocated to another, wasting device memory. Meanwhile, modern LLM architectures introduce heterogeneous tensors with diverse layouts and lifetimes, further complicating memory management. These problems motivate unified memory management for heterogeneous tensors. However, a shared pool alone does not make memory directly reusable across tensors with different allocation granularities and execution layouts. This paper presents Rimuru, a modern LLM serving system that unifies memory management through elastic expert offloading. Rimuru establishes a shared page pool to enables elastic memory sharing across heterogeneous tensors. To accommodate different allocation granularities, Rimuru introduces memory views that allow each tensor type to interpret the shared memory space using its own page size and page table. Furthermore, Rimuru combines huge-page alignment with a multi-level page allocator that splits and merges pages, enabling cross-type capacity reuse. A page-table-aware fused MoE kernel directly accesses non-contiguous expert weights, avoiding copy overhead. Building on these mechanisms, Rimuru employs a workload-aware adjuster that dynamically reallocates pages across tensor types according to runtime demands. Implemented on top of vLLM, Rimuru remains compatible with modern LLMs. Evaluations show that Rimuru achieves up to 19.03x baseline throughput and reduces TTFT by 98.1% on average.

The Illusion of Power Capping in LLM Decode: A Phase-Aware Energy Characterisation Across Attention Architectures 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted by PPAM 2026 (16th International Conference on Parallel Processing and Applied Mathematics)
Show abstract

Power capping is a common GPU energy lever in LLM serving, and it appears to work: the cap is set, the GPU reports drawing less than it, and the energy budget is met. We show the cap does little for the phase that dominates LLM serving: autoregressive decode. Across four attention paradigms (GQA, MLA, Gated DeltaNet, and Mamba2) on an NVIDIA H200 under vLLM eager execution, decode kernels wait on HBM and the GPU idles between kernel launches, so decode draws less than half of the GPU's 700W rating and throughput stays within a few percent under every cap we set. The cap engages only when a large batch runs a long generation. Locking the SM clock instead lowers power without slowing memory-bound decode and recovers up to 32% of decode energy at minimal throughput loss. We identify three architecture-dependent frequency-scaling classes and find that, once prefill is measured without prefix caching, the recurrent architectures pay no prefill penalty: their linear-time mixing undercuts quadratic attention on long prompts, so their efficient decode adds to the prefill saving and lowers total request energy from the first output token at large batch.

MAServeBench: Benchmarking Multi-Agent LLM Serving Systems with Hierarchical Collaboration Workloads 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems (MAS) coordinate specialized agents to solve complex tasks, turning each user request into interdependent model and tool operations. Unlike independent requests, these operations become ready at different times and consume evolving context as agents coordinate and exchange results. This creates changing patterns of concurrency, computation, and memory demand that can expose different bottlenecks in LLM serving hardware. Yet benchmarking these effects requires capturing the collaboration rules that generate serving workloads, beyond the requests observed during execution. We present MAServeBench, a benchmark that treats multi-agent collaboration as a configurable dimension of serving workloads for LLM serving and hardware evaluation. When Modernization Breaks Working Code: A Single-File Study of LLMs on Legacy PHP 2026-10-29
📝 6 pages, 5 figures, 5 tables. Preprint
Show abstract

Pasting a legacy file into an LLM and asking it to "modernize this" is an easy shortcut. But is the result better than leaving the code alone? In this paper, we study this single-file setting with five LLMs, each asked to modernize 100 WordPress 4.0 PHP files to PHP 8.3 while preserving behavior, with no project context or execution feedback. We evaluate every output with 630 hidden behavioral checks and 511 Rector-detected modernization findings. We compare against two baselines: the unchanged originals and the rule-based tool Rector. Often, the answer is no. Untouched, 83 files already pass every check; the best LLM leaves only 59. Each model breaks 32-51 working files while repairing at most 10, whereas Rector breaks 7 and repairs 6. Most of this damage is unnecessary. Only 37 findings (7 percent) require a change under PHP 8.3, yet in compatible files with no required change, the LLMs break 36-60 percent (Rector: 8 percent). The model with the highest modernization score is no more compatible than its closest competitors. Most regressed checks come from files that no longer load at all, often because of type declarations PHP itself rejects. These are errors that need no project context to avoid. Evaluations of LLM-based modernization should therefore measure against a no-change baseline and report modernization and behavioral preservation separately.

EpiAgent: Epistemic Self-Play for LLM Agents 2026-10-29
📝 23 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Epistemic game theory explains an agent's strategic behavior via the beliefs it holds about others and how these beliefs are updated as the interactions unfold. Modern LLM-based agents, however, often lack an explicit mechanism for learning and updating such beliefs to guide decisions under incomplete information. To bridge this gap, we propose EpiAgent, a framework that equips LLM-based agents with an explicit, learnable epistemic layer, which maintains beliefs over others' private information and combines these beliefs with the agent's available information into an epistemic state for action selection. Concretely, the agent's policy is decomposed into two coupled stages: Epistemic Update, which updates these beliefs from interaction histories, and Action Planning, which selects actions conditioned on the updated epistemic state. By formulating their dependency as a bi-level optimization problem, we introduce Epistemic Self-Play, an algorithm that alternately optimizes the two stages through self-play, utilizing an intrinsic prediction reward to evaluate how well inferred beliefs predict subsequent observations. Empirical results across both mixed and competitive scenarios show that EpiAgent consistently outperforms prompting-based and RL-based baselines. Further analyses reveal the benefits of dedicated epistemic training, improvements in belief inference accuracy, and the contribution of learned beliefs to action planning. These results demonstrate the value of an explicit, learnable epistemic layer for effective multi-agent interactions.

World Model Science: Self-Organized Criticality, Weak Chaos, and Metastable Belief Dynamics in Long-Horizon LLM Agents 2026-10-29
📝 Under Review
Show abstract

Long-horizon LLM agents must maintain and update a world model over extended sequences of observations, actions, decisions and tool calls. We study the dynamics of long horizon agents from three perspectives: self-organized criticality, weak chaos, and metastable belief dynamics. Through extensive experiments on controlled puzzles, tool-use tasks, embodied environments, multi-hop retrieval, general-assistant reasoning, and Game of Life, we study how world-model errors develop over long interactions. We find that externally increased task pressure can sharply raise the probability of collapse, while naturally occurring pressure is only a modest predictor of later failure. Errors are temporally correlated rather than independent, and their propagation changes with dependency depth while remaining bounded in finite tasks. We also find that locally valid actions can continue after the agent's overall task state has become incorrect, that error structure depends on task topology and horizon, and that belief states in embodied tasks are shallow rather than strongly trapped in incorrect states. Larger horizons allow larger error cascades, but their scale remains constrained by the task and the model's capability. Overall, we observe power-law behavior, bounded divergence consistent with weak chaos, and metastable belief dynamics across long-horizon agent interactions. We call this perspective world model science: the empirical study of self organized criticality in long-horizon agent world models.

Epistemic Warrant for LLM Recommendations: Characterizing the Basis for Reliance When Ground Truth Is Unavailable 2026-10-29
📝 43 pages
Show abstract

Large language models are increasingly used to support organizational decisions. Despite their rapid and widespread adoption, users often lack a principled basis for assessing whether to rely on a specific recommendation. We introduce \emph{epistemic warrant}, a decision-level construct that characterizes the scope over which a recommendation is supported. We operationalize this construct through a reliance certificate that uses behavioral evidence from four tiers of tests to assess a recommendation's underlying warrant and assign it to a warrant category. We assess this operationalization using complementary forms of construct-validity evidence, including transformation validity, known-groups tests, independent human consensus, discriminant analyses, and robustness across models and alternative implementations. The main conclusions remain robust across models and operationalizations. These findings support the reliance certificate as an operationalization of epistemic warrant for assessing the scope of support for a particular LLM recommendation when ground truth is unavailable.

LAMP: LLM-Driven Analog Placement through Adaptive Tool Composition 2026-10-29
📝 5 pages, 3 figures, 1 table
Show abstract

Analog placement requires circuit-specific trade-offs among area, matching, routability, and electrical performance. Balancing these trade-offs through predefined objectives and fixed placement flows remains difficult. In this paper, we propose an analog placement framework in which a large language model (LLM) acts as a layout engineer through composable placement components.
