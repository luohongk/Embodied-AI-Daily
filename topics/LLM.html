Do LLM Agents Still Need to Be Told How to Self-Improve? 2026-11-17
📝 Project page: https://ssundaram21.github.io/self-org/
Show abstract

LLM agents increasingly drive their own learning. Self-improvement methods delegate various components of model optimization to the models themselves. As models become more capable, this motivates a basic question: do these increasingly capable models still need us to specify how they should self-improve? In this paper we study an autonomous paradigm in which, given basic primitives, resources, and a general goal for self-improvement, agents construct their own self-learning algorithm for context-based optimization. Using the setting of gameplay as a testbed, a frozen LLM agent is given a compute budget and tools for collecting experience and updating text documents, and allowed to freely experiment without an externally imposed loop or access to real-world data. We find that sufficiently capable models do self-improve under these conditions, demonstrating strategies that align with classic cognitive frameworks for self-regulated learning and constructs from machine learning literature on self-generated curricula. Furthermore, we study the extent to which the learned text artifacts transfer to external benchmarks, observing improvement across different game horizons, mechanics, and 3D navigation.

BugGen 2.0: Rethinking the Roles of LLMs for Scalable RTL Bug Synthesis 2026-11-17
📝 7 pages, 2 figures, 7 tables
Show abstract

AI-assisted RTL localization and debugging need ground-truth bug datasets with known locations and observable failures, while testbench grading uses injected bugs to measure detection capability. Creating such datasets at scale remains a major EDA challenge. BugGen 1.0 shows that Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate context-aware RTL mutations. Still, it relies on the model for both choosing where to mutate and generating the edit, which increases inference costs and can lead to invalid choices. BugGen 2.0 changes this division of labor. Deterministic algorithms handle structural analysis, target selection, and validation, while the LLM focuses on localized semantic rewriting. Across four OpenTitan IP blocks and FabScalar with three LLMs, BugGen 2.0 reaches every requested quota of compilable bug scenarios that cause at least two test failures, with 14-16 of 16 configured mutation types represented. In completed same-model comparisons, it reduces token use by 6.8x-15.4x relative to BugGen 1.0 and 1.5x-7.9x relative to Firefly. BugGen 2.0 also expands the dataset without simulating every new bug. After finding accepted scenarios with observable failures, it generates alternative mutations at the same locations, simulates only a subset, and estimates acceptance for the rest. This skips 63.6% of AES and 33.9% of FabScalar variant simulations. Full simulation finds that 77.7% of AES variants and 95.2% of FabScalar variants are accepted, with a 1.09% estimation error on the directly comparable FabScalar set. These results show that focusing LLMs on semantic rewriting lowers inference and simulation cost while improving consistency across models and designs.

Words Speak Louder? Exploring the Language Shortcut of LLM-Driven Incomplete Multimodal Learning 2026-11-17
📝 19 pages, 7 figures, 14 tables. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/CoVeR/
Show abstract

Is a face or a voice still heard once the words are known? Incomplete multimodal learning increasingly uses large language models (LLMs) as fusion engines: modality features enter as token blocks and missing modalities become placeholders. We find that LLM-driven fusion can override non-verbal evidence it can read. Contrasting its full-input prediction with counterfactual views, the same model shown one modality or no content, we identify language shortcuts. We build MR-Bench: MR-Bench-A types every test sample of four sentiment benchmarks; MR-Bench-H contains 900 human-validated clips (620 language-shortcut, LS; 280 non-verbal-shortcut, NS) from seven sources, and every model is scored on all of them. Zero-shot MLLMs solve at most 32.7% of the LS clips; adding the transcript to the muted video lowers vision-language accuracy by 46.1-53.1 points. Yet inverting a text-only model's answers solves 94.4%, so LS accuracy alone cannot establish use of non-verbal evidence. Inside the fusion LLM, fused states align increasingly with the text view late in the decoder. Counterfactual-View Reliability learning (CoVeR) trains views as unimodal judges and withholds fused supervision where they disagree. It achieves state-of-the-art results on five benchmarks without extra inference cost in accuracy mode. On MR-Bench-H, it reaches 61.7% LS and 93.6% NS accuracy; robust mode raises LS accuracy to 63.0% while lowering NS accuracy to 84.8%. Among LS clips solved without text, adding text causes errors on 30.1% with CoVeR versus 32.2% with LLM-Fusion. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/CoVeR/.

Thinking Is Not Telling: Information Disclosure in User-Service LLM Agents 2026-11-17
📝 TMLR
Show abstract

User-engaged LLM agents increasingly operate in service scenarios where task success depends on coordination between the agent, the user, and a stateful environment. In such interactions, the agent often has access to rich information that the user does not observe, such as task policies, tool results, and environment states. This makes agent-user communication a central component of task completion. In this work, we study a communication-level failure mode of user-service agents: internal reasoning is counterproductive for the interactive information disclosure. We introduce information disclosure as a measurable communication mechanism, covering state corrections, available options, constraints, consequences, and proactively retrieved environment information. Motivated by recent advances in LLM reasoning, we evaluate and analyse information disclosure under a controlled setting of agents equipped with a "think" tool, which allows agents to reason internally before responding or acting. Across five models and three user-service scenarios, we first show that enforced thinking has unstable utility. We then analyse response scaling under different think efforts and find that more thinking does not reliably translate into richer information disclosure. Using turn-level counterfactual intervention, we show that injecting golden decision-relevant information significantly intervenes agents to correct action trajectories, indicating that missing disclosure can causally affect the agent's immediate downstream behaviour. We further find that failed trajectories most often lack state corrections and constraints, and that a lightweight disclosure-aware prompt effectively mitigates degradation. These results suggest that the key challenge is not simply making agents think more, but ensuring that reasoning and information are externalised as timely information disclosure.

Can Decision Models Replace LLMs in Real-World Contact Center Classification and Judging Tasks? 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Contact center systems often need to make bounded decisions, such as identifying a caller's intent, retrieving the knowledge-base article that answers a query, assessing agent compliance, or judging the output of AI models used in production. Decision models return a typed answer and its probability directly, making them a promising cost-effective alternative to generative Large Language Models (LLMs) in such bounded conversational tasks. However, their effectiveness in real-world contact center applications remains unclear. In this paper, we compare nine decision models against thirteen generative LLMs in zero-shot settings across eight contact center use cases: six classification tasks and two judging tasks. Our experiments demonstrate that many decision models can match the performance of frontier LLMs on contact center classification, and API-based closed decision models achieve this at the lowest per-request cost among the API models we evaluate. We further observe that their confident predictions are more accurate, enabling selective routing in real-world production scenarios. Across classification tasks, the main weakness of most decision models is abstention: they rarely choose the no-answer option, which accounts for most of their gap to the strongest LLMs (although Gemini-Decision-1 is the notable exception in this regard). We also find that the judging quality of decision models drops sharply for smaller models, and they are also more prone to position bias. In short, our findings provide practical guidance for selecting decision models based on abstention behavior, confidence, and deployment cost, rather than overall accuracy alone.

Screening LLM Judges Without Ground Truth: A Preliminary Evaluation of an AI Dental Hygiene Coach 2026-11-17
📝 29 pages, 2 figures, 19 tables
Show abstract

Evaluating a fine-tuned coaching model with expert graders is slow and expensive, and an LLM judge cannot be trusted without ground truth. We present a staged pipeline that screens candidate LLM judges and student models before expert review, using explicit decision rules at each stage: judge output audit, similarity with hosted judges, bias checks, and decision robustness. We demonstrate it on an AI dental hygiene coach. The case study uses synthetic, clinically unvalidated training and reference data to demonstrate the operation of the pipeline, rather than to establish the clinical performance of the resulting models. Two fine-tuned small language models (Granite-4.1-8B and Qwen3-8B), each trained at three data sizes, were scored on seven metrics by five judges (three local, two hosted). Two local judges, Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it, were not distinguishable from the agreement between the hosted judges on six of seven metrics by rank correlation, and Qwen3.8-27B was the most similar to the hosted judges overall. No judge met our criterion for favoring its own model family or training-data source. The student comparison did not separate the two models on any metric under the pipeline's rule, so the choice of Granite-4.1-8B, which led in 29 of 35 judge-by-metric cells, is a lean that depends partly on truncated Qwen3-8B outputs. The pipeline also surfaced problems that raw scores hid, including the truncated outputs and hallucination flags from one hosted judge that no other judge corroborated. No dental hygiene expert has graded these responses, so the findings are preliminary and the pipeline's final stage, anchoring to expert judgement, is proposed but not performed.

Think Before You Code: A Benchmark for Testing LLMs on Software Engineering Deliberation 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Think Before You Code (TBYC) is a benchmark pipeline for studying whether code-focused language models can reason about software issues in ways that resemble human engineering deliberation. The benchmark curates GitHub issue discussions into structured records, extracts human discussion artifacts such as problems, solutions, constraints, rationales, and trade-offs, and then prompts models to generate issue-level "thoughts" using retrieved code context and few-shot examples. It converts model responses back into the same artifact space and evaluates them against human annotations using complementary metrics for artifact type detection, metadata matching, tag overlap, and semantic summary similarity. Beyond scoring, this study includes visualization, leaderboard, and baseline tools that make it possible to compare models, prompting strategies, and extraction methods across repositories and issues. Think Before You Code is an end-to-end evaluation environment for measuring how closely LLM software reasoning aligns with human strategic judgment.

Who Says What: Symbolic Trimodal Binding Mechanisms in Audio-Visual LLMs 2026-11-17
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Current Audio-Visual LLMs (AVLLMs) struggle with reasoning over videos featuring multi-speaker dialogues. In such videos, resolving "who says what" is crucial, which necessitates trimodal (text-audio-visual) binding. Motivated by these challenges, we systematically investigate how this trimodal binding is achieved in AVLLMs. Specifically, we identify emergent symbolic trimodal binding mechanisms in AVLLMs that utilize modality-specific symbolic variables. By encoding auditory and visual components into symbolic variables-capturing temporal utterance sequences and spatial entity coordinates, respectively-the model establishes cross-modal linking within this abstract space. Crucially, we reveal that when trimodal binding fails, the breakdown predominantly stems from misaligned audio-visual connections. To overcome this bottleneck, we introduce an audio-visual prompting method utilizing an off-the-shelf Active Speaker Detection (ASD) model. By simply overlaying visual bounding boxes on active speakers, this training-free approach yields immediate performance gains across four conversation-centric benchmarks. Moreover, lightweight fine-tuning of fewer than 300 steps on these ASD-prompted-videos extends these gains to three general AV benchmarks, suggesting the generalizability of our method. Our code is available at https://github.com/kaistmm/Who-Says-What.

A Grey Literature Review of Graduate Students' Research Practices and Skills in the LLM Era 2026-11-17
📝 12 pages
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Context: Graduate students in computing now use Large Language Models (LLMs) throughout their research, from literature reviews and coding to data analysis and writing. Yet there is little shared understanding of which skills competent use of these tools requires, so graduate programs struggle to teach, mentor, or assess it, and unverified outputs can propagate into the scientific record. Objective: We investigate which LLM-based practices graduate students employ in their research and which skills they need to benefit from these practices. Method: We conducted a grey literature review of Reddit discussions. We retrieved 1,383 posts from five research-oriented subreddits, screened them against explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria, and analyzed the 54 included posts with thematic analysis, in which two authors independently coded each excerpt. Results: We identified seven categories of practices and nine categories of skills. Commenters locate the problem in accepting generated output without inspection rather than in using LLMs, and warn that delegating the effortful parts of a task, such as debugging, reading, or validating sources, delivers the output without building the skill the task was meant to develop. Traditional skills, such as programming and critical thinking, are what allow students to verify what an LLM produces. Conclusions: Our categories give graduate programs a concrete basis to teach students to work with LLMs without eroding the skills that research training should build, and they add research-specific skills, such as data sharing and scientific reproducibility, to existing AI competency frameworks.

ToolFlood: Beyond Selection -- Hiding Valid Tools from LLM Agents via Semantic Covering 2026-11-17
📝 Accepted to EACL 2027 (main)
Show abstract

Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly use external tools for complex tasks and rely on embedding-based retrieval to select a small top-$k$ subset for reasoning. Prior work on attacking these systems competes for tool selection, assuming benign tools remain in the retrieved set; the robustness of the retrieval stage that produces this set is underexplored. This paper introduces ToolFlood, a retrieval-layer attack on tool-augmented LLM agents. Rather than altering which tool is chosen after retrieval, ToolFlood attacks retrieval itself: it injects a few attacker-controlled tools whose metadata is positioned to exploit the geometry of embedding space, so that these tools semantically span many user queries, dominate the top-k results, and push all benign tools out of the agent's context. ToolFlood uses a two-phase adversarial tool generation strategy. It first samples subsets of target queries and uses an LLM to iteratively generate diverse tool names and descriptions. It then runs an iterative greedy selection that chooses tools maximizing coverage of remaining queries in embedding space under a cosine-distance threshold, stopping when all queries are covered or a budget is reached. We provide theoretical intuition of retrieval saturation and show on a large catalog (ToolBench) that ToolFlood achieves up to a 95\% attack success rate with a ~1\% injection rate. The code is available at: https://github.com/as1-prog/ToolFlood

PSM: Prompt Sensitivity Minimization via LLM-Guided Black-Box Optimization 2026-11-17
📝 accepted at the Trustworthy Agentic AI Workshop @ AAAI 2026 (Singapore, Jan 27, 2026). Workshop PDF: https://trustagenticai.github.io/AAAI2026/AAAI-Workshop/20.pdf
Show abstract

System prompts are critical for guiding the behavior of Large Language Models (LLMs), yet they often contain proprietary logic or sensitive information, making them a prime target for extraction attacks. Adversarial queries can successfully elicit these hidden instructions, posing significant security and privacy risks. Existing defense mechanisms frequently rely on heuristics, incur substantial computational overhead, or are inapplicable to models accessed via black-box APIs. This paper introduces a novel framework for hardening system prompts through shield appending, a lightweight approach that adds a protective textual layer to the original prompt. Our core contribution is the formalization of prompt hardening as a utility-constrained optimization problem. We leverage an LLM-as-optimizer to search the space of possible SHIELDs, seeking to minimize a leakage metric derived from a suite of adversarial attacks, while simultaneously preserving task utility above a specified threshold, measured by semantic fidelity to baseline outputs. This black-box, optimization-driven methodology is lightweight and practical, requiring only API access to the target and optimizer LLMs. We demonstrate empirically that our optimized SHIELDs significantly reduce prompt leakage against a comprehensive set of extraction attacks, outperforming established baseline defenses without compromising the model's intended functionality. Our work presents a paradigm for developing robust, utility-aware defenses in the escalating landscape of LLM security. The code is made public on the following link: https://github.com/psm-defense/psm

Network-Exposed Configuration Attacks on LLM-Controlled Social Robots 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) in embodied robots create security dependencies beyond inference. We investigate a network-exposed configuration attack on the Pollen Robotics Reachy Mini Lite, allowing an unauthenticated attacker to replace personality settings and the cloud LLM's system prompt through a management interface, without adversarial conversational input. Eleven prompt-injection attempts across five categories were reported as resisted, whereas both configuration-layer attack paths produced observable behavioural changes on physical hardware. We demonstrate local and network-adjacent access, mapping the weakness to CWE-306 (Missing Authentication for a Critical Function) and CWE-1327 (Binding to an Unrestricted IP Address), with a reported CVSS v3.1 score of 6.5 (Medium). A configuration endpoint also accepts cross-origin requests without a CORS preflight (CWE-352; 6.5 Medium), while the persistent-memory sink stores content without enforcing its stated restrictions. A source review of 59 public repositories identifies missing authentication in 10 of 11 source-verifiable genuine applications, or 11 of 11 including a framework-default case. These findings indicate recurrence within the sampled repositories, not prevalence in commercial products. We characterise a deployment gap between developer-oriented network assumptions and consumer-facing embodied AI security: inference-layer refusal cannot prevent unauthorised changes to model instructions. Potential consequences include surveillance through onboard cameras and microphones and social engineering of nearby users. ParallelVLM: Lossless Video-LLM Acceleration with Visual Alignment Aware Parallel Speculative Decoding 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Although current Video-LLMs achieve impressive performance in video understanding tasks, their autoregressive decoding efficiency remains constrained by the massive number of video tokens. Visual token pruning can partially ease this bottleneck, yet existing approaches still suffer from information loss and yield only modest acceleration in decoding. In this paper, we propose ParallelVLM, a training-free draft-then-verify speculative decoding framework that overcomes both mutual waiting and limited speedup-ratio problems between draft and target models in long-video settings. DAPS: Dependency-Aware Premise Selection for LLM Theorem Proving 2026-11-17
Show abstract

LLM-based theorem proving in Lean 4 has advanced rapidly, but provers are difficult to leverage newly contributed lemmas, because retrieval skews toward popular foundational lemmas, leaving the right premises buried among hundreds of thousands of candidates. Existing neural selectors rank candidates by semantic similarity, with three intrinsic limitations: frequency-skewed retrieval, isolated pairwise ranking, and degradation on out-of-distribution queries such as competition problems and informal language. Given that mathematics is structured by dependencies rather than similarities, we restore this signal to both the training data and the model. We curate two underused dependency resources. We conduct the first systematic mining of 53 Lean Blueprint projects, a previously untapped corpus of 3,805 manually curated informal+formal nodes that capture rare, research-level premises absent from Mathlib4. We further pair it with the extraction of comprehensive, typed, multi-level Mathlib4 dependencies. We then introduce DAPS, a dependency-aware premise selector with a structural neighborhood encoder, a group-level contrastive objective, and a Mathlib-then-Blueprint adaptation procedure. DAPS reaches 78.74 Recall@16 on Mathlib4-Heldout (+11.41 over the strong selector LeanHammer) and widens the margin to 7xrecall on competition-style queries. Downstream, its selected premises raise Goedel-Prover-V2-8B Pass@4 on miniF2F-test from 49.90 to 54.03 and improve all four general-purpose LLMs on the informal IMO-ProofBench (up to +11.4% relative). Can Activation Steering Substitute for Prompt-Based Affective Priming in LLM Digital Twins? 2026-11-17
📝 Code, generation logs and analysis scripts: https://github.com/JunyiTim/twin-activation-injection
Show abstract

Affective priming -- asking participants to write about a recalled event, such as a time they felt grateful or lacked control, and then measuring the induced state and its spillover onto related judgments -- is a standard way to manipulate emotion in the social sciences, and it also shifts the responses of LLM-based digital twins. The writing task, however, requires generating a full text. We test whether activation steering can replace it. Using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 100 personas in two domains (gratitude, lack of control), we compare a real-prompt path replicating the original Twin-2K-500 Mega Study (J2) with an injection path that skips the writing and adds a mean-difference direction, extracted from human writing, to the residual stream at varying strengths. The real-prompt effects fall between the original human and digital-twin effects in 5 of 6 comparisons. Injection shifts the manipulation check in a dose-dependent way and matches the real-prompt effect at one strength in the gratitude domain (beta ~ 2.7), but overshoots beyond it, on the proximal measure as well, and asymmetrically across the two directions. A structure-matched No-Injection baseline separates two sources of difference: omitting the writing task raises the proximal measure in both domains, whereas the injected direction itself moves a distal measure in the lack-of-control domain. Substitution is therefore measure-dependent: validity established for one way of inducing a state does not carry over uniformly to another.

AutoPBO: LLM-powered Optimization for Local Search PBO Solvers 2026-11-17
📝 Author order updated. No changes to the scientific content
Show abstract

Pseudo-Boolean Optimization (PBO) provides a powerful framework for modeling combinatorial problems through pseudo-Boolean (PB) constraints. Local search solvers have shown excellent performance in PBO solving, and their efficiency is highly dependent on their internal heuristics to guide the search. Still, their design often requires significant expert effort and manual tuning in practice. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated potential in automating algorithm design, their application to optimizing PBO solvers remains unexplored. In this work, we introduce AutoPBO, a novel LLM-powered framework to automatically enhance PBO local search solvers. We conduct experiments on a broad range of four public benchmarks, including one real-world benchmark, a benchmark from PB competition, an integer linear programming optimization benchmark, and a crafted combinatorial benchmark, to evaluate the performance improvement achieved by AutoPBO and compare it with six state-of-the-art competitors, including two local search PBO solvers NuPBO and OraSLS, two complete PB solvers PBO-IHS and RoundingSat, and two mixed integer programming (MIP) solvers Gurobi and SCIP. AutoPBO demonstrates significant improvements over previous local search approaches, while maintaining competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art competitors. The results suggest that AutoPBO offers a promising approach to automating local search solver design.

The Between-Round Decision in Autonomous Research: An LLM Controller That Reads the Whole Trajectory 2026-11-17
📝 16 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Executing an idea often changes how promising it looks, so the next step in autonomous research should rest on results, not on proposals. After each round of experiments, the system must decide whether to refine the best solution or start a new direction, which solution to build from, and what goal to set. We call this the between-round decision. Existing systems wrap it in machinery such as rules, scores, search trees, and schedules; where an LLM makes it, what the LLM needs to read is left open. We ask whether an LLM can make this decision from accumulated evidence, without a search structure around it, and how much of that evidence it needs to read. We present BRED (Between-Round Evidence-based Decision), an LLM-based controller that maintains the best verified solution so far (the Champion) and a log of past rounds, and makes one decision per round. On three widely used research-agent benchmark tasks, BRED improves on the task baselines by 4.80% in MAE on ETTh1 (to 0.4172) and by 3.44 and 3.43 points of accuracy on SST-2 (to 95.07%) and CIFAR-100 (to 84.63%), ahead of the published results. When the controller does not read the accumulated evidence, the same loop gains less: with no history it reaches 0.4299 on ETTh1 and 83.71% on CIFAR-100, whereas the full controller's MAE is 2.95% lower and its accuracy 0.92 points higher. On a new task, speech-based depression detection under a leakage-free protocol, it reaches a macro-F1 of 0.6164 on seeds held out from selection, 5.5 points above its baseline and 4.0 above the strongest published system. Code is available at https://github.com/learninginvision/BRED.

What Do LLMs Use to Judge Synthesis Recipes? An Experimental Analysis on 240 Solid-State Syntheses 2026-11-17
📝 23 pages, 13 figures, NeurIPS Ai4Mat
Show abstract

Inorganic materials discovery is increasingly limited by synthesis rather than by material design. Large language models (LLMs) are being adopted to propose synthesis conditions with little experimental evidence of their capabilities and biases. Here, we carry out an experimental synthesis benchmark of 240 experiments across eight known and unknown inorganic oxides and subsequently evaluate an LLM as a zero-shot ranker over fixed banks of solid-state synthesis recipes. For each pair of recipes for the same oxide target, `GPT-5.6-Sol` selects the recipe expected to yield higher target-phase purity. We aggregate these pairwise preferences using a regularized Bradley-Terry model to obtain a ranked list of synthesis recipes for a given target material. By comparison with the actual purity, obtained through X-ray diffraction, we measure `GPT-5.6-Sol`'s performance as a zero-shot ranker (0.72 pairwise accuracy). By selective removal of the information exposed to the model during this ranking, we conclude that ranking is driven predominantly by thermal features, with reaction chemistry modulating the effect. Ranking accuracy tracks the purity-temperature correlation by target (Spearman ρ= 0.91\), \(p = 0.002\), and on the six thermally-driven targets withholding all chemical identity hardly impacts performance (0.68) compared to withholding temperature (0.56). Ranking performance was higher on known than unknown targets, but the group-level difference was not statistically conclusive. The methodological choices highlight the need for more experimentally validated synthesis benchmarks and position LLMs as a thermally informed prior.

Out of Sight, Still in Mind: Token Compression for Omni-LLMs 2026-11-17
📝 Preprint
Show abstract

The goal of this paper is to reduce the input token cost of Omni-modal large language models (Omni-LLMs) at inference time. Omni-LLMs reason jointly over audio, video and text, but the cost of the three streams is highly unbalanced: visual tokens account for the vast majority of the input, and are highly redundant. In this paper, we propose ReMo, a training-free framework that compresses visual tokens by redistributing their information across modalities: a visual token is kept only if its information appears nowhere else. ReMo achieves this in two ways: (i) it aligns audio and video in a shared space via closed-form calibration, and removes visual tokens already explained by the audio or by other visual tokens; and (ii) it replaces object-level visual tokens, identified by a lightweight object detector, with compact text proxies, short descriptions of each object and its location, conveying the same content in far fewer tokens. On Qwen2.5-Omni at two model scales, ReMo removes 54% of the input tokens with comparable accuracy. It performs comparably to the full-token model, reaching 101.2% and 101.4% of its average accuracy over five audio-visual benchmarks.

Adaptive LLM-Driven Honeypots vs. LLM-Driven Attackers: Does Narrative Coherence Improve Deception? 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) enable honeypots to generate responses and deceptive artefacts on demand, yet existing approaches evaluate generated content one reply at a time and leave open whether artefacts that jointly describe a coherent event retain an attacker better than unrelated ones. This question becomes pressing as autonomous LLM-driven offensive agents emerge as realistic adversaries. This paper proposes narrative deception, a reactive strategy in which the defender maps each observed attacker command to a MITRE ATT&CK technique, selects a compatible scenario from a curated catalogue based on MITRE Engage, and deploys mutually referential artefacts (files, processes, services, and logs) on an emulated Ubuntu host while the intrusion is under way. An LLM-powered defender and an autonomous LLM attacker interact through a real SSH session, and the attacker's termination policy distinguishes abandonment due to suspected deception from abandonment due to loss of interest. A campaign of 1,150 sessions compares four conditions of increasing sophistication (Static, Same-TTP, Multi-TTP, and Chain) across four open-weight attacker models, four defender models, and three attacker skill levels. Engagement increases significantly at every step. With the same artefact budget per reaction, coherent scenarios raise the median session length of advanced attackers from 40 to 165 commands and reduce their abandonment from 86% to 52%. The effect grows with attacker skill, holds across all sixteen model pairs, and comes at comparable inference cost. Nearly all sessions ended through loss of interest rather than detection, which suggests that deception should sustain meaningful findings, not only emulator fidelity.

AfriNORM: An Evaluation Benchmark for Normative Reasoning of LLMs Across African Cultures 2026-11-17
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 5 tables
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed across various sectors. However, current evaluation benchmarks are insufficient to assess the applicability of these models in local African contexts, as most focus on linguistic fluency (grammar/translation) rather than cultural competence. 📝 28 pages, 2 figures. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026

Observational data rarely identify a single causal structure. Such structures are typically modeled as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) whose arrows point from direct causes to their effects, and observed correlations and conditional independencies usually leave a whole family of DAGs that fit the data equally well. A causal claim follows from the data only if it holds in every one of them. Classical causal discovery methods such as the Peter--Clark (PC) algorithm summarize this family as a completed partially directed acyclic graph (CPDAG): an edge is directed when every compatible graph orients it the same way, and undirected when the data leave its direction open. On the Corr2Cause benchmark, large language models (LLMs) that reason in free text often lose track of this family and decide from a few local edges. We propose \emph{Structured Thinking}, a two-turn method in which the model first summarizes the CPDAG in a constrained schema and then checks the claim against that summary.
