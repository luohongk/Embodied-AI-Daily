REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control 2026-10-30
📝 32 pages, 14 figures
Show abstract

Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. To address this, we propose REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1 and reactive System 0 policies cooperate under a single golden rule - no running policy waits on the brain. Instead of querying the brain at each failure, REA employs a route memory that stores each policy's measured success ahead of time, a boundary-triggered judge gate that consults the brain on policy execution, and a shared-budget escalation ladder for bounded recovery. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.

$\nabla$Mimic: Ultra-Efficient Humanoid Control with Differentiable Simulation 2026-10-30
📝 26 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Robot policy learning is dominated by zeroth-order methods such as PPO, which treat the simulator as a black box and pay for it with enormous sample counts. First-order policy learning differentiates through the dynamics, but on contact-rich whole-body control it has rarely worked: its gradients explode. A recent world-model method showed that derivative information can make humanoid motion tracking far more efficient, using surrogate gradients of a learned model. We show that exact gradients of the simulator itself do better, once two obstacles are removed. Exact gradients contain rare unbounded samples, because contact dynamics is not differentiable everywhere and its derivative diverges near some events; and they are expensive, because the simulator's adjoint has to replay the contact solve. $\nabla$Mimic bounds these samples with median-relative per-sample gradient clipping at every step of the backward pass, and reuses each costly simulated window by multistep regression onto gradient-derived action targets, which bounds every action target independently of the gradient scale. NablaEngine, a differentiable GPU simulation engine for the Unitree G1, removes the cost: it computes MuJoCo's full contact dynamics with fused per-world kernels and the adjoint of a whole control step as a single megakernel, with analytic gradients that match float64 finite differences, $11.5\times$ faster than the available MuJoCo Warp adjoint. On whole-body tracking of all 40 LAFAN1 clips with one policy on one GPU, $\nabla$Mimic reaches the score reported by the state-of-the-art world-model method, 0.82, in 20 minutes instead of 1.2 h, and 0.874 in 1.8 h. SHAC, the standard first-order baseline, stops on exploding gradients within minutes. We release NablaEngine and $\nabla$Mimic as open-source infrastructure for first-order humanoid policy learning.

DiffWBC: Unlocking Steerable General Humanoid Motion Tracking via One-Step Diffusion Whole-Body Control 2026-10-30
Show abstract

General humanoid motion trackers typically rely on deterministic policies that cannot adapt online without brittle reference editing or costly retraining. We present DiffWBC, shifting whole-body control to a generative paradigm via a one-step diffusion model. By modeling distributions over future trajectories, DiffWBC unifies general tracking, test-time steering, and simulation-free failure discovery within a single controller: (1) Generative general tracking: DiffWBC requires a single forward pass per 50 Hz control step and can be distilled from an RL teacher or trained from scratch via model-based RL, achieving the lowest local tracking error among six general trackers on held-out benchmarks (20.1 mm on BONES) and deploying on a physical Unitree G1. (2) Zero-shot test-time steering: differentiable cost gradients and body-group masks modulate the frozen controller online, substantially outperforming kinematic reference editing (0.84 clean pass at 1.25 m ceiling vs. 0.10) and zero-shot generalizing across hurdles, spatial reaching, actuator faults, and tray balancing. (3) Simulation-free failure discovery: plan dispersion across generation noise effectively identifies physical tracking failures (91% precision in top 2%) without physics simulations; targeted fine-tuning on flagged clips resolves 50% of failure cases. Videos and interactive demonstrations are available at https://diffwbc.github.io/#selfassess.

CuPIQP: A Batched and Differentiable QP Solver on GPUs for Robot Learning and Control 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Quadratic programs (QPs) are fundamental building blocks of many robotic tasks such as inverse kinematics, whole-body control, and model predictive control. Extending these methods to large-scale robot learning and control requires solving many QPs concurrently and, for end-to-end learning, differentiating through their solutions. Efficient batched optimization is therefore essential to bringing established model-based methods into GPU-based robotics pipelines. We present \textsc{cuPIQP}, a batched and differentiable QP solver on GPUs based on a proximal interior-point method, with unified support for dense, sparse, and multistage problems. The solver exploits parallelism across independent QPs and their internal structure while preserving per-problem numerical updates and termination decisions. Benchmarks with up to 16384 QPs per batch demonstrate solve-time speedups of up to $18.8\times$ over \textsc{Moreau} and $4.26\times$ over eight-core \textsc{PIQP}. In an end-to-end learning task, \textsc{cuPIQP} accelerates training by $4.6\times$ over \textsc{QPAX} and $7.8\times$ over \textsc{Moreau}. By reducing the computational cost, \textsc{cuPIQP} provides efficient, reusable infrastructure for integrating model-based optimization into large-scale robot learning and control.

ELEVATE: Agentic Controller Acquisition from Task Failure for Quadrupedal Manipulation 2026-10-28
📝 23 pages, 14 figures, including appendix. Project page and videos: https://agentic-quad-elevate.github.io/public/
Show abstract

Quadrupedal manipulators can extend their workspace through whole-body interaction, but task-planning agents' capabilities remain limited by their available controllers. We present ELEVATE, a framework that uses persistent failures during task-program refinement to identify and acquire missing whole-body control capabilities. When these failures suggest a missing physical capability, a coding agent formulates and iteratively refines a reinforcement-learning problem, specifying behavior-specific rewards, curricula, and termination conditions. Validated controllers are added to the capability library and composed with existing controllers to retry the task. Starting from a single pretrained loco-manipulation controller, ELEVATE autonomously acquires controllers for wall-supported elevation, supported manipulation, and wall descent across a sequence of seven simulation tasks. These controllers are reused in subsequent reaching and object-manipulation tasks. On the five tasks beyond the initial controller's workspace, ELEVATE achieves mean success rates of 98.4%-100% across five independent runs, while coding-agent baselines with a fixed controller library fail to complete these tasks. The synthesized task programs, together with the acquired controllers, also transfer to a physical quadrupedal manipulator without real-robot training or fine-tuning, achieving 18/20 successes in rack retrieval and 16/20 in floor-to-rack placement. Videos and demonstrations are available at https://agentic-quad-elevate.github.io/public/.

Learning Force-Reactive Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-26
📝 9 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Many everyday tasks require controlling contact force as well as motion. On a humanoid, this force must often be coordinated across both hands and the whole body, and cannot be specified by kinematic trajectories alone. Therefore, we present FLORE, a framework for learning force-reactive humanoid loco-manipulation. We collect robot-free demonstrations using two force-sensing UMI-style handheld grippers for hand motion and contact wrenches, and wearable trackers for body motion. A force-reactive diffusion policy takes vision, proprioception, and measured wrenches as input, then predicts actions and per-hand force commands. A hybrid compliance whole-body controller with force tracking executes these commands while maintaining balance: an integral of the force error measured by real force/torque (F/T) sensors lets each hand track force accurately along the commanded direction and remain compliant along the others. In simulation, the controller tracks per-hand force commands of varied magnitude and direction, with little loss in whole-body motion accuracy. Across three contact-rich tasks on the robot, the force-reactive policy succeeds in 80% of rollouts, compared with 26.7% for a position-only policy trained on the same demonstrations and executed by the same controller. For more details, please visit our website: https://louis-leee.github.io/FLoRe/.

HoloBoxing: A Simple Recipe for Humanoid Boxing with 7 Minutes of Single-GPU Training 2026-10-26
Show abstract

HoloBoxing presents a simple recipe for learning humanoid boxing by building on a pretrained whole-body controller. The recipe separates reusable motor skills from task decisions: a compact planner requests future body-point targets, while the frozen controller handles joint-level execution. A learned decoder maps 32-dimensional actions to structured target windows, and measured-state imitation initializes the planner before reinforcement learning. Task learning then refines interaction behavior while reusing the decoder and controller, avoiding the need to relearn whole-body control. This combination keeps task optimization focused on a compact upper policy and supplies an initial behavior before reward-driven exploration. Starting from that available controller, the recipe accounts for about seven minutes of additional training, including reused representation fitting and a single-GPU task run. This excludes controller pretraining, data preparation, evaluation, and search. Simulated boxing experiments and component controls evaluate the resulting behavior and the roles of imitation initialization and the target representation.

ThinkLocoManip: A Whole-Body Control Agentic Harness for Legged Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-25
Show abstract

In this paper, we propose ThinkLocoManip, an agentic harness for legged loco-manipulation designed to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks in large-scale environments. Existing approaches typically follow either direct action generation or a plan-then-act paradigm, both of which can struggle with the generalization and error-recovery capabilities required for extended task execution. In contrast, ThinkLocoManip adopts an interleaved reasoning-and-acting paradigm, allowing the agent to continuously reason about its current state, execute actions, observe their outcomes, and adapt its strategy accordingly. Building on this paradigm, our agentic harness orchestrates a suite of specialized tools for perception, navigation, manipulation, and locomotion, enabling flexible composition of heterogeneous capabilities. Unlike existing robotic agentic harnesses, ThinkLocoManip explicitly incorporates locomotion as a tool domain for agent reasoning and execution. We further design a set of skills that explicitly account for both tool usage within each functional domain and coordination across domains, supporting effective execution of complex behaviors over long horizons. To tackle task failure during long-horizon interactions, we develop keyframe thinking mechanism to preserve critical execution context and support efficient error recovery. Extensive real-world experiments demonstrate that ThinkLocoManip substantially outperforms representative methods based on both direct action generation and plan-then-act paradigms across challenging long-horizon legged loco-manipulation tasks. Notably, ThinkLocoManip achieves a success rate of 77%, more than twice that of the strongest plan-then-act baseline (37.1%). Our experiments further validate the effectiveness of interleaved reasoning and acting, tool orchestration, and coordinated skill execution.

HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience 2026-10-25
Show abstract

Extending Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to humanoid robots requires coordinated whole-body control and diverse demonstrations that are costly to collect in real-world scenarios. Although egocentric human demonstrations provide a complementary source of task experience, differences in visual embodiment and missing robot proprioception complicate transfer to humanoid policies. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe, a family of Humanoid Generalist VLAs pre-trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe employs three existing representative VLA architectures to predict latent motion tokens for a pretrained whole-body controller, together with hand-control signals. Based on this, we introduce a three-stage training pipeline that combines egocentric pre-training, humanoid adaptation, and downstream fine-tuning. To facilitate transfer across embodiments, we construct pseudo-proprioceptive states through simulation playback during pre-training and modify arm appearance in egocentric observations through re-rendering during downstream fine-tuning. With this pipeline, HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art on simulation benchmark HumanoidArena. Furthermore, real-world experiments on the Unitree G1 indicate that augmenting robot demonstrations with visually adapted egocentric demonstrations significantly increases success on a pick-and-drop task.

X-WBC: A Cross-Embodiment Foundation Model for Humanoid Whole-Body Control 2026-10-25
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026

Scaling humanoid whole-body control toward general-purpose deployment requires large human motion corpora and training experience shared across robot bodies. Existing methods usually train one policy per robot, leaving motion experience isolated across embodiments. We introduce X-WBC, a cross-embodiment foundation framework that separates relatively shared human motion semantics from embodiment-specific physical execution. Human-centered command tokens align full human motion, robot reference motion, and sparse VR observations. A causal Transformer learns reusable temporal structure from mixed multi-robot rollouts, while lightweight robot-specific modules map the shared representation to each robot's proprioception and action space. Across nine simulated embodiments, external motions, and four real robots, experiments show that joint training improves tracking, the aligned representation supports consistent control across command sources, and the learned policy remains competitive beyond the training corpus. These results support heterogeneous humanoids as joint data sources and establish cross-embodiment joint training as a practical route toward whole-body control foundation models.

Motion Retargeting with Spatiotemporal Preferences for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-24
Show abstract

Human motion retargeting bridges the embodiment gap between humans and humanoid robots by converting human demonstrations into reference trajectories for whole-body control and loco-manipulation. For object-interaction tasks, successful execution depends on accurately preserving body--object relations at critical stages. However, uniform per-frame iteration budgets do not account for these localized accuracy requirements, limiting the computational efficiency of retargeting. To address this limitation, we present Spatiotemporal Retargeting (STR), which uses keyframes and key body parts to allocate optimization effort. Specifically, a vision-language model selects relevant motion signals and body parts, and keyframes are identified from changes in the selected signals. Based on these selections, STR assigns additional solver iterations to keyframes and higher interaction weights to the selected parts. To further improve contact fidelity, we introduce Mechanics-Aware Contact Anchoring (MCA) to refine object-surface contact positions using an approximate object-wrench model while retaining the selected surface directions. Building on the resulting references, we develop Spatiotemporal Reinforcement Learning (STRL), which combines adaptive key-stage sampling with a contact-position reward based on target proximity for downstream control. Simulation experiments on a Unitree G1 and real-world experiments on a Unitree R1 demonstrate that STR improves geometric contact coverage and continuity at lower computational cost, while the resulting references improve downstream task completion across diverse object-interaction motions.

SMR: Learning streaming motion retargeting for humanoids from human semantic 2026-10-23
📝 17 pages, 6 figures. Project page: https://logosintelligencelab.github.io/SMR/
Show abstract

Retargeting human motion to humanoids requires preserving motion semantics while accounting for the robot's physical constraints. Kinematic pipelines prescribe a robot trajectory through geometric matching, without establishing whether it can be executed under the robot's dynamics. Prior physics-supervised neural methods train bidirectional full-sequence models on physics-refined kinematic trajectories, remaining tied to prescribed kinematic solutions and requiring noncausal context. We propose SMR, a streaming neural motion retargeter learned from semantic-conditioned physics experts. We convert human motion into robot-aware task-space targets for body landmark positions, orientations, and velocities. Physics experts track these targets directly in simulation under contact, dynamics, and actuator constraints. We train a single strictly causal Transformer on their executed state trajectories that complete the motion and pass semantic and physical validity checks. At each incoming frame, the student uses only current and past human observations to generate a robot motion reference for a downstream whole-body controller. Experiments demonstrate that semantic-conditioned physics experts generate motion with higher semantic fidelity and physical validity than direct optimization-based retargeting, while the resulting streaming retargeter achieves improved closed-loop performance over kinematic and physics-refined baselines under a common whole-body controller.

CoCarry: Contact-Support-Structured Retargeting for Dual-Humanoid Cooperative Carrying 2026-10-23
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Recent advances in large-scale human motion data, motion retargeting, and motion imitation have enabled increasingly capable whole-body control for individual humanoids. Cooperative carrying is more challenging because successful coordination requires not only plausible individual motions, but also consistent cross-agent and agent-object relations, including shared grasp, relative configuration, and whole-body support. We introduce CoCarry, a cooperative-carrying dataset containing 220 synchronized two-person sequences across 21 task schemes, five shared-object geometries, four terrain types, and approximately 61 minutes of motion. We further propose Contact-Support-Structured Retargeting, which starts from nominal individual motions and jointly refines both humanoids around the same prescribed object trajectory. Cooperation-aware object-local hand anchors preserve compatible four-hand grasp relations, while complementary upper object-interaction and lower support structures retain object-relative geometry and coordinated support. Across all 220 CoCarry sequences retargeted to two A3 humanoids, our method achieves 0.9995 contact preservation, reduces penetration duration by 67.0% and 71.0% relative to GMR and OmniRetarget, respectively, and maintains a 100% retargeting success rate. On CORE4D with Unitree G1 humanoids, it achieves 0.9906 contact preservation with zero measured sliding. We further validate the generated references through physics simulation and multiple real-world cooperative carrying experiments on two physical A3 humanoids, demonstrating their usability for downstream dual-humanoid control.

Humans achieve complex manipulation through coordinated whole-body control, whereas most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models treat robot body parts largely independently, making high-DoF humanoid control challenging and often unstable. ReactiveBFM: Reactive Closed-Loop Motion Planning Towards Universal Humanoid Whole-Body Control 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted by CoRL 2026. Project page: https://xiao-chen.tech/reactivebfm/
Show abstract

While current Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) provide robust control priors for humanoids, they only execute pre-defined reference motions. As a result, they are vulnerable to environmental shifts and incapable of reactive whole-body coordination. Naively cascading them with generative motion planners fails to achieve true reactivity, as inevitable tracking discrepancies induce fatal cumulative exposure bias. To bridge this gap, we propose ReactiveBFM, a real-time closed-loop planning-control framework. At its core, we effectively mitigate exposure bias via a scheduled prefix sampling curriculum, forcing the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors from imperfect physical states rather than ground-truth trajectories. Systematically, to reconcile the severe latency mismatch between auto-regressive planning and high-frequency tracking, we introduce an asynchronous replanning mechanism. Combined with trajectory chunking to temporally ensemble spatial references, our system guarantees spatio-temporally fluid execution without physical jitter. Deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid, ReactiveBFM demonstrates unprecedented physical agility across a vast repertoire of text-conditioned closed-loop motions. Notably, ReactiveBFM achieves zero-shot moving target reaching, showcasing intricate whole-body coordination and on-the-fly replanning. In sim-to-sim benchmarking under severe perturbations, ReactiveBFM achieves a 93.1% success rate, significantly outperforming cascaded open-loop baselines by 28.6%.

SP-HLM: Sparse-Pose-Driven Motion Generation and Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effective interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single action representation for both task intent and whole-body coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms. SP-VLA, a vision-language-action model, first predicts task-relevant sparse poses and finger joint positions from multimodal observations and language instructions using asynchronous action-chunk inference. Given these sparse targets, SP-MGM, a motion generation model trained through two-stage distillation, reconstructs coordinated full-body motion in real time. A pretrained motion encoder maps this motion to a compact latent representation. SP-GMT, a general motion tracker, then integrates the explicit sparse targets with this implicit motion prior for whole-body control. Experiments in simulation and on a real humanoid robot demonstrate improved motion tracking, whole-body coordination, and loco-manipulation success over existing approaches. ExtremControl: Low-Latency Humanoid Teleoperation with Scaled Velocity Feedforward 2026-10-21
📝 Project website: https://extremcontrol.github.io/
Show abstract

Building a low-latency humanoid teleoperation system is essential for collecting diverse reactive and dynamic demonstrations. However, end-to-end latencies in existing approaches consistently hover around 200ms, roughly equal to human reaction time, that severely limits responsiveness and prevents tasks requiring rapid feedback. We identify position-only PD control as the root cause of this latency floor and propose ExtremControl, a low-latency whole-body control framework that: (1) incorporates scaled velocity feedforward in low-level control, reducing tracking delay by approximately 70ms with a provably optimal feedforward ratio; (2) operates directly on SE(3) poses of selected rigid links, primarily humanoid extremities, to avoid joint-space retargeting in the control loop. To minimize retargeting time, we derive a closed-form Cartesian-space mapping that uses a single anthropomorphic calibration step to translate human motion into humanoid link targets, accommodating operators with varying body proportions. Furthermore, we provide a theoretical framework for selecting control hyperparameters, and validate the effectiveness of velocity feedforward control in the reinforcement learning for locomotion setting through experiments in both simulation and the real world. Building on ExtremControl, we implement a teleoperation system supporting both optical motion capture and VR-based tracking, achieving end-to-end latency as low as 50ms --- well beneath the threshold at which system delay adds a full human reaction cycle to the control loop. This enables a qualitatively new class of reactive behaviors, including ping-pong ball balancing, juggling, and real-time return, that are beyond the capability of existing teleoperation systems.

Mobile manipulators must adapt not only to objects, but also to the spatial context in which they appear: where to position the base, how to manipulate, and where to look while navigating surrounding scene geometry. To address this, we present COWBOY, a scalable sim-to-real framework for learning such contextual whole-body manipulation. On whole-body dexterous grasping, COWBOY transfers zero-shot to hundreds of objects and diverse in-the-wild scenes, spanning cluttered tabletops, shelves, drawers, and other unstructured settings. SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 (Oral). Project website: https://supermimic.github.io
Show abstract

Tracking large human-motion datasets has become the dominant route to general, expressive humanoid whole-body control, but scaling across many motions is expensive. Current pipelines use model-free reinforcement learning, either merging per-motion specialists through lossy distillation, or brute-forcing a single policy with cluster-scale compute. SuperMimic amortizes learning across all motions with one differentiable world model of their shared dynamics, fit from logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator. The world model and the tracking policy train together in one loop, and backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts gives the policy analytic gradients. Fast imagined rollouts and analytic gradients make policy learning efficient. In 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs. It reduces GPU-hours by more than $1000\times$ and fails on only 25 of 129,514 benchmark motions, versus 179 failures for the baseline. After 9.8 hours on two GPUs, SuperMimic achieves zero failures. From 1 to 264 hours of motion, the world model's prediction error stays stable, and GPU-hours to a target return grow slowly. Our policy transfers to a physical Unitree G1 across diverse and acrobatic motions. SuperMimic removes compute as the bottleneck to scaling humanoid whole-body control, making it practical to measure motion-tracking scaling laws.

Action-Aligned Latent Planning for Versatile Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-20
📝 Project page: https://fiberaa.github.io/
Show abstract

Learning a single perceptive policy for diverse humanoid loco-manipulation requires unifying high-level task decisions without losing the state-dependent physical responses that make each interaction succeed. This makes the representation between high-level planning and whole-body control critical: kinematic or contact targets provide compact abstractions but do not directly encode interaction-specific control responses, while directly predicting low-level actions requires the planner to model high-frequency closed-loop feedback. We propose FIBER, a framework that uses the executable latent space of a frozen behavioral foundation model (BFM) as a shared planning representation for heterogeneous interaction skills. Our latent policy projection (LPP) maps independently acquired task-specific policies into this representation by identifying latent commands whose induced actions match their local control responses, enabling lower-rate planning while retaining the BFM's high-frequency state feedback. After projection, we aggregate closed-loop latent rollouts from all skills and train a single perceptive flow planner entirely offline. We further introduce AH-BFM, an adaptive-horizon BFM trained with multi-gamma forward-backward (FB) learning to support both precise control and temporally extended whole-body behavior. We evaluate FIBER on box pickup, door opening, and object grasping in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid. Across 128 diverse variations per task in simulation, FIBER achieves 42.0% mean success, more than tripling projected latent replay (11.6%) and substantially outperforming kinematic planning (3.5%). Real-world experiments further demonstrate sim-to-real transfer, skill chaining, and conditional steering through the same unified planner.

PAKE: Learning Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation with Partial Kinematic Embeddings 2026-10-20
📝 IEEE ROBOTICS AND AUTOMATION LETTERS 2026
Show abstract

Loco-manipulation has recently shown promising capabilities; however, achieving high-precision control, managing the high-dimensional action space induced by many degrees of freedom (DoFs), and fully exploiting the inherent redundancy of whole-body systems remain challenging. In this paper, we propose a novel whole-body control framework that effectively addresses these challenges by decomposing the complex loco-manipulation problem into partial reference motion generation and low-level imitation control. We introduce a new Kinematic Normalizing Flow (KNF) model, trained on a large-scale kinematic dataset, that generates diverse partial kinematic references. A high-level controller is then trained to navigate the KNF's latent space to exploit redundant solutions, while a low-level controller learns dynamically consistent motion execution. We validate our approach on the quadrupedal robot equipped with a six-DoF robotic arm. In simulation, PAKE outperforms the evaluated baselines in tracking accuracy and feasible workspace coverage on the 4,000-target benchmark. For hardware deployment, we evaluate the system over 24 episodes across 8 different mobile loco-manipulation tasks.
