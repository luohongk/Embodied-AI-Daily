CamChoreo: Benchmarking Temporally Grounded Compositional Camera Motion Understanding for Vision Language Models 2026-11-12
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Understanding camera motion is fundamental to video perception, with applications in spatial intelligence and controllable video generation. Existing camera motion evaluations typically assign labels to entire clips, overlooking within-shot changes and simultaneous movements. We therefore formulate camera motion understanding as temporally grounded, compositional recognition, requiring models to localize motion-consistent intervals and identify all active movements. We introduce CamChoreo, the first benchmark for this task, comprising 4,229 diverse single-shot clips with expert temporal annotations using 20 direction-aware labels. Nearly half of its segments contain compound camera motion. We also construct a separate training corpus of approximately 43K clips with the same annotation format. On CamChoreo, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on this corpus achieves 62.1/64.1 micro F1 with 4B/8B backbones, outperforming advanced closed-source vision language models (VLMs). To complement the primarily semantic representations of VLM visual encoders with cross-frame geometry, we introduce CamInject, which injects features from a frozen 3D foundation model into the VLM. CamInject improves over SFT but requires running the costly 3D model at inference. We instead propose CamDistill, which distills the same geometric knowledge into lightweight camera tokens during training and removes the 3D model at inference. CamDistill improves micro F1 over same-data SFT by 5.4/3.7 points on 4B/8B backbones, closely approaching CamInject. CamDistill-8B also demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer on the external camera motion understanding benchmarks CameraBench and CameraMotionVQA. Together, these resources and CamDistill enable benchmarking and improving temporally grounded, compositional camera motion understanding.

Unified Panoramic Geometry Estimation via Multi-View Foundation Models 2026-11-10
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Geometry estimation from perspective images has greatly advanced, maturing to the point where off-the-shelf foundation models are able to reconstruct 3D scene structure not only from multi-view imagery, but even from a single view. A natural extension is 3D reconstruction from panoramas, with the exciting prospect of recovering a full 360-degree scene from a single panoramic image. In this work, we introduce PaGeR (Panoramic Geometry Reconstruction), a framework to lift powerful 3D foundation models designed for perspective imagery to the panorama domain. Our strategy is to start from a pre-trained transformer for 3D reconstruction and turn it into a unified high-performance model that predicts scale-invariant depth, metric depth, surface normals, and sky masks from both perspective and omnidirectional images, in a single forward pass. By keeping architectural changes to a minimum and mixing perspective and panoramic images during training, PaGeR retains the rich 3D prior of the underlying foundation model while learning to also estimate geometrically consistent 360-degree scenes from single panoramas. We extensively test our method in both indoor and outdoor environments and find that it delivers state-of-the-art performance and excellent zero-shot performance across a wide range of scenes. Sim(3)-TurboReg: Robust and Efficient Scale-Aware Registration via Tri-Scale Consistency 2026-11-10
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables. Code available at https://github.com/ivan-zzj/Sim3-TurboReg
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D foundation models recover geometry at high fidelity, yet inference memory constraints keep them from processing long sequences natively. Current streaming systems work around this limit with sliding-window chunking, which introduces severe monocular scale ambiguity. Resolving this ambiguity requires Sim(3) registration that stays accurate and efficient under outlier contamination. We introduce Sim(3)-TurboReg, which reformulates the compatibility graph construction of TurboReg by replacing absolute distance constraints with a strict Tri-Scale Consistency (TSC) criterion. Our analysis shows that TSC suppresses both mismatching and coordinate-distortion outliers. We integrate the resulting solver into existing streaming systems and obtain high-fidelity, kilometer-scale reconstruction for feed-forward models. On controlled unknown-scale registration at 90% outliers, ours matches RANSAC accuracy with 92.5% lower median latency. In DA3-Streaming on KITTI, ours improves mean ATE RMSE by 22.2% and reduces mean end-to-end time by 16.6%. Code: https://github.com/ivan-zzj/Sim3-TurboReg

Map2SLAM: From Streaming Map Prediction to Real-Time SLAM 2026-11-08
📝 17 pages, 10 figures. Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/Map2SLAM/
Show abstract

Streaming 3D foundation models (3DFMs) estimate camera poses and dense geometry online, but they fall short of SLAM: their predictions are fixed once emitted, so loop closures cannot correct accumulated drift. With such models already providing strong geometric capability, a central question for SLAM becomes how to schedule their inference and how to keep its results as revisable state. We present Map2SLAM, which keeps LingBot-Map frozen, trains only a lightweight retrieval head, and builds SLAM on the model's inference interface, turning its predictions into observations of an external persistent SLAM state. The front-end tracks every incoming frame with the model's causal streaming inference and decides which frames are written to the persistent state as keyframes. The back-end asynchronously decides when and which past frames to re-observe, re-running the model's joint inference over them with fresh context; verified loop observations correct the trajectory online, and segment observations refine dense geometry after the sequence. All invocations share a single frame encoder pass per image, whose cached features also drive loop retrieval, so frequent re-invocation incurs no additional encoding cost. Map2SLAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on KITTI, VBR, and Oxford Spires, reducing mean ATE by over 40% compared with the best uncalibrated baselines, while maintaining real-time tracking at over 28 FPS at a resolution of $518\times154$ on an NVIDIA RTX 5090D GPU. Please visit our project page at https://robbyant-research.github.io/Map2SLAM/.

Latent Riemannian Flow Matching for Geometry-Grounded 3D Foundation Models 2026-11-05
📝 NeurIPS 2026 camera-ready. Project page: https://lisaweijler.github.io/geometry-grounded-rfm/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Geometric foundation models such as the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) provide strong 3D priors from unposed images, but operate deterministically and cannot generate plausible geometry beyond what the observations directly support. We bridge geometric foundation models and generative 3D modeling by performing flow matching directly in their latent space, leveraging learned 3D priors without committing to explicit representations such as Gaussians, meshes, or video-VAE latents. This requires respecting the latent geometry: LayerNorm places the decoder inputs on a product of zero-mean hyperspheres, and treating this space as unconstrained Euclidean substantially degrades flow matching. We therefore formulate Riemannian flow matching on this product manifold, conditioned on unposed context views and a target camera pose, keeping generated tokens within the geometry expected by the frozen decoder. Our main experiments use VGGT, while additional experiments with Depth Anything 3 show that the same formulation transfers to a different independently pretrained 3D backbone without modification. On RealEstate10K, ScanNet++, and ETH3D, our method achieves strong results against recent scene-generation baselines in both novel-view appearance and aggregated 3D geometry, establishing latent-space flow matching on geometric foundation models as a viable paradigm for 3D generation.

MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile Manipulation 2026-11-05
📝 Project page: https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/momasplat
Show abstract

Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multiview observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D. MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.

RDGSplat: Render-Dedicated Geometry for Novel View Synthesis 2026-11-05
Show abstract

Recent 3D foundation models extend to novel view synthesis by carrying a Gaussian head on the representation they already use for reconstruction. However, their rendering quality lags far behind their reconstruction accuracy, because the renderer reads a geometry and a representation that are both optimized for metric reconstruction. Prior methods alleviate this by updating the backbone weights, but they thereby sacrifice the metric predictions the model was built for. To this end, we propose RDGSplat, which decodes a geometry dedicated to rendering from a frozen 3D foundation model while keeping its metric predictions exact. In particular, we devise Render-Dedicated Geometry Decoding, which duplicates the pretrained decoders and optimizes the duplicates under photometric supervision alone. Then, a Target-Pose Conditioned Adapter is introduced to adapt the representation those decoders read towards rendering, conditioned on the predicted target pose. Extensive experiments show that RDGSplat improves novel view synthesis for three feed-forward models of two architectures on four benchmarks, with every pretrained weight frozen. On RE10K, it raises WM2.0 from 20.918 to 25.149dB by training 205.5M added parameters against a frozen 1.4B backbone, and leaves its predicted depth and pose unchanged.

A 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and segmentation 2026-11-04
📝 114 pages, including Supplementary Information; 7 main figures and 2 Extended Data figures
Show abstract

Radiologists interpret computed tomography (CT) scans by jointly determining what a finding represents and where it lies in three-dimensional (3D) anatomy. However, CT models have treated CT interpretation and 3D segmentation as separate tasks, leaving textual findings spatially unverifiable and overlooking potential synergy between clinical understanding and spatial perception. Here we present HoloCT, a generalist 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and voxel-level segmentation by jointly modelling whole-volume context and fine-grained anatomical detail, providing 3D masks as evidence. We curated HoloDATA, comprising 195,921 CT scans from 73 public datasets and six clinical cohorts, with 6.2 million instruction dialogues and 2.8 million CT-mask pairs. Across 346 tasks spanning segmentation, lesion screening, visual question answering, and report generation on 61 internal and 12 external datasets, HoloCT outperformed specialist and foundation models. In multicentre validation across 11,455 clinical CT scans from five hospitals, HoloCT outperformed competing models in lesion segmentation and report generation by 18.2 and 15.3 points. It also achieved 85.1% mean accuracy in screening thyroid tumours and colon lesions, exceeding all four participating radiologists in a retrospective reader study. Further analyses revealed bidirectional semantic-spatial synergy: language-guided segmentation delineated unseen anatomical structures and lesions as well as diffuse free-text findings, outperforming the leading approach by 22.1 points, whereas prompting the model to generate corresponding 3D masks alongside its textual outputs significantly improved interpretation performance. HoloCT shows that clinical understanding and spatial perception can be learned together within a single foundation model and offers a path towards more accurate and reliable CT analysis.

Humans can easily recognize the same objects across different viewpoints of a 3D scene, whereas teaching models such multi-view-consistent perception typically requires videos or calibrated multi-view captures, which are considerably more expensive and less diverse than large-scale monocular data.~In this paper, we introduce Feature Puzzles, a novel framework for learning multi-view-consistent segmentation using only single-image semantic segmentation supervision. OmniGuide: Universal Spatial Guidance Fields for Enhancing Generalist Robot Policies 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026. Project page: https://omniguide.github.io/

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown great promise as generalist policies for a large range of relatively simple tasks. However, they demonstrate limited performance on more complex tasks, such as those requiring complex spatial or semantic understanding, manipulation in clutter, or precise manipulation. We propose OmniGuide, a flexible framework that improves VLA performance on such tasks by leveraging arbitrary sources of guidance, such as 3D foundation models, semantic-reasoning VLMs, and human pose models. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, across diverse sources of guidance, demonstrate that OmniGuide enhances the performance of state-of-the-art generalist policies (e.g., $π_{0.5}$, GR00T N1.6) significantly across success and safety rates. Critically, our unified framework matches or surpasses the performance of prior methods designed to incorporate specific sources of guidance into VLA policies. AnchorHEC: Targetless Hand-Eye Calibration of Wrist and Fixed Cameras 2026-11-02
📝 8 pages, 5 figures, 5 tables. Project page: https://hwanggb.github.io/AnchorHEC/
Show abstract

Recent robot systems combine wrist and fixed cameras, requiring camera-to-robot transforms for both. Most hand-eye calibration methods rely on targets or robot-body observations. Scene-based methods using 3D reconstruction require neither but address only wrist cameras. We introduce AnchorHEC, a targetless hand-eye calibration framework for wrist and fixed cameras in a single procedure using RGB images, forward kinematics (FK), and known camera intrinsics. We initialize both camera types using FK and a shared 3D foundation model reconstruction. FK-constrained wrist-image bundle adjustment reduces the impact of reconstructed pose and scale errors. If this refinement fails or retains too few inliers, we relax the constraints on potentially inaccurate FK poses and lens parameters. We use the refined wrist transform and FK to triangulate metric points and estimate fixed-camera poses. Across three backbones, calibration errors in simulation decrease by 86.4% in translation and 71.6% in rotation, averaged over both camera types, relative to reconstruction-based baselines. GeoCond: A Conditioning-Aware Reliability Adapter for Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted in WACV 2027
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D foundation models such as VGGT predict cameras, depth, and point maps in a single pass, but can fail silently under low overlap, low parallax, and extreme relative rotation. Stratified analyses over these factors show that these failures are governed by geometric conditioning and are poorly captured by native aleatoric confidence. We introduce GeoCond, a lightweight reliability adapter for frozen feed-forward 3D backbones. GeoCond reads geometric conditioning cues and the backbone's own stability signals and outputs pose-level uncertainty and a refinement gate. During training, it can be supervised by frame-permutation orbit variance, ground-truth pose error when labels are available, or cycle residuals from unlabelled independent pose graphs. At inference, the default head requires only one backbone pass and a small MLP. On VGGT, GeoCond improves out-of-distribution (OOD) AUSE (area under the sparsification-error curve; lower is better) from $0.32$ to $0.20$ over native confidence on held-out outdoor scenes (and to $0.21$ when trained on indoor data only) and avoids the collapse caused by applying bundle adjustment uniformly, without improving on feed-forward poses there. For three of six backbones, cycle-distilled variants provide a ground-truth-free adaptation route, including cases where permutation variance vanishes on equivariant models. The same reliability signal supports gated refinement, pose-graph weighting, calibration, curation, and capture decisions. Reliable feed-forward 3D reconstruction requires not only predicting geometry, but also knowing when that geometry should be trusted.

E0.5: From Reconstructive to Future-Predictive 3D Foundations for Robotic World-Action Modeling 2026-10-26
Show abstract

World-action models (WAMs) offer a promising framework for embodied intelligence, yet mainstream approaches remain centered on video foundation models or vision-language models. Even when geometric features extracted by 3D models such as VGGT are incorporated, they are typically passed into a video model or VLM that serves as the primary backbone, leaving 3D geometry as an auxiliary input or prediction target rather than the native foundation for world modeling. We present E0.5, a geometry-native world-action model built directly on 3D foundation models. E0.5 introduces two key innovations. First, it adopts a 3D foundation model as the primary backbone, making 3D geometry the native representation for both world modeling and action prediction. Second, it explicitly predicts future 3D states together with actions, enabling the model to capture geometric world dynamics beyond action-only modeling or future video prediction. This embodied pretraining improves mean real-world manipulation success rates by approximately 15 and 7 percentage points on AgileX Piper and Zerith H1, respectively. Together, these results demonstrate the potential of geometry-native foundations to acquire robot interaction knowledge and transfer it to physical manipulation. E0.5 embodies this geometry-native approach as a member of the VWA family of world-action models.

ProMo3D: Probing Motion Cues in Frozen 3D Foundation Models 2026-10-24
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Main Track). Poster: https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/loc/sydney/poster/151302
Show abstract

Feed-forward 3D foundation models have recently reshaped multi-view reconstruction, yet dynamic 4D understanding still often relies on large-scale motion supervision or hand-crafted, architecture-specific heuristics. We ask whether frozen 3D foundation model tokens already encode motion-relevant information that can be exposed by lightweight readout functions, rather than re-learning motion-specific features with high-capacity decoders. Through probing on three representative backbones, we find that static--dynamic separation is decodable from frozen token representations. Our rank-restricted and spectral analyses on $π^3$X reveal that the recovered motion cues are concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace. Building on this, we propose Probing-based Motion Distillation (PMD), which converts pairwise motion-state compatibility scores against a fixed anchor token into dense motion masks. Trained on only 160 YouTube-VOS videos using a single 16 GB GPU, PMD transfers zero-shot to DAVIS, SegTrackv2, and FBMS-59 without target-benchmark fine-tuning. With multi-view pretrained geometric backbones, PMD outperforms training-free heuristics and approaches substantially heavier supervised and self-supervised methods, suggesting that motion decoding from frozen 3D foundation models can be lightweight and data-efficient.

CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components. First, we diagnose how precise ego motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we strengthen ego-motion state in VGM by aligning the representations at the identified layers to a 3D foundation model through delta-token space. Moreover, with the fine-tuned VGM, CausalAlign improves the success rate of world action models (WAMs) by 10 points on mobile manipulation tasks of RoboCasa365 benchmark. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields noticeable gains in embodied models.

Relational Geometry Forcing for World Action Models 2026-10-21
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have shown strong potential for robot manipulation by jointly learning visual dynamics and executable actions. However, their video and action objectives do not explicitly constrain the 3D geometry that is crucial for precise manipulation. Recently, geometry-aware WAMs introduce depth or 4D prediction branches, whereas representation alignment methods supervise intermediate WAM features with pretrained 3D foundation models. Yet we find that such pointwise alignment benefits the video features but degrades action learning, since dense geometric feature targets overly restrict compact action features. To address this limitation, we propose Relational Geometry Forcing (RGF), which transfers geometric priors from a frozen 3D foundation model by aligning pairwise feature relations instead of pointwise features. Specifically, RGF applies three complementary relational objectives, where intra-frame and cross-view relations transfer scene geometry and multi-view correspondence to the video features, while action-temporal relations couple action dynamics to geometric scene evolution. Applied only during training, RGF requires no explicit geometry branch or extra inference module. Extensive experiments in simulation and the real world demonstrate the effectiveness of Relational Geometry Forcing, showing consistent gains across diverse manipulation scenarios and WAM paradigms.

From Predictive Geometry to Action: Learning Complementary Predictive Representations for Embodied Manipulation 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Effective manipulation requires a policy to capture not only about the current scene geometry, but also about how that geometry will evolve under interaction. Existing approaches either augment policies with geometric information from current observations or introduce future prediction as auxiliary supervision, leaving open how predictive geometry should be represented for action generation. Therefore, we propose a two-stage framework, which learns complementary predictive representations from multi-view video clips. During pretraining, a frozen 3D foundation model provides dense geometric features that are compressed into predictive geometric representations and optimized to preserve scene structure and its temporal evolution. Action supervision is then applied to geometric transitions to extract a complementary motor representation that emphasizes action-relevant transitions. During downstream post-training, predictive geometry is transferred through geometry queries, while the motor representation guides intermediate features along the action-generation pathway. Future observations are used only as privileged supervision during training and are not required at inference time. We further demonstrate transfer across multiple VLA architectures and WAMs, with improvements in both future-world and action prediction. Registration is a Representation Problem: Solving Whole-Body PET/CT Registration 2026-10-18
