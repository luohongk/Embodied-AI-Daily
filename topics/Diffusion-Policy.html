Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy: Learning Precise and Robust Manipulation with a Single RGB Camera 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Recent visual imitation learning systems have widely adopted multi-camera setups with wrist-mounted cameras as the de facto standard. However, manipulation from a single global view remains challenging, as the policy should capture fine-grained interaction details and identify task-relevant regions without local wrist views. To address this challenge, we present Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP), a diffusion-based visuomotor policy that achieves precise and robust manipulation in a single-camera setting. Our key idea is that end-effector trajectories can serve as visual attention anchors that reflect task-relevant regions. Building on this idea, SCDP consists of two key components: (i) a visual encoder that produces multi-scale feature maps to capture both broader context and fine-grained visual features, and (ii) a spatial conditioning module that samples point-wise features along intermediate end-effector trajectories in the diffusion loop. Extensive simulation experiments show that SCDP consistently outperforms strong single-view baselines and achieves performance comparable to multi-camera baselines. Real-world experiments further demonstrate precise manipulation and robustness to visual distractors, highlighting the potential of single-camera imitation learning.

Physical Time as a Missing Dimension of Robot Policy Memory Dynamics 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Existing robot policy memories typically evolve with observation sequences, implicitly treating each update as the same amount of physical time. Yet identical observation histories can span different durations and require different actions, exposing physical time as a missing dimension of policy memory. We introduce Temporal Flow Policy (TFP), which learns memory dynamics to retain past events and evolve the memory state over measured time intervals. Observations and prior memory determine the content and time scales of each transition; elapsed time determines how far the state advances. TFP conditions diffusion and flow-matching policies through this memory state and is trained with their native imitation objectives. We evaluate TFP with Diffusion Policy and $π_{0.5}$ through controlled comparisons of memory designs. These experiments show that successful timing depends on both measured intervals and retained history. TFP achieves higher average success than the recurrent and state-space memories we equip with the same intervals and than a variant that feeds the interval to a learned gate. Memory readouts recover the time since an event and motion information after occlusion. Changing the supplied intervals systematically delays or advances actions, linking time inputs to behavior. Real-robot tasks requiring timing or memory, together with LIBERO evaluations, demonstrate benefits in physical and general manipulation. These results suggest viewing robot memory not as stored observation history, but as a state that retains past events and evolves with physical time.

Refine Actions If Needed: Adaptive Action Chunk Refinement for Robot Manipulation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Computationally lightweight manipulation policies predict executable actions with low inference latency, but prediction errors can limit task success. Iterative generative policies can achieve higher task success rates, but repeated network evaluations increase inference latency. Refining action proposals from computationally lightweight policies can combine these strengths, but the amount of refinement needed varies across states. We introduce RAIN (Refine Actions If Needed), a framework that predicts action errors at different refinement levels to decide whether and how much to refine each executable proposal, before performing any refinement. We train an ensemble to predict these errors by comparing action proposals and their refined versions with expert actions from offline demonstrations, without requiring explicit labels of refinement needs. Calibration balances uncertainty, computation, and temporal consistency. We evaluate RAIN on eight ManiSkill tasks and four real-robot tasks. In simulation, our policy achieves 66.6% success, approaching the best evaluated diffusion-policy success rate with 5.71 times lower policy latency. On the real robot, success increases from 55.0% with Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) to 72.5%, with comparable average successful completion time. Matched controls show that dynamic routing maintains the strongest observed fixed-refinement success level with 26.6% fewer refiner evaluations. Project page: https://rain-policy.github.io/.

Recent advances in 3D hand-object interaction (HOI) perception have opened new opportunities for learning dexterous robotic skills from human videos. However, existing dexterous imitation learning methods often rely on costly and manually teleoperated robot demonstrations, requiring substantial human effort. Therefore, we build DEXCAST, a framework that learns dexterous robotic hand control directly from monocular RGB-D videos by leveraging joint HOI reconstructions, without requiring any teleoperation. Furthermore, how to model high-dimensional robotic hand motion and extract temporal context to inform future action generation remains a key challenge. Immiscible Diffusion Policy: Preserving Multimodal Robot Actions through Label-Free Noise Assignment 2026-10-14
Show abstract

When diffusion policies were first introduced, they were expected to recover multi-modal action distributions. However, we find this expectation does not always hold, as diffusion policies often collapse to a single modality even when we guarantee the balance of dataset modalities and exact within-batch symmetry. Our analysis indicates that independent action-noise pairing contributes to this failure by increasing mixing and crossing among diffusion paths, which can produce averaged denoising responses and suppress modality-specific behavior. This issue is especially severe in robot planning, where action spaces are dense and low-dimensional, significantly increasing such mixing and crossing. To alleviate this problem, we propose Immiscible Diffusion Policy, a label-free training-time add-on to diffusion policy that uses action-noise assignment to preserve relatively distinct noise-to-action routes without modifying the policy architecture or inference procedure. Across five simulated and two real-world humanoid manipulation tasks spanning state, RGB, and point-cloud observations, our method significantly improves the policy's preservation of action modalities while maintaining strong task performance. It increases the proportion of the non-dominant modality by 6.0x-14.6x across three two-modality tasks and recovers demonstrated modalities that are entirely absent from vanilla policy rollouts on both four-modality tasks. These results demonstrate that Immiscible Diffusion Policy provides a simple yet robust approach to preserving action multi-modality in general robot learning tasks.

Back on Track: Deployment-time Policy Improvement via World Model Planning 2026-10-14
Show abstract

State-of-the-art visuomotor policies are increasingly capable of executing complex tasks, yet can fail during deployment in ways they cannot predict or recover from on their own. Improving their performance and robustness typically requires collecting more data, retraining, or modifying the policy architecture, all of which are time- and resource-intensive in practice. We instead propose an offline-trained monitoring-and-intervention framework that improves a policy during deployment using only black-box query access. From a limited set of labeled on-policy rollouts, we learn a policy-conditioned liveness value that measures the nominal policy's progress toward task completion; we additionally train a compact latent world model using these rollouts together with play data. At deployment, the liveness value continuously monitors execution. When it detects a loss of progress, a short-horizon planner searches the world model for corrective actions using the same value as its planning objective, after which control returns to the nominal policy. We evaluate our method on the LIBERO-Goal task suite and on a Franka Panda manipulator, using the vision-language-action model $π_{0.5}$ and a diffusion policy as nominal policies, and demonstrate significant improvements in task success rates, while supporting real-time control. Additional results and videos are available on the project website (https://policy-improvement-wm.pages.dev/).

Collective Learning for Unified Robotic Manipulation via Predictive Dynamics Adaptation 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted at IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS 2026)
Show abstract

Learning a collective policy for state-based Cartesian manipulation across diverse robotic embodiments presents a fundamental challenge due to embodiment-dependent transition dynamics. Collective learning aggregates multi-robot experience to discover shared task structure, yet naive aggregation can average incompatible action modes when different embodiments realize the same Cartesian command through different transition behavior. This leads to strategy ambiguity and performance collapse on dynamically outlying embodiments. To address this, we propose PACE, a two-stage collective learning framework that separates shared task-space intent from embodiment-dependent transition dynamics. The first stage employs a representation layer pre-trained on offline trajectories to extract task-level context from collective data. The second stage introduces a decision layer equipped with a predictive dynamics adapter. By forcing the latent state to predict future transitions, this adapter acts as a latent regularizer that grounds the policy in the transition behavior of the executing robot while preserving real-time inference without online planning. Evaluations across simulated embodiments demonstrate that our method prevents performance collapse on distinct robots. It achieves over 93\% overall success and 89\% on dynamically out-of-distribution zero-shot simulated robots, outperforming the Transformer behavior cloning (82\%) and diffusion policy baselines (66\%). Physical KUKA deployment further validates the feasibility of the full state-based pipeline under zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

Collaborative Planning for UAV-UGV Teams under Operational Constraints via Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Collaborative planning under operational constraints is essential for heterogeneous robot teams in large-scale missions. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provide rapid area coverage but are limited by onboard energy, while Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) can serve as mobile recharging stations yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Effective collaboration must enforce operational constraints during execution while enabling parallel, coordinated actions. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have shown promise through token unmasking but have two key limitations: entropy-guided scheduling assumes distributional alignment and reliance on differentiable constraints for diffusion-time optimization. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND), which formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAV-UGV teams. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution by reweighting the learned action distribution with a logic-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints. MEND introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predictive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. Extensive experimental results in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robot teams demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints.

Steertuning Policies from a Few Human Videos 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Current recipes for robot learning require massive amounts of robot demonstrations to obtain robust and adaptive policies. In this work, we introduce Steertuning, a system that enables robots to learn new tasks in new environments from 5 human demonstrations in under 5 Steertuning consists of two parts. (1) We pretrain a 400M-parameter diffusion policy on our egocentric human dataset, which consists of 100 hours of densely annotated random interactions. This pretraining makes Steertuning robust to background differences and enables spatial and object generalization when deployed on new tasks. (2) Given 5 demonstrations of a new task, we finetune our model for only 2 minutes (2000 training updates) and steer the policy's actions during reverse sampling using the demonstration whose observation is closest to that of the deployment environment. Together with a carefully designed data processing pipeline for embodiment transfer, this enables Steertuning to achieve a 71% success rate across 12 tasks in 12 new environments, ranging from lab settings to kitchens in new homes. The entire process, from data collection for a new task to robot policy deployment, takes less than 5 minutes. Model-Based Online Decision Making via Generative Trajectory Planning 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Online decision-making faces a tension between efficient inference and long-horizon reasoning. Value-based methods rely on short-horizon bootstrapping that limits the propagation of long-term information, while trajectory optimization methods plan explicitly but must solve a high-dimensional optimization problem at every decision step. We propose Generative Trajectory Planning (GTP), a model-based framework that addresses this tension by learning a generative model as a proposal distribution over action sequences. Instead of optimizing sequences from scratch, GTP samples candidate sequences from a diffusion prior and refines them using learned dynamics, reward, and value models, enabling efficient trajectory-level reasoning. Across continuous control and long-horizon manipulation tasks, GTP matches or exceeds state-of-the-art model-free, diffusion-policy, and model-based baselines, and remains stable in regimes where strong baselines degrade. Our results suggest that generative models are best understood not as policies, but as structured proposal distributions for planning, offering a new perspective on how to integrate generative modeling with model-based decision-making.

ForeTac: Predictive Contact Guidance for Generative Robot Policies 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Visually similar actions can produce contact loss, jamming, slippage, or irreversible damage, while tactile feedback may arrive after a critical transition. Contact-rich manipulation therefore requires generative policies to anticipate and evaluate whether candidate actions will produce desirable contact outcomes, then adjust action generation before execution. We present ForeTac, a predictive contact-guidance framework organized as a represent-predict-evaluate-guide loop. ForeTac encodes the current tactile state, predicts the tactile consequences of a candidate action sequence, and evaluates the predicted trajectory using a contact-quality objective learned from successful and failed physical interactions. At deployment, its gradient propagates through the tactile predictor to guide action generation while the pretrained model parameters remain frozen. We evaluate ForeTac on four real-robot tasks (planar and curved-surface wiping, card swiping, and fragile-object grasping) with diffusion and flow-matching policies. Across these tasks, ForeTac raises the average task success rate from 36.3% to 71.3% and the average contact success rate (CSR) from 20.0% to 66.3% compared with vision-only Diffusion Policy. Component analyses support the tactile representation and action-conditioned foresight, and show that predicted touch preserves contact-quality ordering. Project page: https://foretac.github.io/.

PrT-Flow: Probability-Tractable Flow Matching for Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Diffusion models and Flow Matching can represent flexible distributions over robot actions, but many reinforcement learning algorithms also require the probability of each action. We introduce PrT-Flow (Probability-Tractable Flow Matching), which computes probability density while retaining multimodal, multi-step action generation. Its Jacobian-Tractable Transformer (JTT) makes each sampling step invertible and enables efficient computation of its Jacobian determinant. We train these finite steps with Conditional Euler-step Training (CET), which supervises their displacement over one step during sampling rather than their instantaneous velocity. We prove that, under certain assumptions, CET has the same minimizers as fitting the Euler-solver updates of Flow Matching. PrT-Flow achieves 96% success on Robomimic Lift after 300 epochs of imitation learning, matching the diffusion policy reference, and 96% success on OGBench Cube-Triple after 600k online steps. These results demonstrate that invertible networks with tractable probability densities can learn effective robot policies and support SAC-style actor-critic training. Our code is available at https://github.com/Sunlight-zero/PrT-Flow .

BayesFP: Posterior Estimation for Flow-Based Policies via Feynman-Kac Sampling 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Robots must generate trajectories that remain faithful to learned expert behavior while satisfying safety constraints and task-specific objectives specified only at inference time. We formulate constrained trajectory generation for pretrained diffusion and flow-matching policies as Bayesian posterior sampling, with the learned demonstration distribution as a prior and an inference-time, cost-derived likelihood tilting it toward feasible, optimal trajectories. To sample from this posterior without any retraining of the base policy, we leverage the Feynman--Kac corrector framework, originally formulated for diffusion models, and extend it to deterministic flow-matching policies. The result is a unified, inference-time, retraining-free sampler for diffusion and flow policies. We validate the approach on pretrained Diffusion Policy, GR00T-N1.6, and $π_{0.5}$ checkpoints across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, including planning around non-convex obstacles introduced at inference time, and show improvements over the base $π_{0.5}$ on zero-shot tasks.

Smoothness Ranks Skill, Not Success: An Audit of Published Trajectory-Smoothness Curation Metrics Under Episode-Length Controls 2026-10-12
📝 13 pages, 4 figures. Accepted (poster) at the NeurIPS 2026 RoboPAD Workshop. Code: https://github.com/imjbassi/smoothness-metric-audit
Show abstract

Trajectory smoothness has been proposed as a cheap, training-free signal for curating imitation-learning demonstrations, most recently by rinse, which introduces Spectral Arc Length (SAL) and a contact-aware Trajectory-Envelope Distance (TED). We test whether these published metrics survive episode-length controls on real robomimic data. On can/mh, SAL separates better from worse operators with AUROC 0.952, but episode length alone achieves 0.957, and inside a duration-matched band SAL falls to 0.628 while raw mean jerk, the baseline rinse argues against, rises to 0.896. The confound replicates on square/mh. On can/mg, where every episode is exactly 150 steps, SAL is at chance (0.454) and TED is inverted (0.376) against task success, while jerk reaches 0.706. Training GMM behavior-cloning policies on each metric's curated subset (10 seeds), curating by TED costs roughly a third of downstream success relative to random selection of the same size (0.240 vs. 0.360, p=0.0001, surviving Bonferroni correction), while SAL and jerk are indistinguishable from not curating. Under a diffusion policy TED still ranks worst, though its deficit is not resolvable at five seeds, and jerk becomes significantly better than both baselines, so the curation decision does not transfer unchanged across the two policy configurations we tested. Inspecting trajectories shows why TED inverts: successful manipulation requires sustained high-deviation motion through contact, while failure is low-amplitude hovering that a smoothed envelope follows closely.

DiffNash: Finding Nash Equilibrium in High-Dimensional Continuous Action Spaces with Diffusion Policies 2026-10-11
📝 41 pages, 11 figures
Show abstract

Computing equilibria in high-dimensional continuous games requires learning and representing rich, multi-modal strategy distributions, something conventional neural policies struggle to achieve. Diffusion models offer a natural solution, but standard diffusion training requires direct samples from a fixed target distribution, which are generally unavailable under iterative game-solving updates. We propose a diffusion-based framework for equilibrium computation that enables diffusion-based variants of Online Mirror Descent (OMD) and Fictitious Play (FP). Our approach derives a reweighted score-matching objective that trains diffusion policies without requiring direct samples from each updated policy. Our main method, DiffNash, combines the fast convergence of OMD with the sample efficiency of FP by reusing past actions through a replay buffer. To correct the resulting distribution shift from sample reuse, we derive an unbiased estimator of the difference between current and past policies' Evidence Lower Bounds, whose exponential provides a tractable proxy for the corresponding importance weight without evaluating diffusion likelihoods. We evaluate our approach on forest protection games and continuous Colonel Blotto games, reducing the Nash gap over the strongest baseline by 35.6% and 24.7%, respectively, with minimal hyperparameter tuning.

Diffusion ReRoll: Revisable Denoising for Robotic Sequential Prediction 2026-10-11
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026 as Spotlight. Project Page: https://seonsoo-p1.github.io/DiffusionReRoll/
Show abstract

We propose Diffusion ReRoll, a diffusion-based framework for robotic sequential prediction that enables revisable denoising over horizons. Existing diffusion-based sequence predictors typically perform a single monotonic denoising process. In contrast, Diffusion ReRoll selectively re-noises regions that reach a predefined noise threshold while the remaining regions continue denoising, so the re-noised regions can be refined again using context from the rest of the horizon. This structured re-noising enables iterative cross-horizon revision, allowing earlier and later segments to revise one another, while maintaining local consistency. We evaluate Diffusion ReRoll against full-sequence diffusion and causal denoising based on Diffusion Forcing across long-horizon planning, policy learning, unified video-action modeling, and real-world navigation. On OGBench PointMaze and AntMaze, Diffusion ReRoll achieves relative gains in average success rate of 21% over Diffusion Forcing in matched guidance-based planning and 23% over Diffuser in goal-inpainting. In diffusion-policy-style action prediction, Diffusion ReRoll improves average success by 56.5% relative to Diffusion Policy across different prediction horizons and history lengths on LIBERO-10 under the official reset protocol. In unified video-action prediction, Diffusion ReRoll improves policy and inverse dynamics performance, especially under out-of-distribution evaluation, and achieves the best action-video consistency. Real-world navigation further demonstrates improved success under visual distribution shifts with onboard inference. These results show that structured re-noising enables cross-horizon revision and improves performance across robotic sequential prediction tasks.

Compositional Average Velocity Modeling for Efficient Diffusion Offline Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-10
📝 10 pages, IROS 2026
Show abstract

Diffusion-based policies have recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for offline reinforcement learning, but their reliance on multi-step denoising introduces substantial computational overhead and requires backpropagation through time during training, limiting scalability and practical deployment. In this work, we propose \textbf{Instant Flow Q-Learning (InsQL)}, a single-step generative policy learning framework that retains the expressive capacity of diffusion models while eliminating iterative sampling and BPTT. InsQL builds on the flow-matching perspective of diffusion policies, but replaces marginal velocity parameterization with \emph{average-velocity} modeling and introduces a principled compositional mechanism, yielding a theoretically grounded objective that learns average velocity consistently across scales. This formulation provides a direct noise-to-action mapping and enables single-step action generation. InsQL further unifies compositional average-velocity modeling with behavior-regularized actor--critic optimization in a single framework, requiring only a single policy network and avoiding distillation, multi-stage procedures, and Jacobian computations during training. At inference time, actions are generated in a single forward pass and can be refined via value-guided candidate selection to improve performance. Extensive experiments on standard offline RL benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks show that InsQL matches or surpasses state-of-the-art diffusion-based methods while substantially reducing both training and inference time. Overall, these results establish compositional average-velocity modeling as an efficient and scalable alternative to multi-step generative policies for offline RL.

CFG-Policy: Unified Diffusion Policy Learning Framework via Classifier-free Guidance 2026-10-10
Show abstract

Diffusion policies are increasingly used in robotic control and manipulation, vision-language-action models, and world-model-based agents. Their ability to represent multimodal action distributions offers rich opportunities for exploration in reinforcement learning (RL). However, optimizing diffusion policies with RL can reduce the behavioral diversity and further limit their exploration capability. In RL post-training, directly fine-tuning a pretrained policy may also disrupt its acquired capabilities, while action-space residuals may lack the expressive power for multimodal exploration. Together, these issues reveal a common challenge across online and offline-to-online diffusion RL: improving task performance while retaining the behavioral diversity and capabilities needed for continued adaptation. We introduce CFG-Policy, a unified, plug-and-play framework that separates exploration and exploitation into two generative policies and combines their score or velocity fields during sampling through classifier-free guidance. For online RL, a frozen exploration branch sustains behavioral diversity while an exploitation branch learns from task rewards; for offline-to-online RL, a pretrained exploitation branch remains frozen while a trainable branch steers the composed behavior. CFG-Policy preserves the underlying RL objectives and improves performance with QSM and QVPO on 5 MuJoCo tasks and with Flow-SDE+PPO on 4 RoboTwin manipulation tasks. Project page is at https://zhy-liu.github.io/cfg-policy-page.

Behavior Uncloning: Distilling Mode Redirection into Policy Weights without Inference-Time Steering 2026-10-10
Show abstract

Behavior-cloned policies often learn multiple behavior modes from demonstration datasets, including modes that are unsafe or otherwise undesired at deployment. For example, a policy trained on diverse handover demonstrations may learn to pass a knife blade-first. Standard remedies such as data curation and inference-time steering either require access to the original demonstrations for full retraining or add substantial inference-time overhead. To address this gap, we propose MoRE(Mode Redirection), which redirects policy rollouts toward desired behavior modes through a short "uncloning" step. Specifically, MoRE distills the redirection signal from a temporary mode classifier into the policy weights to steer behavior. A retain loss balances this edit by preserving desired-mode competence, allowing the standalone policy to suppress unwanted modes with zero inference-time overhead. Across eight simulated and real-world tasks, MoRE improves the average deployment success rate (SR) by 44 percentage points over the original mixed-mode policy. Among all compared adaptation and steering baselines, MoRE achieves the strongest SR and approaches the filtered-data retraining reference, while preserving task competence and inference speed. MoRE also generalizes across robot policy backbones, including Diffusion Policy and the Pi0.5 VLA, diverse task categories, and real-world deployments.

RARA: Robust Policy Fine-Tuning through Retrieval-Anchored Representation Alignment for Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-09
📝 21 pages, 11 figures, 7 tables
Show abstract

Pre-trained robot policies, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, provide a strong foundation for generalist robotic manipulation, but adapting them to new tasks with only a few demonstrations remains challenging. Directly fine-tuning a pre-trained policy often achieves strong in-distribution performance, yet it can distort the representations learned during pre-training and weaken the model's generalization ability under out-of-distribution shifts. We propose Retrieval-Anchored Representation Alignment (RARA), a two-stage adaptation method, to address this issue. First, RARA retrieves representation features from pretraining with similar task intent and motion, and aligns the policy's representations of the target demonstrations with the pre-trained representations of the retrieved demonstrations. By reducing the representation mismatch before action learning and preserving pre-trained knowledge during adaptation, RARA limits unnecessary representation drift during fine-tuning. Experiments with Diffusion Policy and GR00T on 13 tasks in RoboTwin, RoboCasa, and on a real robot show that RARA improves the average success of fine-tuning by 9.5 points in-distribution and 13.6 points out-of-distribution, and outperforms all compared baselines. Human2Dex: Dexterous Robot Learning from Wrist-Centric Human Demonstrations 2026-10-09
📝 8 pages, 6 figures. Project website: https://human2dex.pages.dev/
Show abstract

Collecting dexterous manipulation demonstrations for each robot hand requires repeated task-specific data acquisition. We ask whether one shared corpus of natural human demonstrations can instead serve as a reusable data interface for different dexterous hands, without collecting task demonstrations on each target robot. We present Human2Dex, a wrist-centric human demonstration interface that separates shared human observation and motion semantics from embodiment-specific execution. It aligns observations through local hand-appearance adaptation and a shared 21-point hand representation with consistent structural semantics across human and robot observations.
