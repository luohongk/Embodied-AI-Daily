From 12 to 200 Demonstrations: How ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA and $π_{0.5}$ Scale on a Low-Cost Arm 2026-10-27
📝 31 pages, 5 figures, 19 tables
Show abstract

We trained four widely used imitation-learning policies, ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA and $π_{0.5}$, on nested subsets of 12, 25, 50, 100 and 200 teleoperated demonstrations of one pick-and-place task with distractor objects, using one fixed training recipe per policy. Every trained model was then run on a physical SO-101 arm on a fixed set of object placements: 1,340 scored episodes in 126 blocks. ACT, trained from scratch, rose from 1 success in 40 episodes at 12 demonstrations to 37 in 40 at 100. $π_{0.5}$ with its action expert fine-tuned was the best policy when data was scarce (16 of 40 at 12 demonstrations, 28 of 40 at 25) and then stayed near 70%; from 100 demonstrations ACT was ahead, by a margin this study does not fully resolve. SmolVLA and Diffusion Policy peaked near 50%, and both were affected by a failure to start: SmolVLA's arm never started moving in 47% of episodes, and Diffusion Policy hardly ever started until an idle lead-in was trimmed from the demonstrations. For $π_{0.5}$ the fine-tuning recipe mattered more than the amount of data: low-rank adaptation succeeded in 4 of 110 episodes, against 130 of 200 for action-expert fine-tuning. From the joint and camera recordings we place every episode on a four-stage ladder (started, reached the ball, carried it, delivered it) and show that for ACT more data moves failures from reaching, to grasping, to none, while the failures $π_{0.5}$ retains are in grasping. We also measure how weak a ten-episode block is as a measurement (the same checkpoint tested twice differs by 2 successes in 10 on average), separate seed-to-seed variation from that noise, and audit the human scoring against the recordings. Training length was fixed within each recipe, so the amount of data and the number of times it was seen change together; we state which conclusions survive that confound and which do not.

TS-DP: Reinforcement Speculative Decoding For Temporal Adaptive Diffusion Policy Acceleration 2026-10-27
Show abstract

Diffusion Policy (DP) excels in embodied control but suffers from high inference latency and computational cost due to multiple iterative denoising steps. Existing diffusion model (DMs) acceleration methods improve inference speed by reducing computational overhead, as DMs for image generation are computation-bound. In this work, we reveal that DP are I/O bound, and thus propose using speculative decoding, a parallel computing framework, to accelerate DP for the first time. However, it is non-trivial to address the following challenges: (1) How to preserve the denoising quality of the base model at reduced computational cost under time-varying task difficulty in embodied settings; and (2) How to dynamically allocate computation according to task difficulty in such environments. Therefore, we propose Temporal-aware Reinforcement-based Speculative Diffusion Policy (TS-DP), the first framework that enables speculative decoding for DP with temporal adaptivity. First, to handle dynamic environments where task difficulty varies over time, we distill a Transformer-based drafter to imitate the base model and replace its costly denoising calls. Second, an RL-based scheduler further adapts to the time-varying task difficulty by adjusting speculative parameters to maintain accuracy while improving efficiency. Extensive experiments show that TS-DP achieves up to a 4.17x NFE reduction and a 3.2x wall-clock speedup with a draft acceptance rate of over 85% in simulation. Real-world experiments further demonstrate a 3.8x NFE reduction while improving success rates on real-time tasks (e.g., a 23% gain on the conveyor-belt task), reaching an inference frequency of 26 Hz and enabling real-time control while maintaining comparable task performance. The code is available at https://github.com/ChildTang/TS-DP.

Grasp-Then-Plan with Failure Attribution: A Closed Two-Stage Framework for Precise and Generalizable Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-27
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 34 pages. Revised after peer review with additional experiments, updated results, and updated author information. Project page: https://sites.google.com/view/gtp-fa/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

In robotic manipulation, the tight coupling between grasping and motion planning often obscures the true source of failure, leading to inefficient trial-and-error. To enable efficient long-horizon manipulation, we propose GTP-FA (Grasp-Then-Plan with Failure Attribution), a task-oriented two-stage 'grasp-then-plan' framework that generates grasp candidates and performs downstream motion planning conditioned on the selected grasp. Given a failed manipulation trajectory, we learn a failure attribution model that generalizes to unseen grasps and produces a stable distribution over failure modes for diagnosis-guided optimization. Based on these attribution results, we then optimize both modules in a diagnosis-driven manner: on the grasping side, we inject task-level priors and risk penalties into grasp candidate scoring and optimization to suppress unstable or task-incompatible grasps; on the planning side, we target high-risk initial states through data collection and fine-tuning to address genuine planning bottlenecks. We evaluate the proposed framework in both simulation and real-robot experiments, and show that GTP-FA improves the corresponding base learners across RL, IL, diffusion-policy, and VLA-based settings, achieving substantially higher overall task success rates.

From Grasps to Dexterity: Large-Scale Grasp Pretraining for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-10-26
📝 Project page: https://yingyuan0414.github.io/grasp2dexterity/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Large-scale dexterous grasp datasets encode rich priors over hand-object interaction, but their use has largely been confined to grasp generation and pick-and-place manipulation. We study whether such data can instead support functional dexterity in articulated tool use, where a robot must acquire a tool, maintain contact, and operate its functional moving parts. We adapt a hierarchical imitation learning framework that combines high-level hand sub-goal prediction with a low-level goal-conditioned controller. We construct a 355k-trajectory grasp-pretraining dataset from large-scale dexterous grasp annotations and use it to pretrain the low-level controller. The controller is then fine-tuned on downstream task demonstrations. To evaluate this setting, we introduce DexCraft, a simulation benchmark with six articulated tool-use tasks requiring coordinated finger motion. Across simulation and real-world experiments, our approach outperforms end-to-end diffusion policy baselines and hierarchical policies trained from scratch. In the real world, it improves full-task success by 33.3 percentage points over DP3. These results show that grasp datasets can serve not only as resources for grasp synthesis, but also as scalable pretraining data for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. Videos are shown on https://yingyuan0414.github.io/grasp2dexterity/ .

Learning Force-Reactive Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-26
📝 9 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Many everyday tasks require controlling contact force as well as motion. On a humanoid, this force must often be coordinated across both hands and the whole body, and cannot be specified by kinematic trajectories alone. Therefore, we present FLORE, a framework for learning force-reactive humanoid loco-manipulation. We collect robot-free demonstrations using two force-sensing UMI-style handheld grippers for hand motion and contact wrenches, and wearable trackers for body motion. A force-reactive diffusion policy takes vision, proprioception, and measured wrenches as input, then predicts actions and per-hand force commands. A hybrid compliance whole-body controller with force tracking executes these commands while maintaining balance: an integral of the force error measured by real force/torque (F/T) sensors lets each hand track force accurately along the commanded direction and remain compliant along the others. In simulation, the controller tracks per-hand force commands of varied magnitude and direction, with little loss in whole-body motion accuracy. Across three contact-rich tasks on the robot, the force-reactive policy succeeds in 80% of rollouts, compared with 26.7% for a position-only policy trained on the same demonstrations and executed by the same controller. For more details, please visit our website: https://louis-leee.github.io/FLoRe/.

S$^2$-RL: Sample-Set Dual Reinforcement Learning for Generative Semantic Segmentation Dataset Distillation 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project Page: https://00why00.github.io/S2RL/
Show abstract

While dataset distillation has witnessed significant advances in image classification, its extension to dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation is plagued by two core bottlenecks: bi-level optimization-based approaches suffer from prohibitive computational costs stemming from pixel-wise gradient unrolling, whereas proxy-based generative methods merely infuse visual textures into fixed semantic masks derived from real data, thus failing to condense rich semantic knowledge into a compact set of informative, novel scene generations. To address both bottlenecks simultaneously, we propose a novel Sample-Set Reinforcement Learning (S$^2$-RL) framework for generative semantic segmentation dataset distillation (SSDD). Specifically, S$^2$-RL formulates SSDD as a diffusion model based text-to-image generation paradigm, enabling flexible generation of images with informative, novel semantic distributions via a concise text prompt. Furthermore, we fine-tune a diffusion policy via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging a sample-set dual reward paradigm: for individual samples, it enforces semantic alignment with input text prompts and intra-class feature diversity; for sample sets, it maximizes inter-sample semantic distribution diversity by solving a contextual multi-armed bandit problem. These design choices enable S$^2$-RL to distill large-scale semantic segmentation datasets into a compact set of generated samples that encapsulate diverse, informative semantic knowledge without the need of pixel-wise gradient unrolling. Extensive experiments on the ADE20K and COCO datasets demonstrate that S$^2$-RL achieves substantial and consistent improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, establishing a new benchmark for SSDD.

HA-DiPO: Diffusion Policy Optimization for Heterogeneous Cooperative Transport 2026-10-24
📝 9 pages, 6 figures. Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L). Project website: https://ha-dipo.github.io/
Show abstract

Cooperative transport with heterogeneous legged robots requires coordinating different motion capabilities while supporting an unfixed shared payload. We present HA-DiPO (Heterogeneous-Agent Diffusion Policy Optimization), a hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework under centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE). The framework decouples team-level coordination from embodiment-specific motion control: at the high level, a centralized critic uses team-value estimates to jointly train independent coordination policies, aligning robot-specific action responses with a shared transport objective; at the low level, embodiment-specific robust motion policies track the coordination commands. The high-level diffusion policies offer more flexible action distributions than single-Gaussian parameterizations, allowing potentially multimodal coordination responses to be represented. The coordination policies learn from scratch through reward-driven simulator interaction without demonstrations. At execution, each robot generates coordination commands using only local robot and payload observations, without partner-state inputs or inter-robot messages. Simulation comparisons show that HA-DiPO achieves higher final mean returns and velocity-tracking success rates, as well as lower velocity errors, than the evaluated baselines. Real-world experiments further demonstrate quadrupedal-wheeled-legged and human-robot cooperative transport.

Show abstract

A dexterous hand can change an object's pose without setting it down. This survey covers 113 learning-based methods that attempt it, the hands they run on, the simulators they train in, and how they report results. The field has largely converged on one recipe: reinforcement learning on privileged state in a GPU simulator, distilled into a vision-only student for transfer to hardware. Almost half the corpus trains that way, nearly all of it in one simulator, whose own successors are barely used. A second and faster-growing branch learns from teleoperated demonstration instead, through vision-language-action models, diffusion policies, and systems whose contribution is the collection rig itself. Published work has tripled since 2022. Grasping remains the largest task family and bimanual coordination the fastest-rising, and almost every experiment runs on one of four hands. What the survey adds comes from reading released code and vendor specifications beside the papers. Eight hands that can be bought or built from published designs appear in no method paper here. Where the depth a hand sinks into the object it holds is reported at all, it is not measured in the physics that produced the motion. Trust Region Continual Learning as an Implicit Meta-Learner 2026-10-23
📝 NeurIPS 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Continual learning aims to acquire tasks sequentially without catastrophic forgetting, yet standard strategies face a core tradeoff: regularization-based methods (e.g., EWC) can overconstrain updates when task optima are weakly overlapping, while replay-based methods can retain performance but drift due to imperfect replay. We study a hybrid perspective: \emph{trust region continual learning} that combines generative replay with a Fisher-metric trust region constraint. We show that, under local approximations, the resulting update admits a Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) interpretation with a single implicit inner step: replay supplies an old-task gradient signal (query-like), while the Fisher-weighted penalty provides efficient offline curvature shaping (support-like). This yields an emergent meta-learning property in continual learning: the model becomes an initialization that rapidly \emph{re-converges} to prior task optima after each task transition, without explicitly optimizing a bilevel objective that couples inner adaptation with outer meta-updates. Empirically, on task-incremental diffusion image generation and continual diffusion-policy control, trust region continual learning achieves the best final performance and retention, and accelerates early-task recovery compared with EWC, replay, and continual meta-learning baselines.

Robotic Optical Coherence Tomography-Guided Geometric Modeling of Soft Tissue: 3D Representations for Data-Driven Closed-Loop Shape Control 2026-10-23
📝 30 pages, 18 figures
Show abstract

In medical robotics, data-driven soft-tissue shape control requires deforming tissue toward desired morphologies through sequential physical interactions. This challenge is particularly important in microscale manipulation, where localized deformation is difficult to observe, represent, and control, while physics-based models can be computationally expensive and difficult to adapt to realistic environments. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) provides high-resolution volumetric perception of tissue deformation, but target-conditioned control requires effective representations of both observed and desired tissue states. In this work, we introduce an OCT-guided data-driven framework for tissue shape control, learned from user-guided demonstrations and executed with closed-loop OCT feedback. We study full-volume, tissue-masked sparse-volume, and voxelized point-cloud representations, with or without residual conditioning, using deterministic (Explicit) and generative diffusion (Diffusion) policies. Across 12 configurations and 576 phantom rollouts on 3-DoF depth and 6-DoF directional tasks, OCT-derived 3D representations support both Explicit and Diffusion policies, with performance depending on representation and residual conditioning. Residual conditioning reduces directional Shape RMSE from 0.748 to 0.555 mm for full-volume Diffusion, while voxelized point-cloud Explicit with residuals achieves 0.534 mm for depth and 0.443 mm for directional control, with comparable accuracy at intermediate unseen targets and consistent performance across deployments. We further evaluate the framework in 60 ex vivo porcine rollouts, extending to biological tissue with final geometric errors comparable to human demonstrations. These results establish volumetric OCT as current and goal representations for learned closed-loop tissue shape control across deterministic and generative action models.

RAPAC-DP: Response-Aligned Pending-Action Compensation for Diffusion Policies under Delayed Execution 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Cloud-side inference expands the computational resources available to imitation-learning policies, but communication and inference latencies create a mismatch between prediction and execution. We propose RAPAC-DP, a response-aligned pending-action compensation framework for diffusion- and flow-based action generators. By conditioning on actions committed during inference, RAPAC-DP generates a full-horizon action chunk aligned with the scheduled handover time. A lightweight pending-action encoder and LoRA adapters learn this compensation while keeping the pretrained base policy frozen. RoboCode: Learning Reusable Action Primitives for Robot Manipulation 2026-10-23
📝 12 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Recent robot manipulation policies commonly predict continuous action chunks directly from observations, without explicitly modeling reusable structure in the action space. However, manipulation demonstrations often contain recurring short-horizon motion patterns that are shared across tasks. We introduce \textsc{RoboCode}, which learns these patterns through a shared soft-gated primitive action interface. Rather than assigning each action chunk to a single discrete code, \textsc{RoboCode} represents it as a soft composition of multiple learned primitives, providing a flexible intermediate representation of short-horizon actions. The primitive interface is learned from action chunks pooled across multiple tasks and then frozen while task-specific state encoders are trained to map current state inputs into the shared latent space. Experiments on five ManiSkill manipulation tasks address three questions. First, the learned primitive interface reconstructs action chunks with an average reconstruction MSE of 0.0187. Second, analysis of code activations reveals structured code reuse across tasks. Third, \textsc{RoboCode} achieves the highest or tied closed-loop success rate on all evaluated tasks, with an average success rate of 75.6%, compared with 68.8% for ACT and 41.6% for the larger Diffusion Policy baseline. These results suggest that soft-gated primitive codebooks provide an effective and interpretable intermediate action representation for robot manipulation.

VecTile: A Low-Cost Flexible Tactile Array for 3-DoF Force Sensing 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages, 8 figures. Submitted to IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA) 2027
Show abstract

Tactile sensing provides robots with contact information difficult to obtain from vision alone. Shear force is particularly important for understanding tangential contact, guiding corrective motions, and maintaining grasp stability, yet flexible and low-cost tactile arrays often measure only normal force. We introduce VecTile, a low-cost, 6 mm thin, flexible tactile array capable of three-axis force sensing. VecTile combines a piezoresistive layer with passive silicone nubs that convert normal and shear forces into distinguishable pressure patterns. The sensor can be fabricated in-house without specialized equipment. The complete system costs less than 35 USD for a 4x4 array. First, shear readings are sensitive enough to stop a sharp scalpel from puncturing a balloon after contact. Second, the sensor substantially improves Diffusion Policy-guided power-plug insertion. Feedback World Model Enables Precise Guidance of Diffusion Policy 2026-10-21
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

World models aim to improve robotic decision making by predicting the consequences of actions. However, in practice, their predictions often become unreliable once the robot encounters states outside the training distribution, limiting their effectiveness at deployment. We observe that execution itself provides a natural but underutilized signal: after each action, the robot directly observes the true next state, revealing the mismatch between predicted and actual outcomes. Building on this insight, we propose feedback world model, an inference-time mechanism that closes the loop between prediction and observation at inference time. Instead of treating the world model as a static open-loop predictor, our method maintains a lightweight feedback state that is updated online to iteratively correct future predictions, compensating for model errors using real-time observations without additional training data or parameter updates. We show that this process can be interpreted as a latent-space observer and admits convergence guarantees under bounded, asymptotically convergent dynamics residuals and a stable feedback gain. We further introduce action-aware guidance to better translate corrected predictions into control by emphasizing action-controllable components while suppressing irrelevant variations. Experiments on LIBERO-Plus, Robomimic, and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that our method substantially improves both prediction accuracy and policy performance under distribution shift, reducing world model prediction error by up to 76.4%. The same feedback mechanism improves the predictions of the pretrained foundation model Fast-WAM-Joint without updating its parameters, demonstrating its plug-and-play applicability. These results show that incorporating real-time feedback at inference time provides a simple yet powerful alternative to static world modeling.

Flow Matching Policy Optimization with Mirror Descent and Entropy Constraints 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Balancing policy expressiveness with the exploration-exploitation trade-off is a core challenge in online Reinforcement Learning (RL). While Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)-based diffusion policies can represent complex, multimodal action distributions, they suffer from two critical limitations: their stochastic reverse processes render entropy intractable (necessitating heuristic exploration), and computing policy gradients through long denoising chains is expensive and unstable. In this work, we show that ODE-based flow matching inherently resolves these issues by enabling both simulation-free policy optimization and tractable entropy computation. Building on this, we introduce Flow Matching Policy Optimization with Mirror Descent and Entropy Constraints (FMER). Our framework exploits this insight in three ways. First, we derive an analytic entropy objective that corrects for the density distortion caused by the $\tanh$ transformation (mapping an unbounded latent space to bounded actions), thereby facilitating entropy-constrained policy optimization. Second, we theoretically establish that minimizing an advantage-weighted conditional flow matching loss acts as a simulation-free surrogate for policy mirror descent. This steers the velocity field toward high-value regions while entirely avoiding backpropagation through the ODE solver. Finally, we dynamically tune the mirror descent temperature based on the effective sample size to enforce a robust trust region during training. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that FMER achieves superior performance on the challenging sparse-reward FrankaKitchen environment, while maintaining competitive results across standard dense-reward MuJoCo benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/gtanaelle/FMER.git.

CLOSER: Evidence-Aware Active Visual Verification for Embodied Object-Goal Navigation 2026-10-20
📝 13 pages, 8 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

Object-goal navigation (ObjectNav) requires an embodied agent to find an instance of a specified category in an unseen environment and stop near it. The agent typically relies on an object detector but must judge online from a limited egocentric video stream, sometimes a single frame, whether a detected candidate is the target. Detectors often confidently mistake similar objects, such as a sofa for a bed, and stopping at a false positive fails the episode. Prevailing systems also approach candidates greedily, leaving them occluded or partially observed. We present CLOSER, an evidence-aware active verification framework that treats stopping as closed-loop evidence acquisition, coupling vision-language model (VLM) verification with active perception. CLOSER fine-tunes an open-source VLM to label each view of a candidate tracked across frames as target, non-target, or uncertain, selects complementary views with an embedding-based diversity gate, and fuses their per-view probabilities with a lightweight network. If the fused judgment remains uncertain, a diffusion policy drives the agent toward the candidate using only the tracked mask and depth until the decision becomes decisive. CLOSER runs locally and plugs into existing ObjectNav systems without retraining them. On Gibson and Habitat-Matterport 3D, it raises average success rate by 3.6 points and success weighted by path length by 2.0 points, showing that verifying evidence before committing improves embodied navigation.

From Instrument-Mounted Demonstrations to In-Vivo Execution: Learning Bimanual Laparoscopic Appendectomy Without Robot-Collected Demonstrations 2026-10-20
📝 Submitted to IEEE ICRA 2027. Project page & Dataset: https://rosota-research.github.io/instrument-mounted-demonstrations-in-vivo/
Show abstract

Most minimally invasive surgery is still performed with hand-held laparoscopic instruments, and the surgeon's instrument kinematics are lost when the operation ends; only the endoscope video is kept. This paper presents an end-to-end pipeline that captures this motion in the operating room and uses it to train a surgical robot policy, validated on live animals. We introduce a surgical instrument-state logger that mounts on the shaft of a standard laparoscopic instrument and recovers its pose and jaw state from an inertial sensor, a time-of-flight sensor and a Hall sensor, with no external camera or tracker. A data pipeline measures the latency of every sensor channel against a robot ground truth and aligns the channels before forming observation-action pairs. On these demonstrations we train a diffusion policy with a fine-tuned DINOv3 backbone, selecting its design by closed-loop rollouts in a physics simulator reconstructed from depth maps of an ex-vivo rabbit appendix. The policy is then retrained on 849 in-vivo demonstrations from four live rabbits and deployed on four additional live rabbits with electrosurgery armed. With the surgeon selecting the surgical phase, the policy completed the appendectomy in three of the four animals. The results show that demonstrations recorded from a surgeon's own instruments are sufficient to train, select and deploy a bimanual surgical policy in vivo. The robot serves only as the timing reference for sensor calibration and as the executor, and collects no demonstrations. Both demonstration corpora are released to support future surgical robot learning research.

ForeTac: Predictive Contact Guidance for Generative Robot Policies 2026-10-20
Show abstract

In contact-rich manipulation, actions that appear equally plausible from vision can produce different contact outcomes, while reactive control based on current tactile feedback may respond too late to prevent failure. We present ForeTac, a predictive contact-guidance framework organized as a represent-predict-evaluate-guide loop. ForeTac encodes the current tactile state, predicts future tactile latent trajectories conditioned on candidate action sequences, and evaluates each predicted trajectory with a contact-quality objective learned from successful and failed physical interactions. At inference, gradients of this objective are backpropagated through the tactile predictor to guide action generation before execution while keeping all model parameters frozen. We evaluate ForeTac on four real-robot tasks (planar and curved-surface wiping, card swiping, and fragile-object grasping) with diffusion and flow-matching policies. Compared with vision-only Diffusion Policy, ForeTac improves the average task success rate (SR) from 36.3% to 71.3% and the average contact success rate (CSR) from 20.0% to 66.3% across these tasks, and yields gains in both metrics with a flow-matching policy. Component analyses support the learned tactile representation, action-conditioned foresight, and generation-time gradient guidance, while predicted tactile latent trajectories preserve contact-quality ordering. What Novice Operators See Changes What the Policy Learns: A User Study of Keypoint Hand Visual Representation for Teleoperated Demonstration 2026-10-20
📝 9 pages, 8 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Imitation learning increasingly relies on novice operators, whose demonstrations fall short of experts' in number and inter-trial consistency. Operators act on what they are shown, yet how the rendering of the robot affects their demonstrations, and the policies trained on them, has not been examined. We study this question with PointHand, a teleoperation interface that renders a multi-fingered robot hand as sparse keypoints at its fingertips and joints, the same keypoints a policy can observe. In a simulated mug-in-box task, 18 novices and 4 experts collected 2,200 trials under three renderings: the real shape of the hand, all keypoints emphasized, or the thumb and index finger alone emphasized. The novices shown the thumb and index finger emphasized collected 441 successful demonstrations from 600 trials, against 320 for those shown the real shape. We then trained diffusion policies on these demonstrations, replacing the scene point cloud and proprioceptive state they observe with the hand keypoints and two object keypoints. Within 400 steps these demonstrations went from the weakest of the three novice conditions under the point-cloud policy (46.3%) to the strongest under the keypoint policy (57.3%), a gain of +11.0 percentage points in all five seeds. Those collected with all keypoints emphasized changed by at most +0.7 percentage points, and in no more than three seeds. The value of a demonstration therefore depends on the rendering shown during collection and on what the policy observes together. A shared representation can pay off on both sides, in the number of successful demonstrations and in the success rate of the policy trained on them. Learning to Be Different: Component Specialization for Compositional Diffusion Policies 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Diffusion policies with linearly composed scores have emerged as a promising approach to multitask robotic learning by combining separate factors to enable modular, task-wise adaptation, compositional generalization, and interpretable functional decomposition. However, current training objectives do not encourage factor specialization, often resulting in uninterpretable denoisers whose composed prediction is an average over identical copies, undermining their architectural premise. To address this, we propose a drop-in score-decorrelation auxiliary loss, motivated by projective composition theory. Learn the Task, Not the Schedule: Reducing Spurious Cross-Arm Coupling in Bimanual Imitation Learning 2026-10-19
Show abstract

A bimanual demonstration specifies both what the robot should do and one particular schedule for when each arm should act.
