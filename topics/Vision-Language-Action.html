PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. Precise IK remains laborious: redundancy and singularities complicate the inverse mapping, execution depends on hand-tuned controllers, and training data on physical robots are costly to collect. We present PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision IK. It updates joint-position references from pose feedback and observes its accumulated reference relative to the measured joints, so it can retain the offset that the position controller needs at rest. Two-scale pose errors with an error-dependent action gain resolve millimeter residuals while preserving large motions. Progress and dwell rewards teach it to both reach the tolerance and stay within it. In simulation, a single attempt by the trained PPO policy keeps the mean errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks, and a deployment protocol raises success to 98.9%. When trained directly on a physical arm with an off-policy RL learner, PolicyIK achieves higher reaching and holding success than all four classical IK controllers under the same time budgets. In simulated pick-and-place, PolicyIK executes the end-effector commands of the $π_{0.5}$ VLA policy with 95.7% success. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.

Fine-Tuning VLAs with Self-Demonstrated Generative Control for Multi-Task Manipulation 2026-11-03
📝 Additional experiments. Project page, code, checkpoints, data: https://self-supervised-control.pages.dev/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

State-of-the-art vision-language-action (VLA) models such as pi0.5 exhibit strong semantic understanding, instruction following and task behavior. However, when deployed on new robots, even minor mismatches in hardware configuration relative to pretraining can cause severe performance drops. Finetuning the VLA on in-domain expert data from the new embodiment improves performance on the expert task but leads to a loss in its original instruction following and behavioral priors. In this paper, we propose a self-supervised method that generates online interaction rollouts from the zero-shot VLA as additional training data for finetuning. Our experiments show this yields strong multi-task manipulation policies on the target robot that (1) inherit pretrained tasks distilled from the zero-shot VLA without expert demos for them, (2) improve success and generalist instruction following beyond expert-only fine-tuning on expert-teleoperated tasks, and (3) mitigate forgetting of held-out tasks from the pretrained policy, not part of fine-tuning. We demonstrate the success of our approach across test sets probing generalization on a real ALOHA robot and a new simulation benchmark in RoboTwin. Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization for Agentic Robotics 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra now directly control robots and can generalize better than vision-language-action models (VLAs). We argue that this ability could arise from two distinct, composable skills: semantic reasoning, to understand what a task asks for in a scene, and motion control, to move the robot to do it. We show that small open VLMs are already capable of the first and mainly lack the second. We introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches this missing motion skill, entirely in simulation on simple primitive tasks and from the model's own successes, with no frontier teacher or distillation and no real-world robot data. ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper, and 74.0% on a bimanual adaptation of RoboLab-120, on par with GPT-6 Astra's 72.2%. ZAP also generalizes to new objects, goals, and strategies, and in simulation to a robot embodiment on which it never trained.

Should the Action Expert Train the VLM? Action-Token Supervision and Gradient Routing in Mixture-of-Transformer VLAs 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly pair a pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) with a flow-matching expert that conditions its action predictions on the VLM's key-value representations. Those representations must carry information useful for control, yet the VLM was never trained for the action expert's flow-matching objective. Two signals can address this: gradients backpropagated from the expert's loss (coupling), or VLM supervision to predict discrete tokens encoding the same target actions. Prominent training recipes forgo the first, blocking expert-to-VLM gradients (insulation) to protect the VLM's existing capabilities, while also supervising it on vision-language data and action tokens. Because vision-language co-training also aims to preserve those capabilities, the additional protection provided by insulation is unclear. At the same time, insulation removes a direct signal about which features continuous action prediction requires. On the other hand, action-token supervision offers an alternative way to learn action-relevant representations, which may explain why insulation succeeds in practice. We disentangle these factors in autonomous driving and manipulation by independently varying action-token supervision and whether the expert's loss updates the VLM. In open-loop driving, insulation is competitive with coupling when action tokens are supervised; without them, coupling reduces trajectory-prediction error by roughly 25%. When both components are trained jointly from the outset, coupling increases closed-loop driving route progress by approximately 17% with action-token supervision and 40% without it, and similarly improves robot manipulation performance under both settings. These findings show that the value of gradient insulation depends on the VLM's action supervision and the evaluation setting, challenging insulation as a default VLA training recipe.

Asynchronous Robot Policies 2026-11-03
📝 For code, checkpoints and more results, check out https://arp.insait.ai
Show abstract

Scaling vision-language foundation models has significantly advanced generalist robot manipulation, but the increased model scale introduces computational latency that degrades real-time physical reactivity. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures execute high-level semantic reasoning and action ex- ecution sequentially within the primary control loop, creating a fundamental tension between semantic-reasoning capability and robot agility. To address this, we propose Asynchronous Robot Policy (ARP), an architectural framework that decouples semantic reasoning from low-level action generation using asynchronous processes operating at independent frequencies. In ARP, a high-capacity Vision-Language Model (VLM) asynchronously updates a semantic memory at low fre- quency, while a lightweight Vision-Action Expert, conditioned on this semantic memory, generates robot actions at a much higher control frequency. Such a formulation, enables having a fast perception-action loop without compromising on the semantic capability. To overcome asynchronous memory staleness, ARP trains the vision-action expert on the semantic staleness and execution delay, with temporally mismatched observation across processes. On Franka manipulation tasks, ARP achieves an 18% higher success rate and 20% less time need for execution in quasi-static setups, while 3-5 times baseline performance in dynamic simulation environments. Furthermore, ARP enables operating the VLM at 10 times lower than its nominal frequency with no significant performance loss making it much more efficient than the baseline. These results highlight the architectural advantages of modeling robot policies as asynchronous processes, enabling real-world reactive deployment without sacrificing the scale of foundation models.

LoopQ: Fair, Closed-Loop Evaluation of Quantized Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Post-training quantization (PTQ) for vision-language-action (VLA) models promises smaller and faster VLA deployments. However, when analyzing existing VLA quantization works, we realised that it is hard to reason about the performance of the suggested quantization method compared to existing, similar, or in many cases, simpler PTQ methods. We present LoopQ, a closed-loop benchmark that compares multiple quantization policies on the same checkpoint, layers and engine, varying only the method and the kernels. We evaluate RTN, AWQ, GPTQ, ActQuant and QuantVLA on four VLAs with more than 180,000 episodes of LIBERO and LIBERO-plus, compare against QVLA's reported results on the same layers, and benchmark whether the four public VLA-quantization releases can be reproduced. We benchmark the methods on an NVIDIA H100, an L4, and a Jetson AGX Thor. Our results show that while quantization reliably saves memory, 2-3x for real low-bit models, VLA-specific PTQ methods underperform compared to simpler generic baselines. At 4 bits, RTN, AWQ and GPTQ match or exceed ActQuant and QuantVLA, plain RTN matches the calibrated methods, and plain AWQ matches the success QVLA reports for itself. Standard LIBERO hides the losses that do exist: configurations with no significant LIBERO loss lose 1.5-4.3 points under paired LIBERO-plus perturbations. Speed is a weaker story. At batch 1 on the H100, no quantized pi0.5 or OpenVLA-OFT configuration is faster than its 16-bit model (1.06-1.58x slower on library kernels); only the L4 with int8 kernels gains (4-14%), the Thor is slower on every 4-bit configuration, and the engine alone changes latency by 2.1x. Profiling explains why: pi0.5's quantized backbone is compute-bound, while its memory-bound action expert, 63% of GPU time and 13% of the parameters, stays in 16 bits.

CRONOS: Benchmarking Non-Episodic Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) is promising for adapting robot policies to unstructured real-world environments. However, standard RL pipelines rely on episodic training with frequent scene resets, which is impractical for real-world deployment due to the need for substantial human intervention. While prior work has studied non-episodic RL in relatively small policies trained from scratch, its behavior when fine-tuning modern large-scale pre-trained vision-language-action (VLA) models remains underexplored. In this work, we introduce CRONOS, a simulation benchmark for RL fine-tuning of VLAs under non-episodic scene resets. CRONOS leverages high-fidelity physics simulators and adopts shared-scene multi-task settings. Using CRONOS, we show that naively fine-tuning pre-trained policies fails in non-episodic settings due to shifts in object configurations and end-effector poses; however, these challenges can be mitigated through targeted reset allocation and by addressing biases in pre-trained models. Moreover, we find that non-episodic training enhances long-horizon manipulation and improves generalization to previously unseen object configurations and task sequences. Caging Vision-Language Navigation for Legged Robots via Barrier-Certified Safety Architecture 2026-11-03
Show abstract

By harnessing the inherent commonsense capabilities of pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), current Vision-Language Action(VLA) models for legged robots excel at interpreting language-based navigation commands but lack the physical grounding and explicit safety assurance required for real-world operation. Therefore, this paper aims to cage Legged-Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) through a safety architecture that provides physical grounding and ensures navigation safety. The key idea is not to abandon the powerful capabilities of end-to-end VLA planning, but rather to augment and cage them. In particular, we integrate a high-level VLA planner with a low-level physical-grounding layer that leverages efficient state estimation, in-situ 3D spatial perception, and an Signed Distance Field (SDF) representation to generate executable trajectories. We provide a navigation planner with provable collision-avoidance guarantees under the stated assumptions by combining a low-dimensional Bezier-parameterized MPPI planner with Discrete Exponential Barrier Function (dEBF) constraints. The proposed Legged-VLN system is modular, making it compatible with various VLM/VLA models and agnostic to the robot platform. The proposed system is validated with extensive real-world experiments on humanoid and quadrupedal robots, demonstrating versatile, efficient, and safe vision-language driven navigation capabilities.

Trusting Imagined Futures: Auditing Imagined Rewards for VLA Post-Training 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Video world models enable candidate actions to be evaluated through imagined outcomes, but high predicted rewards can favor trajectories with poor execution quality. We introduce Trusting Imagined Futures (TIF), an offline supervision selection method for vision-language-action (VLA) post-training that audits predicted rewards using execution feedback. A multidimensional reward model predicts a utility score for candidate ranking and five dimensions of execution quality. An auditor uses reward predictions and uncertainty to identify overestimation patterns learned from paired imagined videos and real executions. TIF accepts the candidate with the highest predicted utility when its advantage over the existing trajectory exceeds a margin and its audit risk passes a threshold; otherwise, it retains the existing supervision. The retained real trajectories then supervise behavior cloning. Across four LIBERO suites and three training seeds, TIF achieves a mean success rate of 76.25%, improving over training on the existing trajectories by 3.00 percentage points with the same trajectory count and training budget. Adding audit gating yields 1.47 percentage points with the reward model and advantage margin held fixed. The gains persist against advantage ranking and logistic regression risk selection at matched replacement counts. On recorded videos from three real robot tasks, TIF increases clean completion among selected trajectories from 58.33% to 70.00% across 60 candidate pools.

LIBERO-RECOVER: Beyond Task Success Towards Failure Recovery in Robotic Manipulation Models 2026-11-03
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) or World Action (WAM) models have recently demonstrated remarkable performance in robotic manipulation. On LIBERO, SOTA method have achieved nearly 100\% success rates, seemingly suggesting that the models are ready for deployment in real world. However, near perfect performance on existing benchmarks can be misleading: success under ideal conditions does not imply real world robustness. Existing benchmarks primarily evaluate task completion from predefined initial states, while real world interactions inevitably involve failures such as failed grasps, collisions, and unintended object movements. A robot must therefore not only execute tasks successfully, but also recognize and recover from failures to continue the task. Yet this capability remains largely unmeasured, revealing a critical gap between benchmark performance and real world reliability. To address this gap, we introduce LIBERO-Recover Benchmark, a large scale benchmark for failure recovery in robotic manipulation. Built upon LIBERO, we collect real execution failures from SOTA embodied models and construct 1,000+ scenarios across four recovery levels: (1) Action Retry, (2) Action Adaptation, (3) Object State Recovery, and (4) Environmental Recovery. We evaluate four core capabilities: spatial understanding, object structure reasoning, interaction understanding, and topological reasoning. As the first large-scale benchmark for embodied failure recovery, LIBERO-Recover shifts evaluation from \emph{Can the robot succeed?''} to \emph{Can the robot recover after failure?''}, promoting robust and generalizable embodied agents. The project will be avaible in \textcolor{blue}{https://liulin815.github.io/LIBERO-Recovery/}.

PRIME W0: Physical Priors from World Models for Embodied Manipulation 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel at instruction following, yet most remain feed-forward: they condition actions only on the current observation and discard both long-horizon temporal context and the hierarchical structure of VLM representations. We present PRIME W0, a hierarchical conditional flow-matching framework that closes this gap through three synergistic designs: (1)per-layer KV injection from a pretrained VLM into an Adaptive MoE action expert; (2)dynamics-aware conditioning via asynchronous world-model subgoal-image prediction and a DINOv2 historical-frame semantic bridge; and (3)Action Dynamic Manifold (ADM) flow matching with covariance-structured noise that respects robot kinematic coupling. CLAP: Direct VLM-to-VLA Adaptation via Language-Action Grounding 2026-11-03
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026. Project page: https://omron-sinicx.github.io/clap
Show abstract

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) inherit semantic capabilities from pretrained VLMs, yet large-scale post-training on robot data and architectural modifications can reshape the backbone so extensively that it becomes difficult to isolate what the VLM contributes to control. Directly converting pretrained VLMs into VLAs with minimal architectural change offers a more transparent path to understanding how VLM capabilities transfer across model scales. The core obstacle is output-distribution mismatch: predicting actions as bare numeric token sequences moves generation away from the VLM's pretrained language distribution, degrading the capabilities we seek to preserve. To address this, we propose CLAP (Causal Language-Action Prediction), which prepends each numeric action sequence with a natural-language action description, causally conditioning precise action-token prediction on a language-action plan without modifying the backbone architecture. With only 4-5 hours of training, 2B CLAP achieves 90.8% on LIBERO (+14.9 pt over VLA-0) and improves robustness on LIBERO-PRO under language, object, and spatial perturbations. Ablations attribute the gains to action-grounded language semantics, and on a real UR5e robot 2B CLAP reaches 60% success against 15% for matched VLA-0. We release CLAP at 0.8B, 2B, and 4B as an open-weight, multi-scale compact VLA family from a single VLM lineage, enabling controlled analysis of VLM-to-VLA capability transfer.

What the Guard Misses, the Robot Executes: Implied Harm in VLA Instructions 2026-11-03
📝 Accepted at the Science of Physical AI Safety (SPAIS) Workshop at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) act on instructions without being able to refuse, so screening harmful requests falls to monitors. We test whether these monitors catch ordinary robot tasks requested for harmful reasons, holding the task fixed while varying only how explicitly the intent is stated. $π_{0.5}$ completes the task at every level of explicitness, as often as for harmless controls. Text guards flag nearly every blunt request but few implied ones: up to 95% of implied-harm runs end with the task done and no flag raised, and most still leak after recalibrating on robot instructions at low false-positive rates. Monitoring the model's activations does not close this gap. Linear probes separate harmful from harmless instructions almost perfectly in the base language and vision-language models, but this separation weakens after robot training in two model families, most for implied harm.

Action Intent as a Bridge: Transferring Manipulation Knowledge from Human-Centric Videos to Robots 2026-11-03
📝 12 pages,6 figures
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have achieved promising performance for generalized robotic manipulation, yet their reliance on expensive real-robot data remains a fundamental bottleneck. A scalable alternative is to transfer knowledge from off-the-shelf human-centric videos. However, existing methods extract latent action representations that are entangled with scene-specific noise, diluting the transferred knowledge. Motivated by this limitation, we propose Action-Intent Transfer (AIT), a two-stage method that transfers knowledge through a clean action representation shared across humans and robots. In the first stage, AIT extracts explicit action intents and disentangles the implicit action intent from manipulation videos. In the second stage, AIT aligns the human and robot domains within this shared intent space and decodes the intent into executable robot actions. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments demonstrate that AIT transfers human knowledge more effectively and consistently outperforms prior baselines.

Aero-VPA: Few-Step Flow-Matching Control over Non-Reconstructive JEPA Latents for Agile Flight on Edge Hardware 2026-11-03
📝 38 pages, 3 figures, 22 tables. Simulation study (PX4 SITL + Gazebo Harmonic). Version 2 of a preprint first posted on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23633375). Code: https://github.com/ylee-sling/Aero-VPA ; trained policy: https://huggingface.co/ylee-sling/aero-vpa ; benchmark dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/ylee-sling/aero-vpa-benchmark
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generalize across tasks, but their autoregressive decoders need sequential forward passes in proportion to the action horizon, each bounded below on embedded aerial platforms by streaming billions of parameters from memory. We present Aero-VPA, a lightweight Vision-Predictor-Action architecture for 3D navigation on edge hardware. A compact RGB-D Vision Transformer and a five-head ensemble predictor, trained without pixel reconstruction, forecast the next latent state; a Conditional Flow Matching solver generates a velocity and yaw-rate chunk in two Euler steps, so each decision takes five sequential network evaluations; and the ensemble dispersion drives an uncertainty fallback. Version 1 specified the architecture, deployment and protocol without results. This version reports the first closed-loop evaluation: PX4 software-in-the-loop simulation of quadrotor missions in procedurally generated forests. Behaviour cloning failed on all 60 missions despite open-loop errors of 0.02-0.04 m/s: the policy copies its last command, a copycat form of causal confusion. DAgger with a privileged, safety-gated supervisor gave the first successes but plateaued at 11.1-21.1% across six checkpoints. A map-free runtime shield with a 15 s depth memory, barrier constraints, a cap on motion outside the 73-degree field of view and backoff along the flown path raised the first DAgger policy's success on 180 paired missions from 21.1% to 52.2% (95% CI [45.0, 59.4]; exact McNemar p = 1.2 x 10^-10) and cut its crashes from 72.2% to 22.2%, while timeouts rose from 2.8% to 23.3%. All six checkpoints gained 30.0-34.4 percentage points, at mean speeds of 1.16-1.36 m/s. The code (https://github.com/ylee-sling/Aero-VPA), the trained policy (72.6 MB, https://huggingface.co/ylee-sling/aero-vpa) and the full benchmark dataset are public.

Human-to-Robot Transfer via Bridging Representation and Action Space in VLAs 2026-11-03
📝 Accepted for Oral Presentation at the CoRL 2026 Workshop on Scaling H2R. Project page: https://junhyeong-park.me/h2rb/
Show abstract

Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies provide a strong starting point for deployment, but adapting them to a target robot and environment still requires costly embodiment-specific demonstrations. Human demonstrations are far easier to collect, yet directly leveraging them is challenging due to the substantial human-robot embodiment gap, particularly when the underlying VLA has been pretrained primarily on robot data. We introduce H2RB (Human-To-Robot Bridge), a human-robot co-training framework for adapting pretrained VLA policies from unpaired human and robot demonstrations. H2RB bridges the embodiment gap at both interfaces: geometry-anchored alignment grounds human and robot observations in a compatible representation, while a decoupled-decoder tokenizer shares action latents without forcing both domains through a single action mapping. This design allows human demonstrations to provide useful behavioral supervision while retaining the rich visual, semantic, and control priors of the pretrained VLA. Across real-world tasks, H2RB substantially improves object transfer performance from 8.0 to 51.8 and enables skill transfer to score 62.7, in settings where co-train baselines totally fail to transfer human-demonstrated skills. The benefit also persists under object-placement shifts, improving out-of-distribution transfer from 6.0 to 45.3. H2RB suggests a way for human demonstrations to serve as a scalable supervision source for adapting pretrained robotic policies, reducing the dependence on costly target-robot data collection.

RAFT: Reasoning-Aligned Fine-Tuning Improves Robustness of VLA Post-Training 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Adapting a generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policy to a target task with limited demonstrations can improve nominal task success while reducing robustness to variations in appearance, objects, and instructions. We introduce reasoning-aligned fine-tuning (RAFT), a two-stage post-training procedure: RAFT first trains low-rank adapters on task-relevant reasoning annotations, then freezes them while fine-tuning the policy on action demonstrations. On a physical DROID robot, RAFT achieves $75.0\%$ out-of-distribution success, compared with $27.9\%$ for plain fine-tuning; on a multi-stage bimanual YAM task, it reaches $70.0\%$ mean success, compared with $36.0\%$ for the strongest baseline. RAFT also improves success under visual, object, and instruction shifts in simulated pick-and-place and open-drawer tasks. Compared with plain fine-tuning, RAFT policies place more attention on annotated task regions and yield higher linear-probe accuracy for object identity in later layers. A broader reasoning-data mixture further improves downstream success across demonstration budgets.

AceAction-5M: Hierarchical Goals for Generalist Action Learning Across 3D Game Worlds 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Video games provide a practical setting for collecting vision-language-action (VLA) data, whose acquisition in physical environments is costly. Existing datasets, however, remain limited by restricted game coverage, sparse language supervision, or temporal hierarchies that do not necessarily reflect changes in task objectives. We introduce AceAction, a dataset of human gameplay across multiple 3D games for learning skill composition and subgoal transitions. AceAction organizes demonstrations around complete tasks, linking overall instructions, ordered subgoals, and local skill instructions to temporally aligned visual observations and keyboard-and-mouse actions. To construct these annotations, we develop a vision-language model pipeline that identifies subgoals from changes in objectives or interaction targets, anchors navigation goals to visually recognizable waypoints, and composes adjacent subgoals into task instructions. The resulting demonstrations cover navigation, reconnaissance, combat, interaction, and recovery, with explicit stage boundaries that connect task progression to fine-grained control. We assess annotation quality through model-based verification and human inspection. An audit of 500 trajectories yields a 95.8% pass rate across independent reviewer assessments. AceAction provides a data foundation for studying how agents execute, combine, and switch skills to follow complex instructions in dynamic game environments.

DiMuRe: Diagnosis-Guided Multilevel Recovery for Robotic Task Execution 2026-11-03
📝 15 pages, 3 figures, including appendices
Show abstract

Disturbances can invalidate the conditions required by a robot's next action even when much of its task program remains useful. A recovery system must therefore choose a response suited to the current state. We present DiMuRe (diagnosis-guided multilevel recovery), a framework for robot-use agents that uses phase-specific evidence and method preconditions to select program reuse or predefined recovery skills, including bounded vision-language-action (VLA) grasping. After the original program exits, a multimodal large language model (MLLM) can generate corrective code from images and execution feedback. Local verification checks the outcome of each recovery attempt. We also introduce LIBERO-Disturb, a benchmark for robustness and recovery under mid-execution disturbances, supporting program-based agents and VLA policies. On 801 scheduled disturbed Encore development cases, Local DiMuRe (program reuse and recovery skills) achieves 77.65% complete-task success, compared with 58.68% for the original program under the same physical-step allowance. Full DiMuRe achieves 81.90% with terminal replanning, an additional step allowance, and early handoff. Local DiMuRe also uses fewer physical steps per scheduled episode than the original program.

Say the Same, Act Differently: Diagnosing and Steering Text-Action Divergence in Reasoning VLAs 2026-11-03
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. 23 pages, 3 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) models pair physical control with natural-language rationales that supervisors can read to monitor embodied safety. Prior attacks on VLA reasoning corrupt the rationale itself and leave visible evidence in the text. We show that visual perturbations can instead alter the physical action while keeping the generated rationale word-for-word identical. At the shared text/action interface of Alpamayo for driving and InstructVLA for manipulation, our text-neutral action-subspace (TNAS) diagnostic shows that the locally most action-sensitive direction is nearly orthogonal to the active text-loss gradients, with 1.87% and 5.28% norm overlap. At equal hidden-state perturbation norms across both models, interventions along this text-neutral residual shift actions 4.64x to 47.8x more than text-gradient perturbations, whereas text-gradient perturbations induce 21.2x to 277x more text drift. Steering visual perturbations along this direction reaches the same decoupling from the image. Across 154 obstacle-annotated driving scenes in Alpamayo, TNAS-guided attacks induce a new collision under a word-for-word identical rationale in 52.6% of scenes under full-image perturbations and 35.1% under a localized forward-camera patch, compared with 4.55% for unguided action attacks. Relative to a text-preserving baseline, TNAS guidance raises exact rationale preservation from 83.0% to 89.5% and cuts teacher-forced text drift by 68.4% at a comparable collision rate. In InstructVLA, TNAS guidance cuts text drift by 40.5% and preserves the exact response on 85.0% of examples alongside a 1.73-unit action-chunk shift. An unchanged rationale therefore cannot certify a safe action, so monitors for reasoning VLAs must verify the predicted action channel directly.

Online Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability Onboard the Robot Control Loop: A Real-Time GPU Solver for Safety-Critical Autonomy 2026-11-03
📝 12 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables
Show abstract

Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability provides certified backward reachable tubes (BRTs) for safety-critical robotic systems, but the level-set computation scales exponentially in the state dimension and has historically been performed offline.
