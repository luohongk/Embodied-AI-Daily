PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates. Project page is available at: https://kl-research.github.io/pgx

Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment 2026-11-19
📝 44 pages, 10 figures, 11 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under four years the field has grown from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. VLA research joins robotics, computer vision and language modelling, so this survey is written for researchers and practitioners from each of these fields. It reviews 89 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. Seven design axes follow a VLA's life cycle: model design (inputs and memory, architecture, action output, intermediate predictions), training (pretraining data, post-training) and deployment (real-time execution). We compare 22 models on these axes, with their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge. (i) Training data has broadened beyond teleoperated robot data (about 10,000 hours for $π_0$) to community-collected episodes and egocentric human video (20,000 hours for GR00T N1.7). (ii) Model sizes have split into two groups: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters; a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs. (iii) Generalist VLAs handling both manipulation and navigation are emerging, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions, and review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail after small scene changes. A practitioner's guide gives a default for each design decision, and a glossary supports readers new to the field.

PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced rapidly in language-conditioned robotic manipulation, yet existing benchmarks mainly evaluate gripper--object contact and largely ignore how objects interact with the environment through gravity, friction, inertia, and mass distribution. As a result, it remains unclear whether current policies truly understand physics or merely interpolate from their training data. We address this gap through the lens of dynamic extrinsic dexterity and introduce PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark in which a robot must infer an object's mass, center of mass, and friction to send it to a destination beyond its kinematic workspace. PhyDEX contains 35K trajectories (143 hours) across nine tasks and 100 scenes, covering uniform, irregular, and dynamic-mass objects with static, multiple, and moving destinations. All data are generated by a scalable, fully automatic pipeline that combines grasp generation, motion planning, and closed-loop throw optimization under randomized physical properties. We evaluate three representative VLAs, GR00T N1.7, DynamicVLA, and ForceVLA, and find that all achieve success rates below 3\%. Our analysis reveals two key bottlenecks: limited robustness of pretrained models, which causes most failures before the throw, and physical blindness, as providing ground-truth physical properties roughly doubles success. Experiments on a humanoid robot and in the real world show the same failure patterns. We hope PhyDEX will drive the development of physically grounded robot policies.

Composable Driving Style for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
📝 13 pages, including appendix
Show abstract

End-to-end driving policies can achieve strong task performance while offering limited control over their driving style. Driving style describes how a vehicle accelerates, brakes, and maintains distance. Policies trained without explicit preference conditioning lack a direct mechanism for adjusting these behaviors at inference. We propose YOUDrive, a vision--language--action planner for continuous and composable driving-style control. Each teacher's behavior is distilled into a task vector, a learned parameter update that induces a style direction in the planner's representation. These directions can be scaled and combined at inference without retraining the backbone. Conditioned on these directions, a flow-matching decoder models a multimodal trajectory distribution and generates kinematically feasible motion alternatives. We introduce the Style Alignment Score (SAS) to describe human-referenced kinematic behavior after safety and task gating. On NAVSIM navtest, a single coefficient monotonically controls assertiveness across six teacher-derived personas, while PDMS remains above 0.90 across the tested individual and blended configurations, reaching 0.954. Further analyses reveal systematic policy-specific kinematics after controlling for scene context, whereas the evaluated discrete style labels provide limited additional explanatory power. Code, checkpoints, and the metric will be released.

GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives 2026-11-19
📝 64 pages, including supplementary material. Project page: https://groundingpi.github.io/ Code: https://github.com/groundingpi/GroundingPI Model: https://huggingface.co/GroundingPI/GroundingPI
Show abstract

Precise grounding matters. It specifies which object is the target and where that object is, even in clutter and for tiny objects, and it has to be fast enough for closed-loop control. Yet vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) take perception from general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones, which still fail in these settings. We introduce GroundingPI, a 4B grounding foundation model that generates points and boxes as quantized coordinates in a shared vocabulary. Training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, using supervision from public datasets and dedicated data engines. Against 44 baselines across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities, GroundingPI establishes a new state of the art, averaging 73.68%, above the larger GPT-6 Astra (71.54%). As a downstream visual backbone, GroundingPI improves performance on robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. Across RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboCasa-GR1, it outperforms all evaluated backbones in all four out-of-distribution settings, with relative gains of up to 24.8% over the strongest baseline. On RoboCasa-GR1, GroundingPI trained with 50% of the demonstrations outperforms those baselines trained with 75%. Its mean open-loop L2 error on nuScenes is 0.296 m. We systematically analyze GroundingPI's pretraining in scale and data composition. Grounding quality and downstream manipulation improve with pretraining scale, with generalization tracking perceptual gains most closely. Analyzing the data recipe across these 11 perceptual capabilities shows dense grounding's substantial benefits for both, and OCR's potential as a catalyst for perceptual learning. These results support grounding as a perceptual foundation, and dedicated perceptual pretraining as a promising direction for foundation models of physical intelligence.

On Agentic Manipulation Policies 2026-11-19
📝 Code and videos at: https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-amp/
Show abstract

Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2% and 42.9% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The agent succeeds zero-shot in 104 of 108 real-world trials spanning twelve tasks and three robot instances, reducing failure rates by 20x and 15x relative to strong VLA and WAM baselines. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation. See https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-amp/ for code and videos.

Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Comparing the three views shows whether a system lacks a capability or fails to compose capabilities it already has. A diagnosis harness further attributes the failed episodes of three representative systems to Mind, Act, or their composition and summarizes each system in a profile card. Our evaluation of multimodal agents, vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world-action models (WAMs) shows that competence in a single view does not carry over to Mind2Act tasks. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem.

Risk-Controlled Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Retrieval-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) models enable efficient test-time adaptation by leveraging expert demonstrations as additional context without updating model parameters. However, even when retrieved demonstrations are relevant to the current task, the VLA may fail to effectively utilize the retrieved information, ultimately leading to task failure. To address this challenge, we propose R2-VLA, a risk-controlled retrieval-augmented VLA framework that enables reliable test-time adaptation by satisfying a user-specified task success rate requirement with minimal expert assistance. Specifically, R2-VLA learns an uncertainty predictor that estimates the uncertainty of the VLA's predicted actions given the retrieved demonstrations, guiding when to act autonomously or request expert assistance. We further introduce a task-level risk-control procedure that calibrates the uncertainty threshold to satisfy a user-specified task-failure risk requirement. Moreover, R2-VLA continually incorporates expert corrections into its retrieval memory to improve future adaptation while preserving the prescribed task-level risk guarantee. Experiments across three VLA backbones and four LIBERO benchmark suites demonstrate that R2-VLA improves average task success by 9.2--11.2 percentage points, achieving the desired target with fewer expert requests. These results highlight the effectiveness of connecting the usefulness of retrieved demonstrations with task-level risk control to enable successful robotic manipulation.

Awaken-HI: Scaling Humanoid Intelligence with Whole-Body Human Experience 2026-11-19
📝 project page: https://engineai-robotics.github.io/Awaken-HI
Show abstract

A central goal of humanoid robotics is for robots to perform the everyday work people do in homes and workplaces. Existing egocentric video datasets cover many of these activities but provide little synchronized whole-body motion data for learning loco-manipulation at scale. We build Awaken-Human, which pairs approximately 50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with synchronized whole-body human motion and covers more than 100 skills. Building on this, we also introduce Awaken-HI (Humanoid Intelligence), a vision-language-action model for humanoid loco-manipulation that jointly predicts original human motion and retargeted robot actions through a shared action expert. We show that loco-manipulation skills transfer more effectively from people to humanoids when human trajectories directly supervise the shared action expert. Increasing human pretraining data reduces held-out robot action prediction error according to an empirical scaling law and raises the mean success rate on downstream real-robot tasks. After full-corpus pretraining and T800 adaptation, Awaken-HI achieves a mean success rate of 93.75% across eight individual tasks and outperforms GR00T N1.7 and $π_{0.5}$ in both individual and long-horizon evaluations. The same policy generalizes to objects unseen during robot post-training and to new pairings of familiar objects and destinations.

TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action 2026-11-19
📝 11 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables. Project page: https://tacinno.github.io
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently been equipped with tactile sensing to perceive visually subtle contact events in contact-rich manipulation. However, adding tactile input yields inconsistent gains, and some visuo-tactile VLA models rely little on tactile input, because much of the tactile change, such as the gel deformation during gripper closure, is predictable from visual observations and executed actions. To address this problem, we introduce TacInno, which isolates the contact dynamics that vision and executed actions cannot explain, termed tactile innovation, and uses tactile innovation only as a training target. Specifically, the TacInno tokenizer, conditioned on visual observations and executed actions, compresses the future tactile change into a discrete code, and the policy, TacInno-VLA, learns to predict the code, adding no module for the code at deployment. TacInno-VLA achieves average success rates of 87.5% across six simulation tasks and 70.5% across four real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by 10.7 and 9.0 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies validate the contributions of visual and action conditioning, multi-frame encoding, and auxiliary code prediction. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness of tactile innovation as a supervision signal for contact-rich manipulation.

FastRoverVLA: Fast Vision-Language-Action Execution for Off-Road Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
📝 14 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Off-road vision-language-action (VLA) models generate scene-conditioned plans, but frequent trajectory updates can repeatedly invoke expensive multimodal inference. Reusing a prediction reduces this demand while leaving its commands exposed to changes in vehicle motion. FastRoverVLA addresses this mismatch by decoupling visual plan generation from motion-conditioned execution. Its training-free interface maintains decoded plans between visual predictions. A speed-curvature action buffer is aligned with measured motion at each update, integrated into a local trajectory, and advanced by consuming the current action. The corrected remainder is written back to the buffer, preserving motion corrections across successive updates. A bounded refresh rule normalizes speed and curvature variations within a newly decoded plan and assigns a short or long reuse budget according to the resulting plan-roughness score. The interface requires neither backbone features nor an additional learned driving policy. Over a 3 s horizon, independent real-request evaluation achieves average displacement error (ADE) and final displacement error (FDE) of 0.328/0.596 m on ORAD-3D and 0.266/0.526 m on RELLIS-3D, reducing FDE by 18.0% and 36.7% at request rates of 6.0% and 4.8%, respectively. These runs retain lower errors across four backbones and both datasets and reduce Qwen3-VL request-processing cost by factors of 16.6 and 20.8 under serial replay, while mechanism and motion-only controls identify persistent state correction as the main source of the observed accuracy benefit. The source code and evaluation materials are publicly available at https://github.com/ADEPT-ORG/FastRoverVLA.

Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models to Stop Safely 2026-11-19
📝 A two-stage pipeline that teaches VLAs to stop prohibited tasks safely while preserving permitted skills
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable robots to perform diverse tasks from visual observations and language instructions. While prior work has explored task suppression and refusal, stopping prohibited behavior once execution is underway and controlling the resulting physical response remain challenging. Our evaluation reveals that policies which suppress a task from its initial state can still complete it when taking over partway through execution. Even when the task remains unfinished, the robot may drop a held object, making non-completion an incomplete measure of safety. To address these challenges, we introduce State-Aware Fallback Imitation (SAFI) for tasks where holding provides an appropriate alternative response. SAFI comprises two stages, (1) fallback target construction, which assigns stationary arm commands and consistent gripper targets throughout prohibited-task demonstrations, and (2) joint policy adaptation, which learns these targets alongside the original actions of retained tasks. This supervision teaches an explicit alternative response at intermediate execution states while maintaining the robot's other skills. Extensive experiments with OpenVLA-OFT across LIBERO and BadLIBERO tasks evaluate prohibited completion, physical incidents, and retained-task performance. On the evaluated LIBERO tasks, SAFI reduces aggregate target success after takeover to 1.3\%, with no recorded physical incidents or action saturation, while preserving high retained-task success.

One View, One Action: Spatially Generalizable VLAs through Observation-Aligned Experts and Cross Graph Consistency 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Multi-view vision-language-action (VLA) models still face challenges in spatial generalization, particularly under changes in camera viewpoints and robot initial states. These models typically receive action supervision only in the robot base frame, requiring them to implicitly learn the spatial correspondence between observations from each view and base-frame actions. We propose One View, One Action (OVOA-VLA), which learns to predict actions in each view's camera frame, allowing the same physical motion to provide diverse, observation-aligned supervision across views. OVOA-VLA jointly trains a base-frame expert and view-specific action experts, whose robot-state inputs and action targets are expressed in their respective camera frames. Two complementary directed attention graphs model both directions of interaction between the base and view-specific experts, jointly leveraging view-specific action supervision to improve base-frame prediction. Cross Graph Consistency further encourages agreement between the two graphs' base-frame predictions for the same sample. With the same multi-view inputs, the model supports both full and base-only inference. Experiments across multiple simulation benchmarks demonstrate improved manipulation performance and robustness to camera-viewpoint and robot-initialization perturbations. Full and base-only inference achieve average success rates of 88.1% and 85.7% on LIBERO-Plus, respectively. In real-world evaluations, base-only inference raises the average success rates from $π_{0.5}$'s 28.3% to 58.3% across three tasks under spatial distribution shifts.

RAVEL: Asynchronous Rolling Inference for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) models are highly effective for generalist robot manipulation, yet their reliance on computationally expensive VLM encoding and multi-step iterative action generation imposes a significant latency bottleneck. The resulting inference latency makes it difficult for robots to respond quickly, especially in dynamic environments. We address this limitation with RAVEL (Rolling Asynchronous VLA Enabling Low-Latency Control), an asynchronous inference framework that addresses the computational bottlenecks of both the VLM backbone and the action expert. To reduce the delay from multi-step action denoising, RAVEL allows near-term actions to be executed after a single additional denoising step by carrying partially denoised future actions forward in a rolling buffer. To avoid blocking on slow VLM encoding, RAVEL decouples VLM encoding from rolling action generation, allowing the action expert to operate continuously using the latest available VLM context, while a lightweight Fast Observation Pathway (FOP) directly conditions the action expert on current observations. Across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, RAVEL consistently achieves substantially lower response latency while maintaining the task capability of the underlying VLA, enabling high-frequency and responsive closed-loop control.

DMT-Attack: Diffusion-Prior Texture Search for Closed-Loop Vision-Language-Action Policies 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on visual feedback throughout manipulation, making them vulnerable to persistent texture attacks. However, optimized patterns can stand out from their surroundings, making an attack easier to notice and its carrier more likely to be inspected or removed. To address this limitation, our key insight is to search the structured image distribution of a diffusion model for natural-looking textures while using closed-loop feedback to identify task-disruptive candidates. Based on this idea, we propose DMT-Attack, a gradient-free gray-box attack that generates textures for ordinary scene objects such as tablecloths. A frozen text-conditioned diffusion model defines the search space, and an eight-expert latent proposal network is initialized from evaluated teacher latents and refined using rollout feedback. We evaluate DMT-Attack against pi_0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT across four LIBERO suites. DMT-Attack achieves a macro clean-conditioned attack success rate (cASR) of 57.9%, compared with 44.7% for UPA-RFAS and 19.7% for random natural textures. It also attains higher scene similarity and a lower clean-calibrated detector flag rate (45% versus 97.5-100% for optimized patch baselines). These results demonstrate that natural-looking textures can disrupt VLA visual feedback and closed-loop decision-making, motivating defenses that jointly assess visual appearance and task behavior.

CauSyncVLA: Causal Latent World Modeling for Asynchronous Robot Control 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies must react while visual inference is still running. Overlapping computation and execution reduces waiting, yet leaves perceptual memory stale and successive action chunks disconnected. We present CauSyncVLA, an asynchronous dual-system controller that aligns perceptual-memory prediction and trajectory correction with actual robot execution. Rather than forecasting pixels or physical states, a Causal Latent World Model predicts current perceptual memory from delayed visual anchors and executed state-action history, supervised through action-sensitive distillation to retain the frozen expert's control response. Conditioned on this updated memory, Predictive Flow Warm-Starting (PFWS) aligns the unexecuted action horizon for single-step flow correction without extra training. On LIBERO-10, CauSyncVLA reaches 93.8% success at 5.04$\times$ inference throughput, improving closed-loop robustness and robot motion smoothness.

A$^2$-VLA: Adapting Vision-Language-Action Policies to New Tools through Agentic Affordance Discovery 2026-11-19
📝 16 pages, 6 figures. Includes appendices
Show abstract

Tools with different shapes can have similar functions, but transferring manipulation skills across tools requires understanding how tool geometry relates to the specific task and adjusting the manipulation strategy accordingly. The key challenge is to discover the common affordances that contribute to task completion across different tools and verify whether they support new tools in performing similar work. We present A$^2$-VLA, a tool-adaptation framework that combines an embodied agent with a VLA policy. It treats tool affordances as hypotheses the agent can construct, test, and revise. Given teleoperated demonstrations with a few source tools, the agent reconstructs the scenes in simulation and autonomously determines task-relevant affordance features: functional regions and their key geometric properties. It develops affordance-extraction skills and refines both the features and skills through autonomous experiments in the reconstructed scenes. Validated extraction skills accumulate in a reusable skill library for unseen tools. Finally, A$^2$-VLA uses encoded functional-region features as conditioning inputs to a $π_{0.5}$ backbone during training and inference. Evaluation examines affordance reliability in reconstructed scenes and real-robot task success on unseen tools. Our method achieves 86.1% success in simulated cross-tool trajectory retargeting based on affordances. On three real-robot tool-use tasks, A$^2$-VLA improves task success by 30.7 percentage points over $π_{0.5}$.

FlashX: Scaling Robotic Experience Reuse for Embodied Agents with CBA-Based NAND Flash 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Embodied agents are emerging as a promising paradigm for complex robotic tasks, leveraging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAMs) to generate actions through Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based iterative denoising. However, existing models often struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. We observe that retrieving successful experiences and using them to warm-start denoising enables agents to better adapt to unfamiliar situations while reducing computational overhead. This creates a new opportunity to improve task accuracy and inference efficiency simultaneously, in contrast to conventional acceleration techniques that trade accuracy for computation. However, realizing this opportunity requires scaling experience banks, introducing substantial storage and search overhead that challenges the capacity, bandwidth, and power constraints of edge systems. We propose FlashX, a CMOS Directly Bonded to Array (CBA)-based NAND flash architecture for scalable experience reuse in embodied agents. FlashX exploits CBA's high internal bandwidth for parallel pSLC key search, employing interleaved Compute-in-Flash (CIF) engines to sustain sensing throughput while retaining dense TLC latent storage. Proprioception-aware clustering further reduces search overhead and enables incremental experience acquisition without global reclustering. Evaluations across VLA and WAM workloads demonstrate up to 12.24\%p and 7.43\%p improvements in task success under dense and compute-efficient configurations, respectively. FlashX achieves 2.57$\times$ higher search throughput than the strongest prior NAND search architecture under pSLC sensing, with a further 9.7$\times$ search reduction through clustering. This delivers 1.29--3.95$\times$ end-to-end speedup and 1.12--2.34$\times$ energy efficiency improvement over SSD-based retrieval.

CoachSI: Self-Improvement of Robot Policies with a Language Coach 2026-11-19
📝 Project page: https://tlxhlll.github.io/CoachSI/
Show abstract

Generalist robot policies execute atomic skills reliably but fail when the same skills are composed into long-horizon tasks. Self-improvement from the policy's own experience does not close this gap: experience collected by the policy alone stays within its own distribution and reproduces what it can already do. We show that what the policy lacks is not a skill but the next step. We present CoachSI, a self-improvement method in which a frozen, off-the-shelf vision-language model (VLM) coaches the policy in language: it watches the robot and names the next step in the format of the policy's atomic instructions, and never outputs an action. Successful coached rollouts are relabeled in hindsight with the original task instruction and used to fine-tune the policy. The fine-tuned policy is then deployed on its own.
