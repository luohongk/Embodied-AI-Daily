SafeDriveVLA: Navigation-Conditioned World Model Dreaming for Conflict-Aware End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-14
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project page: https://safedrive-vla.github.io/SafeDriveVLA/
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are gaining attention in autonomous driving, inheriting commonsense priors from large language models (LLMs) and widening the navigation interface from a fixed command set to free-form natural-language instructions. However, this open-ended interface exposes a new vulnerability surface: an inattentive driver may issue instructions that are unsafe given the current scene, and a malicious user may issue dangerous ones on purpose. We present the first benchmark suite that evaluates navigation following and conflict awareness together, along with adversarial robustness. Existing VLAs fall short on both: SimLingo completes no language-instructed lane change in closed loop, and all models degrade sharply under conflicting signals. We propose SafeDriveVLA, the first driving VLA that (1) explicitly predicts a three-level navigation-compliance mode to decouple conflict reasoning from action generation, and (2) uses navigation-conditioned world model dreaming to inform this prediction. Unlike prior world models that roll out expert or planned actions, ours dreams of strictly following the navigation signal, showing when following is safe. SafeDriveVLA reaches 88.16 Driving Score (DS) and 68.64% Success Rate (SR) on Bench2Drive, lifts closed-loop language following by 49.3% and reduces collisions under unsafe instructions from 60 to 38 compared to the best SimLingo variant. Video demonstrations are available on our project page.

Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-14
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. 25 pages, 16 figures, including appendix. Project page: https://gr-bc.github.io/
Show abstract

Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. More details and videos are available at: https://gr-bc.github.io

AdaRSI: On-Demand Recursive Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) often fail midway through unseen long-horizon tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a remedy, but embodied RSI methods either refine the harness around a frozen executor and so cannot add a missing motion, or train the executor on persistent failures without asking whether a failure lies in how the executor is called or in what it can do. We ask when a self-improving embodied system should change the weights of its executor, and propose AdaRSI, an on-demand RSI framework for embodied agents. A coding agent first improves its skills and harness, then attributes persistent failures by retrying failing sub-steps from saved states, and fine-tunes only the sub-steps that remain unreliable, with demonstrations it collects itself and experience carried over from earlier tasks. On all 50 target tasks of RoboCasa365, with GR00T N1.5 as the executor and no human demonstrations, AdaRSI improves the VLA on all three suites and raises its success on unseen composite tasks from 4.6% to 13.8%. Compared with fine-tuning every sub-step, it reaches slightly higher success while using only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the self-collected demonstrations, and fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.

Towards Agentic Robots: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Foundation models are driving a paradigm shift in robotic intelligence, moving beyond low-level action prediction to goal-directed orchestration of data collection, model training, task planning, and real-world execution. We characterize this emerging paradigm as an agentic robots, in which agents reason over human instructions, interact with robotic environments, rectify execution errors autonomously, and dynamically adjust operational strategies. Although this field develops fast, current studies are not well connected. Researchers have no consistent view of how to combine different agentic functions into a complete robot system. This paper provides a unified overview of existing research on agentic robots. We first trace the development from robot policies to agentic systems, covering vision-language-action models, world models, and world-action models. We then clarify the definition and scope of agentic robots and organize existing research into seventeen categories across four functional domains: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness. Across these domains, we examine how agents shape model learning and robot decisions, collect and curate robot data, construct worlds, tasks, and tests for learning and evaluation, and organize capabilities for robot use. Finally, we discuss open challenges and outline directions for future research. Looking forward, we hope this work contributes to a clearer understanding of agentic robots and supports new approaches to making them more capable, reliable, and adaptive.

Interaction Latents as Actions for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Contact-rich manipulation requires robots to coordinate not only where to move, but also how contact and force should evolve throughout an interaction. Yet most robot policies represent actions primarily as motion trajectories, using tactile signals as inputs or auxiliary prediction targets without explicitly encoding contact dynamics in the action representation. We propose ILA (Interaction Latents as Actions), which uses decodable interaction latents that jointly capture the evolution of motion, contact, and force application as policy action units, providing a shared representation for action generation, evaluation, and offline improvement. We learn these latents through visually conditioned joint action-tactile reconstruction with a time-conditioned implicit decoder. Using them as flow-matching generation targets supports post-training of pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs), as well as training flow-matching policies from scratch. A shared decoder maps generated latents to executable actions. For offline improvement, we learn a tactile-conditioned expert reference model from interaction latents encoded from expert demonstrations. The resulting expert-consistency rewards guide weighted learning from successful rollouts, while observation-compatible expert references guide correction of low-reward predictions from failed rollouts. Across three policy families, ILA improves success rates averaged over four real-world tasks by 21.0-36.5 percentage points relative to tactile-conditioned direct-action baselines. Ablations highlight the benefit of encoding contact evolution in action targets beyond tactile conditioning, while the same latent space supports expert-guided offline improvement. Project website: https://mrsecant.github.io/ILA/.

Human Demonstrations as Post-Training Data for Any Robot Body: Retargeting the Object, Not the Hand 2026-10-14
📝 16 pages, 17 figures, 7 tables, 2 algorithms; 56-page supplement as ancillary file. A subset of episodes with their per-robot outcomes: https://playground.dxtr.co
Show abstract

Human demonstrations are today used largely for pretraining vision-language-action models, with little ability to use the same data for target-embodiment or task-oriented training, which scalable deployment of robots in industrial and general environments depends on. A human demonstration recorded on a simple and scalable device such as a mobile phone can serve as post-training data for a robot whose body differs in structure and capability from the human who recorded it. This is made possible not by retargeting the hand's motion but the object's. A capture yields a body-free record: the object's pose over time, its contact intervals, and a reconstructed 3D object with provenance. Any robot, given as a URDF and a short descriptor, is solved against that record and scored in simulation against the demonstrated object motion as ground truth: the human-to-simulation leg of human-to-simulation-to-robot transfer, measured per robot. Each (robot, episode) pair gets one outcome: pass, failure with a named mode, or refusal, with its reason, before any motion. The method runs as a working system over a growing collection; the set analysed here is a sample of it: 42 captures solved on 8 robots, 157 pairs, of which 31.2% pass, 52.2% fail, 14.0% are refused and 2.5% are unauditable (kinematic feasibility, +/-50 mm / +/-15 deg, in simulation). Refusals held up: every pair the checks refuse on stored evidence yet ran anyway failed (13 of 13; 5 on a body whose public model welds its hand). A description file could not be shown to screen a robot: its outcomes were barely more predictable than guessing. No policy has yet been trained on the record. The record decides what a robot may post-train on; whether the policy then works on the real robot is the simulation-to-robot question, answered downstream.

ObjDream: Guiding Vision-Language-Action Models with Object-Level Understanding and Future State Prediction 2026-10-14
📝 18 pages, 8 figures. Project page: https://huailiangma.github.io/objdream/
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic manipulation, enabling robots to generate actions from visual observations and language instructions. However, most existing VLA models still operate on image-level visual representations inherited from pretrained vision-language backbones, while lacking fine-grained object understanding for manipulation. As a result, their robustness can degrade under out-of-distribution object layouts, clutter, and background variations. To address these limitations, we introduce ObjDream, an object-centric VLA framework centered on object-level understanding and future object state prediction. ObjDream uses a spatio-temporal object encoder to organize visual observations into temporal object slots, where persistent object identity and dynamic object state are represented through separate channels. These structured object representations are inserted into the token sequence of the vision-language model (VLM), where identity-guided routing modulates VLM attention to the corresponding object tokens. In addition, an action-conditioned object dynamics module predicts compact future object states relevant to manipulation during training, explicitly supervising object state evolution under robot actions without requiring future prediction at inference. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0 show that ObjDream improves generalization under diverse distribution shifts and scene perturbations. Real-world robot experiments further demonstrate that ObjDream transfers to physical manipulation settings with novel object arrangements and background changes.

G$^3$VLA: Geometric Inductive Bias for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-14
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have rapidly advanced generalist robot manipulation by harnessing the semantic knowledge of pretrained vision-language backbones. However, their visual representations are typically grounded in 2D image coordinates rather than calibrated camera geometry. This limitation is especially pronounced in multi-camera systems, where views are coupled by known intrinsics and extrinsics but processed as independent images. We propose G$^3$VLA, a camera-aware geometric module that injects calibrated structure into the visual-token stream of a pretrained VLA without altering its action space or original action-learning objective. G$^3$VLA combines intrinsic-conditioned ray embeddings, projective positional encoding (PRoPE), and bidirectional cross-view fusion to establish geometrically consistent representations across camera views. Geometric supervision is provided by ground-truth point maps or confidence-gated $π^3$X teacher predictions, enabling training without depth sensors or manual geometric annotations. On $π_0$, G$^3$VLA consistently improves performance on LIBERO and RoboCasa24 under both supervision regimes, while ground-truth supervision improves five-task RoboTwin2.0 averages with three cameras. Experiments on camera-shifted LIBERO-Goal and real robots demonstrate improved robustness to unseen camera viewpoints. Controlled calibration studies across $π_0$ and $π_{0.5}$ show that these gains depend on correct camera geometry while remaining stable under mild calibration perturbations. These results demonstrate that grounding VLA visual tokens in calibrated camera geometry provides a simple and general mechanism for improving geometric consistency, multi-view reasoning, and viewpoint robustness in pretrained robot policies.

FoveaVLA: Foveation Actions Replace the Wrist Camera in VLAs 2026-10-14
📝 Project website: https://tml.stanford.edu/FoveaVLA/
Show abstract

We present FoveaVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that replaces the wrist camera with learned foveation actions. Wrist cameras provide close-up detail but restrict the view to the end-effector's surroundings. External cameras see the whole scene, but the policy receives them at low resolution, so the fine detail needed for manipulation is lost. FoveaVLA recovers this detail by predicting, alongside each action chunk, the interaction region to observe at the next policy query. The policy then receives crops of these regions, taken from the original-resolution images, together with the full views. We build FoveaVLA on $π_{0.5}$ in two configurations, Single and Dual, using one or two external stereo cameras with full and foveal RGB and depth inputs, and post-train both on DROID with RoboInter interaction annotations. Across four real-world task suites, FoveaVLA-Single achieves 70.8% success with one external RGB-D view, and FoveaVLA-Dual achieves 77.1%, versus 52.1% for the wrist-equipped $π_{0.5}$. Both match the baseline on plain pick-and-place and improve success with distractors, cloth manipulation, and far-away objects.

SnapFlow: One-Step Action Generation for Flow-Matching VLAs via Progressive Self-Distillation 2026-10-14
📝 10 pages, 6 figures, 9 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models with flow-matching action heads, such as π0 and π0.5, have become a standard recipe for generalist robot manipulation, but every action chunk is produced by integrating a learned velocity field over about ten sequential action-expert evaluations, so action generation dominates model latency and caps the achievable control frequency. Existing remedies either shrink each evaluation through layer distillation or pruning while leaving the sequential solver in place, or import one-step objectives from image generation that were designed for training from scratch, rely on an exponential-moving-average teacher or an external critic, and regress finite-span targets onto the conditional velocity, whose variance around the marginal field biases the learned map. We introduce SnapFlow, a self-distillation method that adapts a pretrained flow-matching VLA to one-step action generation. SnapFlow mixes local flow-matching supervision with shortcut samples whose target is the model's own two-step Euler endpoint under stop-gradient, and adds a single zero-initialized destination-time projection so that one action expert serves both objectives while the pretrained predictor is preserved at initialization; no separate teacher network or critic is required. On π0.5, whose 10-NFE teacher operates near ceiling, the archived one-NFE SnapFlow checkpoint reaches 98.75% LIBERO success versus 97.75% for the teacher; under a separate paired evaluation of π0, the one-NFE model surpasses its teacher by 10.5 percentage points. SnapFlow also lowers mean and tail action error on held-out data, cuts batch-one model latency by about 3.3x, and transfers to shelf retrieval on a dual-arm humanoid.

What Matters for World Modeling in Vision-Language-Action Models? 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Integrating world models into Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) has emerged as a promising direction, enabling policies to anticipate future dynamics beyond reactive observation-to-action mapping. However, existing approaches vary simultaneously in their world representations, integration paradigms, and policy backbones, making it difficult to determine what truly matters for effective world model integration. To disentangle these factors, we categorize existing efforts into four representative paradigms, namely Implicit Future Alignment, Explicit Future Query, MoT Future Expert, and External World Predictor. We instantiate each within a unified framework and systematically evaluate these paradigms with diverse world representations and policy backbones across in-domain, out-of-domain, long-horizon, dynamic, few-shot, and real-world settings. Our study yields four main findings: 1) world representation effectiveness is paradigm-dependent, whereas 2D semantic representations offer robust, cross-paradigm gains; 2) no single paradigm is universally optimal, yet the decoupled MoT Future Expert offers competitive overall performance, particularly in OOD robustness; 3) world modeling consistently benefits policies built on standard VLMs, while decoupled designs exhibit greater compatibility with pretrained VLAs; and 4) world modeling improves performance over action-only baselines across multiple settings, with notable gains on long-horizon and dynamic tasks. Project page and video demonstrations are available at https://dylanmqs.github.io/WM-in-VLAs/.

Coordi-pi: Coordination-Aware Dual-Hand Action Representation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but extending them to bimanual dexterous manipulation introduces a high-dimensional and structured action space. Existing bimanual VLA policies commonly concatenate right- and left-side arm--hand actions at each control timestep, leaving cross-hand interactions implicit within a single action vector. We introduce Coordi-$π$, a structured action representation for bimanual dexterous manipulation. Coordi-$π$ represents the right- and left-side arm--hand actions as individual action tokens and organizes them into an interleaved sequence, explicitly exposing the two sides within the action sequence. To explicitly encode hand identity and temporal synchronization in this interleaved representation, a learnable hand-identity embedding distinguishes the two sides, while sequence--control-timestep factorized rotary positional encoding (RoPE) separates sequence position from control timestep, allowing actions at the same control timestep to share a temporal coordinate while retaining distinct sequence positions. Across all five bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in DexJoCo, Coordi-$π$ achieves state-of-the-art performance under both \textit{rand-obj} and \textit{rand-full}, improving the average success rate over $π_{0.5}$ from 31.9\% to 51.3\% and from 18.3\% to 32.3\%, respectively. Coordi-$π$ also consistently outperforms $π_{0.5}$ across four real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.

One-shot Human Video-Guided Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have recently achieved remarkable progress by leveraging large-scale robot datasets and foundation models for general-purpose robot manipulation. However, their ability to rapidly adapt to novel manipulation tasks beyond the training distribution remains limited. Existing approaches often require task-specific robot demonstrations and additional training, which are labor- and compute-intensive, limiting the scalability of VLA adaptation to open-world scenarios. To reduce this dependence on target-task robot data, we propose CapPro, a framework that enables a VLA policy to adapt to previously unseen manipulation tasks using only a single human demonstration video. CapPro consists of two components. First, a phase-aware human video representation module constructs a compact video representation through learned visual token selection and learns task-relevant query representations under phase-caption supervision. Second, a video-conditioned adaptation module jointly incorporates the compact video and query-derived representations into the action-conditioning context of the pretrained VLA policy. % This design introduces task-specific guidance while largely preserving the original VLA action-generation backbone. In this way, the human demonstration provides task-specific execution guidance rather than an embodiment-aligned trajectory to imitate, without requiring target-task robot demonstrations or target-task fine-tuning. Experiments across source and unseen target tasks show that CapPro improves average performance across unseen target groups while maintaining comparable performance on the source tasks. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of human-video guidance for cross-task VLA adaptation.

ACRO: Actor-Critic Rollout Orchestration 2026-10-14
📝 18 pages
Show abstract

Executing a vision-language-action (VLA) policy involves deciding when to continue an attempt and when to intervene to improve its chances of success. We introduce ACRO, an Actor--Critic rollout orchestration layer that makes these decisions for a fixed VLA. ACRO seeks interventions that offer a more promising continuation, are physically feasible, and leave sufficient time to complete the task. A Critic learned from policy rollouts estimates success prospects to decide when to intervene and select a re-entry target along the executed trajectory. Trajectory Retraction shortcuts the visited path to return the robot to that target, where the same VLA resumes execution from a fresh observation. We evaluate ACRO with GR00T N1.7 and $π_{0.5}$ across SimplerEnv, RoboCasa, and real-robot manipulation. Under the same physical execution budget, including all return motion, ACRO improves success by 10.4, 7.3, and 21.7 percentage points, respectively. It also completes additional tasks as the execution budget grows. Accelerating VLA Inference with Perturbation Informed Execution 2026-10-14
📝 23 pages, 11 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

Action-chunking vision-language-action (VLA) models predict sequences of future robot actions. Under fixed-horizon execution, the robot executes a fixedlength prefix of each predicted chunk before invoking the policy again, leaving the remaining predictions unused. For a given number of executed actions, short execution horizons require more policy calls. Extending the execution horizon amortizes policy inference cost over more actions. However, uniformly applying long horizons across episodes can limit opportunities for precise corrections. To address this limitation, we propose PICES (Perturbation-Informed Chunk and Execution Horizon Selection), a training-free method that jointly selects an action chunk and the number of actions executed before the next policy call. Within a single policy call, the action expert generates a batch of action chunks from multiple initial noise samples and their locally perturbed counterparts, using the same conditioning features across all samples. PICES exploits differences between corresponding actions in each nominal chunk and its counterpart to determine candidate-specific execution horizons and select a nominal chunk for execution. Experiments on simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that PICES achieves speedup by up to 2.3x in wall-clock policy inference time on Jetson Orin and Thor relative to fixed horizon baselines while improving the success rate.

OxyGen: Unified KV Cache Management for VLA Inference under Multi-Task Parallelism 2026-10-14
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Embodied AI agents increasingly require parallel execution of multiple tasks, such as manipulation, conversation, and memory construction, from shared observations under distinct time constraints. Recent Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) support such heterogeneous outputs, yet existing inference systems incur redundant computation and resource contention when serving concurrent tasks on-device. We identify isolated KV cache management as the root cause. To address this, we propose unified KV cache management, an inference design that treats the KV cache as a first-class shared resource across tasks and control cycles. This abstraction enables two key optimizations: cross-task KV sharing eliminates redundant prefill of shared observations, while cross-frame continuous batching batches unfinished language requests from different frames and advances them within a per-frame decoding budget. We implement this design in OxyGen and evaluate $π_{0.5}$ on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 and Jetson AGX Thor. OxyGen achieves up to 3.7$\times$ speedup over isolated execution, delivering over 200 tokens/s language throughput and 70 Hz action frequency simultaneously on RTX 4090. We also validate the design on Xiaomi-Robotics-0 and StarVLA across different backbones and expert configurations. We further demonstrate the inference gains on a humanoid robot with on-board Jetson AGX Thor.

SPECTRA-VLA: Base-Fine Hyperspectral Token Transfer at Frozen Vision-Language-Action Interfaces 2026-10-14
📝 34 pages: 11-page main paper and 23-page supplementary material
Show abstract

Hyperspectral imaging distinguishes RGB-similar objects, but a pretrained vision-language-action model needs a state its frozen decoder can use. SPECTRA-VLA provides a native-width spectral upgrade without retraining the visual encoder or action decoder. A learned continuous residual enriches each target token through its own normalized false-color reference; projection, norm control and validation-calibrated injection regulate the change. Across 86 held-out acquisitions, category balanced accuracy rises from 0.812 to 0.883. Category-matched spectral swaps further show that own-token composition approximately doubles the target-specific recovery increment after frozen decoding. Spectral enrichment and receiving-token preservation thus work together at the pretrained interface.

ForceBench: Benchmarking Force Integration for Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-14
📝 14 pages, 8 figures. Project page: https://forcebench-platform.github.io/
Show abstract

Force feedback provides vision-language-action (VLA) policies with physical observations that can guide action selection and execution. However, differences in tasks, demonstrations, and control settings across existing systems hinder reliable comparisons of force integration designs. We introduce ForceBench, a simulation benchmark for evaluating six-axis wrist-force integration under a unified protocol. ForceBench combines eight tasks spanning physical-property inference, resistance-dependent interaction, and contact-rich assembly with 1,600 synchronized demonstrations and task-specific success criteria. We compare one no-force baseline and six force integration designs on a shared $π_{0.5}$ backbone. These findings support ForceBench's practical value for identifying the benefits and limitations of force integration across manipulation demands. Don't Run with Scissors: Pruning Breaks VLA Models but They Can Be Recovered 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are a promising paradigm for field robotics, but their deployment is bottlenecked by strict edge SWaP-C constraints. Compression techniques such as quantization, distillation, and pruning are well-established for LLMs and VLMs. Pruning, however, remains particularly underexplored for VLA models. We surprisingly find that pruning VLA models yields drastic performance degradation and increased safety violations. Through spectral analysis, we identify structural properties of VLA weights that help explain this fragility. Based on this insight, we introduce GLUESTICK, a post-pruning recovery method that restores much of the original model's capability while preserving sparsity benefits. GLUESTICK computes a one-time corrective term by interpolating between the dense and pruned models in weight space. At inference, each pruned layer applies this correction to recover lost performance with minimal overhead. Our approach requires no additional training and is agnostic to the pruning algorithm. Across diverse VLA architectures and domains, GLUESTICK recovers up to 100% of the success rate and restores safety to near-dense levels, while saving up to 5 GB of memory and achieving up to 1.75x speedup. Cracking Mobile Manipulation: From Failure Diagnosis to Agentic Robot System 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong performance on local robot manipulation, yet their success does not readily translate to long-horizon mobile manipulation. In our experiments, a pretrained subtask VLA achieves 80% success rate on atomic manipulation tasks but only 19% when these skills are composed into mobile manipulation. We argue that this gap stems from failures across the execution process rather than from local manipulation capability alone. To uncover these bottlenecks, we systematically diagnose failures in long-horizon mobile manipulation and identify three dominant regimes: task planning, navigation and base positioning, and local manipulation. Based on this diagnosis, we develop failure-guided agentic intervention that augments a fixed subtask VLA with demonstration-based planning guidance, adaptive visual memory, accessibility-aware base adjustment, and local end-effector adjustment. On RoboCasa365, these interventions progressively improve task success from 19% to 47%, with the finalized system reaching 49% on held-out episode seeds, compared with 18% for the baseline. The same design improves performance from 14% to 37% on BEHAVIOR-1K and from 5/20 to 13/20 on real-robot tasks. These results suggest that reliable mobile manipulation can be advanced by diagnosing and intervening on the current bottleneck, rather than uniformly scaling the underlying policy. Back on Track: Deployment-time Policy Improvement via World Model Planning 2026-10-14
Show abstract

State-of-the-art visuomotor policies are increasingly capable of executing complex tasks, yet can fail during deployment in ways they cannot predict or recover from on their own. Improving their performance and robustness typically requires collecting more data, retraining, or modifying the policy architecture, all of which are time- and resource-intensive in practice. We instead propose an offline-trained monitoring-and-intervention framework that improves a policy during deployment using only black-box query access. From a limited set of labeled on-policy rollouts, we learn a policy-conditioned liveness value that measures the nominal policy's progress toward task completion; we additionally train a compact latent world model using these rollouts together with play data. At deployment, the liveness value continuously monitors execution. When it detects a loss of progress, a short-horizon planner searches the world model for corrective actions using the same value as its planning objective, after which control returns to the nominal policy.
