Cairn: Object-Centric 3D Trajectory Priors for Steering Frozen VLAs 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) are capable low-level controllers, but they tie where objects go to the layouts they were trained on: when objects are moved or obstacles block the way, they carry them to the wrong place or into collisions. Fine-tuning on new layouts needs robot data for each one and erodes skills the model already has. We introduce Cairn, which keeps the VLA frozen and steers it at test time along learned 3D trajectories of the object in its gripper. A small prior reads point clouds of the scene and the held object, along with its pose when grasped and the instruction, and samples SE(3) trajectories up to its release that lead it around obstacles and turn it to fit through gaps. The prior and the VLA form the two levels of a hierarchical planner in which feedback runs both ways: the VLA's intended motion chooses and reshapes the trajectories, and the chosen one guides the VLA's denoising. Its predicted actions, mapped to the held object's poses through the grasp, are pulled toward the next waypoint, while the VLA's weights stay untouched. Across 1,941 held-out LIBERO scenes with displaced objects and obstacles, Cairn raises the success rate of a frozen π0.5 from 24.5% to 95.1%, above that of a π0.5 fine-tuned on our training layouts, without hurting standard LIBERO. On the third-party LIBERO-PRO benchmark, it raises success from 27.3% to 38.0%, and from 54.4% to 81.1% on the episodes it steers. On a real Franka, with a prior trained purely in simulation, it guides the frozen DROID π0.5 around an obstacle and to a placement at the limit of the arm's reach, where it otherwise fails. All code, models and data will be made public.

RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Since vision-language-action (VLA) policies can inherit the slow pace of human demonstrations, accelerating these demonstrations before fine-tuning can lead to faster VLA policies without an additional speed-selection module at deployment. Several offline speedup methods have focused on identifying segments that require less precision and assigning predefined speedup factors to the segments. However, even within a single segment, a speedup factor suitable for steady motion may be too high when precise alignment is required. To this end, this paper presents RePace, an offline reasoning framework that determines how much to accelerate each part of a demonstration. For the same recorded movement, RePace measures how much each candidate speedup factor shortens the sequence and compares the resulting observation-action pairs used for training. Given the task instruction, a pretrained language model uses the visual context and original motion of successive windows to interpret these candidate retiming effects and select a speedup profile for the complete episode. Experimental results demonstrate that RePace outperforms the baselines in overall task execution efficiency on both LIBERO and RoboCasa-Kitchen. On all three tasks with a gripper-equipped Franka Research 3 arm, RePace matches or exceeds the acceleration baselines in success rate and completes successful trials faster.

Toward Agentic Robotics: A Large-Scale Study of Harness Design for Robot-Use Agents 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Recent vision-language models (VLMs) can act as robot-use agents that understand diverse scenes and control robots through policy code and tools. However, they predict 3D gripper poses poorly even with extended reasoning, struggling with precise manipulation. Vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action (WAM) models are the opposite: they execute fast, smooth control on trained tasks, yet fail under domain shifts or when tasks require long-horizon memory and reasoning. Agentic robots therefore combine VLM reasoning and perception with precise VLA/WAM control through a policy harness. Yet harness design spans thousands of configurations, shifting success by tens of points with the same models. Moreover, testing even one design takes thousands of rollouts at high API and GPU cost, so prior works often commit to a single design chosen from few alternatives. To guide harness design, we evaluate over 470,000 rollouts across eight simulation benchmarks, six VLMs, and 15 VLA/WAM checkpoints. Seven controlled studies show effective harnesses must adapt to both VLM and VLA/WAM capabilities. Our findings specify what structure the VLM's policy code needs, where VLA manipulation needs help from tools and policy code, and when to involve the VLM at test time. The reference recipe X-Harness shows that these findings compose without weight updates, lifting base policies up to $+42.8$ points across memory, reasoning, and domain shifts.

From VLM Planners to Robotic Agents: Self-Evolving Harnesses for History-Dependent Manipulation 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models achieve strong performance across embodied manipulation tasks by unifying visual understanding, language conditioning, and action generation. However, long-horizon tasks can involve non-Markovian dependencies: successful decisions may require retaining task progress and past interaction outcomes, while partial observability and execution errors compound over time. Existing work has begun to address these challenges by augmenting foundation-model policies with memory and by introducing hierarchical architectures that separate high-level planning from low-level execution. We organize the high-level VLM as a robotic agent through an editable harness to maintain task context and improve planning through execution experience. We instantiate this approach as SERA (Self-Evolving Robotic Agent), with a fixed VLA executor providing low-level control. The harness maintains and updates task context using observations and execution feedback to guide subsequent planning. In simulation, self-evolution uses environment rollouts to propose and evaluate harness revisions, with the selected configuration fixed before held-out testing. On RoboMME, SERA achieves 51.38% overall success with autonomous selection, compared with 44.38% without self-evolution. We also evaluate history-dependent real robot tasks with human-reviewed harness corrections. Our exploration suggests that agentic planning is a promising direction for history-dependent robotic manipulation.

A Failure-Driven Data Flywheel for Post-Training Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models rarely satisfy all behavioral requirements of a new platform, and teams without a large deployed fleet lack the data to address the resulting failures. We propose a failure-driven data flywheel for post-training such models. It constructs target scenarios absent from the available data by inserting task-relevant entities into reconstructed real-world scenes, and evaluates the model against each behavioral requirement of the target task. Failure rates form a failure map across task conditions and difficulty levels, which is re-evaluated after each round and assigns larger data generation quotas to conditions where the model still fails. We instantiate the framework in autonomous driving for occluded-pedestrian emergence and yielding at stop-sign-controlled intersections, and evaluate it on two VLA models and one non-VLA planner in simulation and on a real vehicle. For occluded-pedestrian emergence, five rounds of post-training improve the critical-phase collision-free rate on the sealed test set from 0.261 to 0.579 for AutoVLA and from 0.390 to 0.765 for ReCogDrive, while the unnecessary braking rate in paired control scenarios never exceeds 0.013. On a full-scale vehicle without real-world training, post-trained ReCogDrive achieves 4 of 5 safe passages, versus 1 of 5 for the base model. Fine-grained evaluation reveals a pattern obscured by the collision-free rate alone: both VLA models learn anticipatory deceleration before reaching the occluding vehicle, while braking after pedestrian emergence remains limited. For stop-sign yielding, the stop-before-line rate rises from 0.100 to 0.897 for AutoVLA and from 0.000 to 0.667 for ReCogDrive. With sequential training from the pedestrian-capability models, it reaches 0.599 and 0.358, respectively, while the pedestrian collision-free rate decreases by at most 0.032.

RoboEvo$^2$: Recursive Robot Self-Improvement via Harness-Policy Co-Evolution 2026-11-25
📝 Project page: https://kaiknower.github.io/RoboEvo2/
Show abstract

Robots should adapt to unfamiliar environments and acquire new skills through interaction, then execute those skills efficiently with practice. Vision-language-action policies provide efficient control, but often need additional human demonstrations to handle unfamiliar settings; reasoning agents can discover solutions using broad prior knowledge and robot-control APIs, but repeated deliberation and repair are costly. Inspired by human learning through reasoning and practice, we propose RoboEvo$^2$, a framework for recursive harness-policy co-evolution. In Policy-to-Harness (P2H) evolution, policy failures guide a coding agent to search complementary Code, Code+ICL, and Code+Policy routes, consolidating verified solutions into reusable skills, routing strategies, and experience memory. In Harness-to-Policy (H2P) evolution, verified trajectories provide supervised training, while Counterfactual Preference Distillation (CPD) teaches the policy to favor successful corrections over failures from the same starting state. The improved policy provides stronger motor skills for subsequent search, while its remaining failures redirect harness evolution, closing the reciprocal loop. Through recursive co-evolution without additional human demonstrations, RoboEvo$^2$ improves standalone success from 11.0% to 84.1% and hybrid success from 30.4% to 90.5% on LIBERO-PRO, with similar gains and reduced agentic costs on RoboTwin C2R. Further experiments demonstrate autonomous task acquisition, cross-embodiment harness transfer, and 95.0% hybrid success on the Unitree G1-D. Our models and code will be publicly released.

DiffGR00T: Execution-Aware Differentiable Training for Humanoid Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-25
📝 14 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables. Conference preprint. Corresponding author: Hesheng Wang
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have achieved strong results in arm-centric manipulation, yet humanoid whole-body control (WBC) requires dynamic balance and tightly coupled full-body coordination. Existing hierarchical humanoid VLAs typically decouple motion generation from physical execution, leaving tracking and balance errors unable to directly optimize the VLA generator. We propose DiffGR00T, an end-to-end differentiable training framework that couples GR00T N1.7 with SONIC, a pretrained whole-body behavior foundation model. To enable execution-aware differentiable training, we replace the non-differentiable simulator with a learned, action-conditioned world model, allowing multi-step body-execution losses to backpropagate through the frozen whole-body controller and the flow-matching sampler directly into the VLA action head. On four HumanoidArena simulation tasks, DiffGR00T improves average task success by 12.5 percentage points over the supervised base VLA (59.2% to 71.7%), demonstrating that execution-aware gradients effectively align high-level motion generation with closed-loop whole-body dynamics.

EqC: Equilibrium Energy Control for Introspective Lifelong Robot Learning 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Long-term robot autonomy depends on more than effective action generation: a policy must recognize when its behavior is no longer sufficiently supported and use assistance to improve. We introduce Equilibrium Energy Control (EqC), a framework for introspective lifelong robot learning built around an explicit conditional energy landscape. EqC casts generative action refinement as equilibrium-seeking optimization, while the geometry of the refinement process reveals whether the generated behavior remains consistent with the learned landscape. This competence signal enables Energy-Gated DAgger, where persistent energy-consistency violations trigger expert takeover and the resulting recovery trajectories are aggregated for subsequent policy updates. EqC integrates naturally into both Diffusion Transformer policies and flow-matching-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies. Across simulation and real-world bimanual manipulation, EqC matches or improves control performance and robustness while enabling timely intervention near the policy's competence boundary. A real-world study further shows how these interventions progressively expand the range of situations handled autonomously, providing a practical path from static generative controllers toward self-improving robot systems.

Commit2Drive: Perception-Grounded Language Decisions as Commitments for Trajectory Planning in Vision-Language-Action Driving Models 2026-11-25
📝 28 pages, 9 figures, 19 tables. Includes supplementary material. Code will be released soon
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models introduce language reasoning into end-to-end autonomous driving. They output a language decision together with a trajectory, which makes the planner more interpretable. However, in existing methods the language decision is either an independent output or an index that selects among similar candidate trajectories, and its annotations are not verified against the trajectory. As a result, the language decision and the trajectory are often inconsistent in closed-loop driving. To address this, we propose Commit2Drive, a framework that imposes the language decision as a consistency constraint on trajectory planning. The decision is a commitment, a maneuver class with a continuous parameter that one deterministic rule reads from any trajectory, and it is grounded in facts reported by the perception of the model itself. A Maneuver Bank maps the commitment to a code with a kinematic meaning, and consistency losses train the planner to execute every admissible decision of a scene, so that the trajectory follows the decision. An automatic data pipeline supplies the supervision. Decisions are labeled from the future motion of the expert, counterfactual executions train the decision and the trajectory together, and rule-based functions with a Vision-Language Model (VLM) generate, review and refine the language annotations. The code is a lookup that involves no hidden state of the language model, so Commit2Drive is extensible to rule-based decisions, which the same planner executes without retraining. Commit2Drive uses a 0.5B language model and runs closed-loop inference online on a single consumer graphics card. On Bench2Drive, Commit2Drive reaches a Driving Score (DS) of 78.50 and a Success Rate (SR) of 55.91% with a lightweight model, which is competitive with the state of the art, and greatly reduces the language-action inconsistency. Code will be released soon.

M$^{2}$AdaptVLA: Multimodal Memory-Augmented Vision-Language-Action Model with Predictive Test-Time Adaptation 2026-11-25
📝 16 pages, 11 figures, 8 tables, including supplementary material
Show abstract

Effective robotic manipulation calls for multimodal memory and rapid adaptation to shifts in contact dynamics. However, many existing policies, which retain either visual or wrench history, may miss key cues from the other modality. Moreover, even with wrench memory, fixed policies can struggle when contact conditions shift at deployment. To this end, we introduce M$^{2}$AdaptVLA, a vision-language-action model that integrates visual and wrench memories with predictive test-time adaptation. For visual memory, the model uses test-time training (TTT) to encode observation history in fast weights. Each entry in the bounded TTT bank combines the corresponding frame's features with a readout from these weights, preserving cues that predate the oldest retained frame. For wrench memory, a context gate controls how much the action expert draws on wrench history as it jointly denoises future actions and wrenches. For predictive test-time adaptation, the auxiliary wrench prediction loss also provides online self-supervision. Using measured wrenches as targets, we update low-rank policy adapters by backpropagating only through the final denoising step. Real-world experiments show that the TTT bank outperforms visual memory baselines on progress tracking, scene recall, and demonstration following. On contact-sensitive tasks, the context gate yields higher success rates than direct injection or gated cross-attention. Predictive test-time adaptation improves robustness to shifts in contact dynamics. On eight tasks requiring visual and wrench memory, M$^{2}$AdaptVLA achieves a mean success rate of 82.71%, a 31.46% absolute gain over the strongest baseline. The project page is available at https://tlf-tlf.github.io/M2AdaptVLAPage/.

Dynamic Task Update Benchmark (DTU-Bench): Evaluating Runtime Task Adaptation in Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies perform well on fixed-instruction robotic manipulation benchmarks, but their ability to adapt to instruction changes during execution remains understudied. We introduce the Dynamic Task Update Benchmark (DTU-Bench), evaluating 40 task revisions across seven update types and three execution stages using LIBERO, a simulated robotic manipulation benchmark. We compare performance when a new instruction is given before execution starts versus during execution, and examine whether the new task instructions are followed. Across 13 representative VLA configurations, average task success is 95.8% on the original object-manipulation tasks, compared with only 1.6% when the instruction changes which object to manipulate during execution. Controlled diagnostics show that policies often complete the original task before the updated one, and that their responses depend on the robot's physical state. We further test whether a simple scripted controller that releases the grasped object and moves the arm back helps robots respond to instruction changes. This improves success from 2% to 38% for π0.5 and from 8% to 48% for DM0.5, but can cause unwanted object motion. These findings reveal limitations that fixed-instruction benchmarks and task-success metrics alone can overlook in evaluating robotic manipulation. Project page: https://dtu-bench.github.io/

PriorVLA: Prior-Preserving Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-25
📝 35 pages. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://priorvla.github.io/
Show abstract

Large-scale pretraining has made Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models promising foundations for generalist robot manipulation, yet adapting them to downstream tasks remains necessary. However, the common practice of full fine-tuning treats pretraining as initialization and can shift broad priors toward narrow training-distribution patterns. We propose PriorVLA, a novel framework that preserves pretrained priors and learns to leverage them for effective adaptation. PriorVLA keeps a frozen Prior Expert as a read-only prior source and trains an Adaptation Expert for downstream specialization. Expert Queries capture scene priors from the pretrained VLM and motor priors from the Prior Expert, integrating both into the Adaptation Expert to guide adaptation. Together, PriorVLA updates only 25% of the parameters updated by full fine-tuning. Across RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and real-world tasks, PriorVLA achieves stronger overall performance than full fine-tuning and state-of-the-art VLA baselines, with the largest gains under out-of-distribution (OOD) and few-shot settings. PriorVLA improves over pi0.5 by 11 points on RoboTwin 2.0-Hard and achieves 99.1% average success on LIBERO. Across eight real-world tasks and two embodiments, PriorVLA reaches 81% in-distribution (ID) and 57% OOD success with standard data. With only 10 demonstrations per task, PriorVLA reaches 48% ID and 32% OOD success, surpassing pi0.5 by 24 and 22 points, respectively.

GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models typically predict actions from current 2D observations, whereas existing 3D policies infer geometry per step without retaining it across an episode. Action generation under occlusion and across multiple stages requires a compact spatial abstraction that persists over time without reconstructing a full 3D scene at each step. Based on this insight, we introduce GeoMem-VLA, a framework that internalizes explicit 3D scene and motion supervision into learnable latent GeoPlantokens, leaving the vision-language backbone's native visual embeddings unaligned. During training, a frozen geometry teacher and demonstration trajectories supervise these tokens via an auxiliary flow-matching head over current scene point maps and future end-effector tracks. Across steps, the policy stores observation and GeoPlan representations in separate memory banks, merges similar adjacent states into event memories, and fuses retrieved history with current features to predict actions. At inference, the 3D teacher and auxiliary head are removed, and action generation relies solely on the latent GeoPlan tokens and episode memory without explicit scene reconstruction. GeoMem-VLA achieves average success rates of 98.9% on LIBERO, 91.10% and 91.24% on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean and Randomized settings, and 78.0% across five real-world manipulation tasks.

HoloBrain-0.5 Technical Report 2026-11-25
📝 Technical report. 23 pages, 13 figures, 9 tables. Project page: https://horizonrobotics.github.io/robot_lab/holobrain_0_5/
Show abstract

We present HoloBrain-0.5, a family of vision-language-action (VLA) models for manipulation across robot embodiments. The policies plan in a shared action space using robot state, kinematic structure, and calibrated camera observations. We standardize joint axes, coordinate frames, and tool-center points in robot descriptions (URDFs) and encode their kinematic graphs in a shared 3D representation. HoloBrain-0.5-Continuous generates action chunks by diffusion, while HoloBrain-0.5-Discrete uses residual finite scalar quantization (FSQ) and delayed autoregression. We pretrain both policies on over 10,000 hours of real-robot, simulation, and human-manipulation data spanning 15 embodiment categories. Across six closed-loop simulation settings, the discrete policy converges faster and reaches a higher mean success rate than the continuous policy (56.77% vs. 49.71%). On seven real-robot tasks, it achieves a mean progress score of 87.76% without task-specific post-training. After post-training, the continuous policy outperforms $π_{0.5}$ on the seven-task benchmark (94.52% vs. 86.25%) and an 11-task dual-arm benchmark (79.07% vs. 69.41%). The discrete-initialized continuous policy transfers five tasks zero-shot from Piper to Piper-X, scoring 63.28% on average. With target-robot demonstrations, the continuous policy scores 89.2% on Franka Panda and 92.94% on a mobile dual-arm platform. Although pretraining includes no Unitree G1 data, post-training with limited G1 demonstrations yields a mean progress score of 70.24% across three humanoid tasks, compared with 11.00% for $π_{0.5}$.

Can Visual World Models See Force? 2026-11-25
Show abstract

World models and vision-language-action policy models are increasingly proposed as foundations for robot manipulation. They predict how scenes evolve and which actions to take, yet successful manipulation depends not solely on following observable trajectories but also on the hidden variable of interaction force. Neurophysiology has shown that humans possess a visual prior for this hidden variable, motivating us to ask the question: Do the representations of visual foundation models also capture the forces involved in an interaction? We define force awareness as the ability of representations to (1) indicate when contact force is applied, (2) track how it changes during an interaction, and (3) reflect how large it is. We probe frozen image, video, world- and policy model representations with a lightweight readout trained on synchronized video and force measurements from two manipulation datasets. Our experiments show that most models capture when contact occurs but not how much force is being applied. Surprisingly, robot post-training does not improve this either, highlighting an important gap in current models capabilities.

One Patch, Many Policies: Object-Associated Physical Patch Attacks on Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAMs) are vulnerable to adversarial visual interference, motivating the study of universal and transferable physical patch attacks. We propose Object-Associated Patch Attack (OAPA), a universal and transferable physical patch framework that enables an attacker to select which task object's manipulation to disrupt through patch placement, without re-optimizing the patch. Our framework combines object-associated placement, differentiable physical compositing, and final action-expert representation divergence. Physical compositing accounts for perspective and occlusion across camera views, while representation divergence guides patch optimization using either relative RMS or cosine distance. Experimental results demonstrate strong attack transferability across task suites and policies, together with the ability to selectively disrupt manipulation of an attacker-chosen task object through patch placement.

Beyond Natural-Language Instructions: Temporal Logic as a Task Language for VLA Models with Visual-Logical Fusion 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Robotic tasks combine object relations, logical dependencies, and temporal requirements, which robot planning and industrial control commonly specify in temporal logic (TL). We introduce TL-VLA, a vision--language--action framework that replaces natural-language task instructions with temporal logic, making this spatiotemporal structure explicit. A typed graph encoder and visual--logical fusion condition continuous actions on the task specification and the current observations. To prepare a general vision--language backbone for this interface, we develop TL-aware robot pretraining with separate semantic and structural readouts: a frozen language teacher aligns task semantics, the structural readout encodes TL syntax to support complex, long-horizon tasks, and autoregressive action prediction connects the graph representation to robot behavior. Downstream policies inherit the pretrained backbone and TL encoder and learn new fusion and action modules. On CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D, our TL-aware initializer reaches \textbf{3.800} average chain length and \textbf{62.3\%} five-task success, compared with 3.578 and 54.5\% for a FAST initializer pretrained on the same data and 3.485 average chain length for a Qwen+NL baseline. On LIBERO-10, TL-aware initialization improves success from \textbf{91.2\% to 95.8\%}. On LIBERO-Mem, our policy reaches \textbf{65.0\%} success without observation history, compared with 45.0\% for a Qwen+NL reference. On held-out Panda planning specifications, it reaches \textbf{59.6\%} at Success@256 over three generation seeds, versus 48.4\% for Qwen+TL and 37.0\% for Qwen+NL. These results support explicit logical task conditioning and transferable TL-aware representations for robot action learning.

Escapingthe instruction blackhole of vision-language-action policies via optimal-transport wormholes 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are trained to follow a fixed instruction, yet people change their minds while the robot acts. We identify the instruction black hole: once a VLA policy commits to a task, new instructions are absorbed and the robot completes the old task or freezes. We quantify it with a transmission ratio and an event horizon and prove it is a blind spot of static imitation, which cannot distinguish a policy that listens from a deaf one once the execution state reveals the task. We propose Wormhole, a training-free plug-in that relocates the robot instead of re-instructing it: optimal transport selects an approach-phase state of the new task, a rate-limited traversal carries the robot there, and the unmodified policy takes over. On LIBERO,Wormhole raises committed-switch success of pi to and pi0.5 from at most 1.2% to 93.1%, matching or exceeding scripted retreat-and-restart with one fifth of its action discontinuity.

TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action 2026-11-25
📝 23 pages, 12 figures, 10 tables. Project page: https://tacinno.github.io
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently been equipped with tactile sensing to perceive visually subtle contact events in contact-rich manipulation. However, adding tactile input yields inconsistent gains, and some visuo-tactile VLA models rely little on tactile input, because much of the tactile change, such as the gel deformation during gripper closure, is predictable from visual observations and executed actions. To address this problem, we introduce TacInno, which isolates the contact dynamics that vision and executed actions cannot explain, termed tactile innovation, and uses tactile innovation only as a training target. Specifically, the TacInno tokenizer, conditioned on visual observations and executed actions, compresses the future tactile change into a discrete code, and the policy, TacInno-VLA, learns to predict the code, adding no module for the code at deployment. TacInno-VLA achieves average success rates of 87.5% across six simulation tasks and 70.5% across four real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by 10.7 and 9.0 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies validate the contributions of visual and action conditioning, multi-frame encoding, and auxiliary code prediction. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness of tactile innovation as a supervision signal for contact-rich manipulation.

Aegis-VLA: Reliability-Gated Action Refinement via a Trajectory World Model for Dynamic Object Manipulation 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Dynamic object manipulation requires maintaining spatial and temporal alignment with targets that continue to move during inference and execution. Although future prediction can inform action correction, an accurate forecast does not establish whether modifying an existing action would be beneficial.
