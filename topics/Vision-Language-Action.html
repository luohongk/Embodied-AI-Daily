RobustRLlib: A Unified Library and Benchmark for Robust Reinforcement Learning Algorithms 2026-10-23
📝 Project page, code, and tutorial: https://robustrl-lib.github.io/
Show abstract

Robust reinforcement learning (RL) is critical for reliable real-world deployment, where policies may fail under diverse shifts and disruptions. Existing robust RL algorithms offer a rich set of mechanisms and principles, yet two major barriers limit their broader potential: limited algorithm reusability and an unclear empirical capability boundary. We introduce RobustRLlib, an algorithm-centric library and benchmark that addresses both challenges. It integrates 17 representative robust online, offline, and safe RL algorithms under a unified interface for substantially improved reusability, together with an enhanced evaluation platform spanning diverse real-world shifts and advanced humanoid and vision-language-action tasks. Systematic evaluation over 273 perturbation configurations provides an up-to-date capability map of current robust RL algorithms, revealing where they remain effective and where substantial robustness gaps persist. As an open-source, user-friendly platform, RobustRLlib offers a reproducible and extensible codebase for applying existing methods to new problems and developing new robust RL algorithms.

If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAs 2026-10-23
📝 17 pages, 1 figure, 14 tables
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models perform well on robotic manipulation, yet remain brittle once their own actions carry them into states their training data barely covers. Recovering from such failures usually requires recovery demonstrations, policy retraining, or a separate model at inference time. We introduce Backtrack, a lightweight failure detection and recovery mechanism for frozen VLAs that needs none of these. A small failure prediction head, trained only on outcome-labeled rollouts of the frozen policy, reads the VLA's existing internal representations to estimate whether the current episode will fail. When the predicted failure probability exceeds a threshold, a controller pauses the policy, retraces the arm's recently achieved motion, and hands control back to the unchanged policy so it can retry from an earlier configuration. The head adds only 0.48-0.88% to the policy's parameter count. We evaluate Backtrack on LIBERO with three VLAs spanning two action-generation families: SmolVLA and $π$0.5, which use flow-matching action experts, and the autoregressive $π$0-FAST. On LIBERO-Long, the suite of long-horizon multi-step tasks and the one on which all three policies fail most often, Backtrack reduces each policy's failure rate by 19-48%, raising success by up to 7.4 percentage points; on the three shorter suites, where control success is already 85-99%, its effect is smaller. On MetaWorld, all twelve controller configurations tested with SmolVLA improve on the unassisted policy.

Semantic Anchoring for Robotic Action Representations 2026-10-23
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project page: https://xy02-05.github.io/SemanticMN
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models inherit rich semantic representations from pretrained Vision-Language Models, yet fine-tuning on limited robot demonstrations degrades this structure and undermines generalization. A fundamental question therefore arises: what constitutes a good action representation? Inspired by the mirror neuron theory's insight that observation and execution share an intention-level encoding, we examine whether a robot's action representations preserve the semantic structure captured by pretrained encoders. Systematic probing confirms that this structure erodes during finetuning, and that its quality synchronizes with both task success and out-of-distribution generalization. We further introduce a plug-and-play method that anchors action representations to a semantic manifold while decomposing representations into a shared semantic channel and a private channel, all discarded at inference, leaving the deployed model unchanged. Validated on different VLA backbones across simulation and real-world benchmarks, our method yields up to +18.7% on real-world in-distribution tasks and +21.5% on out-of-distribution generalization.

When Human Corrections Travel: Safety in Federated VLA Adaptation 2026-10-23
📝 Accepted for presentation at SPAIS workshop in CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies can adapt to local human intervention, while federated learning can combine distributed robot experience without pooling raw records. We examine their prospective intersection: sharing adaptations derived from diverse human corrections across tasks, environments, people, and robot embodiments. Corrections differ in modality, safety semantics(urgency, behavioral scope, etc.), and privacy sensitivity. A shared update that improves mean task success may suppress a rare safety intervention or produce an unsafe response on another robot. We argue for three requirements: preserve the context and uncertainty of multimodal corrections when deciding what to share; assess candidate updates in each receiving robot's closed loop before deployment; and evaluate leakage alongside retention of safety-critical evidence. Immediate, legible defensive behavior remains necessary during human interaction. We offer counterexamples and a falsifiable evaluation agenda, not experimental results.

Retiming Robot Demonstrations with a Learned Speed Prior 2026-10-23
Show abstract

The pace of a robot demonstration is often determined by the teleoperator rather than by the task itself. As a result, the same motion can appear at widely different speeds across demonstrations, and policies trained on such data may inherit operator-specific timing along with task behavior. We propose to learn a speed prior directly from large-scale demonstration data. Given a task instruction, a motion path and the images along it, it predicts a distribution over execution speed along the path, from which a chosen quantile defines a consistent clock for retiming each demonstration. Existing methods derive the acceleration signal from the very data they accelerate, so each new task costs another fit, annotation, or pair of predefined rates. A single trained speed prior instead covers every task in a corpus. We fit a speed prior on both robot teleoperation data and handheld demonstrations in which a human arm, rather than a robot arm, moves the gripper, and use it to retime robot demonstrations to the handheld pace before fine-tuning a vision-language-action policy. For execution, we pair the policy with a real-time optimal-control layer that tracks predicted action chunks under robot constraints using the predicted pace. On 120 RoboLab tasks, policies fine-tuned on our learned clocks succeed more often and move with up to half the jerk compared with policies fine-tuned on the original demonstration timing and uniformly accelerated to the same speed. On a real robot, our policy stacks cups in 3.50s, 1.72$\times$ faster than the default policy's 6.02s while succeeding as often, in 29 of 30 trials. Videos and further material are available at https://mllab.snu.ac.kr/projects/learned_speed_prior.

EagleVLA: Towards Onboard Real-Time Robot Control via Foresight-Aligned Asynchronous Inference 2026-10-23
📝 CoRL 2026 (Spotlight,Top 4.4%). Formerly known as "Jetson-PI: Towards Onboard Real-Time Robot Control via Foresight-Aligned Asynchronous Inference"
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have achieved impressive performance on diverse embodied tasks. However, deploying VLA models on low-power onboard devices, such as the Jetson Orin, remains challenging due to their high computational complexity, which leads to substantial inference latency and low control frequency. Asynchronous inference can partially mask this latency by parallelizing action execution and subsequent inference, but it introduces two critical issues: perception-execution misalignment and long reaction time. In this paper, we propose EagleVLA, a method for efficient VLA deployment on onboard devices via Foresight-Aligned Asynchronous Correction. To address misalignment, we train a lightweight future correction module that predicts future environment representations, allowing the action expert to predict actions from the future time step. To reduce reaction time, we introduce confidence-based scheduling optimization that adaptively balances VLM and action expert invocations. We also build a llama.cpp-based inference engine tailored for onboard VLA deployment, with system-level optimizations including CUDA graph reuse, GPU-resident intermediate buffering, and flow unrolling. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EagleVLA achieves 14.71x and 9.20x improvements in control frequency compared with naive PyTorch and vla.cpp on Jetson Orin, while outperforming VLASH by 14.8% in success rate on LIBERO benchmark. The code of our asynchronous algorithm is available on https://github.com/PKU-SEC-Lab/EagleVLA, and our efficient llama.cpp-based inference engine is available on https://github.com/PKU-SEC-Lab/EagleVLA-Edge.

When to Stop Penalizing Actions? Martingale GRPO for VLA Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-23
📝 31 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

A failed vision-language-action (VLA) rollout does not imply that every action contributed to failure. Yet in group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), a single trajectory-level advantage is assigned to all actions in a rollout. Potentially useful recovery attempts can thus be penalized alongside the errors they aim to correct. Ideal stopping conditions for failure feedback are characterized using success-probability and policy-score martingales. At these stops, the removed gradient contribution has zero conditional expectation given the retained information. Truncation therefore preserves the expectation of a reference GRPO group-gradient estimator and can reduce its covariance. One such stop occurs when the conditional probability of success is zero, motivating error and recovery cues for selecting candidate feedback boundaries. Martingale GRPO (MART-GRPO) is introduced with a vision-language model (VLM) for event-guided truncation. The VLM is fine-tuned on our annotated training set using low-rank adaptation (LoRA). The last visible error without observed recovery is localized by the frozen VLM in complete rollout videos. Failure feedback is retained through the selected error interval and removed afterward, while successful-rollout loss terms and original loss normalization are preserved. For retrospective boundaries, changes in expectation and variance are analyzed to assess departures from ideal stopping. Experiments with OpenVLA-OFT on RoboTwin 2.0 show that MART-GRPO improves success rates over full-trajectory GRPO across multiple tasks.

From Tabletop to Walking Bot: Deploying a VLA Model on a Legged Manipulator Robot 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are typically pretrained on broad, diverse datasets and subsequently fine-tuned on a comparatively small dataset tailored to a specific target robot. Such adaptation recipe is now widely established, achieving its most mature results on fixed-base arms due to their static base dynamics and consistent reference frame. Mounting the same arm on a quadruped extends the workspace to the entire floor, but fundamentally changes the embodiment: rather than controlling the arm in isolation, the policy must jointly coordinate base navigation, body posture, and end-effector motion. In this work, we study the practical demands of this embodiment change across data collection, model adaptation, and inference design to achieve closed-loop loco-manipulation on a Unitree Go2 with an AgileX Piper arm. We report the choices that made this real-robot deployment feasible, namely model variant, action horizon, and demonstration conventions, together with a study tailored to a pick-and-place task, separating data coverage from model capacity. We show that expanding demonstration coverage reduces held-out end-effector error by 30%, and incorporating secondary skills (such as pushing rather than grasping) introduces new behavioral modes without degrading baseline performance. Finally, closed-loop real-world experiments show how dataset composition shapes success and failure, validating the pipeline for more complex loco-manipulation.

Do VLAs Know They Will Fail? Interpretable Success and Failure Patterns in Physical AI Systems 2026-10-23

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as powerful general-purpose robot policies, but their successes and failures remain difficult to diagnose and understand. In this work, we show that sparse autoencoder (SAE) representations of OpenVLA activations contain outcome-predictive information and can be used to predict task failure before the robot reaches an unrecoverable state. Moreover, to provide insights into what common behaviors may contribute to these outcomes, we identify individual SAE features whose strong activations are associated with specific stages of success or failure, and uncover recurring behavioral patterns by localizing temporal intervals where these features exhibit significant activation changes. Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success 2026-10-23
📝 28 pages. Project page and code available at https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/
Show abstract

Robot foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, motivating low-cost offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To test this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to robotics and make sensorimotor alignment measurable, offering a low-cost, representation-level view of robot foundation models that closed-loop success rates alone cannot reveal.

Coupling Execution Horizon and Sampling Depth in Flow-Matching VLA Policies 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate expressive continuous trajectories for robotic manipulation, yet balancing real-time responsiveness with execution stability remains a persistent challenge. Standard deployment workflows typically treat numerical solver depth and execution horizon as decoupled choices. The horizon amortizes inference latency, while solver depth is kept high to preserve integration fidelity. In this paper, we reveal a fundamental compute-execution coupling in flow-matching VLAs governed by a systematic sign reversal. While coarse numerical integration degrades precision under rapid replanning, it markedly improves task performance under extended open-loop commitment. Geometrically, numerical truncation contracts lateral action dispersion around an invariant trajectory centroid, serving as an implicit regularizer that suppresses compounding open-loop drift. These findings establish numerical sampling depth as an operational variance regulator, shifting its role from a static precision budget to an active deployment knob in robot learning. Inference-Time Robot Behavior Steering through Physically-Aware Reconfiguration of Task-Structure 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026
Show abstract

A central challenge in deploying robot policies is inference-time policy steering: redirecting a policy to satisfy natural-language preferences unseen in training, without retraining. Existing methods fail in two modes: end-to-end policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) require fine-tuning or expert-level guidance, while neuro-symbolic methods rely on predefined symbols whose edits can yield logically valid but physically infeasible plans. We propose ReStruct, a zero-shot method built on a neural automaton policy that decomposes a visuomotor policy into a high-level state-machine skeleton and a low-level residual controller. A vision-language model (VLM) grounds the preference in the skeleton's learned symbols, refining them if needed, and compiles it into a temporal-logic automaton, whose synchronous product with the skeleton reconfigures the task structure. With the controller frozen, the skeleton's action priors are updated for physically-aware control. In simulation and real-world experiments, ReStruct steers preferences from object-centric specifications to temporal-logic constraints, including novel combinations of demonstrated behaviors that require compositional generalization. Average preference-following success is 70.9% in simulation and 92.5% on a real robot, surpassing baselines like the prompt-steered vision-language-action model π0.5.

Show abstract

Scaling robot learning to long-horizon tasks remains a formidable challenge. While end-to-end policies often lack the structural priors needed for effective long-term reasoning, traditional neuro-symbolic methods rely heavily on hand-crafted symbolic priors. We introduce ENAP (Emergent Neural Automaton Policy), a neuro-symbolic imitation learning framework in which a bi-level policy adaptively emerges from visuomotor demonstrations. Specifically, we employ adaptive clustering and an extension of the L* algorithm to learn the task structure from demonstrations as a Mealy state machine, which serves as an interpretable high-level planner capturing latent task modes. Full-Weight VLA Fine-Tuning Without an fp32 Master Copy Silently Erases Most Updates 2026-10-22
Show abstract

When a vision-language-action (VLA) model is fine-tuned with its trainable weights in bfloat16 (bf16) and no fp32 master copy, AdamW writes each update straight into bf16, silently rounding away any update smaller than 1/512 of a weight. In 9 of 12 open VLA codebases a default or documented recipe trains this way, 4 on full weight matrices, including LeRobot's SmolVLA default and its $π_0$/$π_{0.5}$ guides. In our LIBERO runs without the copy, a probe every 100 steps finds at least 79% of updates to bf16 weights erased. On the Hugging Face Hub, 99% of sampled full-weight SmolVLA fine-tunes store the action expert in bf16, and 84% of recent $π_{0.5}$ fine-tunes with a training configuration set the bf16 flag. On LIBERO-Object, with rules fixed in advance, an fp32 copy raises success over LeRobot's storage from 4.3% to 49.3% for SmolVLA and from 5.1% to 80.9% for $π_{0.5}$ at a ten-times lower learning rate, where both models are undertrained, and from 89.0% to 97.7% for $π_{0.5}$ at its default rate. For SmolVLA at its default rate it adds 10.2 points with open-loop 50-action chunks but only 4.2 when re-planning every 10 actions. In these five comparisons and two more against SmolVLA with every trainable weight in bf16, every run with the copy beats every run without it (p < 0.05); all gaps but the 8.7- and 4.2-point ones clear our 10-point threshold. The fix is to keep the trainable weights in fp32 during training, a one-line change for SmolVLA in LeRobot.

EgoForesight: Effectively Scaling Policy Learning with Noisy Egocentric Actions 2026-10-22
📝 Project page: https://lin-shan.com/EgoForesight
Show abstract

Scaling policy learning requires broad experience and reliable supervision. Robot demonstrations provide high-quality trajectory supervision but are costly to collect. Egocentric videos broaden task and environment coverage, yet motion reconstruction and retargeting introduce noisy action labels. To address this challenge, we introduce EgoForesight, a vision-language-action framework that learns jointly from both sources by reducing the influence of unreliable action labels and exploiting demonstrated outcomes as complementary supervision. A joint vision-action flow couples future visual representation prediction with action generation, unifying reliability-aware action learning with outcome-informed representation transfer. Specifically, reliability-conditioned corruption allocates action supervision across noise levels according to source reliability, while reliability-weighted regression regulates each source's contribution. Complementing this treatment of noisy labels, privileged-future representation distillation draws supervision from demonstrated outcomes. An exponential-moving-average teacher observes the current scene and clean future to encode action-relevant structure from the resulting scene change. Its deeper representations supervise an earlier student layer receiving a corrupted future, providing outcome-informed structure beyond noisy action annotations. Together, these mechanisms support effective scaling with noisy egocentric actions. Over the tested range, EgoForesight exhibits steeper scaling slopes than the baseline, reducing real-world open-loop action MSE by 20.5% and improving RoboCasa few-shot success by 8.1 percentage points at 30,000 hours of pretraining data. After post-training, it also achieves strong absolute performance in both simulated and real-world dexterous manipulation.

Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models 2026-10-22
📝 Project page: https://lyfeng001.github.io/Echo/
Show abstract

Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To learn these signals without fine-grained progress annotations, Echo constructs successful, failed, and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision properties. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

📝 23 pages, 16 figures
