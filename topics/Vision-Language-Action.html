Is Your Robot's Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies 2026-11-10
📝 38 pages
Show abstract

Memory-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) policies are meant to remember what happened. Many of them, however, fill memory by time: they sample frames uniformly across the episode and stamp each frame with its time step. Because time passes even when nothing happens, such a memory may not tell waiting from working. We show that it does not. On the counting tasks of RoboMME, we make the robot briefly pause or fidget its wrist. Such a null event changes nothing the task depends on, yet a released memory VLA counts the interval as work done and stops early. We call this failure phantom progress. It cuts success from 47 to 16 of 50 on one task and from 44 to 10 on the other. When the same frames are withheld from memory, success returns, so the cause lies in memory rather than in the interruption itself. We trace the failure to a temporal shortcut: because demonstrations proceed at a steady pace, elapsed time predicts progress. Indeed, rescaling the time stamps alone makes the policy stop early when the clock runs fast and repeat when it runs slow. Surprisingly, removing the time encoding does not cure the failure, because the frames of a null event still occupy slots of the time-sampled memory. Across RoboMME's released designs, vulnerability follows how memory is indexed. Of the fixes the diagnosis suggests, training with injected pauses does not remove the failure and impairs counting, whereas indexing memory by events recovers half or more of the loss without retraining. A memory meant to remember what happened should count events rather than clock ticks.

One Policy for 100 Household Tasks: Our Solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge 2026-11-10
📝 Code: https://github.com/Robot-Learning-Collective/behavior-1k-solution-2026 ; checkpoint: https://huggingface.co/IliaLarchenko/BEHAVIOR2026VLA ; project page: https://robot-learning-collective.github.io/behavior-1k-2026/
Show abstract

We describe our solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge: 100 long-horizon household tasks in the OmniGibson simulator. We placed 9th on the final leaderboard, the only independent team in the top 10. The core of the solution is a single vision-language-action (VLA) model, one checkpoint for all 100 tasks, built on our 1st-place 2025 solution, which is based on $π_{0.5}$. On the 50 tasks shared with 2025, its Q rises from 0.260 to 0.371; across all 100 tasks it scores 0.392. Our main conclusion from 2025 was that the model was undertrained, so most of the 2026 work went into training the model for longer, optimizing some training parameters, making each training step slightly faster and reducing the gradient noise: a migration from 50 to 100 tasks (semantic stages built from the demonstrations' skill annotations, task and stage embeddings of new tasks warm-started from similar old ones, and a 21-dimensional action space matching the teleoperation controls); variance-reduced multi-sample flow matching with a simple noise-vs-compute model that picks the number of action-expert samples per observation without ablations; and a 200k-step training run, about one pass over the 211M frames of the demonstrations. A cloud evaluation infrastructure keeps a shared record with provenance, episode diagnostics and restorable simulator states, and runs six simulators on one GPU together faster than real time. On top of the learned policy, 14 tasks are routed to a GPT-6 Astra coding agent that controls the robot through observation and motion tools, starting every episode from a frozen copy of its own practice session on training instances. Across these 14 tasks, Astra's mean Q is 0.639, against 0.108 for the learned policy. The public submission included only part of the Astra results and scored 0.446 Q with 27.3% full-task success.

Meituan-Robotics-0: A Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Desktop Manipulation 2026-11-10
📝 25 pages. Project page: https://meituan-robotics.github.io/Meituan-Robotics-0/ ; Code: https://github.com/Meituan-Robotics/Meituan-Robotics-0
Show abstract

We present Meituan-Robotics-0 (MR0), a vision-language-action foundation model and embodied-agent system for stationary-base desktop manipulation. MR0 combines 147 million vision-language samples with 15,060 hours of curated robot trajectories spanning 12 embodiments. A three-stage training recipe aligns multimodal representations with robot behavior through discrete action-token pre-training, transfers these priors to continuous cross-embodiment control through flow-matching mid-training, and adapts the policy through real-world post-training. We introduce Rollback, which pairs replaced policy segments with human corrections to provide positive and negative supervision for CFG-DAgger without value learning or advantage labeling. A unified end-effector interface supports heterogeneous embodiments, while asynchronous action-chunk merging maintains smooth execution under variable inference latency. An event-driven agent maintains persistent task events and reassesses plans when progress or feasibility changes, enabling cross-task retrieval and conditional task resumption. MR0 achieves 93.6% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and ranks fifth in the RoboDojo VLA track. Under matched HG-DAgger post-training, MR0 achieves higher average success rate and progress score than $π_{0.5}$ across three real-robot tasks. On Ethernet insertion, CFG-DAgger trained with Rollback data reaches 70% success and a progress score of 0.90. Qualitative agent deployments demonstrate cross-task event retrieval and recovery from temporarily unsatisfied task conditions.

ODEAction: From Predicting Chunks to Evolving Actions 2026-11-10
📝 23 pages, 5 figures, including appendix. Technical report
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models typically generate actions in chunks, dividing continuous robot execution into separately predicted action chunks, which can introduce discontinuities at chunk boundaries. We propose ODEAction, a chunk-free action generation paradigm that replaces action chunk prediction with Continuous Action Evolution. We use an ordinary differential equation (ODE) to describe the continuous evolution of actions, and define the vector field of this ODE as the Action Transition Field to characterize how actions evolve with respect to evolution time. During inference, the model takes the current action as the initial condition and numerically solves the ODE defined by the learned Action Transition Field, thereby recursively generating subsequent actions with explicit dependencies. ODEAction is implemented as a modular action head that can be integrated with different vision-language backbones. Experiments on LIBERO, EBench, RoboTwin, and real-robot tasks show that, compared with action-chunk-based baselines, ODEAction improves both task performance and trajectory quality, with particularly pronounced gains on fine manipulation tasks.

DexSeed: Expanding Human Hand-Object Interactions into Robot Demonstrations for Dexterous Policy Learning 2026-11-10
📝 Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/DexSeed/
Show abstract

We study how to generate diverse, physically executable robot demonstrations for dexterous vision-language-action (VLA) learning. Existing demonstration-generation methods typically rely on costly teleoperated examples. Meanwhile, human hand--object datasets provide a reusable source, but existing transfer methods mainly reproduce given interactions. Turning these interactions into VLA training data further requires coverage of different objects and manipulation locations, together with executable arm and finger actions, and visual observations. To achieve this, we present DexSeed, a framework for low-cost generation of dexterous robot data from human hand--object interactions. Our key idea is to expand human sequences while preserving their grasps and realizing the resulting motions under arm-tracking dynamics in physics simulation. Specifically, we organize source sequences by grasp mode with shared language descriptions. InteractionMesh-based retargeting and arm trajectory augmentation adapt these sequences to the robot hand, different object geometries, and pick-and-place locations while preserving local hand--object relationships. An RL finger-control policy produces executable rollouts, while the arm tracks precomputed trajectories. Rendered observations and recorded actions from successful rollouts are paired with grasp descriptions to fine-tune a VLA. Experiments show improved contact fidelity in retargeting and more reliable whole-arm execution than the evaluated baselines. Simulation evaluation and real-world deployment further demonstrate that a VLA fine-tuned solely on the generated demonstrations can execute language-specified grasps. Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/DexSeed/.

3A-VLA: Abstraction-Aligned Action Learning for Vision-Language Agents in 3D Game Worlds 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Despite advances in vision-language-action (VLA) models, they remain limited in highly dynamic game settings such as 3D open worlds and competitive player versus player (PvP) settings, where agents must efficiently extract sparse, actionable signals from dense visual input while integrating multimodal cues to track off-screen high-value targets in real time. To address this, we introduce 3A-VLA (Abstraction-Aligned Action VLA), a framework that grounds action learning in explicit intention and environment abstractions rather than superficial pattern matching. We introduce dual task-agnostic abstractions: the intention abstraction (IA), which condenses verbose instructions and reasoning into explicit semantic primitives, and the environment semantics abstraction (ESA), which structures dense visual streams into a spatial-functional affordance representation to guide grounded actions. We further propose an abstraction alignment reweighting (AAR) module that adaptively reweights the action imitation loss. A continuous intention-environment alignment signal is used to emphasize reliable action supervision when abstractions agree and reduce the influence of ambiguous demonstrations when they diverge, thereby learning a policy that balances fine-grained control and high-level reasoning without the need for manual rules. Extensive experiments show that 3A-VLA performs competitively among the evaluated baselines in both open-world (Minecraft) and competitive PvP (Game for Peace) settings. It also demonstrates zero-shot combat transfer from Game for Peace to high-fidelity games, including Valorant, CS2, GTA V, Elden Ring, and Mount & Blade.

XS-VLA: Teaching Tiny Vision-Language-Action Models with Spatial Supervision and Demonstration Conditioning 2026-11-10
📝 Preprint. Project page: https://999tom.github.io/xs-vla/

How can richer training supervision improve robot control while keeping the deployed policy compact? We present XS-VLA, a staged training framework using teacher-derived spatial labels and demonstration-conditioned action learning. Coarse-Grained Spatial Distillation (CSD) initializes the backbone through an auxiliary region-label task. Latent Flow Matching (LFM) then conditions an action-space velocity field on a demonstration latent, using KL regularization while jointly optimizing the backbone and action modules. The deployed policy contains 243.99M parameters and operates without the teacher or posterior encoder. XS-VLA achieves 90.25% average LIBERO success in each of two training seeds, compared with 86.00% for a SmolVLA-0.25B base trained under our settings. Ablations examine both training stages through matched image pretraining and Huber/MSE controls. On three Mobile ALOHA tasks, average strict success increases from 21.7% to 65.0%. These results demonstrate the control utility of auxiliary representation initialization and regularized demonstration-conditioned flow learning for compact VLA policies

How Many Demonstrations Does a VLA Need? On the Sample Efficiency of VLA Finetuning 2026-11-10
📝 14 pages, 8 figures, CoRL Workshop
Show abstract

Pretrained vision language action (VLA) models show strong zero-shot performance but they are rarely deployed directly and are often first post-trained on demonstrations from the target setting. Yet VLAs are typically compared by zero-shot or full-data fine-tuned success which reveals little about the data and compute required for downstream adaptation. We study adaptation efficiency for six VLAs ($π_0$, $π_{0.5}$, and GR00T N1-N1.7) on four LIBERO suites, varying both demonstrations and training steps. This reveals differences hidden by full-data fine-tuning: models which are within two points on the full LIBERO-Spatial dataset differ by up to 3$\times$ in success with 50 demonstrations. $π_{0.5}$ needs 2-5$\times$ fewer demonstrations than $π_0$ and is the most sample-efficient model at every budget, with the largest gap on the long-horizon suite. Within GR00T, N1.5 adapts more efficiently than N1.6 and N1.7 on three of four suites, despite their stronger zero-shot performance. We also study one pretraining factor behind these differences by freezing the VLM during finetuning: this severely degrades the $π$ models whose VLM is jointly trained with the action expert during pretraining, but barely affects GR00T models.

$A^3$-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS2026
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) enable robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process visual observations and language instructions. However, the action prediction objective itself lacks fine-grained task-specific perceptual supervision, making it prone to relying on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts, leading attention away from target objects and key interaction areas, thereby limiting its operational performance and generalization ability. To address this, we propose Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation ($A^3$-VLA), a framework that derives task-specific visual guidance from the manipulation process itself. Specifically, $A^3$-VLA first generates heuristic candidate regions for target objects and the robot from interaction-induced visual changes, and refines them with a visual segmentation model offline. These regions serve as explicit perceptual guidance through two complementary objectives: an attention allocation loss that encourages sufficient focus on task-critical regions when language or robot state tokens query the visual input, and a contrastive learning loss that aligns the representations of candidate target regions with language instructions while separating them from task-irrelevant regions. Extensive experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that $A^3$-VLA consistently improves multiple VLA backbones, with an average success rate gain of 6.5% - 25.2% across benchmarks.

Control-Aware Command Generation for Asynchronous Control and Action Inference with Vision-Based Policies towards Fast Execution 2026-11-10
📝 8th Robot Learning Workshop: Is Physical AI Going Zero-Shot? at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-based control policies, including vision-language-action models, have enabled robots to perform increasingly complex tasks. However, their execution speed is often limited by the high computational cost of action inference. In this work, we investigate what can be achieved at the control level without modifying the underlying policy learning process. We introduce an asynchronous inference and control framework that enables fast task execution while maintaining smooth and stable robot motions. By formulating the generation of continuous target positions from discontinuous model predictions as a tracking control problem, our method makes asynchronous inference practical for real robots. We further accelerate execution through adaptive action skipping based on the variance of predicted positions. Experiments with Action Chunking Transformer and OpenVLA-OFT on a bimanual robot demonstrate substantial speedups while preserving high task success rates.

MAPLE: Latent Multi-Agent Play for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are effective as end-to-end motion planners, but can be brittle when evaluated in closed-loop settings due to being trained under traditional imitation learning framework. Existing closed-loop supervision approaches lack scalability and fail to completely model a reactive environment. We propose MAPLE, a novel framework for reactive, multi-agent rollout of a dynamic driving scenario in the latent space of the VLA model. The ego vehicle and nearby traffic agents are independently controlled over multi-step horizons, while being reactive to other agents in the scene, enabling closed-loop training. MAPLE consists of two training stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on the latent rollouts based on ground-truth trajectories, followed by (2) reinforcement learning with global and agent -specific rewards that encourage safety, progress, and interaction realism. We further propose diversity rewards that encourage the model to generate planning behaviors that may not be present in logged driving data. Notably, our closed-loop training framework is scalable and does not require external simulators, which can be computationally expensive to run and have limited visual fidelity to the real-world. MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art driving performance on Bench2Drive and demonstrates scalable, closed-loop multi-agent play for robust E2E autonomous driving systems.

Generative Scenario Rollouts for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are emerging as highly effective planning models for end-to-end autonomous driving systems. However, current works mostly rely on imitation learning from sparse trajectory annotations and underutilize their potential as generative models. We propose Generative Scenario Rollouts (GeRo), a plug-and-play framework for VLA models that jointly performs planning and generation of language-grounded future traffic scenes through an autoregressive rollout strategy. First, a VLA model is trained to encode ego vehicle and agent dynamics into latent tokens under supervision from planning, motion, and language tasks, facilitating text-aligned generation. Next, GeRo performs language-conditioned autoregressive generation. Given multi-view images, a scenario description, and ego-action questions, it generates future latent tokens and textual responses to guide long-horizon rollouts. A rollout-consistency loss stabilizes predictions using ground truth or pseudo-labels, mitigating drift and preserving text-action alignment. This design enables GeRo to perform temporally consistent, language-grounded rollouts that support long-horizon reasoning and multi-agent planning. On Bench2Drive, GeRo improves driving score and success rate by +15.7 and +26.2, respectively. By integrating reinforcement learning with generative rollouts, GeRo achieves state-of-the-art closed-loop and open-loop performance, demonstrating strong zero-shot robustness. These results highlight the promise of generative, language-conditioned reasoning as a foundation for safer and more interpretable end-to-end autonomous driving.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models now exceed 95% success on the LIBERO simulation benchmark, yet it remains unclear how much of this performance requires generalization beyond the training demonstrations. In this work, we show that retrieving nearby demonstrations and reusing their action chunks achieves a success rate of 91.7 % on LIBERO without policy training. Under LIBERO-Plus perturbations, retrieval shows the smallest drop of all evaluated policies. Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Tactile sensing is an indispensable modality for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, a capability that hints at the ultimate frontiers of embodied intelligence. However, commercially available tactile sensors are still at a relatively early stage of development, with each type of sensor offering irreplaceable strengths alongside its own drawbacks. A key challenge is thus to free tactile understanding from sensor-specific physics, so that embodied models can effectively leverage heterogeneous tactile data. To this end, we develop LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model characterized by the following properties. VLA Adaptation with Real and Simulated Data: A Case Study on Robotic Chess 2026-11-09
📝 CoRL 2026 Workshop "Pretrain to Adapt: What Makes a Pretrained Policy Adaptable?". 8 pages main text, 15 pages total, 15 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. In this task, an SO-101 arm moves a chess king between two squares that are given only by their names, such as e4 and a5. We fine-tune $π_{0.5}$ with various dataset sizes and update budgets, using teleoperated demonstrations as well as simulated demonstrations from a randomized scripted expert that imitates the variability of human demonstrations. In simulation, the success rate scales with both data and updates. Importantly, we find that a short learning-rate cool-down adds up to 31 points and is necessary for comparing checkpoints, and that validation loss and action error are unreliable for model selection. On the real arm, we fine-tune on up to 256 teleoperated demonstrations and evaluate ten policies in 25 trials each. Partial results indicate that pretraining on the simulated demonstrations helps substantially, most clearly when real data is scarce. We also present a negative result, in which training on the policy's own rollouts with filtered behavior cloning, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, corrections from an automatic coach, or residual reinforcement learning did not yield a measurable improvement over plain behavior cloning in our setting. We release the simulator, the expert and the real demonstrations.

Be My Eye: Learning to See Beyond a Robot's Own Perspective 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Robots operating in the real world are inherently subject to partial observability, particularly in multi-robot collaboration, where individual observations may be insufficient for effective task execution. A key challenge is leveraging complementary observations from partner robots to support collaborative decision-making. We argue that information sharing should go beyond passively transmitting raw observations or generic visual features. Instead, robots should selectively encode task-relevant information according to task objectives and execution progress. To this end, we propose a task-oriented active context sharing framework that transforms robot observations into compact representations, preserving critical information under constrained communication budgets. The framework adopts a two-stage training paradigm. In the first stage, onboard observations and task instructions are jointly encoded into compact context representations. Task-oriented future prediction and soft task-progress correspondence learning guide this collaboration-agnostic compression, preserving information about task objectives and execution progress. In the second stage, the learned representations are integrated into a pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, enabling bidirectional context exchange. Each robot combines complementary partner information with local observations to guide action generation. Action supervision jointly refines the context representations and policy, aligning information sharing with collaborative decision-making. Controlled experiments demonstrate substantial performance gains under constrained communication budgets, while three real-world experiments validate the framework in complex dual-robot tasks. These results highlight a shift from passive visual transmission toward active, task-oriented context sharing that directly supports collaborative decision-making.

VLA-MOPD: On-Policy Distillation for Multi-Task VLA Policy Consolidation 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Physical intelligence requires embodied agents to ground language in observations and execute actions while retaining useful skills. Vision-language-action (VLA) policies offer a shared interface, but task-specific post-training often produces separate specialists. Consolidation is difficult: mixed-task reinforcement learning provides sparse trajectory-level credit, while offline distillation may miss student-visited observations and endpoint distillation does not supervise intermediate action-generation states. We introduce VLA Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (VLA-MOPD), a protocol for consolidating flow-matching VLA specialists. After acquiring task or suite experts, a shared student collects robot rollouts and queries the instruction-routed frozen teacher at each visited observation and intermediate ordinary differential equation (ODE) state. Detached per-flow-step transition targets supervise valid action chunks through a masked local matching loss; an optional reference-policy term supports retention on other tasks. Starting from one trajectory per LIBERO task, VLA-MOPD raises mean success-once from 75.4% to 98.9%, compared with 98.15% for endpoint action distillation. It improves mean RoboTwin 2.0 success from 73.0% to 89.3%; on a Piper robot, two consolidation rounds improve average success from 70.0% to 90.0%. In a near-matched Spatial-adaptation study, reference matching limits the Object suite decrease to 3.0 points, versus 13.2 without the reference term.

Robo-MLT: Dual-System Robotic Manipulation for Long-Horizon Medical Laboratory Tasks 2026-11-09
📝 12 pages,7 figures,5 tables,1 algorithm
Show abstract

Reliable medical laboratory automation requires robots to complete entire workflows in protocol order while handling changes during execution. A robot may execute each motion correctly yet fail the workflow by confusing visually similar stages or stalling after human intervention. We present Robo-MLT, a dual-system framework that couples explicit workflow monitoring with learned action generation. A vision--language planner composes an ordered atomic-skill sequence from the protocol and the initial workspace observation. A multiframe execution monitor detects when the active skill has completed or become infeasible, then advances the workflow or triggers replanning. A skill-conditioned vision--language--action policy generates the manipulation trajectories, and an asynchronous runtime coordinates it with the monitor. We evaluate Robo-MLT against four policy baselines and a VLM-based monitor baseline on blood gas analysis and blood sample tube sorting, with 50 nominal configurations per method and 20 sample-removal configurations. Robo-MLT achieves task success rates of 82\% and 70\%, exceeding $π_{0.5}$ by 30 and 22 percentage points, respectively. After an operator removes the sample being handled, the system revises the remaining plan and continues processing the other samples. These results indicate that, under the evaluated conditions, explicit workflow monitoring makes long-horizon laboratory manipulation more reliable and recoverable.

APS-VLA: Action-Guided and Future-Aware Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-09
📝 11 pages, 6 figures, 6 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have demonstrated strong generalization in robotic manipulation, yet processing dense visual tokens often incurs high inference latency. Visual token pruning offers a practical way to reduce this cost, but identifying a compact set of tokens that preserves reliable action generation remains challenging. Existing methods largely rely on static or immediate heuristic cues to estimate token importance. We introduce APS-VLA, a framework that learns visual token selection from robot demonstrations while keeping the pretrained VLA backbone frozen. A lightweight Decision Influence Estimator scores tokens conditioned on current visual observations, language instructions, and robot proprioception. Its training combines two complementary signals: action supervision matches both the full-input policy predictions and demonstrated actions, while an auxiliary visual transition objective uses demonstrated actions to predict subsequent visual features from the selected representation. At deployment, the estimator performs a single hard selection and packing step before the policy's native action-generation procedure; the auxiliary predictor is removed. Experiments with OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO and with $π_{0.5}$ and XR-1 on RoboCasa365 demonstrate inference acceleration with modest changes in task success. Across five real-world tasks on two dual-arm platforms, APS-VLA reduces mean policy inference latency from 137.25 to 93.25 ms, achieving a $1.47\times$ speedup with an average success rate of 61%, compared with 60% for the unpruned baseline. Together, these results support APS-VLA as a modular, plug-in framework for efficient robotic control across VLA architectures, preserving the underlying policy parameters and native action-generation procedure.

Motion Belief Action: From a Visual Scene Map to Physical Decisions 2026-11-09
Show abstract

A robot that works among people must judge what it cannot see: whether an object is still where it was last seen, and whether a pull on a door or drawer is worth its risk. Maps that track object change hold nothing about force, maps of physical properties answer a risk question at a fixed risk level, and vision-language-action models keep no explicit estimate of hidden physics. We propose Motion Belief Action (MBA), a decision layer on the scene map. From how often people move an object and how much force it needs, how much load it holds and whether it is blocked, MBA chooses under a force limit to look again, nudge, open, brace or decline, with its risk set by the task's costs. On real activity in 9 kitchens, it predicts whether an object is still in place better than FreMEn and Perpetua, and decides when to look again at lower cost than these and the persistence filter, because about a quarter of placements end in a pick-up within 30 s. On real pulls of doors and drawers in 17 locations, made by a person with a force-sensing gripper, the map's force estimates decide better than a part-type average. Setting the risk from the costs also beats a published risk rule at its fixed level. In simulation, with the force limit at the training parts' median force, MBA raises SmolVLA's and Octo's scores by declining pulls; most declined door pulls would fail, but on drawers more would have opened, and a part-type average gets about half of this gain. In both, a published risk rule set to the risk these costs imply is MBA's rule for a single pull and decides as well in the main settings. A link between opening and holding force, a probing nudge and an action prior for camera tracking do not help in the main settings, and over the real-pull sweep the nudge costs a little.

Diffusion driving vision-language-action models repeatedly execute the backbone at every denoising stage, incurring substantial inference cost, while it remains unclear whether all scenes require the same decoding computation.
