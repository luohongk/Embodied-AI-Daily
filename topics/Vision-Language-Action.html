La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling 2026-11-20
📝 31 pages, 6 figures. v2: full-RoboTwin results with scaling to 2.1B parameters, leave-one-out ablation, revised figures and text. Code: https://github.com/mt-clemente/lajepa
Show abstract

World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B. Its S and B policies run two to five times faster than Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM while outperforming both on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, and La-JEPA outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to $π_{0.5}$. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction improves the policy even when skipped at inference. Tailoring the policy's design, including its latent space, to control can thus matter more than the knowledge transferred from a pretrained generative backbone, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Subchunk Rollouts in Latent World Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can improve pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies through imagined experiences. However, existing VLA-MBRL methods primarily rely on large-scale image or video generation world models, incurring large computational overhead. Latent world models offer a more efficient alternative but face two challenges: compounding errors during recursive prediction and incompatibility with VLAs' visual input interfaces. In this work, we propose LS-MBPO, a lightweight residual MBRL framework that leverages latent world models to improve upon pretrained VLAs. LS-MBPO introduces two key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Forcing, which combines dense causal supervision with autoregressive feedback to mitigate compounding prediction errors; and (2) Latent Subchunk Rollouts, which leverage action chunks from a frozen VLA policy and a latent residual policy for successive subchunk refinement, bridging the gap between latent imagination and VLA policy execution. We further introduce a Progress-Conditioned Branch-Start Curriculum that progressively shifts rollout starting points from later to earlier task stages, enabling effective policy learning from short imagined rollouts. Extensive experiments on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and a real-world ALOHA robot show that LS-MBPO achieves superior task performance and training efficiency compared to existing methods.

LexiconVLA: Learning Reusable Atomic Action Codebooks for Manipulation 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle to reuse recurring atomic actions across manipulation tasks. Our diagnostic study reveals that reliable task completion does not imply consistent execution of constituent atomic actions across task contexts. We present LexiconVLA, a retrievable atomic-action lexicon for cross-task reuse. Global and detail codebooks capture shared interaction structure and fine-grained execution variation respectively, preserving both reusable patterns and execution details. Visual-Atomic Action Alignment couples trajectory reconstruction from visual state changes with visual outcome prediction from action codes, grounding the lexicon in motion and its effects. We learn these codebooks with trajectory reconstruction and visual alignment on our AtomAction Dataset of 57,803 segments from 69 tasks. A planner and scene-aware adapter translate new goals into code-conditioned subtasks for a shared policy, without skill-specific experts or deployment-time parameter updates. Across five policy backbones on 26 RLBench tasks, LexiconVLA largely maintains performance on 18 seen tasks while improving success on 8 tasks held out from policy training. With BridgeVLA, unseen-task success rises from 16.67% to 34.17% (+17.50 percentage points), and overall success reaches 71.08%, the highest among methods with reported results. Real-robot experiments demonstrate stepwise execution and failure recovery.

Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

2D or 3D? Rethinking Token Pruning in 3D VLAs with Cross-Modal Correlation Awareness 2026-11-20
Show abstract

3D vision-language-action (VLA) models process both image and point cloud tokens at each control step, increasing the cost of action prediction. Token pruning can reduce this cost, but a token's contribution depends on which other tokens remain. We present 2Dor3D, a framework that jointly selects which 2D and 3D tokens to retain under a fixed token budget. Our analysis shows that either modality can compensate for the other within a patch, that neighboring correlations help explain differences in pruning risk, and that shared tokens couple pruning decisions across patches. In one box-closing observation, two patches with nearly identical interaction scores differed eightfold in the action deviation caused by removing their image token. Guided by these findings, 2Dor3D transfers cross-modal prediction residuals to retained tokens, uses neighborhood correlation entropy to explore more retention choices at local outliers, and updates pruning costs after each proposed exchange between coupled regions. A fused GPU kernel packs the selected tokens into a shorter sequence for subsequent Transformer layers. Across RLBench and LIBERO, 2Dor3D speeds up end-to-end inference by $2.06$--$2.10\times$ on MLA and $1.86$--$1.91\times$ on FiS-VLA, with task success rates 0.5--0.6 percentage points below the corresponding unpruned models.

SCAP: Stochastic Context for Action Expert Pretraining 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Single-modality action-expert pretraining controls competition from visual shortcuts before vision--language--action (VLA) adaptation. Yet single-modality pretraining can impair the vision--language interactions needed for subsequent joint prediction. We construct a diagnostic model in which VA or LA pretraining can disable an initially active pathway for joint vision--language modeling. In this model, subsequent training with complete visual and language inputs cannot restore the pathway, even with unlimited adaptation. We therefore introduce Stochastic Context for Action Expert Pretraining (SCAP), a new action-expert pretraining stage that precedes full-input training. During this stage, visual and text tokens are randomly masked before entering the VLM. In this unified diagnostic model, SCAP can preserve the joint vision--language modeling pathway during pretraining and subsequent full-input training. Compared with direct full-input training, it can also reduce reliance on visual shortcuts and increase reliance on language instructions. SCAP consistently improves performance across four different VLA architectures. In real-robot experiments, it achieves 76.67% average success across three instruction-conditioned manipulation tasks, outperforming full-input pretraining by 25 percentage points.

Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.

Learning from Incomplete Actions: Interval Motion Supervision for Vision-Language-Action Policies 2026-11-20
📝 17 pages, including supplementary material
Show abstract

Visual labeling of egocentric demonstrations often leaves contiguous gaps in action supervision. Reliable poses around a gap still provide its net translation and rotation. We use this information to train vision-language-action policies while preserving ordinary flow matching on reliable local actions. A composition objective constrains the aggregate motion of missing-action estimates, combining multiple fixed windows when a gap exceeds the policy horizon. A shared interval branch learns from reliable endpoints and supplies the composition target. On LIBERO with 20% of demonstrations and 70.5% missing actions, the main method achieves 87.9% mean success over three training runs. In one paired comparison with matched observation exposure and training cost, its advantage over masking is 10.5%. On Ego-informed RoboTwin gaps, success is 45.4%, versus 40.3% for masking. On five real-robot tasks, mean success is 59.6%, versus 54.8% for interval flow matching alone and 48.8% for masking.

BeyondEgo: Learning to Act Beyond Egocentric Perception via Humanoid Ego-Exo Foresight 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires coordinating whole-body motion to perform manipulation tasks in complex environments. Yet egocentric vision-language-action (VLA) policies can miss critical body--scene relationships, and action imitation alone provides no explicit supervision for future scene evolution. We propose BeyondEgo to extend VLA perception in space and time. Spatially, we encode a synchronized exocentric view with frozen DINOv3 and concatenate the resulting tokens with egocentric VLM tokens to condition the action expert. Temporally, we use future observations from both views as privileged supervision for a residual adapter on task-conditioned VLM features. A foresight expert regresses DINOv3 representations of both views 0.6s ahead. At deployment, the foresight expert is discarded and the 4.2M-parameter adapter is retained. We also introduce HELM, to our knowledge, the first closed-loop humanoid loco-manipulation benchmark with synchronized egocentric and exocentric views across five whole-body tasks.On HELM, BeyondEgo improves success from 48.0% to 92.0% in distribution and from 23.5% to 65.0% on 200 held-out scenes with layout, asset, material, and lighting shifts. Ablations show that dual-view DINOv3 foresight yields the best transfer results.

FoRe: A Force-Guided Framework for Autonomous Grasp Recovery in Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have demonstrated strong performance in grasp-based manipulation, yet reliable recovery from local grasping failures remains challenging. Specifically, in pick-and-place tasks, policies may continue transport after a failed grasp instead of retrying, potentially reflecting overreliance on proprioceptive cues for stage progression and limited retry supervision. Existing recovery approaches typically rely on deployment-time guidance or demonstration augmentation. We introduce Force-guided Recovery (FoRe), a framework for autonomous recovery from local grasping failures. Using force feedback, FoRe partitions demonstrations into approach-grasp and transport-placement stages to train separate policies. To supplement limited retry supervision, FoRe then initializes a dedicated retry policy from the approach-grasp policy and refines it through local reinforcement learning (RL) from post-failure states. To avoid inappropriate stage progression, a controller combines force feedback with grasp-command status at inference to coordinate the stage policies, detect grasp failures, and invoke the retry policy. We also construct LIBERO-SUCTION, a force-augmented suction manipulation benchmark adapted from LIBERO, with complete demonstration trajectories for policy training. FoRe consistently improves task success rates for VLA-Adapter and $π_{0.5}$ on LIBERO-SUCTION, LIBERO, and real-world tasks. On LIBERO-SUCTION, average absolute gains are 18.4% and 6.5%, respectively. The corresponding real-world absolute gains are 25.0% and 21.7%. FoRe also remains effective with only one demonstration per task on both simulation benchmarks.

RoboDyna: A Benchmark for Evaluating Robotic Manipulation under Physically Grounded Dynamics 2026-11-20
📝 8 pages, 9 figures, 2 tables. Project page: https://robodyna.github.io/robodyna/
Show abstract

Object manipulation in the dynamic real world requires understanding how the environment functions and evolves, and acting accordingly. Existing benchmarks, however, do not adequately address these needs. Physics-oriented benchmarks evaluate predictive fidelity but not closed-loop action. Dynamic robotic manipulation benchmarks often use simplified, physically implausible object motions, limiting evaluation of reasoning and action under realistic dynamics. Evaluation is also typically synchronized, freezing the environment during inference and making computation time irrelevant to task outcomes. To this end, we propose RoboDyna, a benchmark for evaluating the physical understanding and action capabilities of manipulation policies in dynamic scenes. RoboDyna comprises 20 conceptual dynamic tasks spanning five capability categories, with simple objects and minimal scene clutter, under a two-factor difficulty design, and 10 household tasks that instantiate the same capabilities in visually and semantically richer simulated real-world environments. RoboDyna provides 3,500 expert demonstrations, paired synchronous and asynchronous evaluation protocols, metrics for task success and partial completion, and interactive tools for human baseline evaluation. Evaluating vision-language-action models and a world-action model across 5.4K episodes, we find that all policies achieve success rates below 20%, revealing substantial limitations in dynamic manipulation. The world-action model captures simple physical dynamics but struggles with complex interactions, and its prediction fidelity does not consistently align with task success. Sync-async comparisons show that inference latency flips 5-12% of episode outcomes while changing average success rates by at most 3%. Human baselines substantially outperform all policies under matched conditions. Project page: https://robodyna.github.io/robodyna/

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) are a new class of machine learning model for generalizable robot control. While they enable robots to perform complex tasks in unseen environments, serving VLAs leaves hardware idle between inference passes and under-utilized during inference. FORTH: Grounding Tactile VLA Execution via Forward-Only Proxy-Control Pairing 2026-11-19
📝 The code and models are open-sourced at https://github.com/Jordanlllll/FORTH
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are widely credited with force control, operating on the unverified assumption that proxy loss minimization implies physical force grounding. In this work, we demonstrate that this assumption breaks down at three core deployment levels: frozen visual backbones discard contact geometry, downstream losses mask token-level gradient alignment, and discrete indicators trigger optimization collapse. To address these disconnections, we present FORTH, a forward-only measurement harness and execution framework that explicitly pairs the optimization objective at each level with its physical control observable. FORTH incorporates three grounded pillars: a layer-local, forward-only tactile encoder that preserves spatial contact geometry without backpropagation, a token-parallel zeroth-order adapter enabling activation-space updates, and SlipGuard, a continuous tactile verification gate that fires on incipient slip. On an elasto-plastic structural shell, FORTH's closed-loop gate peaks at 29 times lower grip force than a monotone reflex ramp, preventing structural crush where the baseline leaves permanent deformation. Furthermore, FORTH matches state-of-the-art execution performance with a 97.6% average success rate across three evaluated LIBERO suites, while reducing peak activation memory by 7.12 times compared to a backpropagated step on the same backbone. Enhanced perception enables deeper physical intelligence.

PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates. Project page is available at: https://kl-research.github.io/pgx

Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment 2026-11-19
📝 44 pages, 10 figures, 11 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under four years the field has grown from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. VLA research joins robotics, computer vision and language modelling, so this survey is written for researchers and practitioners from each of these fields. It reviews 89 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. Seven design axes follow a VLA's life cycle: model design (inputs and memory, architecture, action output, intermediate predictions), training (pretraining data, post-training) and deployment (real-time execution). We compare 22 models on these axes, with their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge. (i) Training data has broadened beyond teleoperated robot data (about 10,000 hours for $π_0$) to community-collected episodes and egocentric human video (20,000 hours for GR00T N1.7). (ii) Model sizes have split into two groups: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters; a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs. (iii) Generalist VLAs handling both manipulation and navigation are emerging, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions, and review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail after small scene changes. A practitioner's guide gives a default for each design decision, and a glossary supports readers new to the field.

PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced rapidly in language-conditioned robotic manipulation, yet existing benchmarks mainly evaluate gripper--object contact and largely ignore how objects interact with the environment through gravity, friction, inertia, and mass distribution. As a result, it remains unclear whether current policies truly understand physics or merely interpolate from their training data. We address this gap through the lens of dynamic extrinsic dexterity and introduce PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark in which a robot must infer an object's mass, center of mass, and friction to send it to a destination beyond its kinematic workspace. PhyDEX contains 35K trajectories (143 hours) across nine tasks and 100 scenes, covering uniform, irregular, and dynamic-mass objects with static, multiple, and moving destinations. All data are generated by a scalable, fully automatic pipeline that combines grasp generation, motion planning, and closed-loop throw optimization under randomized physical properties. We evaluate three representative VLAs, GR00T N1.7, DynamicVLA, and ForceVLA, and find that all achieve success rates below 3\%. Our analysis reveals two key bottlenecks: limited robustness of pretrained models, which causes most failures before the throw, and physical blindness, as providing ground-truth physical properties roughly doubles success. Experiments on a humanoid robot and in the real world show the same failure patterns. We hope PhyDEX will drive the development of physically grounded robot policies.

Composable Driving Style for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
📝 13 pages, including appendix
Show abstract

End-to-end driving policies can achieve strong task performance while offering limited control over their driving style. Driving style describes how a vehicle accelerates, brakes, and maintains distance. Policies trained without explicit preference conditioning lack a direct mechanism for adjusting these behaviors at inference. We propose YOUDrive, a vision--language--action planner for continuous and composable driving-style control. Each teacher's behavior is distilled into a task vector, a learned parameter update that induces a style direction in the planner's representation. These directions can be scaled and combined at inference without retraining the backbone. Conditioned on these directions, a flow-matching decoder models a multimodal trajectory distribution and generates kinematically feasible motion alternatives. We introduce the Style Alignment Score (SAS) to describe human-referenced kinematic behavior after safety and task gating. On NAVSIM navtest, a single coefficient monotonically controls assertiveness across six teacher-derived personas, while PDMS remains above 0.90 across the tested individual and blended configurations, reaching 0.954. Further analyses reveal systematic policy-specific kinematics after controlling for scene context, whereas the evaluated discrete style labels provide limited additional explanatory power. Code, checkpoints, and the metric will be released.

GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives 2026-11-19
📝 64 pages, including supplementary material. Project page: https://groundingpi.github.io/ Code: https://github.com/groundingpi/GroundingPI Model: https://huggingface.co/GroundingPI/GroundingPI
Show abstract

Precise grounding matters. It specifies which object is the target and where that object is, even in clutter and for tiny objects, and it has to be fast enough for closed-loop control. Yet vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) take perception from general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones, which still fail in these settings. We introduce GroundingPI, a 4B grounding foundation model that generates points and boxes as quantized coordinates in a shared vocabulary. Training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, using supervision from public datasets and dedicated data engines. Against 44 baselines across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities, GroundingPI establishes a new state of the art, averaging 73.68%, above the larger GPT-6 Astra (71.54%). As a downstream visual backbone, GroundingPI improves performance on robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. Across RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboCasa-GR1, it outperforms all evaluated backbones in all four out-of-distribution settings, with relative gains of up to 24.8% over the strongest baseline. On RoboCasa-GR1, GroundingPI trained with 50% of the demonstrations outperforms those baselines trained with 75%. Its mean open-loop L2 error on nuScenes is 0.296 m. We systematically analyze GroundingPI's pretraining in scale and data composition. Grounding quality and downstream manipulation improve with pretraining scale, with generalization tracking perceptual gains most closely. Analyzing the data recipe across these 11 perceptual capabilities shows dense grounding's substantial benefits for both, and OCR's potential as a catalyst for perceptual learning. These results support grounding as a perceptual foundation, and dedicated perceptual pretraining as a promising direction for foundation models of physical intelligence.

On Agentic Manipulation Policies 2026-11-19
📝 Code and videos at: https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-amp/
Show abstract

Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2% and 42.9% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation. Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Comparing the three views shows whether a system lacks a capability or fails to compose capabilities it already has. A diagnosis harness further attributes the failed episodes of three representative systems to Mind, Act, or their composition and summarizes each system in a profile card. Our evaluation of multimodal agents, vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world-action models (WAMs) shows that competence in a single view does not carry over to Mind2Act tasks. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem.

Risk-Controlled Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Retrieval-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) models enable efficient test-time adaptation by leveraging expert demonstrations as additional context without updating model parameters. However, even when retrieved demonstrations are relevant to the current task, the VLA may fail to effectively utilize the retrieved information, ultimately leading to task failure. To address this challenge, we propose R2-VLA, a risk-controlled retrieval-augmented VLA framework that enables reliable test-time adaptation by satisfying a user-specified task success rate requirement with minimal expert assistance.
