ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Agentic robotics places a large language model (LLM) agent in the robot control loop, where it composes perception, motion, and vision-language-action (VLA) tools under the guidance of a memory and thereby generalizes to new tasks without retraining. This generality comes at a steep price in efficiency. Every primitive requires a round of LLM reasoning over the full interaction history, so the cost of an episode grows with the number of primitives. We find this reasoning largely redundant: once a sub-goal is chosen, its primitives follow a routine already prescribed by memory. We present ChunkAct, which introduces action chunking to agentic robotics. Online, a single call launches a chunk of chained primitives, which executes autonomously and returns control only upon failure. Offline, ChunkAct compiles the memory into these chunks, so execution is memory-free at test time and the planner is reserved for decisions that require reasoning. LLM computation therefore scales with the number of decisions rather than the number of primitives. On LIBERO-Pro, ChunkAct improves task success by 5.0 points over the state of the art in agentic robotics while requiring 2.9x fewer LLM calls, 4.4x fewer input tokens, and 2.3x fewer output tokens.

What Matters in Quality-Aware VLA Pretraining: A Benchmark and Empirical Study 2026-10-13
📝 Project Website: https://axis-bench.github.io/
Show abstract

How should demonstration quality be used during vision-language-action (VLA) pretraining to improve performance after target-task fine-tuning? We compare weighting, filtering, and conditioning across reward definitions and label granularities using 5,000 naturally suboptimal human demonstrations and 24 target tasks. Within each VLA, we match demonstrations and training budgets. Additional comparisons examine pretraining data size and the roles of quality tags during fine-tuning and inference. Across three VLAs, we find that approach-aware episode conditioning improves mean success over plain behavioral cloning; conditioning and filtering favor episode and chunk labels, respectively, on average, with model-dependent reversals; and constant-tag fine-tuning improves mean success after conditioned pretraining with either tagged or untagged inference. We introduce AXIS-Bench to support these controlled comparisons. Built on crowdsourced simulation demonstrations, it provides recorded object and contact states for recomputing quality labels on shared trajectories. Replayable trajectories support workspace-aligned rendering and user-defined real-world target tasks. In our real-world experiment, simulation pretraining raises success from 9.6\% to 31.7\%. Project page: https://axis-bench.github.io/.

EmbodiedEvo: Self-improving Coding Agents for Physical Intelligence 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Bringing general-purpose agents into the physical world calls for an executable representation of physical intelligence. Compared with vision-language-action (VLA) models, which map observations and language instructions directly to actions, code makes control logic explicit and reusable. Code can serve not only as a policy representation but also as the workspace in which the agent solves a task. Rather than following a prescribed workflow of planning, execution, and verification, the agent can autonomously inspect the state, revise and execute code, and use feedback to choose the next step. The success of this SWE-style loop in digital environments motivates its extension to physical tasks. Yet physical execution remains prone to recurring failures. To retain and refine experience for later scenes, we introduce EmbodiedEvo, where a coding agent evolves reusable Skills and Lessons through feedback on a single Playground scene, then freezes the resulting harness for diverse new scenes. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves an average score of 44.48 and a success rate of 37.82\%, surpassing all officially verified results. Paired tests show the largest gains on memory-dependent tasks and successful reuse of coordinated procedures, while fine contact control remains difficult. Experience from stronger models also improves weaker executors: Astra's experience raises Sol and Luna's success rates by 8.86 and 5.50 percentage points over no experience. Compared with raw interaction history, Skills and Lessons achieve comparable task performance at 53.0\% lower execution cost.

Interaction Latents as Actions for Contact-Rich Manipulation 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Contact-rich manipulation requires robots to coordinate not only where to move, but also how contact and force should evolve throughout an interaction. Yet most robot policies represent actions primarily as motion trajectories, using tactile signals as inputs or auxiliary prediction targets without explicitly encoding contact dynamics in the action representation. We propose ILA (Interaction Latents as Actions), which uses decodable interaction latents that jointly capture the evolution of motion, contact, and force application as policy action units, providing a shared representation for action generation, evaluation, and offline improvement. We learn these latents through visually conditioned joint action-tactile reconstruction with a time-conditioned implicit decoder. Using them as flow-matching generation targets supports post-training of pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs), as well as training flow-matching policies from scratch. A shared decoder maps generated latents to executable actions. For offline improvement, we learn a tactile-conditioned expert reference model from interaction latents encoded from expert demonstrations. The resulting expert-consistency rewards guide weighted learning from successful rollouts, while observation-compatible expert references guide correction of low-reward predictions from failed rollouts. Across three policy families, ILA improves success rates averaged over four real-world tasks by 21.0-36.5 percentage points relative to tactile-conditioned direct-action baselines. Ablations highlight the benefit of encoding contact evolution in action targets beyond tactile conditioning, while the same latent space supports expert-guided offline improvement. Project website: https://mrsecant.github.io/ILA/.

LIBERO-RIG: A Hardware-Setup Robustness Benchmark for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-13
📝 30 pages (4 pages main text plus appendix), 15 figures, 18 tables
Show abstract

Robot policies are expected to generalize across deployment environments, including variations in the robot itself: cameras are bumped and remounted, calibrations drift, controllers run at different rates, and end-effector pads wear down. We present LIBERO-RIG, a benchmark that perturbs only the robot's hardware and setup while holding every LIBERO task fixed. The benchmark spans eleven perturbation axes, including camera mounting and optics, camera-to-arm calibration, actuation gain, control rate, proprioceptive noise, base pose, table height, and gripper pads. Each axis has five severity levels, yielding 210 conditions across the four LIBERO suites. We evaluate nine recent vision-language-action models using 500 episodes per condition and substantiate each finding with rigorous statistical tests. Pooled success falls from 0.932 to 0.767 at the middle severity and 0.509 at the highest severity. Performance degradation varies substantially across perturbation axes and action interfaces, and training on visual perturbations does not mitigate faults that leave camera images unchanged. We release the benchmark together with manifests specifying the fixed perturbation parameters for each episode.

SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics 2026-10-13
📝 16 pages
Show abstract

Frontier language-model agents can now improve robots on their own: they write control code, test it, and keep what works as skills and memories for the next task. We introduce SEAR (Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics), a framework and benchmark built around two questions. Is the agent really learning, or just trying more often? And does what it learns still help on new tasks, in a new simulator, and on a real robot? The framework runs an agent in three modes: it writes code that becomes the policy (code-as-policy), acts live in the control loop (agent-as-policy), or works as a researcher that fine-tunes a vision-language-action model (auto-research). The benchmark adds controls that separate retained experience from repeated attempts, familiar tasks, privileged simulator state, larger budgets, and luck. Across seven frontier language models, 330 simulated and five physical tasks, and over four years of agent runtime, we highlight three findings. First, the learning curve of a robotic agent depends both on the overall time budget and the task difficulty relative to model capability. Harder tasks make longer sessions worthwhile; on easier tasks, re-rolling short attempts does just as well. Second, control code and shared routines transfer across tasks and simulators, while task-specific solutions do not. On a real robot, we observe a performance gap between agent-designed practice and novel human-designed scenes. Third, different models evolve more effectively in different ways. Some do best acting live, others writing code first. And a text-only model keeps pace with a comparable multimodal one: perception helps with error assessment, but agentic reasoning and tool use can matter more. We release the benchmark and codebase to support future research.

EqC: Equilibrium Energy Control for Introspective Lifelong Robot Learning 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Long-term robot autonomy depends on more than effective action generation: a policy must recognize when its behavior is no longer sufficiently supported and use assistance to improve. We introduce Equilibrium Energy Control (EqC), a framework for introspective lifelong robot learning built around an explicit conditional energy landscape. EqC casts generative action refinement as equilibrium-seeking optimization, while the geometry of the refinement process reveals whether the generated behavior remains consistent with the learned landscape. This competence signal enables Energy-Gated DAgger, where persistent energy-consistency violations trigger expert takeover and the resulting recovery trajectories are aggregated for subsequent policy updates. EqC integrates naturally into both Diffusion Transformer policies and flow-matching-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies. Across simulation and real-world bimanual manipulation, EqC matches or improves control performance and robustness while enabling timely intervention near the policy's competence boundary. A real-world study further shows how these interventions progressively expand the range of situations handled autonomously, providing a practical path from static generative controllers toward self-improving robot systems.

GreenWAM: A World Foundation Model for Robotics 2026-10-13
📝 Technical report. Project page: https://greenvla.github.io/GreenWAM
Show abstract

A robot needs a world model that predicts how its actions will change the scene and a policy that chooses those actions. Models are usually built and evaluated for only one of these roles, and every robot records its actions in its own format. We ask whether one robot world model can serve both roles across different bodies, and what makes this work. We present GreenWAM, a world foundation model for robotics, together with the recipe that produces it: (1) a shared anatomical action space that describes 20 robot families, from bimanual arms to dexterous humanoids, in common joint coordinates; (2) joint training on these families in visual, action-conditioned and world-action modes; and (3) policies that split an action expert off the generator. As a world model, GreenWAM attains top-tier trajectory, depth and 3D accuracy on WorldArena 1.0, ahead of every world-action model and video generator in our comparison. As a policy, it scores among the top entries of the RoboTwin 2.0 leaderboard, reaches the highest zero-shot LIBERO-plus success among the compared methods and is competitive with dedicated vision-language-action and world-action policies on CALVIN and LIBERO. We further adapt GreenWAM to a real humanoid, where it succeeds more often than policies adapted from $π_{0.5}$ and from Cosmos 3 Edge, and distill it into a single-step policy for real-time control. Controlled experiments yield three findings: robot specialization improves world prediction and control together; an action expert helps when split off at the start of adaptation; and keeping world-model modes during policy adaptation preserves robustness to scene changes.

Fewer Tokens, Better Action: GPT-6 Astra Robot Agents with 14% Higher Success Rate but 65% Fewer Tokens 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Vision-language model (VLM) agents can control robots through visual feedback and action primitives, but repeated LLM calls and redundant observations incur substantial token overhead. We introduce PyRUA-Lean, an interactive code-execution framework in which the agent composes robot primitives, including vision-language-action (VLA) policies, into Python cells that check and retry locally and return only the images and state they request. Across 700 simulated task instances from LIBERO-PRO, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboCasa365, it raises success from 63.1% to 71.7% over tool calling with the same GPT-6 Astra planner and primitives, using 49% fewer LLM calls and 65% fewer input tokens on jointly solved instances. ForkCast: Predictive Recovery for Vision-Language-Action Policies via Counterfactual Futures 2026-10-13
📝 9 pages, 5 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies perform manipulation tasks using camera images and language instructions, but object slip or visual occlusion can disrupt execution. Recovery is typically learned during policy training or delegated to a monitor that triggers a scripted repair after detecting a failure. We introduce ForkCast, a predictive recovery framework that compares counterfactual futures to determine whether and how to intervene in a frozen VLA policy. At each decision point, ForkCast evaluates three candidates: executing the policy's nominal action chunk (Commit), adjusting the camera viewpoint (Reobserve), and replanning the arm trajectory (Replan). A candidate-conditioned world model forecasts the latent outcome of each candidate at a common future horizon, and a shared task-progress head scores the predicted outcomes. By evaluating the policy's own continuation alongside the repair candidates, a single comparison based on task progress determines both whether and how to intervene, without a failure detector, an intervention threshold, or updates to the VLA backbone. On five simulated RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with injected occlusion and object slip, ForkCast increases pooled success from 13.3% for the frozen $π_{0.5}$ policy to 30.0% and transfers to two additional VLA backbones without updating ForkCast. After adaptation on real-robot candidate rollouts, ForkCast also improves success over the base policy under both clean and visibility-limited conditions. Code will be released to benefit the research community.

WAVE-0: Bridging VLA Policies and WAMs with Lightweight Latent Future Prediction 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies excel at generalist robotic manipulation but generate actions without explicitly modeling their environmental consequences. World action models (WAMs) incorporate future prediction, yet most rely on costly video generation, while latent alternatives either use predicted futures only as auxiliary targets or infer latent actions from suboptimal visual transitions through costly large-scale video pretraining. We introduce WAVE-0 (World-model-Augmented VLA Execution), a framework that bridges VLAs and WAMs through a lightweight latent world model. By connecting the semantic understanding of pretrained VLAs with explicit future prediction, WAVE-0 allows anticipated action consequences to directly guide action generation. Built on a pretrained VLA, its 257M world model translates VLM-generated latent action plans into predicted future representations that condition the action expert, unifying latent planning, future prediction, and action generation. The world model is trained directly on ground-truth robot actions with a counterfactual ranking objective, requiring neither pixel reconstruction nor large-scale video pretraining. WAVE-0 achieves success rates of 91.47% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 98.7% on LIBERO, along with 88.0%, 61.4%, and 75.0% on LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro, and LIBERO-Para, respectively. On real-world tasks, it improves the success rate of $π_{0.5}$ from 38.3% to 73.3%. These results demonstrate strong manipulation performance and generalization across diverse distribution shifts.

Benchmarking Interaction-Oriented Aerial Mobility across Heterogeneous Environments 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Aerial mobile manipulation requires unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to traverse heterogeneous environments such as indoor and outdoor domains, and connect navigation with subsequent physical interaction within the same trajectories. Existing aerial benchmarks are typically confined to single-environment settings, limiting evaluation of agents across continuous environmental transitions. When interaction is evaluated, agents are often initialized near the target, leaving the transition from navigation to interaction largely unassessed. This work introduces CrossFly, a benchmark for long-horizon interaction-oriented aerial mobility across heterogeneous environments. CrossFly contains 15K trajectories across more than 20 reconstructed scenes under a unified simulation and evaluation framework, covering Indoor, Outdoor, and Indoor$\leftrightarrow$Outdoor trajectories. It further introduces Interaction Readiness Rate (IRR) to assess whether terminal navigation states provide suitable conditions for downstream interaction without assuming a specific manipulation policy. Experiments with five representative aerial vision-language-action (VLA) agents and the proposed CrossFly-Agent show that Indoor$\leftrightarrow$Outdoor settings are more challenging than single-environment settings, with the highest success rate reaching 9.7% on unseen scenes. A downstream manipulation case study further validates the relevance of interaction-oriented arrival to subsequent physical interaction. We hope CrossFly helps advance aerial embodied intelligence toward unified mobility and physical interaction in heterogeneous environments.

Tactile Curiosity Drives Robot Interaction 2026-10-13
📝 17 pages, 6 figures, 1 table. Accepted to the 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026), Austin, Texas, USA. Project website: https://go.klem.nz/tacex
Show abstract

Mastering robot manipulation skills via reinforcement learning (RL) remains largely sample-inefficient. The most common RL algorithms rely on random action sampling to discover new strategies, resulting in agents that allocate most of their training budget to motions in free space, away from the contacts from which manipulation skills emerge. Existing intrinsic motivation methods based on model disagreement or epistemic uncertainty improve on isotropic noise, but they can also reward uncertainty in functionally irrelevant transitions, such as erratic motions in free space. In this work, we argue that tactile feedback provides a natural signal for exploration, and introduce TacEx, a framework that incorporates touch into epistemic uncertainty-driven exploration by decomposing model uncertainty across sensory modalities and directing curiosity toward the tactile channel. By anchoring curiosity to the sense of touch, TacEx drives the robot to discover complex contact dynamics, learning to manipulate and grasp objects without task rewards or expert demonstrations during exploration. The interaction-dense dataset collected through this tactile-driven curiosity supports offline learning of downstream pick-and-place policies without additional environment interaction. We further use tactile-driven exploration to post-train vision-language-action (VLA) models. Although the VLAs are initially pre-trained without tactile feedback, post-training with TacEx substantially improves downstream performance while remaining highly sample-efficient.

OxyGen: Unified KV Cache Management for VLA Inference under Multi-Task Parallelism 2026-10-13
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Embodied AI agents increasingly require parallel execution of multiple tasks, such as manipulation, conversation, and memory construction, from shared observations under distinct time constraints. Recent Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) support such heterogeneous outputs, yet existing inference systems incur redundant computation and resource contention when serving concurrent tasks on-device. We identify isolated KV cache management as the root cause. To address this, we propose unified KV cache management, an inference design that treats the KV cache as a first-class shared resource across tasks and control cycles. This abstraction enables two key optimizations: cross-task KV sharing eliminates redundant prefill of shared observations, while cross-frame continuous batching batches unfinished language requests from different frames and advances them within a per-frame decoding budget. We implement this design in OxyGen and evaluate $π_{0.5}$ on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 and Jetson AGX Thor. OxyGen achieves up to 3.7$\times$ speedup over isolated execution, delivering over 200 tokens/s language throughput and 70 Hz action frequency simultaneously on RTX 4090. We also validate the design on Xiaomi-Robotics-0 and StarVLA across different backbones and expert configurations. We further demonstrate the inference gains on a humanoid robot with on-board Jetson AGX Thor.

FiberTune: Preserving Action-Fiber Visual Residuals in Vision-Language-Action Fine-Tuning 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project page: https://fibertune.github.io/ Code: https://github.com/fibertune/FiberTune
Show abstract

Action-supervised fine-tuning of vision-language-action (VLA) policies can compress visual structure that is weakly constrained by the action objective. We formalize this as residual visual collapse along local action fibers and propose FiberTune, a training-time objective that preserves teacher-structured visual residuals without adding inference-time overhead. FiberTune uses an online action probe to filter action-predictive directions from intermediate visual-token representations. It preserves the residuals through centered directional alignment with a frozen visual teacher and effective-rank regularization. Under identical training conditions, FiberTune improves over task-loss-only fine-tuning in every one of six simulation settings spanning two benchmarks and two architectures ($π_{0.5}$ and OpenVLA-OFT), as well as on physical SO-101 pick-and-place; representative gains include +10.7 pp SR(5) on long-horizon CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D and physical SO-101 task success rising from 72.7% to 78.1%. Representation analyses support the preservation of visual information in the residuals.

TRACE: Efficient Transfer Learning for Neural Whole-Body Control Across Humanoid Embodiments 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Humanoid behavior foundation models (BFMs) enable diverse whole-body control, but adapting a pretrained controller to a new embodiment remains challenging because of kinematic and dynamic mismatch. We present TRACE, a framework for efficient cross-embodiment adaptation that preserves pretrained motion representations while specializing feedback control to the target robot.TRACE first injects kinematic priors into input and output projection layers and aligns nominal joint dynamics to reduce embodiment mismatch and support stable fine-tuning. It then combines encoder distillation with low-rank reinforcement learning on a compact, quality-screened motion set, using a two-phase curriculum to establish stable tracking before refining difficult motions. We apply TRACE to transfer SONIC from Unitree G1 to Unitree H2 using 10 million motion frames and approximately eight hours of training on a single RTX PRO 6000 GPU, at less than 0.1% of the GPU-hour cost of training SONIC from scratch. The adapted controller achieves 96.08\% success on 67,953 evaluation motions under a height-scaled tracking criterion. Real-robot demonstrations span dynamic motion tracking and whole-body teleoperation, with collected demonstrations supporting downstream vision-language-action policy post-training. These results demonstrate practical reuse of a pretrained humanoid controller across robots with different body scales and dynamics.

Breaking the Average: Overcoming Convergent Defaults in Robotic Manipulation via Diagnostic Chain-of-Thought and Structured Guidance 2026-10-13
📝 4 pages, 3 figures
Show abstract

Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as $π_{0.5}$, have demonstrated impressive generalization capabilities by scaling behavior cloning across massive datasets. However, these models frequently suffer from distributional convergence, a phenomenon where the policy regresses to "safe" but suboptimal mean behaviors due to mode averaging in the training data. This often results in generic grasp strategies, Convergent Defaults, that fail in constrained or long-tail scenarios. In this work, we propose a novel framework to identify and correct these behaviors without parameter updates. We introduce a three-stage methodology: (1) Probing for Defaults, identifying task-specific mode collapse; (2) Diagnostic Root Cause Analysis, utilizing the VLA's latent reasoning capabilities to verbalize the logic behind suboptimal actions; and (3) Structured Guidance Injection, where concrete, distinct alternatives are encoded into reusable "Lens Prompts" that shift the sampling distribution. We validate this approach using $π_{0.5}$ in ManiSkill3 simulation environments. We hypothesize that our method outperforms vanilla $π_{0.5}$ and standard Chain-of-Thought prompting by effectively breaking convergent defaults in complex manipulation tasks.

Goal2Skill: Long-Horizon Manipulation with Adaptive Planning and Reflection 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Recent vision-language-action (VLA) systems have demonstrated strong capabilities in embodied manipulation. However, most existing VLA policies rely on limited observation windows and end-to-end action prediction, which makes them brittle in long-horizon, memory-dependent tasks with partial observability, occlusions, and multi-stage dependencies. Such tasks require not only precise visuomotor control, but also persistent memory, adaptive task decomposition, and explicit recovery from execution failures. To address these limitations, we propose a dual-system framework for long-horizon embodied manipulation. Our framework explicitly separates high-level semantic reasoning from low-level motor execution. A high-level planner, implemented as a VLM-based agentic module, maintains structured task memory and performs goal decomposition, outcome verification, and error-driven correction. A low-level executor, instantiated as a VLA-based visuomotor controller, carries out each sub-task through diffusion-based action generation conditioned on geometry-preserving filtered observations. Together, the two systems form a closed loop between planning and execution, enabling memory-aware reasoning, adaptive replanning, and robust online recovery. Experiments on representative RMBench tasks show that the proposed framework substantially outperforms representative baselines, achieving a 32.4% average success rate compared with 9.8% for the strongest baseline. Ablation studies further confirm the importance of structured memory and closed-loop recovery for long-horizon manipulation.

METIS: Multi-Source Egocentric Training for Integrated Dexterous Vision-Language-Action Model 2026-10-13

Building a generalist robot that can perceive, reason, and act across diverse tasks remains an open challenge, especially for dexterous manipulation. A major bottleneck lies in the scarcity of large-scale, action-annotated data for dexterous skills, as teleoperation is difficult and costly. Human data, with its vast scale and diverse manipulation behaviors, provides rich priors for learning robotic actions. While prior works have explored leveraging human demonstrations, they are often constrained by limited scenarios and a large visual gap between human and robots. To eliminate these limitations, we propose METIS, a vision-language-action (VLA) model for dexterous manipulation pretrained on multi-source egocentric datasets. WireCraft: A Simulation Benchmark for Industrial DLO Manipulation 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 (Spotlight). Camera-ready version with additional simulation and real-robot experiments. 29 pages including references and appendices
Show abstract

Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs), such as wires and cables, are central to industrial assembly. Unlike rigid objects, whose state is captured by a 6-DoF pose, DLOs have an infinite-dimensional configuration space and deform continuously under contact with grippers, fixtures, and the workspace, making them a demanding benchmark for general dexterous manipulation. Despite their importance, policy development and comparison remain difficult: existing benchmarks are often tied to specific hardware setups, lack modular and customizable task assets, or study generic deformable-object tasks without the fixtures relevant to real-world industrial wire manipulation. Few benchmarks align simulation, real-world data, and shared evaluation protocols. To bridge this gap, we introduce WireCraft, a simulation benchmark for industrial DLO manipulation with configurable difficulty and assets, spanning three task families: connector insertion, clip routing, and channel seating. It supports two complementary DLO physics models, articulated and deformable, and the released trajectories come from both simulation and a physical UR5. We benchmark reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), and vision-language-action (VLA) policies under shared metrics. Privileged state-based RL solves a representative setting in each task family with over 82% success, confirming the tasks are well-posed. Image-based policies remain less successful across the evaluated tasks, with ACT and DiT VLA falling below privileged State PPO. On a physical UR5, supplementing real demonstrations with simulated data improves success by 15-65 percentage points in the best-performing mixture for each task.
