EmbodiedMidtrain: Bridging the Gap between Vision-Language Models and Vision-Language-Action Models via Mid-training 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) inherit their visual and linguistic capabilities from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), yet most VLAs are built from off-the-shelf VLMs that are not adapted to the embodied domain, limiting their downstream performance. In this work, we propose EmbodiedMidtrain to bridge the gap between VLMs and VLAs. We first characterize the data distribution gap between them, showing that VLA data occupy compact regions that are largely separated from the broader VLM distribution, while the degree of alignment varies substantially both across and within VLM data sources. Then, we build a mid-training data engine that leverages a lightweight learnable proximity estimator to select the most VLA-aligned candidates from a large VLM pool, and produce EmbodiedMidtrain-1M, a VLM dataset that we further use to mid-train the VLM before downstream VLA fine-tuning. Experiments on both simulated robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks show consistent VLA performance gains over off-the-shelf checkpoints across VLM backbones and VLA action heads. These gains extend to downstream tasks not used to guide data selection. Further analysis reveals that mid-training provides a stronger initialization for VLA fine-tuning, with gains emerging from the earliest steps and widening throughout training. Moreover, the data engine captures both dataset-level and sample-level alignment signals, favoring spatial reasoning over text-centric tasks while preserving the diversity of the VLM data. We will release all code, data and models for future research.

REKI: Registering Knowledge in VLAs 2026-10-15
Show abstract

We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. Additionally, we endow it with a memory that gives the policy access to its recent past at negligible cost: a few register tokens in the action expert summarise the output of the VLM at each frame and persist in the expert's cache. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights: https://github.com/kyutai-labs/reki.

FastVLA: Task-Conditioned and Temporally Adaptive Visual Token Compression for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted by WACV 2027
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework. At each step, FastVLA scores token importance via task-to-vision attention and adaptively sets the compression rate from the importance-distribution uniformity, measured by an inverse-Simpson metric with online sliding-window calibration. We evaluate FastVLA on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogACT across LIBERO and SIMPLER, and on a physical Franka Research 3 robot. In simulation, it reduces LLM-backbone FLOPs by 35-59\% and action latency by 9-32\% (a 1.09$\times$-1.46$\times$ speedup) while maintaining task success, achieving a comparable or better efficiency-performance trade-off than existing plug-and-play token compression/pruning/reuse baselines. On the real robot, it cuts end-to-end latency by 20\% (1.25$\times$ speedup) at comparable success. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/FastVLA.

When Does Corruption Break the Plan? A Degradation-Tolerance Benchmark for Camera-Only End-to-End Driving 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027. 17 pages, including supplementary material
Show abstract

Camera-only end-to-end (E2E) driving models are nearing deployment, where the camera stream is degraded by blur, noise, low light, weather, frame loss, and memory faults. How much a policy tolerates before its driving breaks is unclear. Corruption-robustness benchmarks target detection or bird's-eye-view perception, not the planning output that drives the car. We present DriveDegrade, a benchmark for image-degradation tolerance in camera-only E2E driving. Sixteen corruption families at five severities are injected on the fly inside the image loader, one operator reaching fifteen policies, and we evaluate open-loop planning on nuScenes and NAVSIM plus a CARLA closed-loop anchor. First, mild degradation barely affects planning, and the families that break it have a clear threshold at mid severity. Second, fragility is corruption-dependent: blur, JPEG, and raindrop damage planning most, while weather and bit error are tolerated far into the range. Third, a flat curve is ambiguous, so we separate corruptions that degrade the image from those that remove it. A planner that reads its camera must lose accuracy when information is deleted, whatever it does under quality loss. On these two axes the planners separate sharply, quantifying the ego-status shortcut without mistaking indifference for robustness. A released vision-language-action planner is flat on both axes, and blinding all six of its cameras costs it only 11.5 percent.

Memory as Weights: Internalizing Long-Term History for Streaming Videos 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Streaming vision-language models (VLMs) must continuously integrate information from the past while remaining responsive to current observations and user queries. Existing memory mechanisms typically compress, retrieve, or cache historical observations and reintroduce them into the input context, forcing historical memory and current observations to compete for limited context. We propose MaLoW, a streaming memory framework that instead internalizes historical context into evolving lightweight parameter updates. MaLoW uses a recurrent HyperNetwork to incrementally consolidate incoming video observations into fixed-size LoRA weights, allowing historical information to be carried forward without revisiting the full video stream. In doing so, MaLoW represents history as weights, while keeping the current observation as input. To support training, we further construct MaLoW-Instruct, a large-scale streaming video dataset containing 381K question-answer pairs over 56K videos. We conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate that MaLoW achieves strong performance on OVO-Bench, StreamingBench, and StreamArena. On the backward split of OVO-Bench, MaLoW outperforms prior memory approaches by 3.9% to 11.8% across different video backbones while incurring only modest latency overhead. Beyond streaming video understanding, MaLoW also generalizes to vision-language-action models, improving the action success rate on Simpler WidowX by 9.3%.

VeriVLA: Adaptive Test-Time Verification for Flow-Based VLAs with Latent World Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

TS-WAM: Temporal Latent Actions and World-Action Coupling for Vision-Language Robot Control 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Many vision-language-action policies rely on action-labeled robot demonstrations and lack an explicit mechanism for predicting future visual states. These limitations hinder learning from action-free videos and can encourage overfitting to demonstration conditions, weakening generalization and robustness. We present TS-WAM, a lightweight Teacher-Student World-Action Model that addresses these challenges through self-supervised temporal learning and explicit future prediction. Its vlm-jepa framework learns from heterogeneous data, including robot demonstrations and action-free human videos, using an EMA target branch without requiring a separately trained external teacher. By coupling vlm-jepa with delta-lwm, TS-WAM learns to predict latent actions from the current observation and instruction and uses them to predict future visual features that guide action generation at inference. A unified end-to-end training architecture jointly optimizes temporal representation learning, visual dynamics, and robot control without a separate latent-action or world-model pretraining stage. The EMA target branch is removed at inference, keeping the deployed policy lightweight. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and SimplerEnv, together with real-world manipulation experiments on Franka Research 3 and RevoTron, demonstrate that TS-WAM achieves strong manipulation performance, generalization, and robustness.

What Should Robot Policies Learn to Predict? Rethinking Supervision Targets in Vision-Language-Action Learning 2026-10-15
📝 26 pages, 8 figures. Haitao Shen and Hanyu Liu contributed equally
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are typically trained with action prediction as the primary supervision signal, since robots ultimately execute actions. Auxiliary objectives such as latent-action (LA) and future-vision (FV) prediction are often added to improve policy learning, yet they compete with action prediction for the same training budget. We ask whether, under a fixed budget, allocating a substantial share of training samples to such objectives, rather than to further action prediction, can improve policy learning. We build a unified VLA framework that couples a two-stream multimodal diffusion transformer (MMDiT) with a sample-level router, which assigns each training sample to either action prediction or an auxiliary objective and thus makes supervision allocation explicit. Within this framework, we instantiate three LA targets based on LAPA and its DINO-feature variants, and three FV targets based on DINOv3, V-JEPA 2, and Wan2.2 representations. Across LIBERO, CALVIN, and four real-robot tasks, suitable LA and FV supervision outperforms action-only training under the same budget, even when action prediction receives no more than half of the training samples. FV generally outperforms LA, although the strongest LA target remains competitive on CALVIN. Diagnostic analyses suggest that DINO-based LA targets emphasize relative motion, whereas FV targets emphasize absolute position and task identity. We therefore argue that what robot policies learn to predict, and how training is allocated among these targets, should be treated as explicit design choices rather than defaulting to action-dominant training.

Evaluating Multimodal LLMs as Generalist Vision-Language-Action Agents for Drone Control: Commanding, Approaching, Tracking and Searching 2026-10-15
📝 Preprint. Project page: https://naver-ai.github.io/DroneCATS/

Show abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are increasingly used to make decisions for drones, but existing evaluations are mostly limited to simulation, to a single drone, and to targets that are visible from the start and do not move. We present DroneCATS, a benchmark in which the MLLM alone controls the drone through four actions, and evaluate ten MLLMs from frontier models to small open models. The benchmark varies target visibility, target motion, scene and simulator, and extends to real drones. Beyond a single drone, it adds a multi-drone suite, where one model drives multiple drones, and a commander + sub-agents suite, where a commander model directs one sub-agent for each of the drones. Across these settings, we find that frontier models achieve the highest success rates, but their latency causes them to lose moving targets, while small open models often do not declare arrival even when they reach the target. Guided by this failure analysis, we fine-tune a 2B model on drone action data synthesized from public RGB-D images; its success rate rises from 0% to 31% in simulation and outperforms the frontier models in real flight. With multiple drones, we find that every zero-shot model succeeds more often as the commander of one sub-agent per drone than when it drives all of them from one context, and that commanding success follows general VLM ability. On real drones, we observe that the results broadly follow those in simulation, while latency and hardware limits matter more. We will fully open-source the benchmark.

Beyond Single-Axis Testing: Paired Evaluation of Compound Robustness in Vision-Language-Action Policies 2026-10-15
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-language-action policies are typically evaluated one perturbation at a time, providing a useful diagnosis of their sensitivity to individual distribution shifts. Real-world deployment, however, may involve several shifts simultaneously, and it remains unclear how these individual robustness measurements compose. We ask whether compound robustness can be inferred from single-axis evaluations. We introduce LIBERO-CTRL, a six-axis benchmark that pairs each initial state across single-axis conditions and a matched simultaneous condition. This design reveals two opposing outcome changes that aggregate success rates cannot distinguish: emergent failures, where all single-axis rollouts succeed but the simultaneous rollout fails, and compensated successes, where at least one single-axis rollout fails but the simultaneous rollout succeeds. Because one transition decreases compound success while the other increases it, they can cancel, making aggregate compound performance appear consistent with single-axis measurements even when individual outcomes differ substantially. These opposing transitions can largely cancel in aggregate: even when the difference between the two transition rates is not statistically distinguishable from zero, as many as 29.0% of matched initial states still change outcome. Across six policies and three severity levels, such outcome changes reach 34.5% in the most affected condition. The relative prevalence of the two transitions varies across policies and severities, while the transition rates remain similar under independent re-evaluation of stochastic policies. Compound robustness therefore cannot be characterized from aggregate single-axis success rates alone; matched per-instance evaluation is needed to reveal how joint perturbations alter behavior.

LIBERO-Industry: Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation under Industrial Process Constraints 2026-10-15
📝 38 pages, 14 figures, 20 tables, including appendices. Project website: https://IAIE-PhysicalAI.github.io/LIBERO-Industry/
Show abstract

Industrial manipulation in flexible manufacturing, e.g., high-mix, low-volume (HMLV) electronics assembly, requires robot policies to adapt to changing products and production conditions. Such settings impose industrial process constraints, including product variants, moving material flows, finite execution windows, and tight geometric tolerances. However, existing evaluation frameworks do not systematically capture these constraints when assessing generalist robot policies. To address this gap, we introduce LIBERO-Industry, a novel benchmark built on the widely used robot learning benchmark LIBERO, that translates the aforementioned industrial constraints into controllable task conditions and measurable success criteria. Specifically, LIBERO-Industry comprises three classic task scenarios: Static-to-Dynamic (S2D) loading, Dynamic-to-Static (D2S) unloading, and Dynamic-to-Dynamic (D2D) transfer. The suites contain 216 tasks in total that systematically vary object geometry, conveyor speed, interaction-window duration, and geometric acceptance region size. Extensive experiments across five generalist robot policies (including vision-language-action models and world-action models) show that performance progressively degrades as industrial constraints are introduced and combined. Under a shared fine-tuning and evaluation protocol, the LIBERO-Industry co-training condition achieved higher observed target-transfer completion than the original-LIBERO condition.

Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 13 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables. Includes supplementary material
Show abstract

Specialized vision-language-action (VLA) policies acquire strong domain-specific skills, yet consolidating these skills into one shared policy remains challenging. On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers teacher knowledge on inputs induced by the student, addressing the distribution mismatch encountered during deployment. For flow-based VLAs, this mismatch arises at two levels: environment observations and intermediate action-generation states. We propose a two-level on-policy distillation framework that couples student-driven environment interaction with teacher velocity supervision along student-generated action flow trajectories. Frozen domain specialists jointly supervise one student. After each update, the student collects new environment experience and generates fresh action-flow trajectories. Starting from a joint supervised fine-tuning (SFT) initialization, Velocity Loss OPD yields absolute success-rate gains of 22.55% on LIBERO-40, 33.4% on CALVIN five-instruction sequences, and 22.6% on Meta-World MT50. Compared with Action Loss OPD and Teacher-rollout SFT, it achieves absolute success-rate gains of 6.4-9.5% on LIBERO-40, 9.7-18.3% on CALVIN, and 3.2-4.2% on Meta-World MT50. Analysis by sequence length further shows larger success-rate gains on longer CALVIN sequences. These results demonstrate effective multi-domain capability consolidation through two-level on-policy supervision, with a single student deployed across domains.

CarryOn: A Dual-Loop Harness for Personalized and Reliable Robot Assistance 2026-10-15
📝 Project page: https://vincent2311.github.io/Projects/CarryOn/
Show abstract

While generalist vision-language-action (VLA) models have enabled diverse open-world manipulation, real-world robotic assistance requires two critical capabilities they currently lack: personalization and reliable execution. First, executing an instruction like "make me a sandwich" requires grounding subtask decomposition in persistent user preferences rather than relying on a single generic solution. Second, reliable deployment demands explainable safety assurance that proactively prevents hazards and gracefully recovers from occasional physical failures. To address these gaps, we present CarryOn, a dual-loop harness designed to augment a frozen VLA policy. The planning loop maintains persistent user-level and scene-level context that is continually grounded against new observations and revised through interaction, enabling personalized task selection and execution grounding across conversations and changing scenes. The execution loop actively supervises policy rollouts, shielding actions from predicted hazardous contact. When execution fails, the system invokes reusable control-based recovery skills that restore task progress and are refined through reflection on earlier failures. On a real robot and in simulation, CarryOn dispatches preference-consistent subtasks across users and scenes, reduces the violation rate of an already safety-finetuned VLA from 52.6% to 6.1%, and increases recovery success from 13% to 80% when refined skills are reused.

Policy-Aligned Predictive Verification for Adaptive VLA Action-Chunk Execution 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies generate action sequences that reduce repeated inference, but deciding how much of each sequence to execute is critical to task success. Existing adaptive methods make this decision using generation stability or prediction agreement, yet these signals do not establish whether executing a candidate prefix preserves the ability to complete the task. We introduce a policy-aligned predictive verifier (PAV) that anticipates the consequences of candidate action prefixes and evaluates their compatibility with successful continuation before execution. Rather than using future prediction to generate actions, our method evaluates existing proposals in the VLA's internal task-conditioned representation space. To learn local judgments from terminal task outcomes, we combine trajectory-level survival supervision with a localization prior that preserves useful behavior within failed rollouts. The resulting validity scores guide execution-horizon selection. Evaluations with pi_0 and pi_0.5 across LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world manipulation show improved success-query trade-offs. These findings point toward a different role for future prediction in robotics, using it to assess how far to trust a policy's proposed actions rather than to generate new ones.

Fast-VLA: You Only Think Once for Robot Manipulation 2026-10-15
📝 16 pages, 6 figures, 9 tables. Project page: https://garfield-kh.github.io/FastVLA
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies commonly place a multi-billion-parameter model inside the feedback loop, repeatedly recomputing task context whenever they predict a new action chunk. Yet task understanding changes far more slowly than the robot state. We introduce Fast-VLA, which invokes a frozen 4B VLM once per episode to contextualize the instruction and initial views as fixed, multi-layer key-value task memory. During execution, a lightweight online actor fuses learned time- and arm-indexed queries with current visual and proprioceptive feedback to form action queries. These queries retrieve task context from the fixed memory and predict action chunks without re-running the foundation model. Fast-VLA achieves 92.46% and 92.40% success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean and Randomized, and 95.95% and 79.11% on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus evaluation. Task-memory swaps further show that the actor relies on episode-matched task memory. These results show that task context can be established once while closed-loop control remains responsive throughout execution. Project page: https://garfield-kh.github.io/FastVLA

Revisiting Embodied Chain-of-Thought for Generalizable Robot Manipulation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Embodied chain-of-thought (CoT) aims to bridge linguistic reasoning with robotic control, yet its effective form and integration remain underexplored. In this paper, we revisit embodied CoT for robotic control at an unprecedented scale. We curate the largest embodied CoT corpus to date, comprising 978,743 trajectories, 226.3M samples, and 2592.5 hours of data. Through extensive experiments, we show that effective CoT must ground high-level semantic understaning in concrete linguistic action guidance -- such as end-effector movement and image-space trajectories -- whereas high-level reasoning alone yields marginal gains. More importantly, we identify that explicit CoT does not scale reliably as an autoregressive action prefix, suffering from compounding errors during inference. To address these challenges, we propose ERVLA, a vision-language-action (VLA) model that effectively leverages linguistic reasoning in generalizable robot manipulation. ERVLA is trained using a CoT-dropout strategy, allowing the model to leverage rich reasoning traces during training while predicting actions directly without CoT during inference to bypass autoregressive instability. This approach enables reliable scaling with increasing pre-training data. ERVLA achieves state-of-the-art results on LIBERO-Plus with an 86.9\% success rate and reaches 53.2\% on VLABench, showcasing superior performance in out-of-distribution settings. Furthermore, ERVLA outperforms competitive state-of-the-art baselines in real-robot experiments, especially in handling semantic ambiguity and long-horizon tasks. Code, data, and model checkpoints are available on the project website: https://taoshuaiz.github.io/ERVLA/.

LTM: Latent Transition Based In-Context Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 Dataset & Code: https://github.com/buaa-colalab/LTM-pi
Show abstract

Robotic manipulation often requires remembering interactions that are no longer visible. We introduce Latent Transition Memory (LTM), which stores history as compact latent transitions between consecutive observations. Instantiated on $π_{0.5}$ as LTM-$π$, our method uses a frozen latent action encoder, a lightweight adapter, and causal KV caching for incremental inference without memory-specific semantic annotations or external reasoning-model calls. With DreamDojo transitions, LTM-$π$ achieves 58.54% success on RoboMME and 80.5% on LIBERO-Mem, performing best among baselines without external reasoning models or memory-specific annotations. Ablations support the contribution of transition content beyond temporal cues and the efficiency of delayed memory admission. Under a 200-token memory budget, success remains 84.00%, versus 87.50% without compression. Finally, real-robot counting success improves from 11.25% to 82.50%. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness, scalability, and efficiency of latent transition memory, highlighting its capacity for complex temporal information, bounded-context operation, and real-world robotic control.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models transfer semantic knowledge acquired through vision-language pretraining to robot control, achieving strong performance across various benchmarks. However, most VLAs directly map observations to demonstrated actions without explicit supervision of intermediate physical state changes, making them prone to overfitting observation-action correspondences. Do VLAs Really Follow Instructions? Diagnosing How-Level Instruction Following in Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 Preprint
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are expected to follow instructions like their language backbones, yet prior work has focused on What-level instructions that specify the task rather than How-level instructions that specify how to carry it out. Through our diagnoses, we find that How-level instructions do not help current VLAs and lower task success. We hypothesize that pairing each training episode with a single instruction limits How-level instruction following, and show that training with segment-level enriched instructions makes actions respond to such instructions without consistently raising task success. When to Ask for Help: A Decision-Theoretic Account and Measurement Standard for Slow-Module Scheduling in Dual-System Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 41 pages, 7 figures. Preprint
Show abstract

Dual-system vision-language-action (VLA) models pair a high-frequency fast policy with a slow, expensive module, and published trigger mechanisms for invoking it lack a shared objective, measured costs, and any standard separating schedule quality from module quality. We provide the missing account: invocation is formalized as a calibrated decision problem whose fixed-ratio, fixed-budget, and history-gated schedules are degenerate cases of one quantity, the Value of Intervention (VoI); a renewal baseline proves the optimal schedule periodic under state-independent collapse, with adaptive gains governed by the cost ratio $ρ$ and the dispersion of collapse. Measured systems span three orders of magnitude in $ρ$ (FiS-VLA $0.95$; RoboICL about $10^3$), and a direct test confirms the low-$ρ$ prediction: calibrated gains vanish under homogeneous collapse and grow with heterogeneity. Fourteen closed-loop arms on LIBERO test the contested middle of the continuum ($ρ=1.53$): the adaptive trigger allocates its queries exactly as the threshold rule predicts, yet the language-rewrite channel cannot convert them into success on either suite, and a revert actuator that undoes a failed rewrite is the only intervention with zero net downside on both LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Goal, where the rewrite deficit deepens from thirteen to forty-two points. A four-measure leak-free protocol cuts apparent early-warning AUC from $0.93$ to $0.62$ on $929$ deployment traces, a detection-limited frontier theorem converts detection quality into a schedulability statement, and a compilation loop turns measured value functions into per-task refresh periods within $2.4\%$ of the closed-loop oracle. Together these results turn slow-module invocation from a design opinion into a measured decision.

KODA: Kinematics--Observation Data Alignment for Humanoid Learning from Human Egocentric Demonstration 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Training humanoid whole-body vision-language-action (VLA) policies requires costly robot demonstrations with aligned robot-view observations and controller-compatible whole-body state-action labels. Egocentric human demonstrations offer scalable supervision but have misaligned views and sparse motion cues. Existing approaches either pretrain policies that still require robot data for adaptation or generate controller-specific commands that limit reuse. Conversion also requires robot-view observations aligned with the generated whole-body motion. We present KODA (Kinematics-Observation Data Alignment), which converts egocentric RGB demonstrations into whole-body VLA training data using a reusable canonical trajectory to guide robot-view synthesis and generate controller-specific whole-body state-action supervision. KODA first generates a simulator-checked whole-body trajectory using a sparse tracker with sampling-based command optimization. This generated trajectory directly determines the onboard camera poses used to synthesize corresponding robot-view observations. The motion, observations, and task instruction form a canonical record. For each deployment controller, KODA optimizes commands to track the stored motion and records the corresponding action labels and proprioceptive states.
