$Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://junjieye.com/Z-sight/
Show abstract

We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM's native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

NereidVLA: Learning to Understand and Navigate the Underwater World 2026-11-18
📝 11 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Underwater navigation requires translating visual spatial cues into motion, yet marine vision-language datasets rarely include paired robot trajectories. We present NereidVLA, a vision-language-action model that transfers marine spatial knowledge to language-conditioned 3D navigation. We first train Sea2Know on approximately two million marine examples to learn target grounding, spatial relations, and scene structure without paired robot-motion supervision. We then adapt it using expert trajectories executed under simulated underwater vehicle dynamics. Given RGB histories and a language instruction, NereidVLA predicts discrete motion tokens that decode into local 3D trajectories for closed-loop tracking. Evaluation combines an independent six-dimensional underwater navigation understanding benchmark with offline prediction and closed-loop navigation. Under matched navigation training and execution settings, marine spatial pretraining increases simulation success from 72.4% to 84.4%. Without fine-tuning on the real test environment, NereidVLA achieves 78.75% success in laboratory-pool robot trials, compared with 47.50% for InternVLA-N1. These results show that marine spatial supervision can support downstream motion learning and physical underwater navigation.

RoboMorpher: Scaling Robot Morphology Representations for Cross-Embodiment Action Prediction 2026-11-18
📝 Website: https://robomorpher.github.io/
Show abstract

Learning shared action representations is central to cross-embodiment manipulation. However, identical joint values can produce different spatial configurations across robots, limiting representations learned from joint values alone. We introduce RoboMorpher, a morphology-conditioned action tokenizer that jointly reconstructs joint angles and link poses to associate control coordinates with their kinematic effects. MorphGen supplies scalable kinematic supervision by generating serial-arm descriptions from survey-derived geometric priors. We pretrain RoboMorpher on 40 million morphology-state pairs and integrate the frozen tokenizer into a vision-language-action policy for discrete prediction and continuous refinement. We also introduce EmbodiBench, a ten-task benchmark with 30 generated URDFs per task. With downstream URDFs excluded from tokenizer pretraining, the policy achieves 56.40% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and 23.27% on EmbodiBench, exceeding continuous joint-space control by 9.73 and 15.34 percentage points. Removing link-pose supervision reduces EmbodiBench success by 17.94 percentage points, supporting morphology-aware geometric supervision for joint-action representation learning.

On Agentic Manipulation Policies 2026-11-18
📝 Code and videos at: https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-amp/
Show abstract

Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2% and 42.9% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The agent succeeds zero-shot in 104 of 108 real-world trials spanning twelve tasks and three robot instances, reducing failure rates by 20x and 15x relative to strong VLA and WAM baselines. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation.

Inference-Time Robot Behavior Steering through Physically-Aware Reconfiguration of Task-Structure 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026
Show abstract

A central challenge in deploying robot policies is inference-time policy steering: redirecting a policy to satisfy natural-language preferences unseen in training, without retraining. Existing methods fail in two modes: end-to-end policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) require fine-tuning or expert-level guidance, while neuro-symbolic methods rely on predefined symbols whose edits can yield logically valid but physically infeasible plans. We propose ReStruct, a zero-shot method built on a neural automaton policy that decomposes a visuomotor policy into a high-level state-machine skeleton and a low-level residual controller. A vision-language model (VLM) grounds the preference in the skeleton's learned symbols, refining them if needed, and compiles it into a temporal-logic automaton, whose synchronous product with the skeleton reconfigures the task structure. With the controller frozen, the skeleton's action priors are updated for physically-aware control. In simulation and real-world experiments, ReStruct steers preferences from object-centric specifications to temporal-logic constraints, including novel combinations of demonstrated behaviors that require compositional generalization. Average preference-following success is 70.9% in simulation and 92.5% on a real robot, surpassing baselines like the prompt-steered vision-language-action model π0.5.

SHAPE: Discrete Supervision and Parallel Continuous Streams for Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Human Data 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://li-yaping.github.io/SHAPE/
Show abstract

Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. We address both in SHAPE (Supervision from Human Action tokens with Parallel Executable streams), a vision-language-action (VLA) model whose backbone predicts discrete action tokens of EEF chunks as an auxiliary task, while a shared flow-matching expert outputs two executable streams, EEF and joint. On a bimanual robot with 22-DoF hands, a matched study shows that discrete supervision helps learn from human data, driving backbone gradients and shaping action-informative representations across domains. Under standard control pipelines at deployment, direct joint readout excels at fine-grained control with robot demonstrations, scoring 0.80 across six tasks against 0.63 for π0.5, whereas IK-based EEF readout excels at human-to-robot transfer. Pretrained with human data and discrete tokens, SHAPE learns new tasks from human demonstrations without task-specific robot data.

RECLAIM: Robot-Vision-Informed Adversarial Patch Defense via Embodied Attention Feedback 2026-11-18
📝 10 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Vision-language-action policies control embodied agents through a perception-action loop grounded in physical interaction, where adversarial patches can corrupt action predictions and thereby alter subsequent observations. To mitigate these threats, many existing defenses rely on policy adaptation or auxiliary models, but require additional training or inference. We introduce RECLAIM, a training-free, inference-time defense that uses a frozen VLA's own attention response to local visual interventions to guide purification during closed-loop embodied manipulation. Our key insight is that attention changes induced by local edits provide feedback for choosing where and how to purify as the robot interacts with its environment. RECLAIM combines this feedback with robot geometry to refine patch localization and select local treatments, then revalidates them as the agent's embodied actions change the observed scene. We evaluate RECLAIM against patches generated by four adversarial attack methods across multiple manipulation task suites under frozen VLA control. Under adversarial patch attacks, RECLAIM succeeds in at least one rollout on 95% of the evaluated tasks and raises rollout success on LIBERO-Object from 0% without defense to 70.0%. Its mean decision time is only 548.9 ms per control step. These results highlight policy-guided visual purification as an effective approach to defending embodied agents against adversarial patches and recovering closed-loop task execution.

Gamer: A Generalist Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Long-Horizon Real-Time Game Agent 2026-11-18
Show abstract

We introduce Gamer, a generalist agent framework built for long-horizon tasks in complex interactive environments. Its end-to-end architecture combines perception, memory management, and multi-step reasoning. A shared training and deployment pipeline supports interaction with both conventional 2D graphical interfaces and complex 3D simulated worlds. We first continue pre-training on a dataset constructed from tens of thousands of hours of device-level keyboard and mouse interaction. These trajectories span simulated games and embodied environments, allowing the model to learn environmental dynamics across both physical and virtual domains. Sparse-thinking instruction tuning then substantially reduces redundant computation while preserving the reasoning needed for complex decisions. We train the policy for long-horizon interaction with a stable multi-turn reinforcement learning method that addresses finite context windows, accumulated rollout errors, and sparse rewards. For real-time interaction, a cloud-edge inference engine serves numerous simulated game environments concurrently, supports unbounded-context execution, and keeps online inference latency low. Across multiple challenging 3D open-world games, Gamer demonstrates solid generalization and operational efficiency comparable to skilled players. It also achieves competitive results on a range of conventional 2D game benchmarks. These results suggest that even repetitive keyboard and mouse trajectories can support substantial gains from scaling data and learning algorithms. Training on these trajectories helps Gamer learn complex skills more steadily in environments with both physical and digital properties. This approach offers a path toward generalist agents that can interact across digital and physical worlds.

Homeward: No Place Like the Training View 2026-11-18
📝 22 pages, including supplementary material
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies implicitly assume that third-person cameras remain fixed at their data-collection poses; once a camera is bumped, remounted, or swapped at deployment, task success degrades precipitously even though the underlying task and robot remain unchanged. We present Homeward, a training-free, plug-and-play observation adapter that canonicalizes observations from an uncalibrated deployment camera back into the training view for frozen VLA policies. Our design stems from a closed-loop diagnostic paradox in simulation: while re-rendering perturbed views into the training camera with oracle geometry recovers much of the lost performance, substituting an off-the-shelf feed-forward 3D foundation model can leave the policy worse off than applying no correction at all. This degradation concentrates on the thin, articulated robot arm, which feed-forward depth prediction tears and scatters - yet the arm's exact 3D state is already known from robot kinematics. Homeward exploits this asymmetry: it estimates what is unknown - camera parameters and static scene depth - from the live RGB frame and a single training-view reference, and renders what is known - the moving robot links - directly from the kinematic model. Evaluated across $\sim$300,000 closed-loop episodes spanning four frozen VLAs, Homeward boosts success across all 1,599 camera variants of LIBERO-Plus from 62.5%, 76.5%, 68.0%, and 3.6% to 77.6%, 95.1%, 91.2%, and 26.4% for OpenVLA-OFT, $π_{0.5}$, $π_0$, and OpenVLA, respectively. Without any scene fine-tuning or deployment camera calibration, Homeward extends to 6-DoF random placements, focal-length changes, and, with per-step pose estimation, continuous intra-episode camera drift, while matching or surpassing a calibrated, scene-trained view-synthesis baseline on three of four policies on LIBERO-Plus.

Towards Pose-Robust Few-Shot Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-18
📝 17 pages, 13 figures, 17 tables (including supplementary material). Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/WACO/
Show abstract

Fine-tuning a vision-language-action (VLA) model on a few dozen demonstrations is the standard way to put a manipulation policy on a robot, yet the resulting policy silently assumes that the camera and the robot base stay exactly where they were during data collection: a bumped tripod or a base re-docked a few centimetres away makes it reach for the wrong place. We show that both failures are one phenomenon, a change of the camera-to-base extrinsic that the policy has memorised, and that the robot carries everything needed to undo it. We introduce WACO, a world-anchored canonicalization framework for VLA models: the arm, placed by forward kinematics at the measured joint angles, serves as a metric calibration target, and the static scene serves as the world anchor that tells a moved camera from a moved robot. From a single RGB-D frame, WACO estimates both shifts without ground-truth extrinsics or extra data, canonicalises the image to the calibration viewpoint and the state, action and start pose to the calibration base frame, and fine-tunes the unmodified policy under the artefacts of its own estimator. We also introduce LIBERO-PoseShift, the first benchmark for RGB VLAs with graded camera, base and compound shifts. Across LIBERO-PoseShift, the LIBERO-Plus camera track and four real-world tasks on a UR5e robot, WACO consistently outperforms the standard few-shot recipe and prior viewpoint-robust methods, raising shifted success by 13.2 points in simulation and by 36.7 points on the real robot, recovering 90% of the simulation gap to a ground-truth oracle and 87% of the real-robot gap, and establishing a new state of the art among methods that use no perturbed training data, with no test-time calibration. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/WACO/.

Allocentric Memory, Egocentric Acting: A Dual-Frame VLA for Urban Aerial Navigation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Language-goal aerial navigation requires a UAV to reach a destination specified in natural language using observations collected during flight. A key challenge is linking changing egocentric perception with spatial evidence anchored to stable locations for long-range navigation. We propose CityNav-VLA, a dual-frame vision-language-action model for urban navigation integrating dynamic egocentric context with persistent allocentric spatial memory. The UAV incrementally accumulates visual observations on a fixed north-up map initialized with geographic and landmark priors, preserving evidence across viewpoint changes. A dynamic query conditioned on the instruction, current observations, UAV state, and motion history retrieves relevant memory evidence to predict a map-frame target. A deterministic controller converts this target into body-frame commands using the UAV's current pose, connecting global reasoning with egocentric execution. To reduce target fluctuations over long horizons, early goal commitment (EGC) fixes the execution target to the coordinate-wise median of the first $K$ predictions without extra trainable parameters. On Test Unseen, CityNav-VLA reduces NE by 21.2% and improves SR and SPL by 15.8% and 30.9%, respectively. It also reduces NE by 6.2% and improves SR and SPL by 5.6% and 23.2%, respectively, relative to the former SOTA after dataset refinement. Cumulative ablations show that navigation-map features yield the largest gains, while metric coordinates further improve endpoint accuracy. With a 4B-scale backbone, inference averages 0.68 s per decision with 8.6 GB peak memory on a single A100, presenting remarkable model efficiency.

Hardware Acceleration of Vision-Language-Action Models with Weight Precision Rising over the Flow Steps 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models such as GR00T control robots through an action head: a diffusion transformer that turns noise into a chunk of actions in a few flow-matching steps. Every step re-reads all of the head's weights from DRAM but applies each weight to only about fifty tokens. Inference is therefore memory-bound, and its latency and energy are largely determined by the total number of transferred weight bits. We show that later flow steps attenuate the quantization errors of earlier steps, so the weight precision of an action head should rise over the steps. With plain per-channel rounding and without calibration or training, widths rising over the steps from 2 to 4 bits preserve the closed-loop success of robot policies. These widths transfer 25% fewer bits than 4-bit weights at every step. However, we show that on Jetson AGX Thor, current GPU kernels cannot exploit this per-step precision due to fixed overheads. We therefore present a programmable accelerator whose latency and energy both scale with the transferred weight bits. It decodes entropy-coded weights for bit-serial sub-arrays at the DRAM rate. Because the width changes per step, a run-time allocator divides these sub-arrays between the linear layers and the attention to balance their workloads. The same sub-arrays are thus capable of running action heads of different families and sizes, as well as a vision-language model. On the DRAM side, a requantizer and an entropy encoder at each bank let one stored 8-bit copy replace the copies of all widths. Simulated with LPDDR5X timing, our RTL implementation runs the GR00T N1.6 action head in 8.02 ms per inference with 2, 3, 3 and 4 bits in its four flow steps. With a copy per width and 50% of Thor's memory bandwidth, the accelerator runs this schedule 1.7x faster than Thor in NVFP4, at 9.1x less energy and equal closed-loop success.

Adaptive Action Horizon Selection via Flow-Field Uncertainty 2026-11-18
📝 IEEE IROS 2026 Workshop
Show abstract

Reliable uncertainty estimates are important for adaptive execution in vision-language-action (VLA) models. Existing approaches such as Adaptive Action Chunking (AAC) estimate action entropy from multiple sampled action chunks, introducing additional inference cost and capturing both uncertainty within an action trajectory and diversity across alternative strategies. We introduce Divergence-based Uncertainty-aware Adaptive Action Chunking (DUAC), a post-hoc method that extracts trajectory-conditioned predictive uncertainty from the vector field of a pretrained VLA. Building on a closed-form flow-posterior covariance formulation, DUAC derives action-level uncertainty scores from a single generated chunk using Jacobian-vector products, without retraining or multiple endpoint samples. DUAC strongly correlates with AAC, while capturing distinct information: AAC measures global dispersion across sampled chunks, whereas DUAC measures local uncertainty around the trajectory selected for execution. Consequently, DUAC is less sensitive to diversity among distinct but valid action strategies. Across LIBERO, LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboCasa, DUAC-based adaptive horizon selection performs favorably among the evaluated methods, combining strong task success with low uncertainty-estimation latency. These results establish the flow vector field as an efficient, trajectory-conditioned source of predictive uncertainty for robot decision making.

AeroRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation 2026-11-18
📝 8-page main paper with references and supplementary material (15 pages); 5 figures, 13 tables. Code: https://github.com/starsholic/AeroRSI Project page: https://starsholic.github.io/AeroRSI/
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs, i.e., actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches, which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. In this paper, we present AeroRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. Our key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision. Moreover, an episode terminates at its first failure, concealing subsequent failures until the earlier ones are repaired. AeroRSI thus renews its supervision in every round, anchoring the failures of the current policy, verifying repairs by its continuation, and distilling them while keeping its behavior elsewhere. On TravelUAV, three rounds raise the success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and reduce collisions by half, surpassing the previous state of the art by 10.6 points from the same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost. Code and models are available at https://github.com/starsholic/AeroRSI.

Pose6DAug: Physically Plausible Multi-view Object Swapping for Robot Data Augmentation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have shown strong potential for general-purpose manipulation, yet they often fail on novel, out-of-distribution objects whose appearance or geometry deviates from the training distribution. The standard remedy is to collect multi-view teleoperation data for every failure case, but this scales poorly in both cost and time. We introduce Pose6DAug, a failure-driven data augmentation framework that turns existing successful episodes into targeted demonstrations for a policy's failure modes, without any new data collection. Our key insight is that each successful episode already encodes a physically valid action trajectory together with calibrated multi-view observations. By swapping only the manipulated object while preserving this trajectory, we obtain new and physically grounded demonstrations. However, the challenge lies in synthesizing object swaps, e.g., naive 2D video editing breaks multi-view consistency and physical plausibility, particularly under heavy occlusion and egocentric viewpoints. Our method instead operates directly in 3D, anchoring the target object with an explicit mesh driven by a temporally coherent 6D pose trajectory. This ensures geometrically consistent renderings across all camera views. Fine-tuning a VLA on data augmented by our method improves success rates by 16.5% relative to the state-of-the-art baseline on novel objects, while preserving in-distribution performance. On a real robot, it more than doubles the success rate of the base policy. These results show that multi-view and physically consistent augmentation is a practical path to scalable VLA generalization.

CoRA-VTLA: Contact-Guided Attention and Queryable Response Memory for Tactile Post-Training 2026-11-18
📝 10 pages, 5 figures. Project page: https://cora-vtla.github.io/
Show abstract

In precision manipulation, small errors in alignment or force can cause jamming or deformation, making contact feedback essential for adapting pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models to these tasks. A single tactile observation leaves the response to recent actions ambiguous. Direct tactile fusion during post-training can also disperse the action expert's visual attention and reduce the share it allocates to contact regions. To address these challenges, CoRA-VTLA augments pretrained VLAs with lightweight tactile modules. A queryable action-response memory retrieves recent contact responses so that the action expert can combine them with immediate tactile feedback. Contact-guided gaze supervision concentrates the visual attention of the tactile semantic token on image regions corresponding to fingertip contact. CoRA-VTLA achieves 73.33% mean success across three MuJoCo tasks, compared with 50% for direct tactile fusion with the same sensor inputs. It also achieves 67% success on an optical-tactile task from the public UniVTAC benchmark and 66.7% mean success across three real-robot tasks. These results show that tactile post-training helps pretrained VLAs adjust to changing contact.

3DTouch: A Tactile-aware VLA Model with 3D Geometric Relations for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

In contact-rich dexterous manipulation, occlusion by the fingers and interacting objects obscures local geometry, preventing visual observations from fully capturing contact locations. Visually similar scenes may also differ subtly in the relative positions of interacting objects, leading to different contact states. We propose 3DTouch, a tactile-geometric conditioning approach for vision-language-action (VLA) models. 3DTouch constructs a shared 3D visuo-tactile representation by expressing RGB-D scene points and dense five-finger tactile measurements in the robot-base frame, preserving each taxel's spatial location and force vector. Hand-object and object-object relations derived from hand landmarks and pose-aligned reconstructed surfaces explicitly describe the relative arrangement of interacting entities. Together, these representations condition the VLA action expert on spatially localized contact forces and relative surface geometry, providing explicit cues for contact adjustment and object alignment. We evaluate 3DTouch on five real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks using a UR7e robot arm equipped with an XHand dexterous hand. 3DTouch improves the average success rate by 42 percentage points over the corresponding VLA baseline and by 20 percentage points over the strongest evaluated alternative. Ablation studies reveal the complementary benefits of scene geometry and spatial tactile feedback, with further gains from shared spatial grounding and explicit geometric relations.

GTI: Grounding Task Intent Throughout Long-Horizon VLA Manipulation 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 7 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models generate local actions from visual observations, language, and robot state, but long-horizon manipulation requires the global task intent to remain grounded in the currently executable context. We propose GTI (Grounding Task Intent), which uses the current observation and execution history to localize the active subtask and its execution phase. For the same local subtask, GTI generates two phase-dependent visual targets: an interaction/contact state and a local completion state. The local language instruction and the corresponding phase-specific visual target jointly condition the VLA, specifying both what to do now and what state should be reached at the current phase. During execution, anomaly monitoring and local recovery re-establish an intent-consistent execution context through rollback, re-observation, and phase re-evaluation after persistent deviation. We evaluate GTI on RoboCasa365, VLA-Arena Horizon-Long, and real-world PiPER tasks. Relative to matched Global-FT baselines, GR00T success on seen composite RoboCasa365 tasks increases from 14.6%/12.1% to 31.8%/20.4% under ID/OOD evaluation. On VLA-Arena, GTI+$π_{0.5}$ reaches 47.6% overall success versus 45.2% for an adapted ForeAct baseline, with gains of 5.2/6.0 percentage points on the harder L1/L2 tasks. In the three real-robot task experiments, mean full-task success increases from 21.7% to 59.2%. The results indicate that persistent semantic intent localization and phase-conditioned visual grounding improve multi-stage execution, while failure-triggered local recovery provides additional deployment-time support.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation, yet their performance often degrades on long-horizon tasks that require multiple interdependent manipulations. SuperGlance: Driving at a Glance with a Vision-Language Model 2026-11-18
📝 22 pages, 10 figures, 20 tables. Project page: https://guobapei.github.io/SuperGlance/ ; code: https://github.com/GuobaPei/SuperGlance
Show abstract

Autonomous driving is turning to vision-language-action models (VLAs). They answer and plan by decoding, one step at a time. Each step is another forward pass, so a plan can take over a second of GPU time. Embodied AI often pairs such a slow, reasoning System 2 with a fast System 1 that acts. Human drivers plan at a glance, with System 1. Faster designs usually put System 1 outside the vision-language model (VLM), in a planner without its abilities. Yet before a VLM writes its first token, it has already read the scene into one hidden state. We show that, once trained, this hidden state is enough to drive and to answer questions about what the model sees. SuperGlance reads it to turn a decoder-only VLM into an encoder-style driving policy. Its linear head scores a fixed set of 5,273 candidate trajectories. With the set fixed, rewards are computed offline, so supervised targets can be reward-weighted and reinforcement learning maximizes the exact expected reward. Before and during policy training, multiple-choice training keeps the VLM's visual abilities in one pass, so the policy still answers questions about driving scenes. Training questions are built against shortcuts. Each must be answered right in two option orders. Against drift, a KL term keeps each stage near its starting model. One pass offers VLAs a more plausible path to System 1. SuperGlance answers visual multiple-choice questions as well as its base VLM does by decoding. It plans in 84 ms of GPU time, six times faster than Qwen-Drive-1.0, and at least as well on NAVSIM, PAI-AV and WOD-E2E. In AlpaSim closed loop, km per at-fault incident holds for both planners up to a 300-ms delay and drops beyond.
