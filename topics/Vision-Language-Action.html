Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success 2026-10-22
📝 28 pages. Project page and code available at https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/
Show abstract

Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. In experiments with the open-source VLA $π_{0.5}$ and across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, this approach is competitive with or outperforms VLM-based monitors in task failure detection, as well as methods which require failure data for training. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models 2026-10-22
📝 Project page: https://lyfeng001.github.io/Echo/
Show abstract

Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To learn these signals without fine-grained progress annotations, Echo constructs successful, failed, and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision properties. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

FastHarness: Experience-Driven Review Scheduling for Vision-Language-Action Policies 2026-10-22
📝 Github:https://github.com/FastHarness/fast-harness
Show abstract

Agentic robot systems combine language-model reasoning with robot policies to monitor execution and recover from failures. However, reviewing every action chunk incurs substantial overhead, even in familiar situations. We introduce FastHarness, an experience-driven execution harness that adaptively schedules GPT-6 Astra supervision of a π0.5 policy. A fast path uses stage memory and reviewed outcomes to reduce review frequency as experience accumulates, while a complementary recovery path focuses supervision on correcting errors. Once recovery is verified, routine execution resumes, with failure experience retained to guide future interventions. The policy remains frozen and generates fresh actions from current observations. Across four simulated manipulation tasks, reviewer token use decreases by an average of 57.2% per task from the first evaluated episode to the mean of the final five episodes. Mean episode time decreases from 85.5 to 52.0 minutes over the same interval. On the same tasks, mean success increases from 80% for GPT-as-Policy (π0.5 + GPT-6 Astra) to 97.19% with FastHarness. These results highlight adaptive supervision as a practical route to more efficient agentic robotics without additional model training.

FocusVLA: Future-Intent Guided Dynamic View Routing for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have become an important paradigm for robotic manipulation by unifying visual perception, language understanding, and action generation. Since reliable manipulation requires sufficient observation of objects, end-effectors, and their spatial relationships, multi-view inputs are widely used to provide complementary spatial information. However, our experiments show that existing VLA models do not fully exploit such multi-view complementarity. The policy tends to persistently rely on the wrist view, lacking the ability to dynamically adjust global-local view dependency across manipulation stages, which limits stage adaptability and robustness. To address this issue, we propose FocusVLA, a stage-aware multi-view routing framework. FocusVLA predicts future interaction intent from historical actions and uses it to guide dynamic view routing during action generation. By adaptively coordinating global and local observations across different task stages, FocusVLA improves multi-view utilization. On RoboTwin 2.0, FocusVLA improves the average success rate over the strongest baseline $π_{0.5}$ by 10.10 pp. In real-world evaluation, FocusVLA improves the success rate from 71.7\% to 85.0\%, achieving a 13.3\% gain over $π_{0.5}$. Moreover, the intent prediction and dynamic routing modules of FocusVLA can be integrated into existing VLA frameworks without modifying their core architectures.

HEX: Humanoid-Aligned Experts for Cross-Embodiment Whole-Body Manipulation 2026-10-22
📝 Project page: https://hex-humanoid.github.io/

Show abstract

Humans achieve complex manipulation through coordinated whole-body control, whereas most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models treat robot body parts largely independently, making high-DoF humanoid control challenging and often unstable. We present HEX, a state-centric framework for coordinated manipulation on full-sized bipedal humanoid robots. HEX introduces a humanoid-aligned universal state representation for scalable learning across heterogeneous embodiments, and incorporates a Mixture-of-Experts Unified Proprioceptive Predictor to model whole-body coordination and temporal motion dynamics from large-scale multi-embodiment trajectory data. To efficiently capture temporal visual context, HEX uses lightweight history tokens to summarize past observations, avoiding repeated encoding of historical images during inference. It further employs a residual-gated fusion mechanism with a flow-matching action head to adaptively integrate visual-language cues with proprioceptive dynamics for action generation. Experiments on real-world humanoid manipulation tasks show that HEX achieves state-of-the-art performance in task success rate and generalization, particularly in fast-reaction and long-horizon scenarios.

Memory before Goal: Role of Pre-History Memory for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables. Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L). Project page: https://memory-before-goal.github.io
Show abstract

Humanoid loco-manipulation in dynamic scenes is non-Markovian: cues vanish as objects change or are hidden, or leave the view as the robot turns and walks, yet whole-body vision-language-action (VLA) policies see only the present. We present WholeBodyMem, an object-guided perceptual memory that keeps the pre-history of object events seen before the robot acts on its goal. SAM3 tracks the task objects, and their events causally select key frames within an adaptive budget, which the action expert reads by cross-attention together with object tokens encoding appearance, geometry and time. We also contribute HuMeGo, a humanoid memory benchmark with six simulated and six real-world Unitree G1 tasks. Keeping the right frames rather than more frames, WholeBodyMem surpasses memoryless and uniform-memory baselines, raising its base policy's success from 29.1% to 72.6% in simulation and from 36.7% to 88.3% on the real robot, while also improving the standard SIMPLE tasks (70.2% to 86.9%).

Where Success Breaks: Failure-Boundary Learning for Robust Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-22
📝 23 pages, 16 figures
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapted through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) inherit a structural asymmetry: expert demonstrations teach the policy where success behavior lies, but provide no signal about where it ceases to be reliable. We argue that robust VLA adaptation should therefore be viewed not as further demonstration fitting, but as Failure-Boundary Learning -- the problem of Discovering, Localizing, and Shaping the boundary between recoverable deviations and task failure. To instantiate this view, we propose DLS: built on a real-grounded behavioral prior from few real demonstrations and simulated co-training, DLS discovers failure boundaries at scale through on-policy digital twin rollouts. Rather than reducing each rollout to a binary label, semantic progress localization uses privileged simulator states to assign progress-aware signals that capture where the failure boundary is crossed, not merely whether. These signals drive directional boundary shaping in the flow dynamics -- reinforcing success-producing denoising directions and suppressing failure-producing ones, without action likelihoods or auxiliary critics. Across real-robot manipulation tasks, DLS improves robustness over SFT and online RL baselines, especially under randomized initial states and unseen visual conditions.

Measuring the Reasoning-Action Coupling in Driving Vision-Language-Action Models through Counterfactuals 2026-10-22
📝 11 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Driving vision-language-action (VLA) models bring an appealing form of explainability to a safety-critical domain, since a system that can state the reasons for its maneuvers offers the transparency that deployment and public trust in autonomous driving demand. These models read the world into language before they act on it, describing the scene and reasoning over it in text that ranges from chain-of-thought to multi-stage question answering on the way to a trajectory. That framing quietly assumes two separable properties, namely that the trajectory follows the generated reasoning and that the generated reasoning tracks the causes that actually produce the trajectory. To thoroughly investigate these properties, we apply causal, counterfactual interventions to four driving VLAs across different benchmarks. First, we perturb the models' own reasoning and show that altering its meaning produces small trajectory changes relative to blanking the same models' camera inputs, a pattern that persists across benchmarks and model architectures. We then construct scene-level counterfactuals through photorealistic removal of key objects named in the models' reasoning and analyze whether the generated reasoning and trajectories track these changes. Overall, our findings indicate that the reasoning these models present to account for their resulting driving behavior is weakly coupled to that behavior itself, pointing to limits on the explainability these systems are intended to provide.

Beyond Imitation: Reinforcement Learning-Based Sim-Real Co-Training for VLA Models 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Simulation offers a scalable and low-cost way to enrich vision-language-action (VLA) training, reducing reliance on expensive real-robot demonstrations. However, most sim-real co-training methods rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which treats simulation as a static source of demonstrations and does not exploit large-scale closed-loop interaction. Consequently, real-world gains and generalization are often limited. In this paper, we propose an RL-based sim-real Co-training (RL-Co) framework that leverages interactive simulation while preserving real-world capabilities. Our method follows a generic two-stage design: we first warm-start the policy with SFT on a mixture of real and simulated demonstrations, then fine-tune it with reinforcement learning in simulation while adding an auxiliary supervised loss on real-world data to anchor the policy and mitigate catastrophic forgetting. We evaluate our framework on four real-world tabletop manipulation tasks using two representative VLA architectures, OpenVLA and $π_{0.5}$, and observe consistent improvements over real-only fine-tuning and SFT-based co-training, including +24% real-world success on OpenVLA and +20% on $π_{0.5}$. Beyond higher success rates, RL co-training yields stronger generalization to unseen task variations and substantially improved real-world data efficiency, providing a practical and scalable pathway for leveraging simulation to enhance real-robot deployment.

Do VLAs Really Follow Instructions? Diagnosing How-Level Instruction Following in Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-22
📝 Preprint
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are expected to follow instructions like their language backbones, yet prior work has focused on What-level instructions that specify the task rather than How-level instructions that specify how to carry it out. Through our diagnoses, we find that How-level instructions do not help current VLAs and lower task success. We hypothesize that pairing each training episode with a single instruction limits How-level instruction following, and show that training with segment-level enriched instructions makes actions respond to such instructions without consistently raising task success. To measure How-level instruction following beyond standard benchmarks, we introduce SIMPLER-IN, where success requires conditions given only in the instruction. It shows that the gained responsiveness does not extend to demanding or out-of-distribution How-level instructions. How-level instruction following thus remains an open challenge.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models, world-action models (WAMs), and offline reinforcement learning methods are rapidly expanding the design space of embodied policies, yet turning these algorithms into reliable robot systems remains constrained by fragmented data formats, training stacks, evaluation protocols, inference runtimes, and embodiment-specific interfaces. DRILL: Training World Models to Improve Policies, Not Predict Pixels 2026-10-22
📝 NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

World models are increasingly used to reduce environment interaction when post-training vision-language-action (VLA) policies with reinforcement learning. Yet they are typically trained to predict pixels one step ahead, even though their role here is not merely to render visually faithful videos but to produce learning signals from imagined rollouts that improve the policy. We show that this mismatch is substantial: lower pixel MSE can produce worse policy gradients, and a world model trained for policy utility can yield better downstream policies despite 17% higher pixel MSE on the next frame. We propose the Downstream-Return Imagination Learning Loop (DRILL), a bilevel framework that updates the world model to maximize the policy's return after an inner GRPO update on imagined rollouts. DRILL has two instantiations. DRILL-VWI is a closed-form surrogate, requiring no additional simulator rollouts, that reweights prediction errors by policy visitation, the group-relative GRPO advantage, and model confidence. DRILL-IMG is a full meta-gradient method that differentiates through the inner update using truncated Hessian-vector products. We show that DRILL-VWI recovers the local one-step component of the DRILL-IMG meta-gradient under score-compatibility and small-residual assumptions. Across five manipulation simulators and two VLA backbones, DRILL-IMG raises mean success rate by 13.1 points over WMPO and 7.9 points over RLVR-World, and complements RLVR pretraining rather than competing with it. More broadly, we believe video world models for VLA reinforcement learning can be productively trained by the policy gradients they produce, not only by pixel-level prediction.

When One Step Is Enough: An Empirical Study of Euler Integration in Flow-Matching Policies 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted at the 10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026)
Show abstract

Flow matching is now the dominant approach for vision-language action (VLA) robot policies, thanks to its expressiveness and simple training objective. However, inference for standard flow matching requires iteratively solving an ODE, limiting the control frequency at deployment. While several works have proposed novel methods to allow for single-shot action generation, we ask whether such additional objectives are actually necessary. We find that across six flow matching VLAs, one-step action generation largely matches multi-step sampling at scale: multi-step integration confers little to no consistent advantage at inference. We empirically find that this holds as long as models are sufficiently trained and have enough capacity. Practically, the implication is that for large VLAs, the single-step policy comes essentially for free from the multi-step model, enabling low-latency deployment without retraining, distillation, or auxiliary objectives.

SmolVLA-MoE: Sparse Mixture-of-Experts for Efficient Multi-Task Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-22
📝 16 pages, 5 figures, 4 tables; conference paper prepared for IROS2027
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promising results in multi-task robotic manipulation by leveraging visual and language understanding for action generation. However, existing lightweight VLA models employ a single dense action expert to handle all tasks, which limits their multi-task capability under constrained computational budgets. To address this issue, we propose SmolVLA-MoE, a novel framework that introduces sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) into the FFN layers of the action expert in VLA models. SmolVLA-MoE replaces dense FFN layers with multiple parallel expert FFNs and a token-level router, enabling each token to adaptively select the most suitable expert for action generation. We further introduce a shared expert with a tunable weight parameter to balance the inheritance of pre-trained capabilities and task-specific expert specialization. Through sparse activation, SmolVLA-MoE activates only a single expert FFN during inference, with a total activated parameter count of approximately 500M including the frozen VLM backbone, while benefiting from approximately 800M total stored parameters. We evaluate our method on four task suites of the LIBERO benchmark. SmolVLA-MoE achieves an average success rate of 84.25%, outperforming SmolVLA (82.75%) and OpenVLA-7B (76.5%), while maintaining comparable inference efficiency. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/dudu435/smolvla-moe.

NavBind: Diagnosing Instruction-Action Binding at Route Forks in Navigation VLAs 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) navigation models translate visual observations and instructions into actions. Navigation success assesses whether an agent reaches the goal. However, reaching the goal successfully does not establish whether the model genuinely follows the instructions or relies on visual cues or navigation history. We introduce NavBind to diagnose instruction-action binding: whether changing the instruction influences the probabilistic distribution of the actions, with the navigation context fixed. We construct 3,252 route-fork contexts by recombining route-aligned instruction segments from R2R-CE and RxR-CE. Each context pairs full instructions that share the description of the route already traveled but specify different branches. Both are evaluated under the same agent state and navigation history. We compare generated local action sequences, the likelihoods of fixed reference action sequences under both instructions, and changes in visual attention and attribution. Across the evaluated models, higher reported navigation success does not imply that the model more faithfully follows the instructions.

UniMem: Unifying Visual and Textual Memory in VLAs for Non-Markovian Tasks 2026-10-21
Show abstract

While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have leveraged internet-scale pretraining and task-specific finetuning to achieve strong performance on long-horizon tasks, they often struggle with non-Markovian tasks that require memory. AtomicRL: Atomic Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown promise in robotic manipulation, but imitation learning (IL) alone often struggles to master complex long-horizon skills. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers an effective avenue for further improving VLA policies. However, existing VLA-RL methods typically suffer from delayed and biased credit assignment due to trajectory-level sparse rewards, while imbalanced subtask mastery creates bottlenecks that limit overall performance. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework that leverages atomic task structures for reliable credit assignment and adaptive exploration. AtomicRL introduces Semantic-Aware Policy Optimization (SAPO), which uses semantic-aware gating to constrain TD residuals from failed atomic subtasks from propagating across task boundaries, mitigating credit contamination while preserving intra-subtask credit assignment and value bootstrapping. In addition, Bottleneck-Adaptive Target Exploration (BATE) dynamically coordinates task sampling and local exploration incentives based on the mastery of individual atomic subtasks, directing learning toward the remaining bottlenecks. Finally, we develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that bridges online RL in simulation with real-world deployment, facilitating practical transfer to physical robots. On LIBERO-LONG, AtomicRL achieves absolute performance gains of 3.6% and 4.0% over PPO across two VLA architectures. On CALVIN ABC-D, it improves full-task performance by up to 9.5% over PPO. Combined with the Real2Sim2Real pipeline, AtomicRL further improves real-world task performance by an average of 21.3%. These results demonstrate that AtomicRL effectively improves the learning efficiency and manipulation performance of VLA policies on long-horizon tasks.

SP-HLM: Sparse-Pose-Driven Motion Generation and Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effective interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single action representation for both task intent and whole-body coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms. GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Geometric structure is essential for robotic manipulation and understanding the 3D world. Most VLA models rely on 2D representations of current observations, while geometry-aware approaches often require additional 3D information. However, geometry inferred independently from individual observations is often discarded rather than accumulated into a persistent task representation. Our key insight is that action generation needs a compact, task-relevant geometric abstraction that can be continuously updated, rather than a complete, renderable 3D reconstruction at every step. We introduce GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that internalizes explicit 3D supervision into dedicated geometric tokens and maintains them as temporal memory for action generation, without directly aligning the VLM's native visual embeddings with the teacher feature space. During training, a frozen geometry foundation model supervises learnable GeoPlan tokens through a geometric flow-matching objective over the current scene point map and future end-effector tracks. These tokens are integrated with the memory of observation representations, where historical information is retrieved, adaptively fused with current observations, and consolidated to retain event frames as the task progresses. At inference, the policy retrieves historical representations and fuses them with the current working representation to predict actions, without invoking the 3D teacher or explicitly reconstructing the scene. After benchmark-specific downstream adaptation, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average success on LIBERO and 91.10% / 91.24% on RoboTwin's clean/randomized settings. Across five real-world tasks, GeoMem-VLA achieves 78.0% average success. These results suggest that internalizing and remembering 3D scene understanding is a promising approach to robot control.

FoveaVLA: Foveation Actions Replace the Wrist Camera in VLAs 2026-10-21
📝 Project website: https://tml.stanford.edu/FoveaVLA/
Show abstract

We present FoveaVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that replaces the wrist camera with learned foveation actions. Wrist cameras provide close-up detail but restrict the view to the end-effector's surroundings. External cameras see the whole scene, but the policy receives them at low resolution, so the fine detail needed for manipulation is lost. FoveaVLA recovers this detail by predicting, alongside each action chunk, the interaction region to observe at the next policy query. The policy then receives crops of these regions, taken from the original-resolution images, together with the full views. We build FoveaVLA on $π_{0.5}$ in two configurations, Single and Dual, using one or two external stereo cameras with full and foveal RGB and depth inputs, and post-train both on DROID with RoboInter interaction annotations. Across four real-world task suites, FoveaVLA-Single achieves 70.8% success with one external RGB-D view, and FoveaVLA-Dual achieves 77.1%, versus 52.1% for the wrist-equipped $π_{0.5}$. Robotic manipulation is inherently geometric, but what shape does the world take inside the policy that performs it? Prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies establishes that task-relevant variables are linearly readable from activations, yet readability certifies only that a variable is present and says nothing about the shape its values trace, which may be curved and multi-dimensional.
