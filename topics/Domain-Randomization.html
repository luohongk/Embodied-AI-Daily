LocoMuJoCo: A Comprehensive Imitation Learning Benchmark for Whole-Body Control 2026-11-24
📝 28 pages, 11 figures, 5 tables. Code: https://github.com/robfiras/loco-mujoco
Show abstract

LocoMuJoCo is an open-source benchmark for imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) on whole-body control. The library couples MuJoCo (CPU) with MJX and the MjWarp backend (GPU) under a single API, exposes 9 humanoid, 3 (musculo-)skeletal, and 4 quadruped embodiments, and ships a curated native motion-capture dataset for every humanoid, to which it can also retarget AMASS and LAFAN1. A modular plug-in architecture makes observations, rewards, control modes, terrains, and domain randomization fully interchangeable, while compact JAX implementations of many RL and IL algorithms provide JIT-compiled end-to-end training, and the companion loco-mujoco-real repository provides sim-to-real deployment. Co-DRIS: Physics-Informed Predictive Belief and Variable-Impedance Learning for Reactive Catching under Coupled Uncertainty 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Reactive robotic manipulation is acutely sensitive to simultaneous sensing noise, measurement dropouts, joint execution errors, and contact parameter variations. While single-frame frameworks like Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS) achieve strong robustness under isolated uncertainties (86.4%), they degrade severely when perception and actuation uncertainties co-occur (27.6% across 10 coupled stress scenarios) due to memoryless encoding and fixed high-stiffness PD control. We propose Co-DRIS + SuperMimic, a perception-action co-design framework coupling: (i) an inertial world-frame physics-informed world model combining exact ballistic kinematics with a zero-initialized contact residual to roll forward missing observations and accelerate training via zero-cost counterfactual dropout consistency; (ii) an action-conditioned dual-stream causal GRU belief encoder resolving the denoising-vs-lag deadlock; and (iii) phase-aware variable-impedance soft-catching that transitions the 7-DoF Franka Research 3 arm into an overdamped regime (zeta/zeta0 ~ 2.26x) at the torque layer. Under unbiased 3-seed tail evaluation on unseen throws (15,360 throws/scenario), our method achieves 89.6 +/- 0.4% on standard benchmarks (+10.5% on 4-sigma noise to 70.5%) and 66.6 +/- 0.5% across 10 coupled/dropout stress scenarios (+39.0% over DRIS; +30.4% on 50% dropout). We further validate impedance decoupling, causal contact triggering under +0.65 s flight-time shifts, and cross-task transfer to steel-plate ping-pong bouncing (87.5% to 100.0%) and FR3+Hand catching (+8.4% under 4x noise with 6-13% lower peak impact force).

World Models as Native Latent Action Models 2026-11-23
📝 19 pages, 7 figures, 6 tables. Project page: https://pawteam-eai.github.io/native-lam/
Show abstract

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

Beyond Domain Randomization: Real-World Policy Adaptation in Learned Dynamics for Legged Robots 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning policies are often trained with static domain randomization for real-world deployment. However, real-world environments often exhibit unmodeled dynamic variations, such as payload changes or friction shifts, that lie outside the randomization distribution, leading to performance degradation upon deployment. We propose a framework that moves beyond static domain randomization by adapting a learned dynamics model to the deployed target conditions, with a concurrent teacher-student paradigm applied to both the policy and dynamics model. This framework integrates the dynamics model into an on-policy reinforcement learning pipeline, letting the performance of the policy align with the empirical target dynamics. Experimental validations on bipedal locomotion tasks demonstrate competitive adaptability under extreme physical variations, including a payload reaching 80% of self-weight and an oil-lubricated low-friction surface.

Recent progress in robot manipulation has been largely driven by learning from large-scale demonstrations. For humanoid robot loco-manipulation tasks, however, existing data sources force an unsatisfying tradeoff between trajectory quality and scalability. Real-world teleoperation provides the highest-quality trajectories but requires dedicated physical space and time-consuming scene resets. Simulation offers an alternative way out of this dilemma: it can produce clean, embodiment-aligned data at scale without any physical hardware. In this paper, we propose OASIS, a simulation-data-driven framework for humanoid loco-manipulation. OASIS automatically reconstructs realistic object assets from real-world images using a 3D generative model. Based on these assets, trajectories are first collected through teleoperation in simulation, and then augmented under diverse domain randomizations in a post-processing stage. With the resulting simulation data, we further design a hierarchical visuomotor policy for humanoid loco-manipulation. Extensive experiments on the real humanoid robot show that, under zero-shot deployment, the policy trained on our simulation data achieves higher success rates on most tasks than that trained on real-robot teleoperation data, owing largely to the broad lighting and environmental variations covered by our simulation rendering, which real-robot data fails to capture. Probing Invisible Actuation Shifts for Test-time Calibration of Visuomotor Policies 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Visuomotor policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs) are increasingly deployed on robots whose cameras and controllers differ from those used to collect their training data. Such deployment introduces two kinds of shifts. Visual shifts, such as a moved camera, change the observed images; actuation shifts, such as a retuned controller, latency or an added payload, change only how commanded actions translate into robot motion. Invisible to the policy, actuation shifts sharply degrade its success, even under domain randomization. In this work, we propose PEA, a test-time calibration method that identifies actuation shifts through active probing before task execution. Specifically, before each episode, the robot executes a short probe that moves one arm axis at a time with known commands, so that gain, delay and drift leave separable traces. A closed-form identifier estimates them from the response, and PEA compensates every subsequent command so that the robot executes the motion the policy intends. The identifier requires no policy training and thus applies to any frozen visuomotor policy. Since visual shifts remain visible to the policy, PEA further adapts the frozen policy with a removable low-rank adapter, addressing coupled visual and actuation shifts. Experiments on LIBERO with a WAM and a VLA show that, without any policy training, the identifier of PEA recovers most of the success lost to actuation shifts and generalizes to shifts unseen during training (e.g., from 61.0% to 89.7% on LIBERO-Spatial with Cosmos-Policy).

Neural Fidelity Calibration for Informative Sim-to-Real Adaptation 2026-11-20
📝 The code and demonstration videos are available at https://www.youwei-yu.com/nfc/
Show abstract

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) can transfer agile robot skills from simulation to the real world, but closing the sim-to-real gap by domain randomization requires expert physics knowledge, and simulator calibration from real data typically targets a few hand-picked parameters. Even then, simulators miss real-world effects, and environments reconstructed from onboard perception inherit sensing errors that limit policy improvement. We propose Neural Fidelity Calibration (NFC), which uses conditional flow matching to calibrate simulator physical parameters and residual fidelity domains online during deployment. Residual fidelity captures sim-to-real dynamics discrepancies and perception-induced environment uncertainty. NFC operates in a latent space so that realistic environments can be sampled from the inferred posterior for real-to-sim policy fine-tuning. The framework is informative: the pretrained policy is fine-tuned only when an anomaly is detected, sequential NFC seeds its proposal from the simulation-pretrained estimator, and a hallucination policy modulates posterior uncertainty following optimism in the face of uncertainty. NFC attains the most accurate posteriors among established simulation-based inference methods on five robots with up to 39 physical parameters and a 128x128 residual terrain. On a wheeled robot with a broken wheel axle deployed on snow, mud, and wet grass, NFC raises success on rough terrain from 26% to 70% and reduces orientation jerk by 88%. Code and videos will be released upon publication.

PACE: Policy Augmentation and Compensation With Explicit Disturbance Estimation for Robust Locomotion 2026-11-20
📝 12 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning has made quadrupedal locomotion remarkably robust. To handle unmodeled dynamics and external disturbances, domain randomization is usually employed to make a policy hedge against every condition it might meet. However, maintaining accurate tracking across varying dynamics and generalizing beyond the sampled range remain challenging. Here we show that explicitly estimating the disturbance and feeding the resulting estimation back into both the policy and the controller can relax this compromise. We introduce Policy Augmentation and Compensation with Explicit disturbance estimation (PACE). The framework lumps payload variations, center-of-mass (CoM) shifts, actuator degradation, and external forces into one joint-space disturbance. A nonlinear observer subsequently recovers this disturbance online without requiring joint acceleration measurements. The disturbance estimation provides feedforward torque compensation as the foundational control pathway and further augments the policy observation to enable disturbance-aware action generation. Considering the learning difficulty of fast-varying contact effects, we further provide an analytical contact estimation approach from joint and inertial measurements, without demanding force sensors. Across eleven simulated dynamics conditions, PACE reduces velocity-tracking error by 18.1% to 57.0% against state-of-the-art baselines, and maintains improved tracking under out-of-distribution (OOD) commands. Notably, in real-world experiments, the deployed real robot carried an 11.5 kg payload, nearly equal to its own mass. It also negotiated unseen 16 cm step-downs and crossed five outdoor terrains. The policy was trained exclusively on flat ground in simulation with at most 3 kg of added mass.

Distilling World Action Models for Efficient Bimanual Manipulation via Anchored Path Matching 2026-11-17
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) jointly generate future video and robot actions through iterative denoising. Few-step distillation reduces the number of model evaluations, but its coarse updates can move the student away from the teacher's trajectory. We propose Anchored Path Matching (APM), which supervises endpoint and short-transition predictions from states reached by the student. From each state, we continue the teacher to zero noise and use its endpoint and first intermediate state as targets. Modality-routed adapters learn to match both targets from the same starting state. When the two gradients conflict, anchor-priority updates preserve the endpoint gradient and remove the opposing component of the local gradient within each modality. One checkpoint supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step joint generation. The sampling budget can be chosen at deployment without retraining or teacher queries. On RoboTwin 2.0, we train only on Clean demonstrations and evaluate directly on the domain-randomized Random setting. At one step, APM improves closed-loop success over continued base distillation at matched inference budgets by 2.0 percentage points on Clean and 1.4 on Random. Video peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) rises from 30.169 to 32.317 dB, with gains in structural similarity (SSIM) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). Toward Interaction Dynamics: A Predictive Framework for Safe Physical Human Robot Interaction 2026-11-16
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Physical human-robot interaction requires yielding transiently to contact yet recovering the commanded reference under sustained load. Finite-stiffness impedance control retains a static deflection there, while predictive alternatives typically optimize a nonlinear model online. Operational-space cancellation instead exposes a translational error double integrator with a fixed transition matrix and a configuration-scheduled input map, making interaction a predictive quantity. We build on it a compact offset-free interaction-error MPC for torque-controlled manipulators: a force-domain random-walk state estimates persistent interaction and model error, and a 30-variable convex QP maps the correction through the current task inertia while constraining the applied joint torque. Conditional results establish impedance equivalence of the unconstrained passive feedback, offset-free regulation at feasible frozen configurations, and quadratic stabilizability of the scheduled backbone. In a 1 kHz MuJoCo simulation of a 7-DOF Franka FR3, the estimator cuts steady-state error under a repeated 15 N step from 2.73 mm to 0.033 mm when added to the otherwise identical 100 Hz MPC, at unchanged peak joint power. A stiffness-and-damping-calibrated impedance baseline attains 2.63 mm at the same actuator cost, and 1.36 mm with ideal measured-force cancellation, so constant-load rejection is not unique to MPC; the sensorless controller still reaches 0.033 mm. The torque constraint holds the command inside a budget tightened to the lowest the task can meet, at no tracking cost. On a physical UR5e, five alternated pairs under a nominal 1.5 kg hanging load give 1.505 mm without the estimator; with it the controller is offset-free, its 0.023 mm error being the noise level of the encoder-based position measurement. A tuned anti-windup integral also removes the offset, with larger transients.

Clustered Randomized Smoothing for Stochastic Prediction Functions 2026-11-07
Show abstract

Modern stochastic predictors can model rich, multi-modal outcome distributions. However, this expressive power comes with challenges in ensuring robust predictions $-$ a critical requirement in safety-critical domains. Randomized smoothing is a leading technique for improving robustness, particularly against adversarial perturbations. Yet, in stochastic multi-modal regression settings, randomized smoothing often fails due to mode collapse, yielding averaged predictions that do not reflect the underlying distribution. To address this limitation, we propose clustered $α$-smoothing, a framework that (1) partitions noisy samples using an arbitrary clustering algorithm, (2) applies $α$-smoothing locally within each cluster, and (3) combines the resulting predictions into a mixture distribution. By interpreting the smoothing distribution as a mixture of $α$-smoothers, we derive a lower bound on the probability that the smoothed prediction lies within a union of compact regions corresponding to distinct modes. We empirically evaluate our framework on two benchmarks, demonstrating substantial improvements over state-of-the-art methods. In stochastic trajectory prediction on a driving simulator dataset, our approach achieves, on average, a $27\%$ lower Wasserstein distance to the ground-truth distribution compared to $α$-smoothing. In quadrotor control, where modes correspond to distinct feasible paths to a target, our method reduces the collision rate by $90\%$ relative to the state-of-the-art randomized smoothing.

📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Evaluations and Datasets Track). Camera-ready version with updated content, acknowledgments, and resource links. Benchmark renamed to AgentRobustBench. Code, dataset, checkpoints, and leaderboard: https://github.com/WillChow66/AgentRobustBench
Show abstract

Tool-use language agents are evaluated on benchmarks that assume clean inputs, unambiguous tool registries, and reliable APIs. Real deployments violate all these assumptions: user typos propagate into hallucinated tool names, a misconfigured request timeout can stall an agent indefinitely, and duplicate tool names across servers can freeze an SDK. We study these failures as a sim-to-real gap in the tool-use partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where deployment noise enters through the observation, action space, reward-relevant metadata, or transition dynamics. We introduce AgentRobustBench, a benchmark with 22 perturbation types organized by these four POMDP components, each grounded in a verified GitHub issue or documented tool-calling failure. Across 21 models from 1.5B to 32B parameters (including the closed-source o4-mini), the robustness profile is sharply uneven: observation perturbations reduce accuracy by less than 5%, while reward-relevant and transition perturbations reduce accuracy by roughly 40% and 30%, respectively; scale alone does not close these gaps. We then propose ToolRL-DR, a domain-randomization reinforcement learning (RL) recipe that trains a tool-use agent on perturbation-augmented trajectories spanning the three statically encodable POMDP components. On a 3B backbone, ToolRL-DR-Full retains roughly three-quarters of clean accuracy and reaches an aggregate perturbed accuracy comparable to open-source 14B function-calling baselines while substantially narrowing the gap to o4-mini. It closes approximately 27% of the Transition gap despite never seeing transition perturbations in training, suggesting that RL on adversarial static tool-use inputs induces a more persistent retry policy that transfers to unseen runtime failures. Code, dataset, checkpoints, and leaderboard are accessible through our GitHub repository.

Joint-Health-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Robust Robot Control Under Actuator Degradation 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Robots deployed in unstructured environments are susceptible to actuator-authority degradation, which reduces the effective control authority of individual actuators and can cause reinforcement learning policies trained under nominal dynamics to fail. Existing robustness approaches rely on discrete fault labels, broad domain randomization, or global latent variables, often without explicitly modeling continuous per-joint residual actuation capability. We propose a joint-health-aware reinforcement learning framework for robust robot control under simulated actuator degradation. Our framework encodes actuator condition in a continuous joint-health vector and models degradation as multiplicative attenuation of executed actions. A dedicated HealthNet estimator infers per-joint health from recent observations and actions, allowing the policy to redistribute control effort across healthy and degraded joints. Ablations support the contribution of per-joint health estimation, action-degradation modeling, and health-conditioned control.

Show abstract

Synthetic-to-Real Text-Based Person Anomaly Search via Multi-Model Retrieval Fusion and Cross-Encoder Reranking 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Text-based person anomaly search requires a model to retrieve individuals from a large image gallery using natural-language descriptions of both appearance and behavior. This task is challenging because model training relies on synthetic image-text pairs, whereas evaluation is performed on real surveillance images containing domain differences, near-duplicate frames, and many visually similar distractors. We present a multi-model retrieval and reranking framework designed to improve synthetic-to-real transfer. The framework first adapts Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B to synthetic person descriptions using bidirectional contrastive learning, explicit negative sampling, false-negative masking, and domain randomization. It then combines the normalized scores of six complementary Qwen3-VL checkpoints with those of a frozen SigLIP2 model using four-view gallery encoding. The fused retrieval scores identify the strongest candidates, which are jointly evaluated with the query using Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B.
