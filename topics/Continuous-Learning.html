CARES: Coverage-Aware Replay Exemplar Selection for Continual Learning 2026-10-23
📝 17 pages, 2 figures. Includes supplementary material
Show abstract

Replay-based Continual Learning mitigates catastrophic forgetting by keeping a buffer of past samples, making buffer composition critical under a fixed memory budget. Recent coreset-based replay methods improve buffer quality using model-derived gradients, losses, or auxiliary optimization, but incur substantial computational overhead as memory is repeatedly updated. We propose CARES, a continual model-optimization-free approach for buffer construction that instead exploits the geometry of a frozen pretrained feature space using class-aware, density-weighted coverage. We evaluate CARES across balanced, imbalanced, and label-noisy streams under online class- and task-incremental settings. On Tiny-ImageNet, CARES is 2.3 to 8.8 times faster than existing coreset-based selectors. Although CARES achieves this speedup using no training signal from the continual learner, it ranks among the state of the art across balanced, imbalanced, and label-noisy streams under online class- and task-incremental settings, outperforming the strongest baseline by up to 7.58 percentage points under class imbalance while also attaining higher average accuracy on Tiny-ImageNet. Code will be released upon publication

Score-Preserving Continual Lasso under Task Shift 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Continual learning (CL) aims to learn from sequential tasks while mitigating catastrophic forgetting when historical data are no longer accessible. We study continual learning for high-dimensional sparse linear regression, where $\ell_1$ regularization creates a difficulty absent from continual ridge regression: that recursively reusing an $\ell_1$-penalized estimator can propagate earlier shrinkage and accumulate regularization bias across tasks. We propose score-preserving continual Lasso (SP-CL), which separates sparse estimation from state transmission by preserving the historical squared-loss score. Under squared loss, its cumulative estimator exactly recovers a full-history Lasso minimizer. When task coefficients coincide, it inherits the full-history sparse rate. For related task-specific coefficients, SP-CL uses a regularized current-task correction that leaves the transmitted state unchanged and attains the offline source-assisted rate under an oracle related-task condition. We also derive Overall/BWT/FWT decompositions that distinguish historical information loss, task conflict, and current-task specialization, and validate the resulting regimes in simulation.

Deployed machine learning models in real-world IoT and edge/cloud systems face a critical reliability bottleneck: the requirement for online adaptation to non-stationary data streams while operating without access to the full original training dataset. PRISM: Promoting Sampling Diversity of Large Language Models with Implicit Stochastic Modulation 2026-10-23
Show abstract

Large language models (LLMs) exhibit mode collapse, where their output distribution concentrates on familiar semantic modes. When diversity disappears, repeated sampling becomes semantic repetition rather than discovery, making rare but stronger solutions unlikely to surface and leaving self-evolution and continual learning difficult to sustain. Most remedies intervene only at inference time, shaping sam- ples without making diversity a model capability. We propose PRISM, a training-time framework that internalizes diversity within the model. PRISM combines (i) submodular curation that pairs each prompt with diverse-yet-correct multi-teacher solutions, and (ii) stochastic embedding modulation, which turns special-token perturbations into implicit signals for sampling distinct outputs. A theoretical analysis establishes the stability of this mechanism. On WritingPrompts and BookMIA, PRISM moves the quality-diversity frontier outward, achieving the best generation quality in all model-benchmark settings while maintaining the strongest or near-strongest semantic diversity across two open-weight model families. It also improves sampling efficiency on math and code benchmarks. Beyond inference, PRISM yields a stronger foundation for reinforcement learning, sustaining diverse rollouts and mitigating the diversity collapse commonly observed during RL training.

REDAR: Reinforcement-Driven Adaptive Regularisation for Class Incremental Learning 2026-10-22
📝 The 28th International Conference on Digital Image Computing: Techniques and Applications (DICTA 2026)
Show abstract

Regularisation-based class-incremental learning (CIL) methods control the stability-plasticity trade-off through fixed coefficients tuned offline, independently of the continual learning objective and blind to phase-specific variations in data volume, task difficulty, and class similarity. We argue this is a fundamental mismatch and propose REDAR: Reinforcement-Driven Adaptive Regularisation for Class Incremental Learning, which treats regularisation strength selection as a sequential decision problem. At each incremental phase, a lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) agent observes a compact state of current performance metrics and incoming data statistics, and outputs the distillation coefficients for that phase. To respect CIL's constraint on historical data access, the policy is trained entirely on pseudo-CIL tasks constructed from the initial phase, then deployed on the incremental sequence without further adaptation. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet demonstrate that REDAR reduces forgetting and improves forward transfer over fixed and heuristic baselines, and that policies trained on short pseudo-task horizons transfer effectively to longer incremental runs.

RobustNCL: Robustifying Neuromorphic Continual Learning under Transient Faults with Lightweight Detect-and-Cure Strategy 2026-10-22
📝 12 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

In neural networks, continual learning (CL) is a prominent approach for enabling deployed systems to adapt to dynamic operational environments by effectively learning from new unseen data at runtime. Recently, the neuromorphic continual learning (NCL) concept has emerged as an energy-efficient alternative to CL by leveraging sparse event-based operations. However, NCL performance can be undermined by transient faults in the hardware platforms, which may come from high-energy particle strikes at any time. Currently, NCL reliability under transient faults has not been studied, and hence the corresponding fault-tolerance techniques remain unexplored. To address this, we propose a novel RobustNCL framework to improve NCL reliability in the presence of transient faults in the DRAM-based off-chip memory of hardware platforms through a lightweight detect-and-cure strategy. Specifically, it first monitors the presence of faults during weight data access from off-chip memory, and then cures the accessed weights if anomalous signatures are detected. It provides three curing variants: replacement with zero, min/max value, and most-probable value from the original/clean weight distribution. To support this, the weight curing values are stored in the radiation-hardened memory. Experimental results show that, in the presence of high fault rates (e.g., 5x10^{-4} for memory replay), RobustNCL maintains high accuracy close to the accuracy of fault-free models (i.e., within -0.5% accuracy for the best RobustNCL variant) and significantly improves accuracy from the baseline without fault mitigation across different NCL methods and datasets (i.e., Split MNIST, Split Fashion-MNIST, Split CIFAR-10, and Split CIFAR-100), while incurring negligible latency and energy overheads. These results highlight that our RobustNCL framework is a promising approach for enabling reliable and energy-efficient NCL systems.

ENCL: Enabling Efficient Neuromorphic Continual Learning with Local Learning Rule and Sub-Network Freezing Strategy 2026-10-22
📝 9 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Continual learning (CL) in neural networks is a promising learning approach for updating the knowledge of deployed systems based on new data encountered at run time, that have not been learned during the offline training phase. To enable their power/energy-efficient CL processing, neuromorphic continual learning (NCL) concept with sparse event-based operations have recently been introduced. However, state-of-the-art NCL methods typically employ the memory-hungry data/experience replay, coupled with costly global learning rule (i.e., backpropagation-based surrogate gradient). To address these limitations, we propose ENCL, a novel methodology that enables efficient NCL by employing a lightweight local learning rule and sub-network freezing strategy. ENCL leverages the temporally- and spatially-local learning rule to perform efficient credit assignment for training new tasks, alleviating the expensive cost of global learning rule. ENCL also employs a sub-network freezing strategy based on the given tasks to avoid the needs for network architecture expansion, hence further improving the efficiency and scalability of the NCL training process. To efficiently support this, we train each task with a small number of epochs as well as adjusted learning rate and timestep. Experiment results on task-incremental learning (TIL) scenario show that, our ENCL consistently achieves strong performance across different datasets as compared to state-of-the-art works. Specifically, ENCL achieves competitive accuracy comparable to that of the state-of-the-art data/experience replay, while reducing memory cost by up to 88%, NCL training time by up to 51%, average power consumption by up to 40%, and energy consumption by up to 65% across different benchmarking datasets (i.e., Split MNIST, Split Fashion MNIST, Split CIFAR-10, and Split CIFAR-100).

Nested Sparsity for Scalable Pretraining and Continual Learning 2026-10-22
📝 10 pages main body
Show abstract

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) transformers, the standard backbone for modern large-scale deep learning, employ conditional computation: only a sparse subset of model weights is used in each forward pass. In MoE transformers, this sparsity arises in the MoE operator, which generalizes the multi-layer perceptron in the classical transformer. In this work, we explore an alternative mechanism for conditional computation that analogously generalizes the attention operator. The sparsity induced by this mechanism naturally nests (i.e., hierarchically composes) with the sparsity induced by the MoE operator. We show that a transformer employing this nested sparsity mechanism, which we call Nestor, performs better than a conventional MoE transformer at equal compute and equal sparsity in language model pretraining. We also show that Nestor is well-suited for continual pretraining: it can be adapted to longer contexts more effectively than conventional MoEs, as well as adapt more efficiently to new domains while forgetting less on old domains. Furthermore, we demonstrate that these benefits persist as model size, dataset size, and context length grow. Our results demonstrate the potential of alternative conditional computation mechanisms for deep neural network architectures. Code and checkpoints are available on GitHub at https://github.com/Ma-Lab-Berkeley/Nestor .

Fine-Grained Analysis of Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Learning via Algorithmic Stability 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Understanding catastrophic forgetting, in which learning new tasks degrades performance on previously learned tasks, remains a fundamental challenge in continual learning (CL). Existing theoretical analyses often rely on specific model or task structures and offer limited insight into how iterative optimization affects forgetting. To address this gap, we develop an algorithmic stability framework for expected population forgetting under SGD without prescribing a particular model architecture. Specifically, we decompose forgetting into intra-task and cross-task generalization gaps and empirical forgetting. To bound the generalization gaps, we track how a sample perturbation affects updates within a task and propagates through subsequent tasks. We consider constant, intra-task decaying, and continuously decaying learning-rate schedules. Comparing the two decaying schedules allows us to characterize how restarting learning-rate decay at task boundaries affects the propagation of historical sample perturbations. For empirical forgetting, we derive bounds for all three schedules by retaining cumulative gradient alignment along the optimization trajectory, without requiring convexity. Combining these results yields upper bounds on expected forgetting that capture the effects of training set size, the number of updates per task, the total number of tasks, learning-rate schedules, and cumulative gradient alignment. Finally, we conduct experiments on Split CIFAR-100 and Split MiniImageNet to examine the empirical implications of our theoretical findings.

Jev-DLC: Decision-Level Continual Learning from Typed Feedback 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Individual preferences often become visible only after deployment, when a user accepts one decision or revokes it in favor of another. Jev-style typed interfaces make this feedback explicit by identifying the selected action and, under revocation, the decision it replaces. We study how these consequences persist across requests as Decision-Level Continual Learning from Typed Feedback (Jev-DLC). Jev-DLC treats each correction as both evidence about a recurring action identity and a directed relation between the preferred and rejected choices. It preserves these signals in complementary bounded channels above a frozen scorer and combines their scores to shape the next decision. The method retains no raw interaction history, adds no scorer call, and performs no runtime language-model backward pass. We also introduce TCPA-Bench, a chronological suite that credits a correction only through its effect on later decisions. On TCPA-Repeat, Jev-DLC reaches 44.83% user-macro accuracy overall, 2.43 points above Recent-4 history replay. After at least two earlier feedback events, it reaches 56.07%, a 7.54-point margin over the strongest reported comparison at this depth. These results show how explicit typed corrections can become compact causal state for continual personalization.

HERD: Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation for Continual Learning 2026-10-22

Continual learning requires a model to keep acquiring new skills and knowledge without compromising its existing capabilities. Parametric methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting and loss of plasticity. Non-parametric methods, such as prompts, skills, and harnesses, leverage in-context learning but are limited by context length and lossy plain-text compression. To effectively combine their complementary strengths, we propose HERD (Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation), a hybrid continual learning framework that learns efficiently in the harness and internalizes experience into cumulative capabilities via distillation. To Retain or to Adapt? Generalizing Continual Learning 2026-10-21
📝 https://openreview.net/forum?id=C4zRmT9jYO
Show abstract

The Continual Learning (CL) literature has long been driven by the goal of mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This objective rests on an often unstated assumption: that a lifelong learner should approximate the Joint-Task Learning (JTL) solution and retain all previously acquired knowledge. We challenge this retention-centered premise, arguing that in non-stationary environments prioritizing retention can impede real-time adaptation. Shifting the focus to the Average Lifelong Error (ALE), we formalize CL as an online optimization problem governed by the interaction between environmental and learning dynamics. We introduce Transfer Efficiency as a quantitative measure of the tension between Instability, the bias inherited from conflicting past experience, and Transient Error, the optimization cost of learning new tasks from scratch. Under mild convergence conditions, holding across linear and neural network models, this decomposition yields a Critical Task Duration: a closed-form threshold beyond which historical knowledge transitions from a warm-start advantage to an optimization liability whenever retention induces a positive stationary bias. We validate these theoretical predictions on continual image classification and reinforcement learning benchmarks. Finally, by connecting continual learning to the online learning framework of predictable sequences, we show that JTL is only one instance of a broader family of objectives, and we propose a new general class of continual learning algorithms, which we call Predictive Continual Learning. Predictive CL algorithms optimize expected future performance under an explicit, dynamically updated model of future tasks. As a proof of concept, we analyze a Window algorithm that interpolates between JTL and Independent-Task Learning (ITL), outperforming both under controlled distributional drift.

Medical AI systems must remain reliable as data distributions shift across hospitals, imaging devices, and patient populations, yet privacy constraints often prevent storing or revisiting past patient data. Rather than relying on multiple backbones, repeated ViT inferences, or independent layer-wise prompt pools, UniPrompt-CL shares a unified prompt pool across transformer layers and expands it with only a few prompts per new domain. Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by employing a compact draft model to propose candidate tokens that are verified in parallel by a target model. Conventional training relies on soft-label distillation, which forces the draft model to mimic the target's full output distribution, including high-entropy regions that degrade acceptance rates and increase early rejection risk. We propose RCSD (Reinforcement and Continual Learning for Speculative Decoding), a training framework that aligns draft model optimization with the system-level objective of maximizing acceptance length. Memory-Centric Continual Learning for AI Agents 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Autonomous agents powered by foundation models can reason, plan, and use tools, but their fixed weights and context limits hinder sustained learning from experience over long-term deployment. While external memory systems attempt to bridge this gap, they mostly act as passive retrieval caches, evaluated primarily by whether they can recall stored facts or past interactions. Whether memory can drive genuine continual learning remains an open question. In this paper, we introduce Memory-Centric Continual Learning (MCCL), arguing that memory should be judged by whether it improves future actions, not merely what it retrieves. MCCL has three main parts. First, a three-axis taxonomy organizes memory by where state is stored (substrates), what form it takes (assets), and how it is managed (policies). This defines a two-loop lifecycle that updates memory state in an inner loop while refining management policies in an outer loop based on task feedback. Second, an evaluation protocol measures retention under interference, adaptation to change, and task transfer to isolate genuine behavioral gains from simple context expansion. Third, a memory scaling framework models how agent performance scales with experience, storage capacity, and compute budgets across non-parametric and parametric memory. Together, these components reframe agent memory from passive lookup to continuous learning, offering a principled route toward self-improving autonomous agents.

Show abstract

Visual world models have shown strong potential for robot planning, yet long-horizon manipulation remains challenging due to compounding prediction errors, large search complexity, and latent plans that the low-level controller cannot reliably reach. In this work, we introduce Vector-Quantized World Model (VQ-WM), a latent feedback motion planning framework that integrates discrete long-horizon search with continuous visual feedback control: a discrete latent predictor models transitions between vector-quantized latent states, allowing the planner to look far ahead and find a shortest path to the goal through discrete search and a continuous learned controller sequentially reaches the planned states through visual feedback, correcting deviations to mitigate compounding errors. Our solution also outperforms strongest world-models by 13.3--85.3 percentage points on simulation benchmarks spanning manipulation and locomotion, demonstrating consistent progress and new capabilities.

Optimal L2 Regularization in High-dimensional Continual Linear Regression 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted to ALT 2026
Show abstract

We study generalization in an overparameterized continual linear regression setting, where a model is trained with L2 (isotropic) regularization across a sequence of tasks. We derive a closed-form expression for the expected generalization loss in the high-dimensional regime that holds for arbitrary linear teachers. We demonstrate that isotropic regularization mitigates label noise under both single-teacher and multiple i.i.d. teacher settings, whereas prior work accommodating multiple teachers either did not employ regularization or used memory-demanding methods. APRIL: Ambiguity-Aware Expert Routing for Respiratory Sound Domain-Incremental Learning 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Automated respiratory sound classification enables continuous, non-invasive respiratory assessment, yet long-term deployment remains challenging due to domain shifts from evolving recording devices and acquisition periods. When privacy and governance regulations prohibit replaying historical data, sequential learning easily overwrites previously acquired knowledge. Preserving dedicated domain experts prevents parameter interference, but selecting the correct expert at inference is difficult under overlapping acoustic distributions and absent domain labels. To resolve this dilemma, we propose APRIL, an exemplar-free domain-incremental framework for RSC. Built on a shared frozen BEATs backbone, APRIL sequentially inherits and isolates low-rank experts to enable new-domain adaptation without altering earlier models. A multi-level prototype router retrieves the top-two candidate experts and dynamically selects the final model by integrating domain-routing posteriors with classification confidence, adaptively weighted by matching ambiguity. Crucially, average forgetting remains under 1.0% across all protocols, demonstrating robust domain adaptation and long-term knowledge retention without data replay. Overall, APRIL offers a reliable and privacy-preserving paradigm for deploying continuous respiratory sound classification across real-world healthcare settings.

The modality gap is intrinsic to multimodal alignment, where embeddings from different modalities remain partially separated in a shared representation space. In continual learning, CLIP-based vision-language models (VLMs) not only suffer catastrophic forgetting but also experience modality gap drift that disrupts the shared embedding geometry. It also maintains more stable modality gaps and stronger zero-shot retrieval performance on COCO Captions and Flickr30k than competing methods.
