BISECT: Bilevel Spectral Decomposition for Low-Rank Continual Learning 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Exemplar-free continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers increasingly relies on Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), yet prevailing methods govern the low-rank update space through strict subspace isolation. By projecting each incoming task into the approximate null space of historical token activations, isolation-based learners equate any cross-task feature overlap with catastrophic interference. We show that this orthogonality premise induces a structural bottleneck we term \emph{spectral starvation}: when down-projection bases are locked before task optimization, the gradient norm reaching the trainable up-projection is upper-bounded by the energy of current-task features captured by the chosen basis. Because natural visual tasks share low- and mid-level covariance structure, historical null tails contain barely $5.5\%$ of incoming task variance, starving new-class plasticity while blocking positive consolidation along shared directions. We propose BISECT (Bilevel Spectral Decomposition), a continual LoRA framework that partitions a fixed rank-$r$ budget into two orthogonal functional blocks: an invariant carrier spanned by the dominant modes of the joint historical-and-current Gram matrix, and a novelty complement extracted via generalized Rayleigh whitening followed by orthogonal complement projection. After single-task training, BISECT folds both blocks back into the frozen backbone using a closed-form precision gate that scales each rank-1 invariant atom by its current-to-joint Rayleigh ratio while preserving novelty atoms at unit gain. Across eight incremental settings on ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and DomainNet, BISECT outperforms prompt, adapter, and LoRA baselines with zero extra inference parameters or FLOPs, and remains complementary to sparse analytic expansion.

RASP: Read-All, Sparse-Plasticity Continual Learning in a Stationary-Size LoRA 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Parameter-efficient continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers largely relies on a costly premise: preventing catastrophic forgetting requires either spawning a dedicated LoRA branch per session---causing adapter parameters to scale linearly with task horizon---or applying sparse Mixture-of-Experts gating symmetrically to both forward and backward passes, which sacrifices representational rank at test time. We argue that reading consolidated knowledge and writing new task credit are fundamentally asymmetric operations. Because visual features in a shared low-rank space distribute across rank-1 atoms rather than localizing in disjoint task silos, forward inference should read all atoms at full rank capacity, whereas backward optimization should restrict plasticity to a sparse subset of input-aligned channels. We introduce RASP (Read-All, Sparse-Plasticity LoRA), which adapts a single stationary-size adapter across sequential tasks. At each step, a scene-modulated cosine router selects the top-$q$ unlocked atoms to receive gradients, blocks updates on the remaining $r-q$ channels, and penalizes Frobenius Gram coherence between active and non-updated output directions. Across sessions, the router's empirical activation trace and each atom's exact spectral norm form a zero-overhead geometric consolidation index $ω_j = \sqrt{\tilde{c}_j \tildeσ_j}$ that drives Cauchy step attenuation and boundary anchor locking. Across CIFAR-100, CUB-200, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A, RASP matches or surpasses branch-expanding LoRA learners under a strictly constant parameter footprint.

CL3DOD: Understanding the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma in Outdoor Class-Incremental 3D Object Detection 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Class-incremental learning (CIL) for outdoor 3D object detection remains largely underexplored. Existing outdoor studies are limited to single-benchmark evaluations, with SDCoT++ considering KITTI and CL2OD considering nuScenes under different incremental settings. This makes it difficult to assess whether observed continual-learning behavior generalizes across autonomous-driving benchmarks. To the best of our knowledge, we provide the first unified cross-benchmark analysis of outdoor class-incremental 3D object detection across multiple autonomous-driving benchmarks, covering both two-task and multi-stage settings. We investigate: (1) how severely existing 3D detectors forget previously learned classes, (2) how incremental training exposes the stability-plasticity dilemma between knowledge retention and new-class acquisition, and (3) which forms of supervision are most effective for balancing these objectives. We introduce CL3DOD, a class-incremental 3D detection framework that restores missing supervision for previous classes using predictions from the previous-stage detector, preserves old-class responses through query-wise knowledge distillation, and employs EMA-based temporal consistency regularization. Experiments on KITTI and nuScenes reveal a consistent stability-plasticity dilemma across both benchmarks. CL3DOD generally achieves a more favorable stability-plasticity balance than controlled incremental baselines and improves upon published prior methods in the reported benchmark comparisons. This advantage persists across multiple incremental stages, demonstrating strong retention of previously learned knowledge while maintaining effective new-class acquisition. Our code, evaluation setup, and incremental task splits will be publicly released.

Motion-language agents must possess the bidirectional capability to both understand human movement (motion-to-text, M2T) and generate it from natural language (text-to-motion, T2M). While foundational models have achieved strong performance in static settings, autonomous agents operating in dynamic environments must continuously incorporate new motion concepts -- such as novel athletic styles or specialized gestures -- without catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills. We investigate the stability-plasticity trade-off in bidirectional motion-language learning under sequential task exposure. Building on a frozen large language model backbone, we introduce low-rank adaptation (LoRA) variants designed to mitigate inter-task interference. We specifically propose mixture-of-experts architectures that utilize an autoencoder-based router to select task-specific experts at inference time, so that no task-label is needed. To evaluate these methods, we establish a reproducible five-task benchmark derived from HumanML3D through semantic clustering of motion descriptions. Our experimental results demonstrate near-zero forgetting across both M2T and T2M directions while maintaining high generation and captioning quality. Furthermore, we show that hard expert selection via routing significantly outperforms soft expert blending in quality metrics, indicating that preserving expert isolation is critical for maintaining performance in our continual learning setting. Finally, we observe that a divergence between token-level accuracy and downstream generation quality may occur, highlighting the need for more comprehensive evaluation protocols in future research on lifelong motion-language agents.

CARE: Grounded Recursive Self-Improvement for Production Customer-Service Agents 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) in production agents involves transforming accumulated experience into reliable model updates while adapting to evolving business policies. We present CARE (Customer-service Agent Retrospective and Evolve), a grounded RSI framework that operationalizes this process in customer service. Its goal is to replace a frontier proprietary incumbent with an in-house mid-sized model that continues learning after deployment. After a one-time cold start on governed production traces, the current model serves production traffic alongside the incumbent, and its own failures drive the next update. A 24/7 inspection process establishes failures through evidence-blinded, independently reviewed judgments. Attribution separates model errors from defects in Skills, tools, or the harness, routing component failures to repair. Model-attributable trajectories are truncated at the first erroneous decision and paired with verified repairs. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) then updates the model using the same model as teacher, with the diagnosis and repair supplied as privileged information unavailable to the student. To support continued evolution as business requirements change, training mixes failures from different Skill/SOP versions, each judged against the policy in force when it occurred. Candidates run an executable simulation test compiled from the latest SOP and then frozen, followed by offline inspection of the resulting traces. Promotion requires a combined simulation-and-inspection score at least at parity with the deployed version, confirmation through an online comparison with the incumbent, and human approval.

Replay for LLM Continual Learning with Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) distills domain experts into one student but needs every expert at once. When domains arrive one at a time and earlier experts are gone, the previous-stage student can teach replayed prompts of earlier domains inside every stage. Some pipelines instead store every stage snapshot for a final multi-teacher recovery stage. On five domains with a 1.5B student and five public experts, this paper compares both with joint MOPD. It varies the replay budget and the replay signal. The signal is token-level distillation alone or with alignment of hidden states, attention and value relations. Sequential distillation without replay or recovery is 6.2 points below 208-step joint MOPD on a 14-benchmark average and 3.5 points below on the 11 benchmarks outside safety. Without snapshots, replay toward the previous student closes 3.8 of the 6.2 points when the earlier domains share the replay budget of a stage. With a fixed share of seven replay steps per earlier domain and stage, it closes 5.8 over two training runs. This is level with the 5.9 of recovery from all snapshots, both at its default length and when given as many training steps as the fixed share. Alignment terms on replay steps change the average by about one point or less. If refusal and compliance count equally in safety, shared-budget replay ranks first among the trained configurations, and the fixed share stays at or above recovery. Against the shared budget, the fixed share lowers the score on the newest domain. Recovery lowers this score as well, in five of six cases. The replay steps of the fixed share grow quadratically with the number of stages.

InTAct: Interval-based Task Activation Consolidation for Continual Learning 2026-11-01
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 (main track)
Show abstract

Continual learning seeks to acquire new knowledge while preserving previously learned representations. Despite the success of prompt-based methods, they remain fragile in domain-incremental learning scenarios, where shifts in the input distribution cause representation drift in shared layers and lead to forgetting. We introduce InTAct, a method that preserves functional behavior in shared representations without freezing parameters or retaining past data. InTAct constrains updates within activation regions associated with prior domains, while allowing flexible adaptation elsewhere, thereby stabilizing neuron functionality rather than directly restricting parameter values. The approach is architecture-agnostic, integrates seamlessly with existing prompt-based frameworks, and matches or outperforms state-of-the-art methods on domain-incremental benchmarks.

HQNN-CL: Regime-Aware Hybrid Quantum Neural Network with Continual Learning for Financial Forecasting 2026-11-01
📝 20 Pages, 8 Figures
Show abstract

Financial time-series forecasting is still difficult owing to the non-stationary nature of the market, regime changes, and catastrophic forgetting, which makes conventional deep learning models hard to adapt in the long term. To achieve uncertainty-aware robust financial forecasting in the face of changing market conditions, this study proposes a Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network with Continual Learning (HQNN-CL). The proposed framework combines Gaussian Hidden Markov Model (HMM) based regime detection, BiLSTM temporal encoding, adaptive-depth parameterized quantum circuits, Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), experience replay, and probabilistic forecasting in a single architecture. The framework was tested on four real-time stock datasets: Microsoft, Systems Limited, Teradata and Tata Consultancy Services, and compared with seven state-of-the-art forecasting models, including recurrent and Transformer-based models. The experimental results show that HQNN-CL has outperformed other methods on the Systems Limited (RMSE = 3.0149) and Teradata (RMSE = 0.8909) datasets and is within 0.32% and 0.40% of the best baseline method for Microsoft and Tata Consultancy Services, respectively. The proposed framework also generates well-calibrated probabilistic forecasts within the range of 89.5% to 96.5% empirical coverage rate, has zero catastrophic forgetting (BWT = 0.0000) for all sequential learning tasks, and consistently shows positive forward transfer. Ablation and statistical significance analyses also validate the performance of adaptive quantum feature learning and continual adaptation under heterogeneous market conditions, regime-wise.

Can Large Language Model Agents Learn by Watching Tutorial Videos? 2026-11-01
Show abstract

Humans continuously acquire knowledge from carefully crafted materials such as books and tutorial videos, carrying that understanding into future tasks. Millions of such instructional videos are uploaded online every day, yet LLM agents remain unable to exploit this ever-expanding wealth of human teaching. Their capabilities are frozen after training, while parameter updates at scale remain prohibitively expensive. This raises the central question: can LLM agents learn from this vast instructional resource without parameter updates? We introduce Tutorial Agentic Learning (TAL), a nonparametric continual-learning paradigm that consolidates tutorial videos into a persistent, structured tutorialbook memory through a two-stage adversarial process that harvests hard, video-grounded questions and uses them to refine the memory. At inference time, TAL retrieves through a Table-of-Contents (TOC) tree, tying retrieval directly to the model's reasoning rather than to embedding similarity. To study this setting, we introduce VidLearn, a multimodal QA benchmark built from 72 chess tutorial videos spanning 12 openings. On VidLearn, TAL outperforms all evaluated baselines and improves monotonically as more videos are consumed, achieving up to 113% and 91% relative gains over the corresponding vanilla MLLMs on the training and test splits, respectively. In actual gameplay, TAL reaches a stronger opening-phase position than its vanilla counterpart in 69% of head-to-head games. Evaluation in a second domain further demonstrates the generality of TAL. Together, these results highlight instructional videos as a scalable resource for parameter-update-free agent continual learning.

Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in Task-Free Federated Continual Forecasting via Two-Tier Replay under Concept Drift 2026-10-31
📝 Peer-reviewed and accepted for presentation at the AGCS 2026 conference
Show abstract

Federated time-series forecasting is increasingly important in applications where temporal data are distributed across privacy-sensitive clients, such as environmental monitoring, smart cities, and industrial sensing. A central limitation of current continual-learning protocols is that they usually assume predefined task boundaries, whereas deployed forecasting systems receive an uninterrupted stream and must decide autonomously when adaptation is necessary. This paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in task-free federated forecasting under concept drift. We introduce a drift-aware continual-learning protocol in which each client monitors prediction-window RMSE, calibrates a robust median threshold from the offline base period, and triggers adaptation only after persistent degradation. To mitigate forgetting, we propose a TwoTierReplay mechanism that separates fixed offline representative memory from online memory. The offline tier preserves long-term knowledge learned during the base phase, while the online tier captures recent distributional changes after drift. Experiments on the Beijing Multi-Site Air- Quality dataset with 12 clients, three forecasting targets, and three horizons show that TwoTierReplay achieves the best stability-plasticity score in all nine target-horizon configurations. It improves over the strongest baseline by approximately 11.4%. These results suggest that explicit temporal structuring of replay memory is a practical and effective mechanism for task-free federated continual forecasting.

Skill++: Towards Lifelong Agents via Routing-Aware Skill Refinement 2026-10-31
Show abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents extend their capabilities with skills, each with a name, a description, and a body. Under progressive disclosure, an LLM router selects skills by name and description alone and loads only the selected bodies. Skill libraries grow continually as users and agents add new skills. Because the router relies on this metadata, a similar new skill can displace an earlier one, breaking previously correct routing even though the earlier skill and the router are unchanged. This routing interference is aggravated for public skills, whose descriptions may not match their bodies. We formulate the problem as lifelong skill routing, a class-incremental continual learning problem. A solution must keep earlier skills selectable without suppressing new ones. Existing approaches fall short, since continually retraining the router risks catastrophic forgetting and rewriting methods either ignore similar skills or require per-skill feedback. We therefore propose Skill++, which rewrites only the name and description of each incoming skill, without router training or labeled data. The new description states what the body supports, how the skill differs from its closest predecessor, and what related work it excludes. As a library grows to 731 skills over ten sessions, Skill++ selects all required skills for 76.3% of queries, versus 36.4% under the original metadata. Across three benchmarks and five LLM routers, it outperforms all compared approaches, and its gains carry over to the Codex CLI agent harness.

Exo: An Architecture for Self-Improving Systems at Runtime 2026-10-30

Agent harnesses govern how language models act in an environment; designing their policies by hand is difficult, while automated techniques require representative datasets and costly executions. We present Exo, an agent runtime architecture for safe learning during ongoing work that collapses the optimization loop into task execution, allowing agents to revise both their harness and their strategies for further improvement. A minimal protected mechanism layer preserves execution history and governs policy activation while the harness evolves through inspectable code changes. On long-running objectives in an open-world game, Exo reduces model cost by 12-53% relative to Codex and Pi in standalone runs and 45-82% in transfer runs, while matching task completion rates. Implicit Assumptions About Tasks in Continual Learning 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted to the Workshop on Continual Learning in the Era of Foundation Models and Embodied Agents at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Continual learning is the study of how a model accumulates knowledge from data received over time, rather than all at once. In practice, this is typically achieved by dividing a dataset into tasks and presenting them sequentially. Explanations of the resulting measurements take into account factors such as the similarity between segments, the rate at which the data changes and the order of presentation. Each of these factors can be defined in terms of the underlying data, the partition imposed on it, or the learner that encounters it. These levels do not necessarily coincide, and it is usually left implicit which level a reported quantity refers to. We separate these and identify which properties acquire a value at each level. In controlled teacher--student experiments, we demonstrate that partitioning the same underlying trajectory at different levels of granularity, changing the order of presentation, and varying the similarity structure of the underlying data all result in different measured outcomes. We review representative benchmarks to determine which of these properties are stated. Where the underlying data cannot be inspected, as in foundation models built on undisclosed pre-training corpora, its properties are inherited by the model's initial state, and the training applied on top of it can be described.

ResMerge: Residual Policy Learning and Merging for Continual Adaptation of Pre-trained Robot Policies 2026-10-30
📝 9 pages. Code: https://github.com/ResMerge/resmerge-official
Show abstract

Continual adaptation to new tasks is essential for robots deployed in the real world, but adapting pre-trained policies remains challenging due to parameter growth and the need for continual demonstration collection. Parameter-Efficient Continual Fine-Tuning (PECFT) isolates task-specific updates, but adapter-per-task designs require demonstrations and grow with the task set. We propose ResMerge, a two-stage framework that learns mergeable residual policies atop a frozen pre-trained policy: residual reinforcement learning (RL) acquires task-specific corrections without additional expert demonstrations during adaptation, and parameter-space merging consolidates compatible residuals to reduce stored parameters. We introduce Residual-Field Consistency (RFC), a simple, bounded, and interpretable metric that assesses compatibility in residual action-field space by comparing correction direction and magnitude. Experiments on LIBERO across two backbones and on a real-world Franka robot show improved continual learning performance over strong PECFT baselines, while selective merging reduces the residual-parameter footprint. ImplicitHop: Evaluating knowledge integration across context and memory 2026-10-30
📝 6 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

The ability to integrate new information and deduce its consequences is central to learning, understanding, and reasoning in large language models (LLMs). Whether information arrives during training or in-context, models must not only store local facts, but propagate their long-range implications globally. To explore information integration in LLMs, we introduce two complementary natural language benchmarks for large-scale evaluation of deductive reasoning as a function of inference depth. The first is a formal graph benchmark enabling controlled evaluation over a wide range of graph topologies and integration tasks; the second is a knowledge composition benchmark grounded in real-world knowledge graphs. We use these benchmarks to compare knowledge integration from information encoded in context versus information encoded in model weights during pretraining, evaluating a set of open models implicitly, without chain of thought. We find that accuracy decays sharply with increased composition length for both pretrained and in-context knowledge. Information presented in-context, however, substantially outperforms deduction over information encoded in weights. These results quantify a fundamental limitation of current models with direct implications for prompt-based control of agent behavior and for continual learning strategies, and they provide a foundation for developing training, prompting, and integration methods that support deeper reasoning.

Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm 2026-10-29
📝 67 pages, 31 figures, 22 tables
Show abstract

Foundation models are typically trained in stages: pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), with changes in both data and learning algorithms. We show that this algorithmic separation is not fundamental. By deriving suitable RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions, we place all of these stages under a single likelihood-based training algorithm, unified training. This perspective turns the foundation-model training lifecycle into a problem of choosing which data to train on, in what proportions, and when, with conventional staged training as a special case. We instantiate this framework in a unified training system that mixes static data and RL generations within a single trainer. Across controlled and real-world experiments, unified training improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities, and provides a stronger starting point for continual learning. This work lays the groundwork for a unified science of foundation-model training, connecting mathematical principles, training infrastructure, and the development of model capabilities.

AutoHebb: Self-Modifying Neural Networks that Zero-Shot Infer and Continually Learn 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Humans and animals have a unique capacity to generalize and learn in novel circumstances, including "0-shot" inference (ZI) and continual learning (CL) -- two powerful cognitive abilities. Despite this importance, efforts to model ZI and CL are seriously limited in several ways. First, existing models typically lack components critical to ZI and CL in the brain, namely, strong inductive biases (for ZI) and autonomous control of learning (for CL). Second, models can be less tractable to analysis if model components are overly complex (lacking simplicity), or too task-restricted if model components are overly bespoke (lacking expressivity). Here we introduce autonomous Hebbian networks ("AutoHebb"), a class of online learning feedforward neural networks that realize strong inductive biases and autonomously controlled learning, and are both simple and expressive. Unlike standard models that learn via externally controlled optimization (e.g. gradient descent), AutoHebb networks combine external feedback with internally generated activity to produce a global learning signal ('neuromodulator') fed back to the network. The neuromodulator in turn gates a biologically grounded local learning rule (3-factor Hebbian rule) that updates networks' plastic weights. Using meta-learning to search for fixed (non-plastic) weights, we find that AutoHebb networks (1) succeed on ZI tasks, (2) show emergent (untrained) ZI/CL abilities, (3) admit reverse-engineering into explicit algorithms, and (4) outperform modern learning models on ZI/CL tasks. Lastly, performance comparisons to related models implied an essential role for three model components: meta-learning, Hebbian plasticity, and autonomous control of learning. These findings indicate that AutoHebb may be useful in hypothesis generation, analysis, and discovery of biologically grounded forms of learning and generalization.

GUI-AC: Enhancing Continual Learning in GUI Agents 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs) serve as the dominant medium for human-computer interaction, yet building GUI agents that generalize across the vast diversity of real-world interface environments, with the same flexibility and robustness that humans naturally exhibit, remains unsolved. Notably, GUI data are inherently non-stationary: the continual emergence of previously unseen interface instances (e.g., novel domains and resolutions) induces persistent distribution shifts, significantly impeding the continual learning of existing GUI agents. Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) has attracted considerable attention as a promising approach. Nevertheless, RFT exhibits pronounced instability in its grounding capability, where rollout outcomes are highly imbalanced and fixed clipping can suppress valuable exploration. More importantly, policy probability alone does not reveal the semantic role of a grounding prediction. To address these challenges, we propose GUI-AC, a continual RFT framework that jointly characterizes grounding rollouts through advantage and policy probability. This yields a four-region view of grounding behavior and enables targeted optimization of Exploration and Overconfidence. GUI-AC further introduces grounding certainty to support two core mechanisms: (i) Adaptive Advantage, which strengthens the negative correction for overconfident grounding failures; and (ii) Dynamic Clipping, which relaxes the upper clipping bound for exploratory grounding rollouts. Extensive experiments show that GUI-AC consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/GUI-AC-Code.

AdalReID: Auto-selected Knowledge Adapters for Lifelong Person Re-identification 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted by TPAMI
Show abstract

Lifelong Person Re-Identification (LReID) extends traditional ReID by requiring systems to continually learn from non-overlapping datasets across different times and locations, adapting to new identities while preserving knowledge of previous ones. Existing approaches, either rehearsal-free or rehearsal-based, still suffer from the problem of catastrophic forgetting since they try to cram diverse knowledge into one fixed model. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a novel framework AdalReID, that adopts knowledge adapters and a parameter-free auto-selection mechanism for lifelong learning. Concretely, we incrementally build distinct adapters to learn domain-specific knowledge at each step, which can effectively learn and preserve knowledge across different datasets. Meanwhile, the proposed auto-selection strategy adaptively calculates the knowledge similarity between the input set and the adapters. On the one hand, the appropriate adapters are selected for the inputs to process ReID, and on the other hand, the knowledge interaction and fusion between adapters are enhanced to improve the generalization ability of the model. Extensive experiments are conducted to demonstrate the superiority of our AdalReID, which significantly beats SOTAs by about 5\% and 3\% mAP on seen and unseen domains.

Surprise Alone Learns Lies: Evidence-Gated Continual Learning for LLMs from Streaming Claims 2026-10-29
📝 21 pages, 5 figures. Code: https://github.com/Metalesaek/sura-metales
Show abstract

Continual learning methods for large language models increasingly decide what to learn by surprise, keeping the inputs the model finds least predictable. This rule is unsafe when the stream contains lies. A planted falsehood about something the model knows has low probability under the model's belief, so it is highly surprising by construction, and a surprise-driven learner absorbs nearly every such lie, at a rate similar to random selection with the same budget. We study the problem with LoRA-adapted students (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B) that learn from a stream of true and false claims about invented people and real CounterFact facts. We report four findings. (1) A verifier key, a judge that must approve each claim, removes lies to the extent that its verifier is accurate; real LLM judges are cautious and reject unsourced truths and even source-backed updates. (2) Calibrated regression tests on unrelated knowledge slow learning down without filtering out lies. (3) The original model's own prior flags claims that contradict what it knows, without a judge. It separates lies about known facts from true new facts (AUC 0.78-0.83 across three models) but not from legitimate updates of the same kind of fact (AUC 0.53). It saves 43% of judge calls, but an attacker with access to the model can choose lies that pass it. (4) If trusted sources exist that an attacker cannot plant, routing contradicted claims to evidence recovers legitimate updates and the combined system has the lowest poison rate among learned claims; when an attacker plants matching sources for 30% of the lies, the system ends up worse than the judge alone. The acceptance signals answer different questions, and none of them decides on its own whether a claim is true.

Always Learning, Always Mixing: Efficient and Simple Data Mixing All the Time 2026-10-28
📝 NeurIPS 2026 camera ready
Show abstract

Data mixing decides how to combine different sources or types of data and is a consequential problem throughout language model training. In pretraining, data composition is a key determinant of model quality; in continual learning and adaptation, it governs what is retained and acquired. Yet existing data mixing methods address only one phase of this lifecycle at a time: some require smaller proxy models tied to a single training phase, others assume a fixed domain set, and continual learning lacks principled guidance altogether. We argue that data mixing is fundamentally an online decision making problem---one that recurs throughout training and demands a single, unified solution. We introduce OP-Mix (On-Policy Mix), a data mixing algorithm that operates across pretraining, midtraining, and instruction tuning. Our main insight is that candidate data mixtures can be cheaply simulated by interpolating between low-rank adapters trained directly on the current model, eliminating separate proxy models and ensuring the search is always grounded in the model's actual learning dynamics. Across the language model training lifecycle, OP-Mix consistently finds near-optimal mixtures while using a fraction of the compute of the baselines. In pretraining, OP-Mix improves upon training without mixing by 6.3% in average perplexity. For continual learning, OP-Mix matches the performance of both retraining and on-policy distillation while using 66% and 95% less overall compute, respectively. OP-Mix suggests a different view of language model training: not a sequence of distinct phases, but a single continuous process of learning from data.

Routed Continual Learning 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Agents deployed at scale repeatedly encounter related problems, and it is natural to let them learn from each other's experience rather than starting over each time. Unfortunately, there is no single way to retain that experience that works well across settings, and no way to know in advance which future problems a given experience will help with. A global history preserves every detail but becomes expensive and mixes together experience that may not belong together; compressing that history into one fixed representation limits the cost but can throw away valuable details. We introduce Routed Continual Learning (Routed CL), an architecture that decides how to partition and retain experience. Online Continual Learning (OCL) requires models to learn from non-stationary data streams under a strict single-pass constraint, making them highly susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. While existing studies have explored various strategies from data, architecture, and optimization perspectives, the optimizer often directly uses generic approaches such as SGD and AdamW. In this work, we reveal a new link between optimization dynamics and OCL needs by recasting Polyak-Ruppert averaging as the engine of "plasticity" and Primal averaging as the anchor of "stability". This inspires our novel optimizer, SPIN (Stability-Plasticity INterpolation), which explicitly decouples plasticity and stability by interpolating between a fast-moving "plasticity" sequence and an adaptive "stability" sequence. We theoretically demonstrate that SPIN implicitly employs an inverse Hessian approximation, providing crucial Tikhonov regularization to damp noisy, single-sample Hessian estimates and mitigate forgetting. More importantly, SPIN can be seamlessly integrated into existing OCL methods by taking resulted gradients as input and replacing standard optimizers. Extensive experiments show that using SPIN instead of standard optimizers yields consistent and significant performance gains across multiple OCL approaches, including the challenging rehearsal-free setting and under strict memory constraints.

Dense linear probes assess task readability from language model (LM) representations, but do not directly reveal how many features suffice for prediction. We measure readout sparsity with \(k^*\), the smallest feature count found by a fixed search protocol that recovers 95% of dense-probe AUROC above chance. We evaluate 19 LMs from five labs on 14 core tasks. Ten base models give the nine reliably readable tasks similar feature counts and nearly the same order. This agreement extends to the selected features themselves, which on three tasks show cross-model response correspondence beyond class-mean differences and a label-correlation-matched null. Instruction-tuned deployment selectively reduces \(k^*\), with both weights and input format contributing. In controlled synthetic experiments across 20 models, successfully learned targets admit single-feature readouts, whereas readable controls require tens of features. Reference-model \(k^*\) is associated with label requirements across eight students, following an empirical scaling of \(N^*\propto(k^*)^{0.65}\). After continual learning, saved heads can fail while linear readability remains high, and recovery requires fewer labels than initial task learning under the tested procedures. Pruning reduces linear readability, with wider pre-pruning readouts generally associated with larger losses. These findings distinguish task readability from readout sparsity and establish their complementary value for predicting learning requirements and diagnosing LM changes.

Adapting a forecasting system to a changing world classically means retraining. We ask whether the task of continually learning regimes for time series forecasting can be represented by a piece of text: a guidance block, evolved by outcome-driven reflection and used in a frozen LLM forecaster's prompt. On a refreshed multimodal benchmark (weekly search indices, daily currencies, daily commodities, each window paired with web-retrieved news), we run GEPA-style prompt evolution under strict contamination control to prevent the data leakage common in timeseries benchmarks: the forecaster's knowledge cutoff predates every evaluated window. A 900-word evolved guidance carried by Llama-3.3-70B beats TimeSeries Foundation Models (TSFMs) on held-out data (GM MASE 0.776 vs. 0.898 for TabPFN-TS-3), transfers to more recent models without measurable loss, and costs dollars rather than GPU-days to update. We characterize the loop: out-of-loop gains concentrate in the first reflection, in-loop improvements overstate out-of-loop gains 2-3x unless an untouched monitor arbitrates, and error-only objectives silently suppress the text modality. We provide two extensions. First, compiling the guidance into executable code with a TSFM as a callable tool recovers most of the gain deterministically. Second, ablations show the advantage widens at short horizons and that removing the news text does not hurt the guided forecaster, consistent with the text's value being distilled into policy during learning. We argue that continual prompt evolution is a practical, training-free alternative to retraining for multimodal forecasting.

Language Guidance for Supervised Vision Training: An Empirical Study of Generalization 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success on vision benchmarks, yet they continue to struggle with many generalization challenges. Supervised vision training relies on one-hot labels, which provide limited information about semantic structure and shared attributes between classes. This limited supervision can leave visual representations vulnerable to distribution shifts, spurious correlations, texture bias, adversarial perturbations, and forgetting in sequential learning settings. We study whether pretrained language models can serve as lightweight auxiliary supervision for vision training without requiring paired image-text data, prompt engineering, or contrastive objectives. Specifically, we evaluate two forms of language guidance, Explicit Language Guidance (ExLG) and Implicit Language Guidance (ImLG). We conduct a comprehensive evaluation across six generalization regimes, including in-distribution, out-of-distribution generalization, shortcut and spurious correlation resistance, texture and shape bias, adversarial robustness, and continual learning. Our analyses show that the two mechanisms have complementary strengths, with explicit guidance consistently benefiting in-distribution, low-data performance, and continual learning retention, while implicit guidance is often more useful in shortcut-sensitive settings and under stronger adversarial perturbations. Importantly, both are lightweight and add minimal parameters and training overhead. The analyses characterize when language-derived structure helps supervised vision training and provides a practical roadmap for using off-the-shelf pretrained models from another modality as auxiliary supervision.

Survival analysis on Whole Slide Images (WSIs) is important in computational pathology for prognosis estimation and treatment planning. However, existing survival models are typically trained independently for each cancer cohort, making continual adaptation computationally expensive for gigapixel-scale WSIs. In this study, we propose MergeSurv, a merging-based continual learning framework for WSI survival analysis. A pathology vision-language foundation model is independently fine-tuned on each task, and the learned parameters are sequentially merged into a unified model without storing previous training data. We further investigate two inference strategies: One-for-All (OFA) and Voting-Expert Aggregation (VEA). Experiments on four TCGA cohorts demonstrate that MergeSurv outperforms naive fine-tuning as well as representative regularization-based and rehearsal-based continual learning methods, while effectively reducing catastrophic forgetting. The results suggest that model merging is a promising direction for scalable and privacy-preserving continual learning in computational pathology.
