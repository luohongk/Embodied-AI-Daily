Consistent Distributed Invariant EKF SLAM with Point and Object Features 2026-10-22
📝 Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)
Show abstract

Multi-robot SLAM allows robots to improve their estimates by exchanging map information, but repeated exchanges create unknown correlations between local estimators. Covariance intersection (CI) can fuse estimates without tracking these correlations. In right-invariant extended Kalman filter (RI-EKF) SLAM, however, point features obtain their invariant errors through the robot rotation, so a neighbor's map estimate is expressed in its own error coordinates and is correlated with its pose, and cannot be fused by CI directly. This paper presents Joint-CI, a unified fusion method for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed point--object maps. Using a neighboring robot's local posterior and an inter-robot observation, a joint estimate of the receiving robot's pose and the common features is constructed in the receiving robot's error coordinates, retaining the robot--feature and feature--feature correlations. Partial-state CI then fuses this estimate with the receiving robot's local posterior. We show that the unified formulation and the fusion preserve the six unobservable directions associated with global rotation and translation, and that communication and fusion preserve first-order covariance consistency given consistent local posteriors. Monte Carlo simulations and a real-world dataset-derived experiment show that Joint-CI improves estimation accuracy while maintaining consistent, mildly conservative uncertainty.

Calibration-free IMU-based Relative Orientation Estimation under Dynamic Human Motion 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages, 7 figures, 1 table. Submitted to IEEE Sensors Journal
Show abstract

Recent magnetometer-free inertial measurement unit (IMU) methods can reduce drift in joint motion estimation using rigid-body constraints, but they typically require pre-calibrated sensor locations, assumptions of specific joint models (e.g., hinge joints) or limited motion types, or an external reference for global orientation. Furthermore, challenges related to high-frequency impacts (e.g. at foot strikes during gait) and numerical differentiation of noisy measurements have not been explicitly addressed. We propose an extended Kalman filter that estimates relative IMU orientation and sensor location simultaneously, requiring neither offline sensor location calibration nor a global orientation anchor. A systematic simulation study shows that the quality of the relative orientation and the sensor location estimates depends on different motion-intensity sources: the joint center acceleration and the relative angular velocity, respectively. A low-pass filter (LPF) is used to mitigate real-signal outliers before applying the rigid-body constraint. When applied to the OpenSense dataset with real IMU measurements across four subjects with three lower-body joint types during walking and complex tasks including various motion types, the proposed method achieves an overall relative orientation error of 2.61°, compared with 10.40° for a baseline using offline-estimated sensor locations and 7.43° for a baseline using marker-derived sensor locations. These results show that the proposed calibration-free estimation framework handles real IMU data effectively and achieves accurate relative orientation without offline sensor location calibration.

Consensus-Based Distributed Filtering for Angles-Only Tracking in Cislunar Space 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted for presentation at IEEE NAVICON 2026
Show abstract

This paper investigates consensus-based distributed filtering strategies for multi-observer angles-only navigation in the cislunar space. The objective is to estimate the inertial state of a target spacecraft using only line-of-sight measurements collected by a network of observer spacecraft. This problem is particularly relevant for autonomous cislunar navigation, where ground support may be limited, and centralized fusion architectures may be problematic due to communication and computational constraints, and single-point failure risks. Three distributed filtering approaches are considered: the Distributed Extended Kalman Filter, the Information-Weighted Consensus Filter, and the Kalman Consensus Filter. All methods share the same local prediction and measurement-processing steps, but differ in the way information is exchanged and fused across the network. Their performance is evaluated and compared to a centralized Extended Kalman Filter, adopted as benchmark, and independent local filters in a high-fidelity cislunar scenario. Numerical simulations show that the distributed approaches significantly improve estimation accuracy with respect to independent filters. Monte Carlo sensitivity analyses are then conducted to characterize the robustness of the considered methods to finite consensus iterations and prior misalignment among the agents.

JAXbounce: A Continuous-Control Pong-Like Benchmark for Active Inference Beyond Low Dimensional Environments 2026-10-20
📝 Published in the Springer CCIS series as part of the 7th International Workshop on Active Inference (IWAI26)
Show abstract

We introduce JAXbounce, a continuous control pong-like benchmark designed to evaluate active inference beyond discrete, low dimensional environments. JAXbounce combines continuous states and stochastic dynamics in discrete time with flexible and composable observation models that allow representing the environment as a state vector or a pixel grid, corruptible through the rich family of noise models we provide. It is fully differentiable and parallelisable in JAX. We run over 10,000 agent simulations on our environment comparing a baseline deterministic solution, to a state-based active inference agent with Unscented Kalman Filter perception and expected free energy planning, and a pixel based agent whose measurement model is a pretrained neural network encoder. All agents are evaluated across three scenarios of increasing difficulty with eight different noise conditions. Our experiments characterise how performance of continuous state agents scales with planning, planning horizons, observation modalities and knowledge of the noise process, exhibiting preference-oriented behaviour even in the presence of increasing uncertainty. We release JAXbounce as a reproducible benchmark for continuous control active inference research.

Efficient Bayesian Filtering and Inference for Integro-Difference Equation Models 2026-10-20
📝 19 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables; supplementary material (11 pages, 21 figures, 8 tables); accompanying Python package at the repository https://gitlab.com/tatephughes/JAX-IDEM
Show abstract

Integro-Difference Equation (IDE) models are a class of dynamic spatio-temporal models which describe the temporal evolution of a spatial process, usually to model advection and diffusion of some random field over time. While several papers have focused on inference and performance of these models, little attention has been given to computational comparisons of various methods of computing posterior densities and performing inference. We address this problem in two parts. Firstly, in inference for these models, Kalman filtering (and smoothing) is often used; either to compute marginal likelihoods, or to sample from the hidden process conditional on the rest of the parameters of the model. There are many variations of the Kalman filter and smoother with substantially varying performance profiles and stability: the standard Kalman filter, its square-root form, its information form, to name a few. The information filters, when set up correctly, can compute the likelihood in $\mathcal O(nr^2 + r^3)$ time per time point ($n$ being the number of observations per time point), where the computation in $n$ can be done in parallel, on models with independent observation error. In this paper, we analyse these forms in the context of IDEs, considering computational speed and stability. Secondly, there are two main methods of inference used for the kind of dynamical system that IDEs can be reduced to; directly using the marginal likelihood without the hidden state, or constructing a block Gibbs sampler with the hidden state. We compare these two approaches in terms of computational efficiency and practical performance in the IDE setting. Using a block Gibbs sampler for inference on the system parameters proves to be more efficient in most cases, with a specific real-data example providing an exception.

SpecKCast: Spectral Kalman Forecasting for Diffusion Acceleration 2026-10-19
📝 34 pages, 19 figures
Show abstract

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve high-fidelity image and video generation, but they suffer from substantial inference costs due to repeated transformer evaluations across many denoising steps. Feature caching reduces this cost by reusing features across denoising steps. Recent forecasting methods extend this idea by extrapolating features to skipped steps instead of reusing them. However, existing forecasting methods extrapolate the features they predict uniformly, even though the spatial-frequency components of these features change most at different stages of denoising. They also overlook that denoising progresses nonuniformly, so equal step intervals can correspond to very different amounts of change. These mismatches amplify forecast errors as more steps are skipped, limiting the achievable acceleration. We propose SpecKCast, a training-free method that forecasts the model output in the frequency domain. SpecKCast applies a 2D discrete cosine transform (DCT) to the output and tracks each coefficient with a lightweight Kalman filter along a band-specific time coordinate derived from the noise schedule. The filter tracks each coefficient's value and rate of change, and the transformer is evaluated only when the filter's forecast uncertainty exceeds a threshold. Experiments on FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, Wan2.1-14B, and HunyuanVideo show that SpecKCast achieves a better trade-off between acceleration and fidelity than existing caching and forecasting methods, reaching up to 6x speedup with less than 0.05 GiB of additional memory.

Channel-Dependent State Space Model for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF) is critical across many real-world domains. Existing deep learning approaches fall into two paradigms with distinct limitations: channel-independent (CI) methods unconditionally ignore cross-variable dependencies and model only temporal dynamics, while channel-dependent (CD) methods consider both but typically rely on architectural compromises to mitigate overfitting and computational overhead. We therefore propose Chameleon, a specialized CD state space model (SSM) that enables data-dependent, fine-grained interactions across variables while scaling linearly with their number. By connecting selective SSMs with the Kalman filter, we leverage the missing measurement update in the former for cross-variable modeling while preserving the SSM backbone for robust temporal modeling. We further identify favorable inductive biases of GatedDeltaNet for time series, adapt it as our backbone, and improve generalization through additional techniques, including a previously unexplored stochastic perturbation of reversible instance normalization. On strongly dependent ODE and PEMS datasets, Chameleon achieves the best MSE and MAE across all settings, while its CI ablation and prior CD methods incur 61-178% higher MSE on average. Across 28 standard benchmark settings, Chameleon also achieves better MSE and MAE than each baseline in at least 27 and 22 cases, respectively. Training-time and peak-memory analyses on Traffic and ETT further demonstrate competitive efficiency and favorable memory scalability across different variable counts.

A Causal Framework for Evaluating Jointly Longitudinal Outcomes and Surrogate Markers: A State-Space Approach 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Surrogate markers offer the potential to reduce the burden of data collection by replacing costly or invasive primary outcomes with more accessible measurements, provided that they can faithfully indicate the effectiveness of a treatment. However, appropriate evaluation of a surrogate is particularly complex in longitudinal studies, where both outcomes and surrogates can evolve dynamically over time and interest lies not only in the treatment effect at one time, but rather treatment effects that may vary along the entire trajectory. In this paper, we develop a statistical framework for surrogate evaluation when both the surrogate and primary outcome are measured over time. Specifically, within the potential outcomes framework, we propose a formal causal definition of the proportion of the treatment effect on the longitudinal primary outcome that is explained by the treatment effect on the longitudinal surrogate. For estimation, we leverage state-space models, together with the Kalman filter and smoother, enabling efficient estimation of treatment effects under realistic conditions of temporal evolution and patient-level variability. We introduce a nonparametric bootstrap strategy for state-space models, a temporal homogeneity test, and demonstrate the finite-sample performance of our proposed methods via a simulation study and application to a diabetes clinical trial.

SA-LIVO: Efficient LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry with Subspace-Aware Degeneracy Handling 2026-10-17
📝 Preprint under review; not the final version, which may differ after peer review. 17 pages (11 main text + 6 supplementary material), 8 figures

Show abstract

Light detection and ranging (LiDAR)-inertial-visual odometry fuses geometric depth with visual measurements, but its sensors fail independently: LiDAR in under-constrained geometry, vision under poor illumination or texture. Weak information is harmless, since the filter then relies on the other sensor or the inertial prediction; over-confident information is not. Existing countermeasures act on weak information or switch vision by hand-set thresholds. This article presents SA-LIVO, which fuses vision through explicit, online-estimated error models. A frame-level noise scale, estimated by covariance matching, absorbs whole-image errors, and Schmidt filtering carries the correlation of reused observations. Subspace-aware information fusion models the error shared by a frame's observations as a pose offset capping the visual information per direction; the exact fusion attenuates vision only above that cap and reduces to the information sum when the visual model is consistent. LiDAR and visual residuals share one invariant extended Kalman filter loop. On 24 handheld and aerial sequences, SA-LIVO is the most accurate or tied on 22 and stays bounded where others diverge; on the HILTI 2022 benchmark it needs 13.4 ms per frame on a laptop processor and 2.0-6.2x less memory than FAST-LIVO2, R3LIVE, and SR-LIVO.

Causal adaptive intensity estimation for non-stationary point processes: a Kalman-kernel duality framework 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Estimating the instantaneous conditional intensity of a non-stationary point process from a single realization of discrete event timestamps is a fundamental problem in computational neuroscience, quantitative finance, and network monitoring. Traditional adaptive kernel methods achieve variable bandwidths through Abramson's square-root law, yet existing implementations require non-causal access to the complete event record and incur $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ or at best $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$ computational cost. Causal alternatives based on reciprocal inter-arrival times, meanwhile, suffer from systematic divergence under Poisson and bursty arrival dynamics due to Jensen's inequality. This paper addresses both limitations by establishing a formal duality between steady-state Kalman filtering and adaptive exponential kernel smoothing. An endogenous state-space observer is constructed in which Poisson counting statistics dictate the measurement noise ($R \propto λ$) and geometric diffusion governs the process volatility ($Q \propto λ^2$). Under these physically motivated axioms, the algebraic Riccati equation autonomously yields an effective smoothing bandwidth scaling as $τ\propto λ^{-1/2}$, giving a causal, recursive, $\mathcal{O}(1)$ per-step realization of square-root adaptive bandwidth scaling. A second-order constant-velocity extension produces a $λ^{-1/4}$ scaling that reduces approximation bias, and a probabilistic dormant-state gating mechanism suppresses phantom intensity leakage during structural zeros. Synthetic benchmarks together with neural and financial data show that the observer adapts across orders of magnitude in intensity with an empirical per-step cost of approximately 30\,$μ$s.

Retrospective Evaluation of Bluetooth RSSI Fingerprinting for Bin Localization on an Industrial Shop Floor 2026-10-15
📝 2 figures, 1 CSV
Show abstract

This paper evaluates an archival Bluetooth Low Energy received signal strength indicator (RSSI) dataset collected for bin localization on an industrial shop floor. The installation used 24 fixed relays positioned on walls and Bluetooth beacons attached to bins, with each relay applying a Kalman filter to its own readings in firmware before publishing them. The primary survey produced 19,034 usable two-minute fingerprint samples across 90 known positions, with 23 relay measurements available as model features. We compare calibrated path-loss multilateration with two radio-map estimators: distance-weighted $k$-nearest neighbours and Random Forest regression. A random 70/30 row split measures recognition at surveyed positions, while a coordinate-grouped split reserves 27 entire positions for testing and measures interpolation to unseen positions. Random Forest achieved a median error of 0.66~m for surveyed positions, with 77.9% of estimates within 3~m and 96.3% within 10~m. For unseen positions, median error increased to 9.42~m and 55.5% of estimates were within 10~m. Path-loss multilateration produced a 19.41~m median error on unseen positions, close to the 19.62~m mean-position baseline. The results support RSSI fingerprinting for a fixed inventory of surveyed bin positions, but not precise continuous localization between survey points. Git history dates the original implementation to September 7, 2019. The evaluation code in this paper was written in October 2026 and is reported separately from the seven-year-old prototype.

ARC-SORT: Adaptive, Robust, Compensated Multi-Object Tracking with Interacting Multiple Models 2026-10-15
📝 14 pages, 4 figures, 13 tables; supplementary material included as appendix; qualitative video as ancillary file
Show abstract

Multi-object tracking in sports broadcasts is hard for two reasons: athletes move fast and change direction abruptly, and the camera pans and zooms at the same time. We present ARC-SORT (Adaptive, Robust, Compensated SORT), a tracking-by-detection tracker that trains nothing and runs in real time. Its design rule: the tracker measures its own uncertainty, and that measurement decides how it acts. An Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) Kalman filter gives every track a predictive covariance, and we turn it into a per-track search region: the less reliable the motion prediction, the larger the region in which a detection may match (uncertainty-adaptive Expansion IoU, EIoU). On identical inputs, this continuous coupling is what matters: the same covariance used as a hard Mahalanobis gate hurts at every threshold we swept, and richer covariance summaries add nothing measurable. When two tracks cross, the assignment cost itself reports whether the decision was close. ARC-SORT keeps such pairs under review and applies at most one corrective identity swap once post-crossing appearance evidence shows the first decision was wrong; this adds +1.0 HOTA (higher order tracking accuracy) on both the SportsMOT and DanceTrack test sets at under a millisecond per frame. For players who leave the frame for seconds, motion extrapolation carries no information, so our offline re-identification drops the usual motion gate and links tracklets by appearance alone. ARC-SORT reaches 81.82 HOTA on the SportsMOT test set with the fewest identity switches of the 69 leaderboard entries as of August 2026 and tied-third multi-object tracking accuracy (MOTA, 97.09), and 62.1 HOTA on DanceTrack test with the same association settings. On Deep-EIoU's public detections and embeddings it reaches 80.31 HOTA, +3.1 over Deep-EIoU. It runs at about 50 frames per second on a laptop RTX 3060 GPU. Code will be released.

Inertially Significant Humanoid Teleoperation via Whole Body Model Predictive Control and Online Parameter Estimation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

The effective deployment of humanoid robots in practical tasks of interest will require a capability of sensing and adapting to variations in inertial parameters, as occurs during heavy tool use or when carrying significant payload. Such payloads constitute a significant proportion of the combined payload-robot system, the effects of which cannot be ignored by delegating to feedback control. This paper showcases a general solution to this problem by integrating a whole body model predictive controller (WB-MPC) with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online estimation of fully physically consistent time-varying inertial parameters. These estimates are fed through to the WB-MPC's full rigid body dynamics model, resulting in more accurate feedforward torques for task demands under external load. Additionally, a virtual reality (VR) interface is provided that enables an operator to provide task-specific references without regard to payload while delegating dynamic balance to the WB-MPC. The framework is validated with simulation and hardware experiments on a humanoid system.

Towards Accurate State Estimation: Motion Dynamics Kalman Filter for 3D Multi-Object Tracking 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Robust 3D state estimation in multi-object tracking is crucial for object localization in self-driving vehicles. Misalignment between the Kalman filter (KF) motion model and an object's motion can cause state drift, particularly in distant and occlusion cases. The static motion model assumption in the conventional KF is widely used in 3D MOT due to its low latency; however, its estimation accuracy degrades as the object's motion disaligns with the chosen model due to traffic. Although recent work introduces a multimodel KF that runs multiple motion models simultaneously to improve state robustness, it incurs significant computational overhead, so many studies still favor the constant-motion KF to maintain efficiency. This work addresses this trade-off by introducing a motion dynamics Kalman filter (MD-KF) that accounts for motion variation through temporal modeling of object motion and adaptively scales the contribution of the motion components in the motion model to correct state-prediction drift caused by this variation, while maintaining computational efficiency by retaining a single-model structure. Evaluations on KITTI and the Waymo Open Dataset, complemented by vehicle experiments, show that MD-KF achieves tracking performance comparable to multimodel KF with an average 1.72x speedup, while outperforming it in handling highly distant and prolonged occluded objects.

A perturbation theory for Feynman-Kac models and their particle interpretations 2026-10-15
📝 84 pages (including a 33-page Appendix)
Show abstract

We develop a perturbation analysis of Feynman--Kac models, describing how local errors introduced at each step of the Feynman--Kac recursion propagate over time. Under general conditions on the potential functions and the Markov transitions, which are shown to hold for a large class of state-space models, we prove that the global error for any time horizon $T\geq 1$ is bounded by $C\,Δ_T\log(1+Δ_T^{-1})$, where $Δ_T$ is the largest local error; this bound holds both for the prediction and the updated models. These conditions require no Lyapunov function and no stability of the Markov transitions, and allow for non-compact state spaces: confinement is provided by the potential functions. Our perturbation bounds hold in total variation and in the Kolmogorov norm; for the latter, we extend the Hardy--Krause variation and the Koksma--Hlawka inequality to unbounded domains. Our perturbation theory is applied to derive time-uniform bounds for linearized Kalman filters, Euler schemes for partially observed diffusions and nudged state-space models, as well as high-probability error bounds, uniform over whole trajectories, for particle filters and, more generally, for mean field particle methods such as sequential Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo algorithms.

Feature-preserving Latent-EnKF for Data Assimilation of Flows with Shocks 2026-10-14
Show abstract

The ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF) is widely adopted for sequential data assimilation, but fails for solutions with discontinuities, such as shocks in compressible flows. For ensembles with uncertainty in discontinuity location, the EnKF analysis can lose physical consistency, which can be viewed through three lenses: (i) bimodal pointwise statistics in the forecast ensemble, (ii) mixed-sign analysis weights in the affine superposition of forecast members, and (iii) spatially distributed Kalman-gain profiles that modify the state around a discontinuity rather than translating it coherently. In order to preserve flow features in the analysis state and, as such, maintain physical consistency, a feature-preserving latent-EnKF is introduced. The proposed method performs the ensemble update in a low-dimensional latent space learned by a coordinate-conditioned auto-decoder. The latent representation retains near-Gaussian ensemble statistics and provides coordinates in which flow features vary coherently across the forecast ensemble. The EnKF updates performed on the latent codes are decoded into coherent changes in the represented flow features, translating shocks through coordinate morphing rather than superposing them at displaced locations. The corresponding Kalman-gain profiles generated by the latent-EnKF exhibit an approximately step-like variation across the discontinuities, allowing the fronts to translate while preserving sharp features and monotonicity. Sod shock-tube, Mach~2 shock-cylinder, and immersed-body shape-inference tests with sparse, noisy observations demonstrate accurate recovery of shocks and contact discontinuities without spurious oscillations.

Kalman-Bucy Filtering with Randomized Sensing: Fundamental Limits and Sensor Network Design for Field Estimation 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Stability analysis of the Kalman filter under randomly lost measurements has been widely studied. We revisit this problem in a general continuous-time framework, where both the measurement matrix and noise covariance evolve as random processes, capturing variability in sensing locations. Within this setting, we derive a closed-form upper bound on the expected estimation covariance for continuous-time Kalman filtering. We then apply this framework to spatiotemporal field estimation, where the field is modeled as a Gaussian process observed by randomly located, noisy sensors. Using clarity, introduced in our earlier work as a rescaled form of the differential entropy of a random variable, we establish a lower bound on the expected spatially averaged clarity and show that its steady-state value converges to a grid-independent limit as the grid is refined. This result exposes fundamental performance limits through a composite sensing parameter that jointly captures the effects of the number of sensors, noise level, and measurement frequency. Simulations show close agreement between the continuous-time analytical solution and the discrete-time covariance, with the agreement improving as the sampling interval decreases. The analytical solution also avoids the recursive computations required in the discrete-time formulation. Most importantly, the derived limits provide principled and efficient guidelines for sensor network design prior to deployment.

LAG-IO: Multi-IMU State Estimation for Legged Robots Using Factor Graphs 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Robust proprioceptive state estimation is crucial for legged robots, particularly in challenging environments where exteroceptive sensors are unreliable. While traditional leg odometry typically relies on a single body-mounted inertial measurement unit (IMU) fused with leg kinematics via an extended Kalman filter (EKF), such methods are vulnerable to linearization errors and hard-contact assumptions, leading to severe drift during slippage or dynamic locomotion.In this paper, we propose LAG-IO, a factor graph-based multi-IMU state estimation framework that tightly couples measurements from a main body IMU, multiple leg-mounted IMUs, and joint encoders within a sliding-window optimization. To handle non-stationary foot-ground interactions, we introduce a continuous probabilistic contact detection module based on kernel density estimation (KDE), which adaptively scales the covariance of zero-velocity update (ZUPT) factors. Experimental results on a quadruped robot across various unstructured terrains demonstrate that, compared to existing baselines, LAG-IO achieves higher planar trajectory accuracy, reduces unobservable vertical divergence, and provides smoother trajectory transitions during unstable contacts, while maintaining competitive attitude observability.

ALIVE-LIO: Degeneracy-Aware Learning of Inertial Velocity for Enhancing ESKF-Based LiDAR-Inertial Odometry 2026-10-13
📝 23 pages, 18 figures
Show abstract

Odometry estimation using light detection and ranging (LiDAR) and an inertial measurement unit (IMU), known as LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO), often suffers from performance degradation in degenerate environments, such as long corridors or single-wall scenarios with narrow field-of-view LiDAR. To address this limitation, we propose ALIVE-LIO, a degeneracy-aware LiDAR-inertial odometry framework that explicitly enhances state estimation in degenerate directions. The key contribution of ALIVE-LIO is the strategic integration of a deep neural network into a classical error-state Kalman filter (ESKF) to compensate for the loss of LiDAR observability. Specifically, ALIVE-LIO employs a neural network to predict the body-frame velocity and selectively fuses this prediction into the ESKF only when degeneracy is detected, providing effective state updates along degenerate directions. This design enables ALIVE-LIO to utilize the probabilistic structure and consistency of the ESKF while benefiting from learning-based motion estimation. The proposed method was evaluated on publicly available datasets exhibiting degeneracy, as well as on our own collected data. Experimental results demonstrate that ALIVE-LIO substantially reduces pose drift in degenerate environments, yielding the top-ranked result in 22 out of 32 sequences. Our code is available at https://github.com/cocel-postech/alive-lio.

Accounting for model error in ensemble variational data assimilation through learned generative dynamics 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Many approaches have recently been proposed to combine data assimilation with machine learning for improved forecasts with geophysical models. Here, we focus on combining advanced sequential data assimilation schemes with a generative forecasting model which accounts for complex model errors. This generative model provides a stochastic approximation of the conditional distribution of the successor state given the current state. The proposed variational formulation is a novel one-step weak-constraint 4D-Var scheme in which a learned conditional stochastic forecast replaces the prescribed additive Gaussian model error representation. We derive and implement this formulation as an ensemble variational algorithm, the generative iterative ensemble Kalman filter (IEnKF-G), which generalises the iterative ensemble Kalman filter with additive Gaussian model error (IEnKF-Q). We extend the method to a smoother with an assimilation window spanning multiple time steps and propose a simplified variant. The IEnKF-G is evaluated on a stochastic variant of the Lorenz 1963 model to assess its robustness. It is then tested on a multi-scale variant of the one-dimensional Lorenz 1996 model, in which the resolved variables are affected by model error originating from the hidden small-scale variables. The algorithm is then compared to an ensemble Kalman filter that adaptively mitigates model and sampling errors, and to the IEnKF-Q. The IEnKF-G is generally more accurate in the tested configurations, with substantial gains under partial observations and significant model error. This demonstrates the potential of generative data-driven models when paired with classical data assimilation methods, particularly when model error is substantial.

Design of Privacy-Preserving Estimators Under Prior Information Exposure 2026-10-12
Show abstract

State estimation is widely used in networked dynamical systems, but released estimates may inadvertently reveal sensitive information about private state variables. Existing privacy-preserving estimation frameworks often do not explicitly account for adversarial prior knowledge. Motivated by this gap, we study privacy-preserving state estimation for discrete-time linear stochastic systems under prior information exposure using pointwise maximal leakage (PML). We first reveal a privacy anti-optimality property of the Kalman filter: in the absence of additive release noise, the steady-state Kalman filter maximizes PML privacy leakage among the considered class of stable linear estimators. We further establish a nontrivial lower bound on the steady-state estimation error enforced by $(\varepsilon,δ)$-PML privacy. We then derive sufficient release-noise conditions that guarantee $(\varepsilon,δ)$-PML privacy, first for the Kalman filter and then for stable linear estimators. Finally, a convex semidefinite program is formulated for a joint design of the estimator gain and release noise covariance, providing a tractable privacy-preserving estimator with an explicit upper bound on the steady-state estimation error. Simulations on a source--load power system demonstrate the proposed privacy--utility tradeoff.

BAT3R: Robust Online 3D Reconstruction with Bayesian Adaptive State Updates 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Feed-forward transformer models have driven remarkable progress in 3D vision,yet their quadratic complexity renders them impractical for long video sequences. Streaming 3D reconstruction addresses this bottleneck by processing frames sequen?tially with constant memory. However, existing recurrent architectures inevitably suffer from progressive degradation over long sequences, as they fail to account for the intrinsic reliability of observations and the accumulated confidence of the historical state, leading to severe error accumulation. To address these issues, we introduce BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework comprising two synergistic modules: State Innovation Gating (SIG) and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU). SIG acts as a distributional regularizer that anchors state evolution to its geometric initialization, effectively arresting representational drift during long-term inference. However, conventional multi state constraint Kalman filters (MSCKFs) use random walk bias assumptions and fixed noise parameters, which can limit robustness when visual information is unreliable. To address this problem, we propose LBDU-VIO, a learning-augmented MSCKF with learned continuous time bias dynamics and an IMU uncertainty model. A neural ordinary differential equation (ODE) models continuous time bias dynamics to propagate the filter's bias states, replacing their random walk model. The IMU uncertainty model predicts motion adaptive measurement noise covariances for covariance propagation. Both models are trained with pose supervision without direct labels. Experiments on real world EuRoC and TUM-VI benchmarks show lower errors than representative visual-inertial baselines, including a 25.1% reduction in mean relative position error compared with S-MSCKF on EuRoC sequences with 10s visual outage.

Prompts Live on an Arc: Gaussian Curricula in Fisher--Rao Coordinates for Rollout-Efficient GRPO 2026-09-29
Show abstract

Group relative policy optimization (GRPO) learns only from prompts whose sampled responses disagree: a group that is entirely correct or entirely incorrect has zero reward variance, contributes no gradient, and still consumes its rollouts. Prompt-selection methods reduce this waste by steering sampling toward intermediate pass rates, but they choose the target, its width, and the uncertainty model heuristically, in raw pass-rate or logit coordinates. We show that GRPO comes with a natural coordinate for pass rates: the arc length $ψ=\arcsin\sqrt{p}$ on the Bernoulli Fisher--Rao manifold. In arc length, the expected GRPO update is uniform up to two boundary ramps; the probability of a zero-variance group is bounded by two Gaussian boundary layers of width $1/\sqrt{2G}$; pass-rate evidence has constant noise; and the gradients of the pass@$k$ and pass$^k$ objectives are Gaussians whose center and width follow from $k$ in closed form. A prompt curriculum for GRPO is therefore a Gaussian in arc length, and choosing its center amounts to choosing the objective. We turn this observation into ARCUS, a drop-in sampler that tracks every prompt with a Kalman filter in arc length, scores prompts by an objective-matched Gaussian kernel times the predicted probability of an informative group, keeps only informative groups for the unchanged GRPO update, and paces the target toward the hardest objective whose predicted yield stays within a small slack of the best. Across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three backbones, ARCUS improves the average accuracy of GRPO by 2.8--2.9 points and that of dynamic sampling by 1.1--1.2 points, while generating 48--57\% fewer rollouts than dynamic sampling.
