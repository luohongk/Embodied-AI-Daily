ARC-SORT: Adaptive, Robust, Compensated Multi-Object Tracking with Interacting Multiple Models 2026-10-15
📝 14 pages, 4 figures, 13 tables; supplementary material included as appendix; qualitative video as ancillary file
Show abstract

Multi-object tracking in sports broadcasts is hard for two reasons: athletes move fast and change direction abruptly, and the camera pans and zooms at the same time. We present ARC-SORT (Adaptive, Robust, Compensated SORT), a tracking-by-detection tracker that trains nothing and runs in real time. Its design rule: the tracker measures its own uncertainty, and that measurement decides how it acts. An Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) Kalman filter gives every track a predictive covariance, and we turn it into a per-track search region: the less reliable the motion prediction, the larger the region in which a detection may match (uncertainty-adaptive Expansion IoU, EIoU). On identical inputs, this continuous coupling is what matters: the same covariance used as a hard Mahalanobis gate hurts at every threshold we swept, and richer covariance summaries add nothing measurable. When two tracks cross, the assignment cost itself reports whether the decision was close. ARC-SORT keeps such pairs under review and applies at most one corrective identity swap once post-crossing appearance evidence shows the first decision was wrong; this adds +1.0 HOTA (higher order tracking accuracy) on both the SportsMOT and DanceTrack test sets at under a millisecond per frame. For players who leave the frame for seconds, motion extrapolation carries no information, so our offline re-identification drops the usual motion gate and links tracklets by appearance alone. ARC-SORT reaches 81.82 HOTA on the SportsMOT test set with the fewest identity switches of the 69 leaderboard entries as of August 2026 and tied-third multi-object tracking accuracy (MOTA, 97.09), and 62.1 HOTA on DanceTrack test with the same association settings. On Deep-EIoU's public detections and embeddings it reaches 80.31 HOTA, +3.1 over Deep-EIoU. It runs at about 50 frames per second on a laptop RTX 3060 GPU. Code will be released.

Inertially Significant Humanoid Teleoperation via Whole Body Model Predictive Control and Online Parameter Estimation 2026-10-15
Show abstract

The effective deployment of humanoid robots in practical tasks of interest will require a capability of sensing and adapting to variations in inertial parameters, as occurs during heavy tool use or when carrying significant payload. Such payloads constitute a significant proportion of the combined payload-robot system, the effects of which cannot be ignored by delegating to feedback control. This paper showcases a general solution to this problem by integrating a whole body model predictive controller (WB-MPC) with an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online estimation of fully physically consistent time-varying inertial parameters. These estimates are fed through to the WB-MPC's full rigid body dynamics model, resulting in more accurate feedforward torques for task demands under external load. Additionally, a virtual reality (VR) interface is provided that enables an operator to provide task-specific references without regard to payload while delegating dynamic balance to the WB-MPC. The framework is validated with simulation and hardware experiments on a humanoid system.

Towards Accurate State Estimation: Motion Dynamics Kalman Filter for 3D Multi-Object Tracking 2026-10-15
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Robust 3D state estimation in multi-object tracking is crucial for object localization in self-driving vehicles. Misalignment between the Kalman filter (KF) motion model and an object's motion can cause state drift, particularly in distant and occlusion cases. The static motion model assumption in the conventional KF is widely used in 3D MOT due to its low latency; however, its estimation accuracy degrades as the object's motion disaligns with the chosen model due to traffic. Although recent work introduces a multimodel KF that runs multiple motion models simultaneously to improve state robustness, it incurs significant computational overhead, so many studies still favor the constant-motion KF to maintain efficiency. This work addresses this trade-off by introducing a motion dynamics Kalman filter (MD-KF) that accounts for motion variation through temporal modeling of object motion and adaptively scales the contribution of the motion components in the motion model to correct state-prediction drift caused by this variation, while maintaining computational efficiency by retaining a single-model structure. Evaluations on KITTI and the Waymo Open Dataset, complemented by vehicle experiments, show that MD-KF achieves tracking performance comparable to multimodel KF with an average 1.72x speedup, while outperforming it in handling highly distant and prolonged occluded objects.

A perturbation theory for Feynman-Kac models and their particle interpretations 2026-10-15
📝 84 pages (including a 33-page Appendix)
Show abstract

We develop a perturbation analysis of Feynman--Kac models, describing how local errors introduced at each step of the Feynman--Kac recursion propagate over time. Under general conditions on the potential functions and the Markov transitions, which are shown to hold for a large class of state-space models, we prove that the global error for any time horizon $T\geq 1$ is bounded by $C\,Δ_T\log(1+Δ_T^{-1})$, where $Δ_T$ is the largest local error; this bound holds both for the prediction and the updated models. These conditions require no Lyapunov function and no stability of the Markov transitions, and allow for non-compact state spaces: confinement is provided by the potential functions. Our perturbation bounds hold in total variation and in the Kolmogorov norm; for the latter, we extend the Hardy--Krause variation and the Koksma--Hlawka inequality to unbounded domains. Our perturbation theory is applied to derive time-uniform bounds for linearized Kalman filters, Euler schemes for partially observed diffusions and nudged state-space models, as well as high-probability error bounds, uniform over whole trajectories, for particle filters and, more generally, for mean field particle methods such as sequential Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo algorithms.

Feature-preserving Latent-EnKF for Data Assimilation of Flows with Shocks 2026-10-14
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

The ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF) is widely adopted for sequential data assimilation, but fails for solutions with discontinuities, such as shocks in compressible flows. For ensembles with uncertainty in discontinuity location, the EnKF analysis can lose physical consistency, which can be viewed through three lenses: (i) bimodal pointwise statistics in the forecast ensemble, (ii) mixed-sign analysis weights in the affine superposition of forecast members, and (iii) spatially distributed Kalman-gain profiles that modify the state around a discontinuity rather than translating it coherently. In order to preserve flow features in the analysis state and, as such, maintain physical consistency, a feature-preserving latent-EnKF is introduced. The proposed method performs the ensemble update in a low-dimensional latent space learned by a coordinate-conditioned auto-decoder. The latent representation retains near-Gaussian ensemble statistics and provides coordinates in which flow features vary coherently across the forecast ensemble. The EnKF updates performed on the latent codes are decoded into coherent changes in the represented flow features, translating shocks through coordinate morphing rather than superposing them at displaced locations. The corresponding Kalman-gain profiles generated by the latent-EnKF exhibit an approximately step-like variation across the discontinuities, allowing the fronts to translate while preserving sharp features and monotonicity. Sod shock-tube, Mach~2 shock-cylinder, and immersed-body shape-inference tests with sparse, noisy observations demonstrate accurate recovery of shocks and contact discontinuities without spurious oscillations.

Kalman-Bucy Filtering with Randomized Sensing: Fundamental Limits and Sensor Network Design for Field Estimation 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Stability analysis of the Kalman filter under randomly lost measurements has been widely studied. We revisit this problem in a general continuous-time framework, where both the measurement matrix and noise covariance evolve as random processes, capturing variability in sensing locations. Within this setting, we derive a closed-form upper bound on the expected estimation covariance for continuous-time Kalman filtering. We then apply this framework to spatiotemporal field estimation, where the field is modeled as a Gaussian process observed by randomly located, noisy sensors. Using clarity, introduced in our earlier work as a rescaled form of the differential entropy of a random variable, we establish a lower bound on the expected spatially averaged clarity and show that its steady-state value converges to a grid-independent limit as the grid is refined. This result exposes fundamental performance limits through a composite sensing parameter that jointly captures the effects of the number of sensors, noise level, and measurement frequency. Simulations show close agreement between the continuous-time analytical solution and the discrete-time covariance, with the agreement improving as the sampling interval decreases. The analytical solution also avoids the recursive computations required in the discrete-time formulation. Most importantly, the derived limits provide principled and efficient guidelines for sensor network design prior to deployment.

LAG-IO: Multi-IMU State Estimation for Legged Robots Using Factor Graphs 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Robust proprioceptive state estimation is crucial for legged robots, particularly in challenging environments where exteroceptive sensors are unreliable. While traditional leg odometry typically relies on a single body-mounted inertial measurement unit (IMU) fused with leg kinematics via an extended Kalman filter (EKF), such methods are vulnerable to linearization errors and hard-contact assumptions, leading to severe drift during slippage or dynamic locomotion.In this paper, we propose LAG-IO, a factor graph-based multi-IMU state estimation framework that tightly couples measurements from a main body IMU, multiple leg-mounted IMUs, and joint encoders within a sliding-window optimization. To handle non-stationary foot-ground interactions, we introduce a continuous probabilistic contact detection module based on kernel density estimation (KDE), which adaptively scales the covariance of zero-velocity update (ZUPT) factors. Furthermore, kinematic relative position factors are formulated to structurally anchor the independent leg inertial states to the robot's main body. Experimental results on a quadruped robot across various unstructured terrains demonstrate that, compared to existing baselines, LAG-IO achieves higher planar trajectory accuracy, reduces unobservable vertical divergence, and provides smoother trajectory transitions during unstable contacts, while maintaining competitive attitude observability.

ALIVE-LIO: Degeneracy-Aware Learning of Inertial Velocity for Enhancing ESKF-Based LiDAR-Inertial Odometry 2026-10-13
📝 23 pages, 18 figures
Show abstract

Odometry estimation using light detection and ranging (LiDAR) and an inertial measurement unit (IMU), known as LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO), often suffers from performance degradation in degenerate environments, such as long corridors or single-wall scenarios with narrow field-of-view LiDAR. To address this limitation, we propose ALIVE-LIO, a degeneracy-aware LiDAR-inertial odometry framework that explicitly enhances state estimation in degenerate directions. The key contribution of ALIVE-LIO is the strategic integration of a deep neural network into a classical error-state Kalman filter (ESKF) to compensate for the loss of LiDAR observability. Specifically, ALIVE-LIO employs a neural network to predict the body-frame velocity and selectively fuses this prediction into the ESKF only when degeneracy is detected, providing effective state updates along degenerate directions. This design enables ALIVE-LIO to utilize the probabilistic structure and consistency of the ESKF while benefiting from learning-based motion estimation. The proposed method was evaluated on publicly available datasets exhibiting degeneracy, as well as on our own collected data. Experimental results demonstrate that ALIVE-LIO substantially reduces pose drift in degenerate environments, yielding the top-ranked result in 22 out of 32 sequences. Our code is available at https://github.com/cocel-postech/alive-lio.

Accounting for model error in ensemble variational data assimilation through learned generative dynamics 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Many approaches have recently been proposed to combine data assimilation with machine learning for improved forecasts with geophysical models. Here, we focus on combining advanced sequential data assimilation schemes with a generative forecasting model which accounts for complex model errors. This generative model provides a stochastic approximation of the conditional distribution of the successor state given the current state. The proposed variational formulation is a novel one-step weak-constraint 4D-Var scheme in which a learned conditional stochastic forecast replaces the prescribed additive Gaussian model error representation. We derive and implement this formulation as an ensemble variational algorithm, the generative iterative ensemble Kalman filter (IEnKF-G), which generalises the iterative ensemble Kalman filter with additive Gaussian model error (IEnKF-Q). We extend the method to a smoother with an assimilation window spanning multiple time steps and propose a simplified variant. The IEnKF-G is evaluated on a stochastic variant of the Lorenz 1963 model to assess its robustness. It is then tested on a multi-scale variant of the one-dimensional Lorenz 1996 model, in which the resolved variables are affected by model error originating from the hidden small-scale variables. The algorithm is then compared to an ensemble Kalman filter that adaptively mitigates model and sampling errors, and to the IEnKF-Q. The IEnKF-G is generally more accurate in the tested configurations, with substantial gains under partial observations and significant model error. This demonstrates the potential of generative data-driven models when paired with classical data assimilation methods, particularly when model error is substantial.

Design of Privacy-Preserving Estimators Under Prior Information Exposure 2026-10-12
Show abstract

State estimation is widely used in networked dynamical systems, but released estimates may inadvertently reveal sensitive information about private state variables. Existing privacy-preserving estimation frameworks often do not explicitly account for adversarial prior knowledge. Motivated by this gap, we study privacy-preserving state estimation for discrete-time linear stochastic systems under prior information exposure using pointwise maximal leakage (PML). We first reveal a privacy anti-optimality property of the Kalman filter: in the absence of additive release noise, the steady-state Kalman filter maximizes PML privacy leakage among the considered class of stable linear estimators. We further establish a nontrivial lower bound on the steady-state estimation error enforced by $(\varepsilon,δ)$-PML privacy. We then derive sufficient release-noise conditions that guarantee $(\varepsilon,δ)$-PML privacy, first for the Kalman filter and then for stable linear estimators. Finally, a convex semidefinite program is formulated for a joint design of the estimator gain and release noise covariance, providing a tractable privacy-preserving estimator with an explicit upper bound on the steady-state estimation error. Simulations on a source--load power system demonstrate the proposed privacy--utility tradeoff.

BAT3R: Robust Online 3D Reconstruction with Bayesian Adaptive State Updates 2026-10-12
Show abstract

Feed-forward transformer models have driven remarkable progress in 3D vision,yet their quadratic complexity renders them impractical for long video sequences. Streaming 3D reconstruction addresses this bottleneck by processing frames sequen?tially with constant memory. However, existing recurrent architectures inevitably suffer from progressive degradation over long sequences, as they fail to account for the intrinsic reliability of observations and the accumulated confidence of the historical state, leading to severe error accumulation. To address these issues, we introduce BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework comprising two synergistic modules: State Innovation Gating (SIG) and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU). SIG acts as a distributional regularizer that anchors state evolution to its geometric initialization, effectively arresting representational drift during long-term inference. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks (7-Scenes, TUM, ScanNet, KITTI, etc.) demonstrate that BAT3R consistently outperforms state-of-the-art online 3D reconstruction methods, with the most significant gains observed on long sequences and complex scenes.

Invariant Stochastic Filtering on SE(3) for Inertial-Encoder State Estimation of Serial Rigid Manipulators 2026-10-12
📝 This document is an arXiv preprint posted for open access and citation purposes. It is under review and subject to revision
Show abstract

An invariant extended Kalman filter (IEKF) is developed for state estimation of serial rigid manipulators with an arbitrary number of links, formulated entirely within the Lie group $SE(3)$. The group-affine property of the kinematic equations makes the linearised error dynamics autonomous, so the Riccati equation governs the true error covariance rather than a local approximation. A physically separated noise model treats gyroscope and accelerometer channels independently: the accelerometer provides translational twist via gravity-compensated integration, yielding a measurement covariance that scales with the sample interval in exact analogy with process noise discretisation; a state-dependent Coriolis noise term captures gyroscope noise propagating through the nonlinear dynamics, vanishing at rest and growing with twist magnitude. The filter is structured as a modular chain of per-link IEKFs in which the predicted covariance of each link depends on its predecessor only through the Adjoint-transformed posterior, giving linear computational cost in link count. Exponential ultimate boundedness in mean square is established via a Lie algebra Lyapunov function, with per-link bounds chained through the Adjoint operator norm to yield a stability certificate that is modular and scalable to arbitrary chain length. The state is augmented with gyroscope biases without loss of the autonomous error dynamics or the modular structure. The design is validated in a three-dimensional simulation of a two-link chain with encoder and IMU fusion, in a software-in-the-loop study of an industrial hydraulic rammer-boom manipulator in which the encoder row is replaced by a gravity-referenced virtual encoder for IMU-only estimation.

Model-Based Trailer-Sway Suppression for Autonomous Passenger Vehicles 2026-10-11
📝 17 pages, 21 figures
Show abstract

Trailer sway is a persistent contributor to severe crashes among light passenger vehicles towing trailers, yet it remains largely unaddressed by the autonomous vehicle systems now entering this new era of automation. Improper cargo placement rearward of the trailer axle reduces, and can invert, the tongue load transmitted through the hitch. The resulting loss of vertical load at the tow vehicle's rear axle diminishes the lateral tire force available there and degrades yaw stability of the coupled system, admitting a divergent lateral oscillation of the trailer known as trailer sway. Under adverse combinations of speed and loading, this oscillation can progress to jackknifing, in which the articulation angle diverges rapidly and directional control is lost. This work develops a linear state-space model of the yaw-plane dynamics of a light vehicle-trailer combination, parameterized to expose tongue load as an explicit stability-determining quantity. From this model a linear quadratic regulator is synthesized to suppress articulation-angle divergence. Because consumer trailers carry no sensors, the two trailer states the controller needs, trailer yaw rate and articulation angle, are estimated by a Kalman filter from measurements taken only on the tow vehicle. The controller is then implemented and evaluated in a MuJoCo multibody simulation across a range of longitudinal speeds and tongue-load conditions, including the negative-tongue-load regime. Results show a significant decrease in trailer oscillation when controlling a given vehicle's steering with an LQR using tow-vehicle IMU and LiDAR data. These findings indicate that LQR controlled anti-sway systems are a viable control method for future autonomous vehicles.

Learning Contact Representation for Leg Odometry 2026-10-10
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026. 21 pages, 12 figures. Code: https://github.com/egirgin/leg-odometry-contact-representation
Show abstract

Leg odometry relies on the assumption that the velocity of a foot in stance is zero with respect to the world, which makes accurate contact detection a critical subproblem. Many approaches classify contact using ground reaction force (GRF) sensors at the foot tip, yet these sensors are not available on all legged robots and remain unresponsive to disturbances such as slippage while the foot is still in contact. Approaches without GRF sensing instead rely on heuristic pseudo-labels, model-based foot-velocity assumptions, or supervised contact learning. In this study, we propose a self-supervised representation learning framework for contact detection that uses only standard proprioceptive sensing (joint encoders and IMU) and requires neither force sensors nor contact labels. A denoising autoencoder is trained on kinematic features, a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model is fitted in the learned latent space, and its posterior is used as a continuous stance belief that scales the zero-velocity measurement covariance of an error-state Kalman filter. Experiments in simulation and on real-world terrain show that our framework yields the lowest odometry error among supervised and probabilistic baselines, and that higher pseudo-label classification accuracy does not translate into better downstream odometry. Our code and dataset are publicly available.

An Unscented PrO Kalman Filter 2026-10-10
Show abstract

Predictively-oriented (PrO) filtering for state-space models was proposed in Shen et al. [2026] as a promising route to avoiding over-confident inferences in settings where either the dynamical model, the measurement model, or both, could be misspecified. Our focus is on nonlinear state-space models, where the local linearisation used to construct the PrO filter in Shen et al. [2026] deteriorates as nonlinearities enter either the dynamical model or the measurement model. Our proposed solution is based on unscented transforms, an established tool from classical filtering which provides a more accurate mechanism to propagate Gaussian uncertainty through a nonlinear functional, and which includes the cubature rule as a special case. We introduce two variants: replacing the linearisation in the predict step alone, which we call UKF-PrO, has a computational cost comparable to the existing PrO filter; replacing it in the update step as well, UKF-PrO+, requires a substantially more expensive joint optimisation. Performance is empirically assessed on the Lorenz96 model under conditions of increasing difficulty, in which the state-space model is systematically misspecified; we find that UKF-PrO with the parameters of the cubature Kalman filter offers consistent improvements, which become substantial under strong nonlinearity, whereas the default unscented parameters are unreliable, and that the full update-step extension with the same parameters does not justify its additional cost.

Decomposing the spectral density function of an ARMA process 2026-10-09
Show abstract

Quasi-periodic oscillations are characterized by fitting Lorentzian functions to a nonparametric estimate of the power spectral density, a two-step procedure that separates estimation from modeling and does not accommodate measurement error. We develop a parametric alternative based on autoregressive moving-average (ARMA) processes observed with measurement error. We derive an exact decomposition of the ARMA spectral density into real-valued components, one for each class of autoregressive roots under complex conjugation, plus a polynomial term when the moving-average order is at least the autoregressive order. Each component is a Wrapped Cauchy kernel, and for complex roots it is accompanied by a conjugate-Poisson term that makes the peak asymmetric and displaces its maximum from the frequency given by the argument of the root. We obtain closed-form expressions for the location, height, width, and asymmetry of each peak, and a matching variance decomposition. Parameters are estimated by maximum likelihood through the Kalman filter, and all descriptors follow by plug-in. Simulations show that the closed-form peak location is recovered without appreciable bias. An X-ray binary light curve illustrates the methodology.

Mapping Tire-Road Friction Coefficients Along Driven Trajectories 2026-10-09
Show abstract

Information of the tire-road friction coefficient is critical for intelligent vehicle systems, as friction establishes the limits at which the vehicle can be manoeuvred without loss of control. While there is extensive literature on online friction estimation, no previous studies have explored post-processing and mapping friction coefficients along a driven trajectory based on recorded sensor data. Mapped friction information could be shared by connected vehicles, enabling proactive measures in low-friction areas. This paper presents an offline post-processing method, FrictionMap, for mapping friction coefficients from recorded driving data. The method operates on commonly available on-board vehicle sensor information, and requires only a basic vehicle parameter set and a slip threshold as prior knowledge. FrictionMap runs an extended Kalman filter combined with a Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother to estimate vehicle dynamics. Data points where the friction limit is observable are identified by filtering points based on tire slip. A Gaussian process is fitted to these points to acquire a continuous estimate of the friction coefficient along the trajectory. FrictionMap is validated via a simulation study, and shown to outperform commonly used online friction estimation techniques. The code implementation and the simulation benchmark are released as open-source after paper acceptance to enable further research.

Neural posterior inference with state-space models for calibrating ice sheet simulators 2026-10-09
Show abstract

Ice sheet models are routinely used to quantify and project an ice sheet's contribution to sea level rise. In order for an ice sheet model to generate realistic projections, its parameters must first be calibrated using observational data; this is challenging due to the nonlinearity of the model equations, the high dimensionality of the underlying parameters, and limited data availability for validation. This study leverages the emerging field of neural posterior approximation for efficiently calibrating ice sheet model parameters and boundary conditions. We make use of a one-dimensional (flowline) Shallow-Shelf Approximation model in a state-space framework. A neural network is trained to infer the underlying parameters, namely the bedrock elevation and basal friction coefficient along the flowline, based on observations of ice velocity and ice surface elevation. Samples from the approximate posterior distribution of the parameters are then used within an ensemble Kalman filter to infer latent model states, namely the ice thickness along the flowline. We show through a simulation study that our approach yields more accurate estimates of the parameters and states than a state-augmented ensemble Kalman filter, which is the current state-of-the-art. We apply our approach to infer the bed elevation and basal friction along a flowline in Thwaites Glacier, Antarctica.

Neural Conjugate Aggregation: Identifiable Unsupervised Multi-Sensor Regression under Heterogeneous Sensor Bias 2026-10-08
📝 10 pages
Show abstract

We study regression-based data fusion under uncertainty, where multiple noisy and biased measurement sources are available but ground-truth labels are absent during training. This setting arises in sensor networks, simulation ensembles, and scientific monitoring systems where supervision is costly or infeasible. We propose the Neural Conjugate Aggregation Model (NCAM), a hierarchical Bayesian framework that combines neural networks with conjugate Gaussian inference for unsupervised multi-source fusion. NCAM learns source-specific bias and reliability conditioned on contextual covariates, yielding an analytically tractable posterior over a latent target variable with decomposed epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. Structural non-identifiability is resolved through sensor anchoring and variance regularization, enabling stable and interpretable posterior aggregation. To complement Bayesian uncertainty with finite-sample guarantees, we integrate locally adaptive Monte Carlo conformal prediction, producing heteroscedastic prediction intervals with coverage guarantees under exchangeability assumptions. Experiments on synthetic and real-world air-quality datasets demonstrate improved predictive accuracy and well-calibrated uncertainty compared to unsupervised baselines, including mean aggregation, probabilistic PCA, and Kalman filtering.

Tactile Reconstruction of Contact Task Frames and Forces for Hybrid Force/Motion Control 2026-10-07
📝 14 pages, 9 figures, 7 tables. Video: https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLejKMZmW8AvI. Dataset: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23209032
Show abstract

Hybrid force/motion control requires knowledge of the interaction force and of a task frame defining the force- and motion-controlled directions. These quantities are usually obtained from force/torque sensing or model-based residuals, often assuming also a nominal environment model. This work addresses the online estimation of the contact force and a possibly time-varying task frame using only soft optical tactile sensing, under the assumption of locally planar contact with a negligible contact moment. The proposed method maps a single image of the deformed elastomer of a soft optical tactile sensor to observable contact variables: indentation depth, two surface-to-sensor tilt angles, and 3D contact force, each with a per-sample uncertainty estimate. The mapping is learned through a self-labeling acquisition procedure, in which a manipulator imposes controlled contacts while an auxiliary Force/Torque sensor is used offline to provide ground-truth labels. The tactile measurement is then fused with robot proprioceptive data in an Extended Kalman Filter, producing a continuously updated estimate of the contact task frame and of the interaction force. Control experiments with a DigiTac sensor mounted on a UR10 manipulator demonstrate closed-loop contact force regulation against a flat rigid board in linear and angular motion by a human operator, with touch as the only exteroceptive feedback.

LEO Signals-of-Opportunity for Navigation in Urban Environments: A GLRT-Assisted Off-Grid SBL Approach 2026-10-06
📝 11 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

The vulnerability and unavailability of global navigation satellite system (GNSS) signals in dense urban environments have motivated the use of low earth orbit (LEO) signals of opportunity (SoOP) for positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT). However, multipath and non-ideal noise can significantly degrade delay--Doppler estimation and, consequently, navigation accuracy. This paper proposes a hybrid generalized likelihood ratio test (GLRT) and off-grid sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) framework for LEO-SoOP navigation under multipath and non-Gaussian, temporally correlated noise. First, a GLRT-based framework is developed, demonstrating reliable detection under ideal Gaussian noise but degraded estimation under heavy-tailed or colored noise and unresolved multipath. Next, an off-grid SBL framework is introduced for high-resolution separation and estimation of line-of-sight (LOS) delay--Doppler parameters. The estimated LOS parameters are subsequently tracked and incorporated into an extended Kalman filter (EKF) to obtain the navigation solution. Simulation results demonstrate that, in urban environments, the proposed method reduces delay RMSE by up to 77\% and position RMSE by 88.5\% compared with GLRT-based estimation. These results demonstrate the potential of the proposed framework for robust and accurate LEO-SoOP navigation.

LARK: A Low-Cost, Accurate, Occlusion-Resilient, Kalman Filter-Assisted Tracking System for Image-Guided Surgery 2026-10-06
📝 24 pages, 15 figures, including appendices. Supplementary document included as an ancillary file. Supplementary video: https://youtu.be/ApZ8q9DjB-4
Show abstract

Image-guided surgery (IGS) depends on accurate tracking of surgical instruments to provide real-time navigation relative to anatomical structures.
