A perturbation theory for Feynman-Kac models and their particle interpretations 2026-11-12
📝 84 pages (including a 33-page Appendix)
Show abstract

We develop a perturbation analysis of Feynman--Kac models, describing how local errors introduced at each step of the Feynman--Kac recursion propagate over time. Under general conditions on the potential functions and the Markov transitions, which are shown to hold for a large class of state-space models, we prove that the global error for any time horizon $T\geq 1$ is bounded by $C\,Δ_T\log(1+Δ_T^{-1})$, where $Δ_T$ is the largest local error; this bound holds both for the prediction and the updated models. These conditions require no Lyapunov function and no stability of the Markov transitions, and allow for non-compact state spaces: confinement is provided by the potential functions. Our perturbation bounds hold in total variation and in the Kolmogorov norm; for the latter, we extend the Hardy--Krause variation and the Koksma--Hlawka inequality to unbounded domains. Our perturbation theory is applied to derive time-uniform bounds for linearized Kalman filters, Euler schemes for partially observed diffusions and nudged state-space models, as well as high-probability error bounds, uniform over whole trajectories, for particle filters and, more generally, for mean field particle methods such as sequential Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo algorithms.

A Class of Bayesian Conjugate Distributions on $SE_n(3)$ for Motion Estimation 2026-11-12
📝 17 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

This paper proposes a novel class of Bayesian Conjugate Distributions (BCDs) on extended special Euclidean groups SE_n(3) for global motion uncertainty representation and estimation. BCDs are constructed by restricting matrix Gaussian distributions on ambient Euclidean space to SE_n(3), avoiding singularity of local coordinates. The unique feature of BCDs is their conjugacy to a broad class of sampling distributions arising in motion estimation, enabling exact closed-form Bayesian inference. Optimal estimation with respect to BCDs is further investigated for implementing motion estimation. Two means are extracted as random state estimates, which maximize probability and minimize mean squared error, respectively. Moreover, within the BCD class, moment matching is shown to minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence, and maximum likelihood estimation is also derived, which provide analytical methods to approximate underlying distributions with the optimality guarantee. As an application, a Bayesian estimator for pose and velocity estimation of an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) is developed within an assumed density filtering architecture. The propagation and estimate extraction leverage the optimal estimation related to BCDs, and the landmark measurements are exactly fused with the BCD prior in closed form due to the conjugacy. Numerical simulations of a UAV flight indicate the advantages of BCD-based estimators over two classic Kalman filters on Lie groups with various initial uncertainties and measurement noise.

The Dynamic-Probabilistic Consistency Gap in Chaotic Surrogate Modeling 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (main track)
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) aims to learn surrogate models that capture the dynamics underlying time-series data. Reliable scientific inference with these surrogates requires uncertainty estimates consistent with the learned dynamics. We expose a dynamic-probabilistic consistency (DPC) gap: optimizing finite-horizon probabilistic objectives can degrade the learned dynamics or decouple predictive uncertainty from the local tangent dynamics it should reflect. We isolate three mechanisms behind this gap: core collapse, noise masking, and blind uncertainty. Specifically, we show that open-loop rollout objectives can penalize covariance growth driven by the dynamics, encouraging optimization shortcuts that weaken local expansion. To mitigate this gap, we propose KAFFEE (Kalman-Aware Framework For Ergodic Emulation), a differentiable extended Kalman filter-based training framework that jointly learns dynamics and noise covariances by coupling filtered innovation likelihoods with covariance propagation through learned local Jacobians, while differentiating through the Kalman updates. On stochastic hyperchaotic Lorenz-96, KAFFEE mitigates the identified failure modes and improves both reconstruction of dynamical invariants and predictive scores relative to open-loop objectives. The DPC gap also appears when probabilistically adapting a DSR foundation model across 13 chaotic systems, where KAFFEE improves predictive likelihood over open-loop adaptation while preserving the pretrained model's long-term dynamics and enabling in-context Bayesian filtering.

Differentiable Filtering for Inertial Odometry 2026-11-10
📝 19 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Accurate inertial odometry is critical for autonomous systems but suffers from unbounded drift caused by the integration of sensor noise and biases. In approaches such as TLIO, neural pseudo-measurements are trained independently of their downstream filter. This paper introduces Differentiable Filtering for Inertial Odometry (DFIO), a learning framework that embeds the update stages of an error-state Kalman filter directly into a computational graph. Following standalone pre-training, DFIO jointly optimizes the displacement and covariance output heads using posterior-position supervision through differentiable measurement updates. The proposed system is validated on public pedestrian motion datasets featuring both calibrated and uncalibrated inertial measurements. Experimental results on a pedestrian dataset demonstrate that DFIO reduces mean absolute and one-second relative translation errors by 13.2% and 39.6% relative to the TLIO baseline, and by 7.7% and 17.4% relative to its pre-trained counterpart. Furthermore, evaluations on uncalibrated smartphone inertial data indicate that the proposed method effectively mitigates translational drift and maintains trajectory alignment under highly noisy sensor conditions. Source code will be made publicly available.

Modeling and Estimation of a Towed Marine System 2026-11-09
📝 preprint submitted to Ocean Engineering, 23 pages (highlights + 22p manuscript), 12 figures
Show abstract

This paper addresses the modeling and state estimation of a towed marine system consisting of two autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs), a pair of umbilical cables, towfish, and a trailing loop cable. Inspired by the existing literature, we employ a lumped-mass modeling approach for the cables and towfish, capturing the essential dynamics while maintaining computational tractability. A Lyapunov-based analysis is conducted to verify the stability and convergence properties of the proposed model. Building on this model, we propose two state estimation methods, namely a reduced-order observer and a perfect-measurement extended Kalman filter (PMEKF), using only the measurements from the ASVs' onboard sensors. Numerical simulations and experimental results are provided to validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.

When Does Physics Beat Data? Data-Efficient Traffic State Estimation with Amortized PINNs 2026-11-09
📝 8 pages, 5 figures. Accepted at the SSTDM workshop, IEEE ICDM 2026
Show abstract

Traffic state estimation (TSE) reconstructs traffic density at unmonitored locations from a sparse set of sensors. Data-driven models work well when sensors are dense but degrade sharply as coverage thins. We show that imposing the Lighthill-Whitham-Richards conservation law as a physics-informed prior makes TSE more data-efficient. On four real corridors (NGSIM, I-80, PeMS04, PeMS08), physics-informed estimators generalise to held-out sensors where data-driven models, including classical and deep kriging, overfit the observed ones. On the two shock-rich corridors the effect is a clear crossover: as more sensors are held out, data-driven models collapse while the physics-informed estimator degrades gracefully, leading the no-physics network by about 26-38% at 85-90% held out and the strongest classical kriging by about 10-21% at 90%. A matched smoothness prior does not reproduce the gain, and an extended Kalman filter on the same LWR model is outperformed on the shock corridors, so the advantage comes from how the conservation law is imposed. Finally, one loss-conditional PINN removes the per-corridor loss-weight search, matching a 20-point search at about 5x lower cost.

K-PWM: Control-Oriented Structured Bilinear World Models under Partial Observation 2026-11-08
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

World models learn internal simulators of environment dynamics for prediction, planning, and control, yet learning them from limited, noisy, and partial observations remains challenging. While much progress has focused on pixel-based observations, many physical systems instead provide vector-valued measurements that reveal only part of the underlying state. We introduce K-PWM, a probabilistic world model for prediction and control under partial observation. K-PWM couples Koopman-bilinear latent dynamics with a nonlinear observation decoder and performs probabilistic latent-state inference through the learned model via extended Kalman filtering and smoothing, without a separately trained inference network. Its bilinear structure enables exact propagation of the maintained Gaussian belief through the latent transition and efficient rollouts for uncertainty-aware model predictive path integral (MPPI) control. For training K-PWM, we present a generalized expectation conditional maximization (GECM) procedure with closed-form updates for the generators, noise covariances, and latent prior, complemented by gradient-based decoder learning. A Koopman-consistent initialization selects the latent dimension from data and initializes the action-dependent dynamics. With limited training data, K-PWM achieves lower long-horizon prediction error than the evaluated baselines on partially observed Gymnasium MuJoCo systems, while substantially reducing training time relative to the strongest prediction baseline. On high-dimensional MuJoCo Menagerie robots, it achieves competitive tracking accuracy with low per-step computation times, demonstrating potential for real-time deployment. These results support structured probabilistic dynamics as a data- and compute-efficient approach to world modeling and control under partial observation.

Analysis increments for model bias diagnosis and correction: Theoretical foundations 2026-11-08
📝 15 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

A common method for diagnosing and correcting model bias in weather and climate prediction is based on analysis increments: the difference between the analysis and the forecast at each data assimilation cycle. Their time average is used to estimate and correct the bias, and machine learning is increasingly used to map the forecast state to the increments, giving a state-dependent correction. Despite its widespread use, the method lacks theoretical foundations. We first analyse a Kalman filter whose forecast model has a constant bias. We show what the averaged increment measures, and prove that iterating the correction removes all of the bias seen through the observations; for linear dynamics, no method can identify the rest. We then extend the analysis to state-dependent biases. For linear dynamics, each increment records the model error over the filter's memory, as seen through the observations. We use this to determine what the increments measure when averaged over a long period, averaged over periods with similar slowly varying conditions, and used without averaging, as in machine-learned corrections. Numerical experiments confirm the theory for a linear model and suggest that its main conclusions carry over to the nonlinear Lorenz '96 model, including for a correction learned by a neural network.

Maze Runner: Autonomous Maze Navigation with Line-Based Localization and Control Barrier Functions 2026-11-06
📝 10 pages, 8 figures, 1 table. Code: https://github.com/alvgaona/maze-runner
Show abstract

Maze navigation requires localization from sparse, repetitive geometry, planning through narrow corridors, and control that stays safe when the estimate or the map is wrong. We present Maze Runner, an open autonomy stack in C++ and ROS 2 that addresses these needs end to end on a simulated Husarion ROSbot XL with a 2D LiDAR in Gazebo. Perception extracts wall lines from each scan with RANSAC and total least squares, with per-line uncertainty. The same line front end feeds two estimators: an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that localizes against a line map built from the maze mesh, and an EKF SLAM variant that builds the line map online. Planning runs online on an inflated occupancy grid with A* plus line-of-sight shortcutting, or with a forward-only Hybrid A*. A nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) tracks the plan while discrete-time Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) on world-frame LiDAR points keep the robot clear of walls and unmapped obstacles. The algorithms live in a ROS-free, unit-tested core library, and the whole stack builds and runs from a single reproducible environment. In simulation, the robot drives a 135 m route through the maze with 1.0 cm localization RMSE against the known map and 24 cm in SLAM mode, the planner returns a path in 6 ms, and the NMPC solves in 11.7 ms on average, never exceeding its 80 ms budget.

Locating Contact on Tendon-Driven Continuum Robots From Tendon Measurements Alone via Constrained Moving Horizon Estimation 2026-11-06
Show abstract

Tendon-driven continuum robots can reach through constrained spaces, but contact along their bodies is invisible to the sensors at their motors. We show that this is a structural limitation rather than a tuning problem: the map from contact forces to tendon measurements has a null space of dimension at least seven, so no estimator that leaves the contact support free can locate contact. When the obstacle geometry is known, however, non-penetration and complementarity make the contact force locally identifiable, and we prove that the resulting system with unknown contact inputs is locally exponentially strongly detectable. We present a constrained moving horizon estimator (MHE) that encodes these conditions and runs in real time as one quadratic program per sample, and we give a local, mode-wise robust stability result for it. In a finite- element benchmark of a two-section robot pressing and sliding on a wall, the estimator located contact to 0.9 mm and estimated contact force to 0.22 N, compared with 65 mm and 0.72 N for a tuned extended Kalman filter (EKF) with state projection. The estimator tolerated millimeter- level errors in the obstacle position and unmodeled wall friction, under which the projected EKF failed. Under randomized model mismatch over 40 trials, including unmodeled tendon friction, it remained better in force and location in every trial. Without contact, the MHE offered no advantage over the EKF, which confirms that its benefit comes from the contact constraints. This report extends a letter submitted to IEEE Control Systems Letters with complete proofs and additional experiments.

Robust Ensemble Kalman Filtering Under Observation Noise Misspecification 2026-11-06
Show abstract

We address observation noise misspecification in Bayesian filtering of dynamical systems via generalised Bayesian inference. Mismatch in tail decay between the true data generating process and an assumed observation model, often indicated via outliers, can strongly impact Bayesian updates in Kalman filters and their ensemble approximations. Adjusting information processing in the analysis step by employing diffusion score matching achieves robustness while maintaining closed-form Gaussian updates and good uncertainty quantification. We provide theoretical properties of this novel Kalman filter in linear Gaussian state space systems covering conjugacy of the analysis step, robustness, covariance stability, and tuning. We extend the approach to non-linear signal dynamics by adapting ensemble Kalman filter variants. Simulation studies on 2D target tracking and the Lorenz-63 and 40D Lorenz-96 systems highlight a critical trade-off between robustness and stability in filtering.

Extended Model-Based Learned Inertial Odometry 2026-11-06
📝 8 pages, Published at ICUAS 2026
Show abstract

Inertial odometry is a compelling approach to state estimation for agile quadrotor flight due to its affordability, low weight, and robustness to perceptual degradation. However, relying only on integrated inertial measurements is impractical, as sensor errors and time-varying biases lead to significant pose drift. Although recent advances have enabled inertial odometry for drone racing, existing approaches show limited generalization to trajectories not seen during training. This work improves generalization by adopting a body-frame representation and incorporating body-frame torque dynamics into the learning pipeline. The learned model predicts short-horizon relative displacements, which are fused with IMU measurements in an Extended Kalman Filter. Experimental results show that the proposed method outperforms the previous state-of-the-art learned inertial odometry approach for quadrotor pose estimation on unseen trajectories. An additional evaluation on four public quadrotor datasets, with whole trajectory shapes held out of training, confirms a lower velocity error on every held-out set and shows that the torque input is most informative when it is derived from the rotor signals. Code, data pipeline and trained models are publicly available.

CoMET: Tracking the Shared Pose Error of Roadside Messages for Asynchronous Vehicle-Infrastructure Cooperative 3D Detection 2026-11-06
📝 25 pages, 4 figures. Code and experimental results archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23999418
Show abstract

A roadside sensor can detect vehicles hidden from the ego vehicle, but a pose error shifts every detection in the same message. We study asynchronous vehicle-infrastructure cooperative 3D detection on V2X-Seq using only information available at decision time, without ground truth, future frames, or per-pair calibration offsets at inference. Objects seen by both sensors reveal the shared translation, but even at zero added delay, 38% of validation messages have no matched pair. We propose CoMET (Common-Mode Error Tracking), which estimates this translation over time. At each message arrival, CoMET matches vehicle and roadside detections, estimates the offset from mutually nearest pairs, and updates a Kalman filter for that sender. When no pair matches, the filter carries its estimate forward. The correction applies to all roadside boxes, including those unseen by the ego vehicle. The estimator has no learned parameters; a separate frozen head ranks the merged detections. Across four delays from 0 to 400 ms on 21 validation sequences, CoMET averages 66.63 bird's-eye-view AP at IoU 0.7, compared with 63.18 for the same late fusion using uncorrected online poses and 62.96 for the best intermediate-fusion baseline. Across five detectors from three architectures, CoMET gains 2.2 to 3.5 AP points over online poses and 0.3 to 0.6 over estimating the offset from the current message alone. We find that the benchmark uses a zero offset as a placeholder in 28% of validation messages and that its offset search reaches the boundary in 45% of the rest. These offsets affect the labels for objects seen only from the roadside. Under two alternative label references, CoMET improves on uncorrected online poses in every evaluated population. Retraining intermediate fusion with CoMET poses and corrected labels improves detection on all three architectures tested, with statistically significant gains on two.

SCRUB: Contact-Based Surface-Frame Estimation for Force-Motion Control with an Armless Quadruped 2026-11-04
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Wiping, sanding, and inspection robots press a sliding tool on a surface with controlled force. Controlling that force needs the surface normal, which is unknown on real-world, unmapped surfaces. Surface-normal estimators can help estimate the surface normal, but they either need a friction model or assume that the instantaneous tool velocity lies along the surface; however, walking violates this assumption. We propose SCRUB, a contact-based surface-frame estimator, which lets an armless quadruped slide a body-mounted tool along near-vertical surfaces in the absence of known, axis-aligned, or vision-predicted normals. SCRUB's predictive controller presses and slides the tool using a Kalman-filter estimate of the normal from two cues: 1) A force cue measures the estimate's tilt across the motion. If friction directly opposes the motion, the cue is zero exactly when that tilt is zero, for any friction coefficient. However, if the cue's sliding direction is wrong, friction appears as a false tilt. 2) A velocity cue measures tilt along the motion. Walking and pressing move the tool in and out. SCRUB therefore subtracts the commanded press rate from the tool velocity, averages the result over each gait cycle, and treats this mean as lying along the surface. On Unitree Go1 hardware at nominal speed and load, SCRUB completed every wall traverse and column orbit. No orbit completed with either cue removed, without gait averaging, or with a fixed normal.

3D Pose Estimation in Projection Space via Pixel-level Visible Light Communication 2026-11-04
📝 submitted to IEEE Access
Show abstract

Projector-based augmented reality enables natural interaction between users and devices by overlaying digital information onto the physical world through projected imagery. A key technical requirement of such systems is accurate positional alignment between the projector and the devices. Pixel-level Visible Light Communication (PVLC) achieves this alignment without external sensing infrastructure by embedding invisible spatial codes into each projected pixel through high-speed brightness modulation that preserves image quality. However, the information that PVLC transmits is inherently planar. PVLC has therefore been limited to devices moving on a flat surface and cannot estimate the 3D pose of devices in three-dimensional space, such as aerial robots. To extend PVLC-based pose estimation to three dimensions, we formulate it as a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem. The 2D pixel positions that four coplanar photosensors on the device receive via PVLC serve as the image points, and their known 3D layout provides the object points. Experiments show that the proposed system estimates 3D pose with a latency of approximately 8~ms and a 3D position RMSE of 19.2~mm after Kalman filtering. These results confirm the first realization of real-time 3D pose estimation coordinated with high-quality projected image content without external sensing infrastructure.

Reflection-Robust Stereo Visual-Inertial Odometry via Kalman-Filtered Ground-Height Estimation 2026-11-04
📝 6 pages, 5 figures, 3 tables. Presented at the 26th International Conference on Control, Automation, and Systems (ICCAS 2026), Sapporo, Japan. Included in the conference proceedings
Show abstract

Visual-inertial odometry relies on visual features that correspond to 3D points with consistent projections across sequential frames. In reflective indoor-floor environments, however, specular floor patterns can be detected and triangulated as virtual features below the physical ground plane, producing unreliable feature associations and inconsistent visual residuals. This paper proposes a lightweight reflection-aware front-end for feature-based stereo VIO. Rather than suppressing all floor-region features, the proposed method estimates the ground height in real time and rejects only feature candidates whose inferred 3D positions are below the estimated ground height. A floor-region mask restricts the validation process to reflection-prone regions, while stereo height uncertainty and pose uncertainty are used to construct frame-level ground-height measurements. A scalar Kalman filter with Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS) gating then provides a stable ground-height estimate, and an uncertainty-aware safety margin is used for selective below-ground virtual feature rejection. Experiments on real reflective-floor indoor sequences show that the proposed front-end generally improves trajectory accuracy in reflection-dominant environments while maintaining accurate ground-height estimates and recovering from actual ground-height changes.

System-level performance in massive ISAC networks 2026-11-03
Show abstract

We propose a dynamic stochastic geometry framework to model and evaluate large-scale integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) networks. Base stations are distributed according to a homogeneous Poisson point process, while user equipments and sensing objects undergo localised random motion around their serving base station. Full spectrum reuse with a common waveform generates mutual interference that couples the two functionalities. Communication performance is characterised through downlink queues whose service rates are governed by the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) via the Shannon capacity. Sensing performance is captured by a Kalman filter (KF) whose measurement noise covariance depends on the sensing SINR, yielding a non-classical state-dependent filtering problem. We define system-level performance metrics under the Palm distribution of the base station process and prove the consistency of their empirical estimators. We then prove that, for a typical base station, communication and sensing metrics are associated, so that operating regimes favourable to one functionality tend to benefit the other. The proof combines the monotonicity of the queue and filter recursions in the configuration of interferers with the Harris--FKG inequality for Poisson functionals. We also show that the excess association ratio of the two interference levels decays as the inverse of the network density. Simulations based on a ray-tracing channel model corroborate the analysis.

An ensemble of graph generative models for assisted history matching of the Brugge field case 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Assisted history matching remains non-unique because many geological realisations can reproduce equivalent production responses with substantial differences between individual well matches. This ambiguity increases when a field comprises several depositional settings and modelling strategies. The paper applied a graph autoencoder approach to history matching of a benchmark Brugge field that comprises different depositional zones. Each stratigraphic zone is modelled with its dedicated generative model that comprises a joint latent space for optimisation history matching loops. Graph-based generators operate directly on the active cells of the corner-point grid, so its irregular geometry is handled without conversion to a regular lattice. The optimisation search in the 160-dimensional joint latent space is done with the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy. The multi-component objective function includes static well data mismatch, dynamic production mismatch, and a geological realism penalty based on the Decoder induced latent metric. Latent space diagnostics with topological data analysis are applied prior to history matching to test whether one generator is sufficient or whether a multiple generator formulation is required. The results show that the zone-based formulation gives coherent latent spaces, preserves the main porosity and permeability relations that vary from one zone to another, and supports history matching while keeping the solutions within the learned geological manifold. The matched models reproduce field oil and water production within the prior uncertainty range and provide a comparable well-level match to previously published Brugge studies based on Ensemble Kalman Filter, Particle Swarm Optimisation with principal component analysis of parameterisation, and Multiple Kernel Learning with Particle Swarm Optimisation.

A Tightly Coupled Magnetic-Inertial SLAM System for Field Robots 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Field robots operating in mines, tunnels, and disaster areas require reliable localization despite GNSS denial and degraded camera and LiDAR measurements caused by dust, smoke, or poor illumination. Missions involving both ground and aerial robots further motivate complementary sensing modalities that do not depend on visibility, preinstalled infrastructure, or wheel contact. This letter presents MISLAM, a tightly coupled magnetic-inertial simultaneous localization and mapping system using an IMU and a nine-magnetometer array, without relying on external odometry or platform-specific motion assumptions. The system combines the inertial navigation model with local polynomial magnetic-field models for short-term motion estimation and a global Gaussian process model for drift correction using an error-state Kalman filter. Offline evaluation on three indoor datasets collected with a handheld rig shows reductions in total endpoint closure error of 45.7% - 78.0% relative to magnetic-inertial odometry. Under poor illumination, MISLAM maintains tracking after visual-inertial odometry fails, achieving a horizontal closure error of 0.332 m, although vertical drift remains a limitation. These results support magnetic-inertial SLAM as a complementary localization modality for robotic sensor suites operating under degraded visual conditions.

Recent years have seen multiple impressive results in visual-inertial odometry (VIO) techniques, by which accurate state estimation can be achieved via the extended Kalman filter (EKF) or nonlinear optimization. However, these approaches are rarely open source, and they tend to fail in real experiments due to the temporary lack of feature points and fast motion. Compressed delayed-information projection for underwater navigation under acoustic measurement latency 2026-10-30
📝 31 pages, 7 figures, 6 tables. Submitted to The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America (JASA)
Show abstract

Delayed acoustic position measurements describe past states but may reach an underwater vehicle after its navigation estimate has advanced. This paper presents compressed delayed-information projection (CDIP), a causal delayed-update method implemented in a 15-dimensional error-state Kalman filter (ESKF). CDIP retains source-associated inertial measurement unit (IMU) snapshots and maintains current-to-snapshot cross-covariances, projecting delayed corrections without repeated full-from-start filtering. In 154 paired simulation recordings with synthetic horizontal-position fixes delayed by 1.5~s, CDIP reduced mean trajectory-position root-mean-square error (RMSE) from 1.062~m for a reception-time Baseline to 0.456~m (57.1\%). Its mean RMSE approached that of full-from-start Replay (0.451~m), with 99.2\% lower mean core-loop time per processed IMU step for the tested implementations. A six-delay sweep, scheduled permanent-loss outages of 50--300~s, and packet-erasure tests further characterized sensitivity to measurement timing and availability. A separate 256-trial nonlinear synthetic assessment identified departures from the prespecified calibration criterion for CDIP and two fully augmented ESKF references. Together, these results demonstrate an accuracy--computation trade-off for causal underwater navigation under the evaluated delayed-measurement simulation conditions.

Optimal Path Planning with Localization constraints 2026-10-29
Show abstract

This paper studies UAV path planning in areas where GPS signals are not present. Because these drones can easily get lost when they don't have GPS, they must rely on dead reckoning and occasionally pass near cellular towers, which act as landmarks to reset and fix their position. The goal is to find the shortest path from a start to a goal location while keeping the estimation uncertainty within a given bound. To solve this problem, we propose a localization-constrained A* planner that treats uncertainty as a hard constraint during search. The robot state and covariance are propagated using an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), and only paths that satisfy the covariance limit are considered. We also show that the proposed method does not affect heuristic used in A* and remains admissible and consistent, ensuring optimality among all feasible paths. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations and PyBullet quadrotor test show that the proposed method finds shorter and more reliable paths compared to RRT* and PRM, while always satisfying the uncertainty constraint.

Measuring the Value of Communication in Multi-Agent Tracking Under Finite-Rate Belief Sharing 2026-10-29
Show abstract

This paper investigates the value of communica- tion (VoC) in cooperative multi-agent tracking under rate- constrained information exchange. Although prior work has studied distributed tracking, quantized communication, and robust fusion, it remains unclear at what communication rate most of the achievable tracking gain is obtained, beyond which additional communication resources yield only marginal benefit. We consider stationary agents that observe a moving target through heterogeneous range and bearing measurements, perform local extended Kalman filtering (EKF), quantize their posterior state means, and fuse exchanged information via covariance intersection (CI). Performance is evaluated versus communication rate relative to no-communication baselines, a centralized oracle, and a CI-perfect reference. The results show that communication is not uniformly beneficial: very low rates can degrade performance due to coarse quantization, whereas moderate rates recover most of the achievable gain. In the considered scenario, increasing the rate from 4 to 12 bit/s per agent reduces the root-mean-square error (RMSE) from 13.93 m to 6.35 m, while a further increase to 32 bit/s per agent improves it only marginally to 5.93 m. This reveals a clear knee region at moderate rates, beyond which the remaining loss is mainly due to the conservatism of the fusion architecture. Overall, the proposed framework characterizes low-rate degradation, communication-efficient operation, and diminishing returns, while highlighting the joint role of rate and sensing geometry in determining communication benefit.

Deep Bayesian Filtering for Nonlinear Structural System Identification: A Tutorial on Structural and Physical Inductive Biases 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Nonlinear structural system identification remains challenging when governing physics is only partially known and measurements are sparse. Deep Bayesian filtering addresses these challenges by combining learnable dynamical representations with probabilistic state-space models, physical inductive biases, and uncertainty-aware inference. This paper presents, for the first time, a unified tutorial treatment of deep Bayesian filtering for nonlinear structural system identification, connecting model formulation, learning, posterior inference, and evaluation. Amortized variational inference and differentiable Kalman filtering are introduced as complementary strategies within this broader framework. The Deep Markov Model (DMM) provides a data-driven reference, while the Physics-guided Deep Markov Model (PgDMM) and Neural Extended Kalman Filter (Neural EKF) serve as illustrative instances through which methodological choices and their implications are examined. Dynamic state estimation and joint state and parameter estimation are demonstrated on a three-dimensional shear-frame benchmark with hysteretic Bouc-Wen connections and sparse acceleration measurements. The comparative analysis yields new observations concerning the relationships among reconstruction accuracy, predictive fidelity, physical interpretability, and estimation robustness. Physical structure improves latent-state interpretability without necessarily ensuring faithful predictive dynamics, while comparable typical reconstruction accuracy can conceal substantial differences in estimation robustness. These findings motivate practical guidelines for model construction, training, and evaluation that distinguish reconstruction, forecasting, state recovery, and parameter identification. The tutorial concludes by outlining challenges in uncertainty calibration, identifiability, and validation on real structures.

Forward-Backward Map Matching for Airborne Magnetic Anomaly Navigation 2026-10-27
📝 37 pages, 11 figures, submitted for publication: The Journal of Navigation (CUP)
Show abstract

Airborne magnetic anomaly navigation provides a passive, unjammable alternative to Global Navigation Satellite Systems positioning by matching magnetometer measurements against a lithospheric magnetic anomaly map. Existing map-matching algorithms either assume a Gaussian posterior (extended Kalman filter), require Monte Carlo sampling (particle filter), or discard posterior uncertainty in favour of a single optimal path (Viterbi algorithm). Here we formulate map matching as inference on a chain-structured factor graph and solve it segment by segment using the forward-backward algorithm, which yields a discrete posterior over candidate positions and its Shannon entropy $H_t$ at time $t$. Over the \texttt{Renfrew\_395} map, $10^3$ synthetic trials yield a median position error of $32.7$ m, $91\%$ below the median inertial drift of $362$ m. On these trials and $10^3$ flight paths, the median error is $43\%$ below the extended Kalman filter and particle filter. Across these paths, a threshold on segment-level entropy detects $47\%$ of segments with errors above two map cells and $94\%$ of those above three, at a $10\%$ false-alarm rate. The foregoing results indicate that $H_t$ provides a truth-free diagnostic of map-conditioned navigation ambiguity.

Smoothing splines with some discontinuities 2026-10-23
📝 15 pages, 5 figures, 4 tables; 77 pages of supplementary material. R package jumps available on CRAN
Show abstract

We generalize cubic smoothing splines to signals with a finite number of discontinuities at unknown positions. Rather than changing the roughness penalty, we deform the domain over which it is computed: every interval between consecutive observations can be stretched by a non-negative warp, subject to a total budget, and the optimizer spends the budget only where a jump makes stretching cheaper than bending. The estimator inherits the state-space form of smoothing splines, so it can be fitted by maximum likelihood with the Kalman filter, and it also admits a fast alternating algorithm that solves a banded linear system and takes a projected-gradient step on the warps. The budget is selected by an extended Bayesian information criterion whose constant is calibrated by simulation. In a Monte Carlo comparison the new estimators reconstruct a piecewise-smooth signal more accurately than the cubic smoothing spline with discontinuities of Storath and Weinmann, at the cost of more false alarms. Two energy applications illustrate the method: the temperature response of electricity demand in Northern Italy, and retail fuel prices, whose discontinuities, changes in excise duties, are dated by public record. Multi-robot SLAM allows robots to improve their estimates by exchanging map information, but repeated exchanges create unknown correlations between local estimators. Covariance intersection (CI) can fuse estimates without tracking these correlations. In right-invariant extended Kalman filter (RI-EKF) SLAM, however, point features obtain their invariant errors through the robot rotation, so a neighbor's map estimate is expressed in its own error coordinates and is correlated with its pose, and cannot be fused by CI directly. This paper presents Joint-CI, a unified fusion method for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed point--object maps. Using a neighboring robot's local posterior and an inter-robot observation, a joint estimate of the receiving robot's pose and the common features is constructed in the receiving robot's error coordinates, retaining the robot--feature and feature--feature correlations. Partial-state CI then fuses this estimate with the receiving robot's local posterior. We show that the unified formulation and the fusion preserve the six unobservable directions associated with global rotation and translation, and that communication and fusion preserve first-order covariance consistency given consistent local posteriors. Monte Carlo simulations and a real-world dataset-derived experiment show that Joint-CI improves estimation accuracy while maintaining consistent, mildly conservative uncertainty.

Integro-Difference Equation (IDE) models are a class of dynamic spatio-temporal models which describe the temporal evolution of a spatial process, usually to model advection and diffusion of some random field over time. While several papers have focused on inference and performance of these models, little attention has been given to computational comparisons of various methods of computing posterior densities and performing inference. We address this problem in two parts. Firstly, in inference for these models, Kalman filtering (and smoothing) is often used; either to compute marginal likelihoods, or to sample from the hidden process conditional on the rest of the parameters of the model. There are many variations of the Kalman filter and smoother with substantially varying performance profiles and stability: the standard Kalman filter, its square-root form, its information form, to name a few. The information filters, when set up correctly, can compute the likelihood in $\mathcal O(nr^2 + r^3)$ time per time point ($n$ being the number of observations per time point), where the computation in $n$ can be done in parallel, on models with independent observation error. In this paper, we analyse these forms in the context of IDEs, considering computational speed and stability. Secondly, there are two main methods of inference used for the kind of dynamical system that IDEs can be reduced to; directly using the marginal likelihood without the hidden state, or constructing a block Gibbs sampler with the hidden state. We compare these two approaches in terms of computational efficiency and practical performance in the IDE setting. Using a block Gibbs sampler for inference on the system parameters proves to be more efficient in most cases, with a specific real-data example providing an exception.

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve high-fidelity image and video generation, but they suffer from substantial inference costs due to repeated transformer evaluations across many denoising steps. Feature caching reduces this cost by reusing features across denoising steps. Recent forecasting methods extend this idea by extrapolating features to skipped steps instead of reusing them. However, existing forecasting methods extrapolate the features they predict uniformly, even though the spatial-frequency components of these features change most at different stages of denoising. Causal adaptive intensity estimation for non-stationary point processes: a Kalman-kernel duality framework 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Estimating the instantaneous conditional intensity of a non-stationary point process from a single realization of discrete event timestamps is a fundamental problem in computational neuroscience, quantitative finance, and network monitoring. Traditional adaptive kernel methods achieve variable bandwidths through Abramson's square-root law, yet existing implementations require non-causal access to the complete event record and incur $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ or at best $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$ computational cost. Causal alternatives based on reciprocal inter-arrival times, meanwhile, suffer from systematic divergence under Poisson and bursty arrival dynamics due to Jensen's inequality. This paper addresses both limitations by establishing a formal duality between steady-state Kalman filtering and adaptive exponential kernel smoothing. An endogenous state-space observer is constructed in which Poisson counting statistics dictate the measurement noise ($R \propto λ$) and geometric diffusion governs the process volatility ($Q \propto λ^2$). Under these physically motivated axioms, the algebraic Riccati equation autonomously yields an effective smoothing bandwidth scaling as $τ\propto λ^{-1/2}$, giving a causal, recursive, $\mathcal{O}(1)$ per-step realization of square-root adaptive bandwidth scaling. A second-order constant-velocity extension produces a $λ^{-1/4}$ scaling that reduces approximation bias, and a probabilistic dormant-state gating mechanism suppresses phantom intensity leakage during structural zeros. Synthetic benchmarks together with neural and financial data show that the observer adapts across orders of magnitude in intensity with an empirical per-step cost of approximately 30\,$μ$s.
