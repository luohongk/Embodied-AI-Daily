GHySSeg: Real-Time Geometric Ground and Road Segmentation for Hybrid Solid-State LiDAR 2026-11-16
📝 14 pages, 8 figures, 3 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems. This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible. HySoLiD-Drive dataset: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21705196
Show abstract

Hybrid solid-state LiDAR sensors are making their way into production vehicles, yet the data they produce differs structurally from that of the established spinning mechanical sensors behind today's autonomous-driving datasets: it is ring-free, dense, cluttered, and concentrated in a frontal field of view. The classical geometric methods that autonomous vehicles rely on for drivable space computation and navigation assume the ordered ring-and-column structure of a 360-degree spinning LiDAR and degrade or fail on this data, while learned alternatives are computationally heavy, hard to certify, and trained almost exclusively on spinning-LiDAR data. We demonstrate that the same dense, per-curve geometry is instead an opportunity to close the divide between the industry's preferred sensors and autonomous-driving research. We present GHySSeg, a purely geometric method that segments ground and drivable road from a single hybrid solid-state LiDAR frame by tracing each scan curve and reading two geometric signatures, a step in height and a fold-back in range, with no training and no graphics processing unit. To evaluate it, we release HySoLiD-Drive, the first public benchmark dedicated to ground and road segmentation on an automotive hybrid solid-state LiDAR, comprising 100 manually labeled and 7000 unlabeled frames recorded by two vehicles in real driving. On this benchmark, GHySSeg surpasses both geometric and learned state-of-the-art baselines across ground and road segmentation, reaching an intersection-over-union of 0.907 for road and 0.896 for ground at over 0.96 precision, while running in real time at about 22 milliseconds per frame on a single core.

VGGT-VLA-World: Intent-Conditioned 3D World Evolution for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-16
📝 12 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

VGGT provides a strong foundation for geometry-centric world models by recovering unified 3D scene geometry from visual observations. Although recent extensions enable temporal 3D prediction, their future evolution remains weakly conditioned on driving intentions and actions, limiting their ability to model alternative action-dependent futures. We propose \textbf{VGGTWorld-VLA}, an intention-conditioned extension of VGGT-World for controllable 3D world evolution in autonomous driving. First, we introduce an action--semantic conditioning mechanism that injects complementary driving semantics and ego-motion representations into the future-token stream, enabling different future geometry predictions for the same observed scene under alternative ego actions. Second, we develop a geometry--language--action bridge that adapts historical geometry, VLA semantic features, and maneuver and trajectory representations for joint conditioning of future geometry prediction. We evaluate future geometry prediction on NAVSIM, with ablation studies examining the contributions of semantic and action conditioning. Compared with baseline, our method reduces future-depth RMSE by 13.7\% and improves $δ_1$ by 4.11 percentage points on NAVSIM motion subset. Controlled action-input interventions further show that the model uses the supplied action information. These results support semantic and action conditioning for controllable VGGT-based world prediction in autonomous driving.

IGRecon4D: Dynamic-Object-Aware Feed-Forward Reconstruction of Driving Scenes 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Feed-forward 4D driving-scene reconstruction enables efficient modeling of dynamic autonomous-driving scenes, but current methods suffer from two complementary failure modes: semantic leakage of traffic participants into the static representation and geometric drift of Gaussian locations across viewpoints. We propose \textbf{IGRecon4D}, a feed-forward dynamic scene reconstruction framework that addresses these failures with training-time instance-prior distillation and LiDAR-anchored Gaussian positioning. Specifically, an instance-level semantic branch provides a potentially dynamic participant prior, which is distilled into dynamic-mask learning through the Instance-Dynamic Consistency Constraint to encourage high-recall localization of traffic-participant regions prone to dynamic-static leakage. In parallel, sparse LiDAR measurements guide dense depth estimation, and the resulting depths are back-projected to anchor Gaussian positions for improved multi-view geometric consistency. Experiments on the Waymo Open Dataset and zero-shot evaluation on nuScenes demonstrate improved full-image reconstruction on the main benchmark and stronger traffic-participant-region reconstruction under cross-dataset evaluation.

STC-MGNet: Spatial-Temporal-Channel Attention-Enhanced Multimodal Graph Network for Pedestrian Intention Prediction 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Accurate prediction of pedestrian crossing intentions plays a critical role in enhancing the safety of pedestrian-vehicle interactions in autonomous driving. However, effectively capturing subtle spatiotemporal patterns in pedestrian pose sequences and integrating complementary visual and ego-vehicle motion information remain challenging. This paper proposes a spatial-temporal-channel attention-enhanced multimodal graph network (STC-MGNet) for pedestrian crossing intention prediction. STC-MGNet introduces a spatial-temporal-channel (STC) attention-enhanced pose graph encoder to learn discriminative representations from pedestrian pose sequences. Three parallel attention branches selectively emphasize informative skeletal joints, observation frames, and feature channels, with their outputs integrated through learnable fusion weights. To leverage complementary visual and ego-vehicle motion information, a multi-stage cross-modal channel modulation mechanism is introduced to adaptively refine pose representations through modality-specific channel gating at successive graph encoding stages. Experimental results demonstrate that STC-MGNet achieves accuracy rates of 70%, 87%, and 88% on the JAADbeh, JAADall, and PIE datasets, respectively, outperforming several representative baseline methods. Ablation experiments further highlight the complementary contributions of visual and ego-vehicle motion information to prediction performance.

Adversarial Tracking: Attacks on Image Sensors for Object Tracking in Autonomous Vehicles 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Object Tracking systems estimate the motion of surrounding vehicles by linking detected objects across video frames. In particular, Tracking-By-Detection (TBD) systems combine object detectors with motion prediction and data association to maintain object identities over time. However, neural network-based detectors are vulnerable to adversarial attacks that perturb input images, and their impact on downstream trajectory estimation is not fully characterized by detection accuracy alone. Therefore, this paper evaluates image-specific and image-agnostic attacks on YOLOv3 and YOLOv4 detectors paired with SORT, Deep SORT, Strong SORT, and OC-SORT tracking systems. The evaluation uses a CARLA autonomous driving simulator and measures tracking quality through Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA), Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA) and Identification F1 (IDF1). Under image-specific attacks, HOTA for YOLOv3-based trackers declines from 53.45-56.19% to 14.85-17.03%, while MOTA reaches -772.60% in the worst case. YOLOv4-based trackers exhibit greater robustness, while HOTA still declines to 25.06-26.86%. In addition, image-agnostic attacks can reduce the tracking accuracy using a single Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP), without generating a separate perturbation for individual frames.

CoopDistill: Equilibrium-Guided Multi-Agent Policy Distillation for Cooperative Driving 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Cooperative driving extends autonomous driving from individual vehicle decisions to joint maneuvers that improve traffic safety and efficiency. Optimization-based methods impose safety constraints but often fix paths to simplify multi-vehicle planning, limiting spatial flexibility. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) explores alternatives through interaction, but coordinated learning and safe execution remain challenging. Their integration nevertheless faces challenges in exploration efficiency, experience allocation, and execution reliability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes CoopDistill, an equilibrium-guided multi-agent policy distillation framework. A potential-game (PG) formulation provides a fixed-path longitudinal reference, around which a residual MARL policy learns longitudinal acceleration, conflict-approach timing, and lateral path corrections. A hard-case curriculum concentrates experience on interactions that PG leaves unresolved or substantially delayed. A comparative rollout selector evaluates the joint consequences of learned corrections against PG before choosing the executed action. Experiments in merging, roundabout, and intersection scenarios compare CoopDistill with six baselines at four connected automated vehicle penetration levels. Relative to PG, success improves by 2.1--27.1 percentage points and delay decreases by 7.3--27.3\%, with no observed collisions. Code is available at https://github.com/FanGShiYuu/CoopDistill

Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving operate primarily through feedforward inference across the perception--reasoning--planning hierarchy. While modern architectures maintain temporal recurrence within the perceptual module, early perception remains blind to downstream reasoning and navigation goals, processing visual inputs agnostically without prioritizing cues informed by prior decisions. To bridge this gap, this paper introduces PRIME, a learned feedback mechanism that conditions the VLA perceptual queries on a novel Situational Memory. By aggregating latent representations of past perception, reasoning, navigation goals, and predicted behaviors across an L-step window via cross-attention, PRIME enables intent-driven perceptual attention at minimal computational cost, adding only a maximum of 29.7M parameters (0.41% of the 7.3B-parameter base model). Evaluated on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, PRIME achieves a state-of-the-art Driving Score of 82.47 (+4.73 over ORION) and a Success Rate of 60.00% (+5.38 percentage points), the highest reported Driving Score among published VLAs trained on Think2Drive demonstrations.

STA: A Split Temporal Adversary Method for Evaluating Robustness of Multimodal RL-based Driving Policies 2026-11-16
📝 10 pages, 6 figures, submitted for publication
Show abstract

Multimodal reinforcement learning (MRL) offers a promising paradigm for autonomous driving by jointly leveraging complementary sensor observations to learn effective decision-making policies. However, its effectiveness critically depends on temporal alignment across modalities, making temporal inconsistency a fundamental vulnerability. Even subtle misalignment or latency can corrupt fused representations and ultimately lead to decision failures. Existing studies have explored adversarial attacks to induce cross-modal temporal inconsistencies, thereby enabling systematic evaluation of the temporal robustness of MRL policies. However, existing methods remain limited in generality, realism, and adaptivity. To address these limitations, we propose the Split Temporal Adversary (STA), which formulates multimodal temporal attacks as a unified decision process that jointly optimizes attack timing and modality selection under realistic policy-aware and budget-constrained settings. STA includes two mechanisms: a) a unified multimodal temporal perturbation mechanism that delays observations via historical states, removing the need for modality-specific noise; b) a split multimodal adversarial decision mechanism that jointly optimizes attack timing and modality selection through adaptive optimization while leveraging the victim policy's clean actions to identify sensitive decision patterns. We provide a theoretical analysis of the induced distribution shift and a modality selection principle. Experiments in the CARLA simulator show that STA more than doubles the collision rate of the strongest baseline against PPO while using 17.8\% fewer attack attempts, and that it generalizes to a VLM-based driving policy. These results reveal temporal consistency as a critical vulnerability, highlighting temporal robustness as essential for safe autonomous driving.

UNIVERSE: Unified Video Action Models for Autonomous Driving with Flexible Mask-Modulated Modality Generation 2026-11-16
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/xiaomi-mlab/UNIVERSE

Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) can improve action generalization in autonomous driving by learning scene dynamics through dense future-video supervision. However, deploying such models often requires costly future-video generation, while architectures that bypass video rollout typically separate video and action parameters or alter their learned dependencies at inference. We introduce UNIVERSE, a unified video-action model built on a single mask-modulated Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that combines shared-parameter learning with modality-independent inference. Its Modality-Decoupling Visibility Mask allows future video and trajectory tokens to attend to a common historical context while blocking mutual attention between the two future modalities. As a result, future-video supervision directly updates the parameters used for trajectory denoising, yet video tokens can be omitted at test time without changing the trajectory branch's training-time attention dependencies. The same model supports trajectory-only, video-only, and joint video-action generation. Trajectory-only inference achieves a 4.3x speedup over coupled video-action rollout with comparable planning accuracy. UNIVERSE achieves 91.0 PDMS on NAVSIM, compared with 89.6 for a Two-DiT variant, and demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer to nuScenes and Bench2Drive without target-domain fine-tuning. Controlled ablations further support the complementary benefits of shared parameters, dense video co-training, and modality-decoupled visibility.

Learning-based Autonomous Oversteer Control and Collision Avoidance 2026-11-16
📝 14 pages, 8 figures, 4 tables. Accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles
Show abstract

Oversteer, wherein a vehicle's rear tires lose traction and induce unintentional excessive yaw, poses critical safety challenges. Failing to control oversteer often leads to severe traffic accidents. Although recent autonomous driving efforts have attempted to handle oversteer through stabilizing maneuvers, the majority rely on expert-defined trajectories or assume obstacle-free environments, limiting real-world applicability. This paper introduces a novel end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving approach that tackles oversteer control and collision avoidance simultaneously. Existing E2E techniques, including Imitation Learning (IL), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Hybrid Learning (HL), generally require near-optimal demonstrations or extensive experience. Yet even skilled human drivers struggle to provide perfect demonstrations under oversteer, and high transition variance hinders the accumulation of sufficient data. Hence, we present Q-Compared Soft Actor-Critic (QC-SAC), a new HL algorithm that effectively learns from immature demonstration data and adapts rapidly to new conditions. To evaluate QC-SAC, we introduce a benchmark inspired by real-world driver training: a vehicle encounters sudden oversteer on a slippery surface and must avoid randomly placed obstacles ahead. Experimental results show that QC-SAC attains near-optimal driving policies, significantly surpassing state-of-the-art IL, RL, and HL baselines. Hardware-in-the-loop simulation (HILS) validation with physical steering hardware further demonstrates that QC-SAC can operate beyond a software-only simulation, providing an intermediate validation step toward real-vehicle implementation. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that achieves safe autonomous control of vehicles in oversteer while simultaneously avoiding obstacles.

The proliferation of cloud-based latency-sensitive workloads, such as autonomous driving, real-time healthcare services, industrial automation, immersive interactive services, and intelligent AI-based applications requires infrastructures tuned to meet stringent latency constraints. Competent Autonomous Driving Through Fine Tuning Vision Language Action Models with Symbolic Traffic Rules 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models offer a unified approach to perception, reasoning, and control for autonomous driving, but accurate action prediction alone does not establish compliance with traffic regulations. This paper presents a rule-guided VLA framework that incorporates symbolic traffic-rule feedback into policy optimisation. The framework combines supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations with reinforcement learning (RL) refinement using a reward that encourages encoded-rule compliance while penalising deviations from expert actions. Selected provisions of the United Kingdom Highway Code are formalised as executable first-order logic rules using PyReason, covering speed adaptation, following distance, and road-user priority. A frozen vision-language model extracts scene predicates, which are combined with vehicle-state information to assess the projected consequences of candidate control actions. Offline evaluation on held-out data collected in the CARLA simulator compares three group-based RL methods. The results show improvements in both action prediction accuracy and encoded-rule compliance over the supervised baseline. The best-performing variant increases the overall rule pass rate from 92.9% to 97.6%, with zero observed violations of the encoded road-user-priority check in the evaluated samples. These findings support symbolic rule feedback as a means of improving VLA policy alignment with explicit traffic-rule criteria.

VoxSAOR: Semantic-Aware Occupancy Robustification via Aleatoric Uncertainty Modeling 2026-11-16
📝 The source code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/MengfeiD/VoxSAOR
Show abstract

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction provides a dense semantic representation of scenes for autonomous driving and embodied perception, yet remains vulnerable to occlusion, depth ambiguity, and projection errors that induce spatially varying reliability in voxel features. Existing methods typically map voxel representations to semantic predictions without explicitly modeling such observation-induced feature variations. To address this limitation, we propose VoxSAOR, a semantic-aware occupancy robustification framework that models aleatoric uncertainty through feature-space proxies. It first quantifies Local Semantic Consistency (LSC) to derive a voxel-wise structural stability prior and uses cross-view semantic consensus to filter unreliable semantic correspondences. Within each semantic category, spatially matched stable and ambiguous voxels form stable-to-ambiguous feature transitions, which are modeled by a class-conditional diagonal Gaussian distribution, capturing both feature displacement and dispersion. Guided by these proxy distributions, Aleatoric Regime Ensemble Learning (AREL) trains independently parameterized prediction heads using feature perturbations at progressively increasing scales, with perturbation strength modulated by structural reliability. At inference, proxy modeling and sampling are disabled, and all heads process the same observed features; their output probabilities are then averaged to produce a deterministic consensus prediction without requiring sampling. Experiments across multiple datasets and occupancy backbones demonstrate the effectiveness of VoxSAOR in improving semantic occupancy prediction, long-tail recognition, and predictive calibration. The source code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/MengfeiD/VoxSAOR.

Camera recordings for autonomous driving contain faces and license plates that should be anonymized before labeling, sharing, or training. SAFER: Navigating the Exploration-Exploitation Dilemma in World-Model-Based Planning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-15
Show abstract

An autonomous vehicle must foresee the consequences of its actions, especially in the rare, time-critical situations where crashes happen. A world model offers this foresight, but only within the coverage of its training data, which poses an exploration-exploitation dilemma. Within this coverage a planner can hardly improve on the data, and beyond it the model's errors mislead it. Logs of competent driving rarely contain the failures that would widen the coverage, and agents that learn from interaction gain them only by failing themselves, without being shown what to do instead. We propose SAFER, a framework that learns offline from failures that others make, judged by an expert, and keeps learning online from its own driving. Offline, partially trained agents and scripted, recoverable perturbations Stage failures in simulation, and an expert Annotates every visited state with its action and value, so that much of what the planner explores is covered before it drives. In planning, a chunked latent world model Foresees the consequences fast enough for real-time use, and a risk-aware sampling-based planner Evaluates candidate maneuvers by their return minus a bounded collision penalty. Online, a loop continuously Refines the model wherever this driving leaves the coverage of the offline data. In closed-loop evaluation against the compared methods, SAFER reaches the highest route completion and reward per step on the CARLA Leaderboard routes, 0.95 and 0.77 from a privileged bird's-eye view, and the highest episode reward in MetaDrive, 53.4 in regular and 49.0 in safety-critical traffic. Project page: https://ys-qu.github.io/safer-website.

D'BEAST: Logged Bad Experiences as Explanation for Autonomous Driving Agents 2026-11-15
Show abstract

Reinforcement learning agents learn from mistakes, yet their decisions can be difficult to trace back to those experiences. We investigate whether a tabular agent's decisions can be explained by its bad experiences, defined as the training transitions associated with safety or task events. A fixed training log is replayed from the same initial Q-table under three conditions. FULL retains all updates, SKIP omits bad-experience updates, and NEUTRALIZE retains those updates without their event penalties. We illustrate the method on six classic Markov decision processes and evaluate it in closed-loop driving in CARLA. On the full driving task, FULL reached the goal from 33 of 40 test starts, whereas SKIP and NEUTRALIZE reached it from none, indicating that the bad experiences carry decision-relevant information. At the local level, for 47 decisions shortly before a failure of SKIP or NEUTRALIZE, removing or neutralizing a small group of bad experiences, with a median size of one, caused the trained agent to select the failing policy's action in 46 cases. For this agent, the results indicate that its decisions can be explained by its bad experiences.

ReactBench-AD: Do Driving VLAs and World-Action Models React to Imminent Hazards in Their Path? 2026-11-15
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) and World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged in autonomous driving with the promise of strong generalization to long-tail scenarios. Although these models achieve state-of-the-art results on autonomous driving benchmarks, most existing benchmarks are dominated by nominal scenarios and therefore cannot test this promise. To test one such long-tail capability that is critical for safety, we introduce ReactBench-AD, a benchmark that measures how driving models react to objects that appear in the ego vehicle's path. Built on real NAVSIM and PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles scenes, ReactBench-AD composites a crossing pedestrian, a standing pedestrian, or a stationary car consistently across all camera views and history frames. Each edited scene thus forms an exact, pixel-level counterfactual pair with its original, which allows us to isolate the causal effect of the inserted object on the predicted trajectory. Evaluating four VLAs and two WAMs, we find that they react weakly, often barely at all. Finally, we test how easily one of the evaluated VLAs, ReCogDrive, can be taught to brake hard for imminent hazards: a short fine-tuning stage on edited scenes substantially improves its reactions, with the learned behavior generalizing to unseen objects and fully real scenes, at minimal cost to nominal driving. These results call into question the out-of-the-box long-tail capabilities of these models, but suggest that they can be a strong foundation for acquiring them with little additional training, highlighting the value of real or synthetic data that demonstrates the long-tail behaviors nominal datasets rarely contain. Code and data will be made publicly available.

Beyond Vehicle-Centric Data: Scaling Autonomous Driving through Roadside-to-Vehicle Generation 2026-11-15
Show abstract

Training autonomous driving models requires large and diverse datasets, collected primarily by vehicles. Scaling them entails investment in fleet operation, data processing and annotation. Road infrastructure records traffic and multi-vehicle interactions, yet the viewpoint gap between roadside and onboard sensors limits its direct use for training. Here we introduce AnyR2V, a framework that transforms multimodal roadside observations into seven-camera surround-view videos for different vehicle perspectives. It aggregates sequential observations into a shared scene representation of geometry and road-user motion, then renders appearance, depth and layout conditions along target-vehicle trajectories. Supervised by spatiotemporally aligned roadside-vehicle pairs, AnyR2V generates surround-view videos guided by scene structure and road-user motion. Existing roadside 3D bounding boxes are transformed into target-vehicle coordinates and paired with generated images, allowing one traffic recording and its annotations to yield training data from multiple vehicle perspectives. AnyR2V outperforms six scene reconstruction methods in image fidelity and in depth and semantic agreement between generated and real onboard images. We further evaluate generated videos along other vehicles' trajectories by adding them to real training data. Adding generated frames equal to 50% of the original real training frame count improves mean average precision (mAP) by 3.3-3.4 AP-points across three 3D object detectors on the real onboard test set, relative to real-data-only baselines. These results demonstrate that roadside traffic records can provide training data from multiple vehicle perspectives, offering a new data source for autonomous driving.

Do VLMs Really Look at the Road? A Diagnostic Evaluation for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-14
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly being evaluated for autonomous driving through question-answering benchmarks, yet high benchmark accuracy does not necessarily reflect visually grounded reasoning.
