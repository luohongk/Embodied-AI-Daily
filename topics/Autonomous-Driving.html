FutureLift: Lifting Trajectory Proposals into Bidirectional Future Graphs for Motion Forecasting 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted for presentation at WACV 2027
Show abstract

Accurate motion prediction is critical for safe autonomous driving, yet even strong forecasters leave residual errors in their multimodal proposals. Existing refiners compress complete proposals or update their segments sequentially, preventing the full proposed future from serving as explicit relational context for correction. We introduce FutureLift, a backbone-agnostic refiner that represents each multimodal proposal as a heterogeneous future graph, with spatially grounded temporal nodes connected to agent histories, map elements, alternative modes, and, for joint prediction, other agents future trajectories. Bidirectional temporal edges allow later evidence to revise earlier segments in a single pass, and the updated nodes predict residual coordinate and confidence corrections. Requiring only proposals, scores, and scene context, FutureLift provides a common refinement interface for structurally different forecasting backbones. On Argoverse2 and the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, it consistently improves frozen proposals and outperforms native and specialized refiners across marginal and joint forecasting. On the WOMD motion-prediction leaderboard, FutureLift ranks first in minADE (0.5049m) and minFDE (0.9942m) among the compared standard-input methods, without LiDAR or ensembling, while achieving a competitive 0.4874 Soft mAP.

FALCON: FPGA-Accelerated Latency-Certifiable Occupancy Networks for Camera-Based Autonomous Driving 2026-10-15
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027 (Applications track). 10 pages
Show abstract

A perception system that must brake for a pedestrian does not need a low average latency. It must deliver an answer before the deadline on every frame, the worst case included. Commodity edge accelerators optimize the mean through dynamic scheduling, OS jitter, thermal throttling, and contention, so their per-frame latency can be measured but not bounded. We build FALCON, a camera-based 3D occupancy front-end on the AMD Kria KR260, an embedded FPGA drawing 4.3 W idle and up to 6.6 W under inference, and make the latency guarantee layered and explicit: programmable-logic (PL) compute is cycle-bounded analytically; inference is a measured envelope over 100k-frame traces; and a watchdog enforces output delivery, publishing the previous output flagged stale when a frame is late. The deadline runs use a fixed-work proxy of six ResNet-50 DPU inferences per frame, not the full occupancy network: across three 100k-frame enforced runs, all 300k proxy frames delivered an output within the 100 ms deadline plus at most 10.2 ms, the worst of the three runs. The full six-camera FlashOcc pipeline composes to about 1.3 s per frame on the board and is not yet a 10 Hz system. The two operations the DPU cannot run, a view-transform gather and a bilinear resize, become fixed-trip-count PL IPs numerically matched to their software references. On the full Occ3D-nuScenes validation set the board reaches mIoU 29.88, 92.8% of an FP32 baseline the DPU cannot run. On a ResNet-50 probe over 100k frames the KR260 tail stays within 1.5 times its median (2.0 times for the six-inference proxy), where Jetson Orin reaches 4.4 times and the worst datacenter GPU run (NVIDIA L4) 23.6 times. The deployment recipe, HLS sources, and raw timing traces are released.

GRID-Planner: Graph-Structured Relational Reward Shaping for Diffusion Planning 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Autonomous driving planning requires selecting trajectories that remain safe, rule-compliant, and comfortable under complex multi-agent interactions, yet robust closed-loop decision making in planning-critical scenes remains challenging. Existing diffusion- and RL-based planners often rely on generic rewards and use relational structure mainly for representation, limiting how planning-critical rollouts shape policy improvement. We present GRID-Planner, a graph--reward integrated diffusion planning framework that aligns generative planning with structured relational signals. Specifically, GRID-Planner combines graph-aware importance weighting, a time-indexed relational reward surrogate, and an advantage-weighted diffusion objective with on-policy self-distillation, forming a rollout-return-advantage-update loop without explicit likelihoods. Experiments on nuPlan and interPlan show that, compared with the diffusion-only baseline, GRID-Planner reduces collision rate, lane deviation, and jerk by 35.8\%, 60.8\%, and 27.4\%, respectively, and improves the planning score by 3.4\%. Learning Rewards in Strategic Games 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Understanding the objectives that drive agents' behavior is crucial in strategic environments, where multiple agents interact and adapt to one another. This challenge arises in domains such as cybersecurity, autonomous driving, and online markets, where agents' underlying incentives are typically unknown and must be inferred from their observed behavior. Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) provides a natural framework for this problem, but extending IRL to strategic multi-agent settings introduces a fundamental challenge: a reward that explains observed behavior does not necessarily induce optimal strategies when used for decision-making. In this work, we study this problem through the lens of \emph{strategic identifiability}, and show that learning from a fixed policy pair can be insufficient to strategically identify rewards, independent of the number of expert samples $K$. Motivated by this, we introduce an active IRL algorithm for two-player repeated Stackelberg games, in which the leader adaptively selects policies to elicit informative responses about the follower's reward. Combining maximum-likelihood estimation with experimental design, we derive finite-sample guarantees on the Stackelberg sub-optimality induced by the learned reward, matching up to polylogarithmic factors a new minimax learning rate of $K^{-1/2}$. Finally, we evaluate our approach in a realistic anti-poaching setting, demonstrating the benefits of active exploration for reward recovery and downstream strategic performance.

Physical AI requires models to ground visual and linguistic understanding in real-world environments while accounting for environmental constraints and execution feedback. We introduce MachEmbodied-VLM (ME-VLM), a unified vision-language model with two variants, 4B and 35B-A3B, that brings together embodied cognition and multimodal agent capabilities. Our work emphasizes physical perception and spatiotemporal reasoning, together with planning, interaction, and outcome assessment in both digital and physical environments. Drive As You Like: Multi-Head Diffusion with Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Driving 2026-10-15

Personalized autonomous driving requires understanding the driving needs implicit in user requests. This is challenging when driving demonstrations lack style annotations and behavioral adaptation risks degrading planning quality. We propose M-Diffusion Planner with a dual-decoupling design: separating intent understanding from trajectory planning, and general planning capability learning from strategy alignment. A large language model infers driving needs and selects a strategy, which is executed by the corresponding head of a shared diffusion planner. Following imitation pretraining, strategy-specific rewards specialize individual heads without style annotations. We innovatively adapt Group Relative Policy Optimization using surrogate policy-ratio estimation and constrained head-specific updates to mitigate degradation of pretrained planning capabilities. SpaceCache: Intermediate Feature Reuse for Efficient Semantic Segmentation on Point Cloud Streams 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Point-cloud streams are central to autonomous driving and robotics, yet existing segmentation networks process every frame from scratch, although consecutive frames largely overlap. We observe that the intermediate features of point-cloud transformers remain semantically consistent across frames for the same spatial region, especially in deep stages. Building on this, we present SpaceCache, a training-free feature cache that reuses these features across frames. Tokens of previously observed regions skip a sequence of transformer blocks and take their cached features, only the remaining tokens are computed, and the full token layout is restored for dense prediction. Lightweight cache management keeps reused features reliable by limiting each entry's age and evicting low-confidence entries. Unlike token merging, SpaceCache selects tokens at negligible cost and runs its cache operations once per encoder stage. On both outdoor and indoor benchmarks, such as nuScenes, Waymo, and ScanNet, with two transformer backbones of Utonia and LitePT-B, SpaceCache achieves up to 1.65x speedup with less than 1-point mIoU drop and consistently outperforms token-merging methods.

Trajectory Prediction Method for person under WSN with Hierarchical Envelope Adversarial Domain Adaptation 2026-10-15
📝 24 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Pedestrian trajectory prediction holds significant applications across autonomous driving, intelligent surveillance, and smart cities. However, the complexity and unpredictability of pedestrian interactions make this task a persistent challenge. Current models face two common limitations: 1) Consider only a single type of social coupling. This introduces redundant interactions. More critically, it fails to account for the varying nature of trajectory coupled across different scenarios and between different pedestrians. Consequently, models cannot deeply adapt to diverse scenes. 2) Inadequate domain shift handling. Although very few methods address domain shift, they rely on statistical criterion-based domain distribution alignment. Such approaches exhibit strong dependency on predefined statistical metrics. This leads to significant limitations in complex and dynamic environments. To address these problems, a Hierarchical Envelope Adversarial Domain Adaptation with Loosely-Tightly coupled model (HEADA-LTCTPM) is proposed. First, an envelope sample construction mechanism was proposed. It constructs envelope samples and extends them into graph structures. Secondly, an adversarial domain adaptation module was proposed. This integrates both local and global domain adaptation strategies. It dynamically adapts to diverse coupled relationships across scenarios. These innovations collectively enhance prediction accuracy and robustness. In the experimental section, two representative public datasets were employed for validation and comprehensive comparisons with six relevant baseline algorithms were conducted. Results demonstrate that our model achieves significantly higher accuracy compared to existing methods, with the ADE and FDE metrics reduced by 17.6% and 19.1% respectively. The time overhead meets practical requirements, which verifies the effectiveness of our key innovations.

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing 2026-10-14
📝 13 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles
Show abstract

Vehicles that lose control at the handling limit through icing, hydroplaning, or an abrupt evasive maneuver fall outside the operating envelope of production safety systems such as electronic stability control, which focus on preventing instability rather than recovering from it. Expert drivers regain authority in these conditions by drifting. Existing autonomous recovery approaches are validated on idealized scenarios, define "recovered" states empirically and inconsistently, or rely on offline-planned trajectories rather than acting online. We address these gaps by first training a racing policy with reinforcement learning that learns drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, then adapting it to recovery through continued training with a minimum-time reward. We define recovery relative to a phase-plane-grounded stable equilibrium and introduce Gym-Khana for controller training and evaluation. Across 294 initial loss-of-control states, the transferred policy improves recovery by 186.8% over a Stanley path tracker and outperforms both a nonlinear model predictive controller and a recovery-only policy trained from scratch, while also recovering faster on states the baseline can already handle. An ablation shows that racing pretraining, rather than broader initial-state exposure alone, drives this gain. Deployed on a physical 1:10-scale F1TENTH vehicle using system identification, actuator-lag modelling, and domain randomization, the transferred policy outperforms Stanley on every tested loss-of-control state, including recovering 50% of the time from a hard state the baseline never recovers from.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are gaining attention in autonomous driving, inheriting commonsense priors from large language models (LLMs) and widening the navigation interface from a fixed command set to free-form natural-language instructions. However, this open-ended interface exposes a new vulnerability surface: an inattentive driver may issue instructions that are unsafe given the current scene, and a malicious user may issue dangerous ones on purpose. We present the first benchmark suite that evaluates navigation following and conflict awareness together, along with adversarial robustness. Existing VLAs fall short on both: SimLingo completes no language-instructed lane change in closed loop, and all models degrade sharply under conflicting signals. Investigating Behavior Cloning from Few Demonstrations for Autonomous Driving based on Bird's-Eye View in Simulated Cities 2026-10-14
📝 14 pages, 6 figures, 2 tables. Accepted manuscript (author version) of the paper published in the proceedings of BRACIS 2024 (34th Brazilian Conference on Intelligent Systems, Belém, Brazil, 17-21 November 2024). Project page: https://sites.google.com/view/weighted-bc
Show abstract

This paper investigates the use of Behavior Cloning (BC) for autonomous driving from a bird's-eye view (BEV) perspective in simulated urban environments. BC uses supervised learning to mimic expert driving behaviors. Previous works have applied BC in the CARLA simulator but did not fully address the challenges of traffic light compliance. Our approach enhances BC by integrating a kernel density estimator to adjust training sample weights based on action density, thereby improving the learning of rare but critical actions such as stopping at red lights and accelerating at green lights, specially in scenarios of scarce number of expert demonstrations. Using BEV inputs, which provide an abstract top-down view of the driving environment, our method simplifies the policy learning process. The trained convolutional neural network (CNN) outputs steering and acceleration actions based on these BEV inputs and additional state variables. Experimental results in the CARLA simulator demonstrate that our weighted BC method significantly improves driving performance, achieving higher route completion compared to standard BC. This weighted approach proved to be crucial in learning correct driving behaviors, particularly in test environments not encountered during training, highlighting its potential for enhancing autonomous vehicle navigation.

Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Model Decision-Making for Autonomous Driving Guided by Chinese Philosophical Wisdom 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Autonomous driving decision systems must balance safety, efficiency, and social norms in complex traffic interactions. Philosophical and ethical considerations have received limited attention in existing autonomous driving decision-making approaches based on numerical optimization, sequence prediction, and large language models (LLMs). We propose Chinese Philosophical Wisdom-Guided Driving (CPW-Drive), a closed-loop retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that incorporates value guidance derived from Chinese philosophy into autonomous driving decision-making. The results suggest that structured value guidance can improve simulated closed-loop safety and stability while introducing efficiency and latency trade-offs.

CorrelationFlow: A Training-Free Geometric Approach for LiDAR Scene Flow Estimation 2026-10-14
Show abstract

LiDAR scene flow is the task of estimating point-wise motion from sequential point clouds. It is essential for autonomous driving, as it provides critical information about moving objects and their trajectories. In recent years, unsupervised methods have gained significant traction by eliminating the need for expensive point-wise annotations. However, despite incremental performance improvements, these methods uniformly rely on a single paradigm: training feed-forward networks through optimizing a loss function based on the Chamfer distance, a framework that inherently limits their progression. This paper presents CorrelationFlow, a new paradigm that reformulates LiDAR scene flow as a correlation maximization problem between Bird's-Eye View (BEV) images. Instead of using learnable parameters, our approach employs a chain of image processing operations to solve this problem. The learning-free nature of CorrelationFlow enables strong generalization across diverse domains. This capability is demonstrated by the second-place ranking in the unsupervised track of the Argoverse 2 Scene Flow Challenge at CVPR 2026, which featured a test set spanning five datasets that cover varied vehicle platforms and environments. Our results show that this classical, model-based approach can match or even surpass the performance of learning-based methods, thereby opening a new direction for unsupervised LiDAR scene flow research. The source code will be released.

MMTA: Benchmarking Multimodal Temporal Analysis with Time Series, Text, and Vision 2026-10-14
📝 Accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track. Project page: https://windowso.github.io/MMTA/
Show abstract

Time-series analysis is increasingly studied in multimodal settings, yet most benchmarks pair temporal signals with text or with visualizations derived from the same signal. This leaves open whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can integrate time series with real-world visual evidence. We introduce MMTA (multimodal temporal analysis), a benchmark of tri-modal tasks with diverse cross-modal complementarity patterns among time series, text, and vision. MMTA spans nine domains -- robotics, autonomous driving, human action, manufacturing, AR assistance, healthcare, recommendation, finance, and climate -- under video+TS and image+TS layouts, with a 3,524-sample test split covering classification and forecasting. We then introduce TimeOmni-v, a multimodal LLM extension with a dedicated time-series encoder, temporal positional alignment, modality-aware token layout, and a forecasting head. TimeOmni-v improves long-video classification and time-series inference efficiency, showing that MMTA not only exposes representation and grounding failures but also motivates targeted model design.

Comprehensive Comparisons of Reinforcement Learning Algorithms in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-14
Show abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive comparison of four representative reinforcement learning (RL) methods in autonomous vehicle (AV) decision-making, including: Fitted Value Iteration (FVI), Rollout Classification Policy Iteration (RCPI), Deep Q-Network (DQN), and a discrete-action adaptation of Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (D-DDPG). The four methods are applied to the same AV decision-making problem at an unsignalized intersection and are compared in terms of optimality and safety, training stability, and training and decision cost. RCPI is the closest to a reference solution and has the most stable training process. However, it is the least data-efficient. D-DDPG reaches the second-smallest gap to a reference solution with the highest data efficiency, but has the least training stability. DQN is the most stable model-free method but has the highest collision rate. FVI achieves a lower collision rate than DQN, but is less data-efficient and less stable. Based on these results, we provide suggestions for selecting a method according to whether a model is available, the budget for training, and whether stable training is required.

AutoAgent0: An Agentic Runtime for Safe Closed-Loop Driving 2026-10-13
📝 Project page: https://jiagengliu02.github.io/AutoAgent0/
Show abstract

Safe autonomous driving requires more than accurate planning at a single frame: it demands reliable closed-loop execution in dynamic environments. Although end-to-end driving policies have advanced rapidly, the accumulated closed-loop errors, especially in unseen scenarios, can push vehicles into states from which the same policy struggles to recover. We address the challenge by treating closed-loop driving safety as a test-time execution problem. We introduce AutoAgent0, an agentic runtime and harness framework that augments existing driving experts with complementary detection skills, rule-based action verification, and feedback-driven recovery. To address inaccurate scene perception, an independent detection module constructs scene context using complementary perception skills, and a rule-based verifier checks proposed trajectories against this context before execution. When the nominal trajectories are unreliable, the orchestrator of the runtime activates Recovery Planning, where an agentic loop constructs alternative action proposals from action primitives and iteratively refines them using verification feedback. This design combines independent scene assessment with feasibility checks and online recovery, addressing both perception errors and the limited recovery capabilities of individual experts. Evaluations on Fail2Drive and NavSafe-$\infty$ demonstrate improved closed-loop robustness over single-expert execution in long-tail and safety-critical scenarios. These results demonstrate that runtime supervision can improve closed-loop robustness across multiple driving experts without retraining the underlying policies.

Quadruped robots performing outdoor deliveries must interpret complex scenes, anticipate changes, and navigate in socially compliant ways. Robot demonstrations are costly to collect at the scale needed for generalization. Egocentric web videos offer abundant human navigation experience, but differences in viewpoint and motion make this experience difficult to transfer to robots. We introduce DR-VLA, a Mixture-of-Transformers framework that transfers human navigation experience through a domain-invariant scene representation (DISR). As a future-prediction target, DISR outperforms five generic visual representations and yields performance close to task-specific 3D perception without dedicated geometric labels or point clouds for target construction. Trained separately on NAVSIM v1 without web pretraining, DR-VLA achieves 92.1 PDMS, suggesting that the framework also applies to autonomous driving.

Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved strong image and video understanding, yet their visual-spatial representations remain geometrically fragile, leading to failures in spatial reasoning needed for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous driving. Prior approaches to geometry grounding either fine-tune VLMs on spatial question answering, which can perpetuate spurious visual representations, or fuse features from large geometry-grounded vision models, which substantially increases model size at inference. Knowledge distillation from geometry-grounded vision models offers an alternative, but directly matching multi-view teacher features can disrupt the pretrained alignment between visual and textual representations, degrading object- and language-semantic capabilities. We propose multi-view relational distillation (MVRD), which distills patch-wise cosine similarities across views instead of the teacher features themselves. These relations encode geometric correspondences adequate for spatial understanding, while leaving the student representation underdetermined, allowing it to remain close to its pretrained vision- language space. Across representative VLMs, MVRD improves visual-spatial reasoning, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and feature distillation while approaching feature fusion methods with considerably fewer added parameters and lower latency. We show that MVRD makes visual representations more geometric while retaining language alignment, and generalizes to 3D scene understanding tasks such as object grounding, dense captioning, and question answering.

Beyond Planning: Differentiable Control-in-the-loop End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-13
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved remarkable progress by planning trajectories directly from raw sensor data. Yet the step from planning to control still relies on hand-tuned tracking controllers that block the gradient path from vehicle motion back to the network. This gap stems from non-differentiable vehicle dynamics, aliased supervision from coarsely sampled expert labels, rate mismatch between high-rate control and low-rate re-planning, and vehicle model discrepancies between training and deployment. This paper proposes DIVA (Differentiable Identified Vehicle dynamics for end-to-end Autonomous driving) to close this gap. An identified actuator-level vehicle model is embedded in the training loop of the end-to-end driving network, forming a differentiable chain from perception to vehicle states. A learning-based control decoder replaces the external controller and is supervised on the rolled-out vehicle states, bypassing the aliased control-input labels. A rate-consistent control-input parameterization reconciles the heterogeneous sampling rates and enables asynchronous execution with a 3.8 ms control-only cycle. On-policy re-identification and an online disturbance observer address training-time distribution shift and deployment-time model mismatch respectively. On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, DIVA achieves a driving score of 86.7, surpassing the widely adopted state-of-the-art approach by 3.3 points while nearly halving mean jerk and reducing brake switching by 64%, and maintains superior path tracking accuracy under significant vehicle model mismatch at deployment.

Before Parc Fermé: RL-Time Pruning for Efficient Embodied LLMs in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-13
Show abstract

Embodied Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used as reasoning modules in robotic control pipelines to improve human-robot interaction, but their memory and generation latency make real-time deployment difficult. Pruning can reduce these costs, but for controllers that undergo multiple pre- and post-training phases, the crucial question is not only how much to prune, but when pruning should occur. In this work, we propose Before Parc Fermé (BPF), a pruning strategy performed during RL that compresses embodied LLM controllers while they are still being optimized for closed-loop behavior. This allows pruning decisions to account for the task-specific supervision and closed-loop feedback that shape the final controller. We propose two variants: BPF-RL, which performs iterative pruning during RL by removing part of the model at predefined training intervals, and BPF-SFT/RL, which first prunes part of the model structure during SFT and then further compresses it during RL using the same iterative strategy as BPF-RL until the target pruning ratio is reached. We evaluate BPF on RobotxR1, an LLM-based autonomous-driving control pipeline, using an established LLM pruning framework (LLM-Pruner), and compare it against post-training pruning, post-training pruning with RL recovery, SFT-stage pruning, and smaller dense models from the same family. Our results show that BPF provides the best task-performance vs. memory and throughput trade-off among the considered pruning strategies. When compressing the larger RobotxR1 models, BPF-SFT/RL achieves a $1.69\times$ better size-end-to-end performance trade-off than directly selecting a smaller dense model from the same family, measured as removed parameters per lost percentage point of control adaptability. On the Jetson AGX Orin mounted on the target robotic platform, the compact models improve decode throughput by up to $27\%$.
