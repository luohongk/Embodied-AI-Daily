CARE-Drive: Coupled Alternating Refinement for Ego-trajectories 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models bring multimodal understanding and world knowledge to end-to-end autonomous driving. To further improve the quality of predicted trajectories, existing studies introduce candidate refinement, residual denoising, and token correction. However, these methods typically focus on unified refinement of the entire trajectory, paying limited attention to the distinct physical quantities and their interactions. We further investigate physical specialization and coordination in trajectory refinement, centering on position and heading for simpler and more intuitive correction modeling. To this end, we propose CARE-Drive (Coupled Alternating Refinement for Ego-trajectories), which decomposes trajectory refinement into waypoint residual correction and position-conditioned heading generation. Through imitation learning and a future-token auxiliary task, the backbone provides predictive representations and a base trajectory. The position expert predicts bounded metric waypoint residuals, while the heading expert reconstructs vehicle orientation from adjacent angular increments conditioned on the corrected path. With the backbone frozen, the two experts are optimized alternately through supervised initialization and reinforcement fine-tuning. This design simplifies refinement through clear physical specialization while preserving conditional coupling during prediction and optimization. On NAVSIM v1, CARE-Drive achieves 88.8 PDMS, improving by 2.4 points over the unrefined base trajectories generated by the same backbone. Ablation studies further support the effectiveness of specialized experts and staged training.

Towards Robust Vision-Language Reasoning: A Multi-Stage Optimization Pipeline with Masked Diffusion Model 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Discrete diffusion generation is emerging as a competitive alternative to autoregressive generation by denoising the entire token sequence through iterative refinement. Its global planning and progressive correction capabilities are particularly well suited to autonomous driving. However, transferring this generation paradigm from general-purpose settings to driving introduces substantial domain gaps in both training and inference. To unlock its potential, we propose a multi-stage optimization pipeline for standard vision-language model architectures, without auxiliary action experts or specialized mixture-of-experts Transformer, spanning post-training and inference. During supervised fine-tuning, we optimize the representation of planning inputs to suppress hallucinations in task responses. During reinforcement learning, we introduce metric-guided diversity filtering to identify stronger learning starting points and improve policy exploration. At inference time, we propose Dynamic Autonomous Driving Decoding (DAD), which adaptively adjusts decoding modes according to scene complexity. Based on this pipeline, we develop Masked Diffusion Drive (MD-Drive), improving its performance by 29.7\% over the unoptimized model. Experiments on the NAVSIM closed loop benchmark and the nuScenes and DriveCoT open loop datasets show that MD-Drive outperforms autoregressive VLM baselines with the same architecture, while achieving a more favorable balance between performance and computational cost than systems with different architectures through discrete diffusion training and inference.

Can MLLMs Reason over Surround-View Driving Videos? A Multi-View Autonomous Driving QA Benchmark 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Surround-view video understanding in autonomous driving is a crucial capability for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve reliable driving reasoning. Real-world driving often requires information beyond the front-view camera. Existing MLLMs primarily focus on front-view information, while their spatio-temporal understanding and reasoning abilities remain unknown. To address this gap, we propose MVDriveBench, a high-information-density video QA benchmark for autonomous driving, characterized by synchronized surround-view inputs, spatio-temporal understanding, and reasoning-chain-oriented evaluation. We design 12 categories of QA tasks that cover the complete reasoning pipeline from low-level visual perception to high-level driving decision-making, enabling a systematic evaluation of MLLMs surround-view spatio-temporal understanding and reasoning capabilities in complex driving environments. We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of 23 open-source, closed-source, and expert MLLMs on MVDriveBench. The results show that current MLLMs still exhibit insufficient surround-view spatio-temporal reasoning ability. Meanwhile, closed-loop evaluations of specialist models in AlpaSim reveal a correlation between surround-view spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities and closed-loop driving performance, highlighting the importance of comprehensive multi-view understanding for reliable vehicle control.

Are We Ready for Snow-tonomous Driving? Challenges Toward Robust Automated Driving in Extreme Weather 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Automated driving has gained remarkable momentum over the past decade, with both industry and academia investing substantial resources in developing perception and control solutions to advance vehicle autonomy. However, these efforts have largely focused on clear-weather scenarios, leaving harsh conditions, such as snow, rain, and fog, comparatively understudied. As a result, inclement weather remains one of the most significant hurdles for autonomous vehicles, often leading to substantial performance degradation in downstream perception and control tasks. In this paper, we present insights from the EU-funded Horizon Europe project ROADVIEW, which aims to develop robust, weather-aware perception and control modules for connected and automated vehicles operating under severe conditions, including snow, rain, and fog. We summarize these challenges and lessons learned toward enabling reliable snow-autonomous driving, highlighting critical directions for future research and standardization.

3D simulation platforms are critical for autonomous driving because they enable end-to-end policy evaluation, thereby reducing development costs and improving safety. In recent years, neural simulation has become predominant, with methods such as NuRec playing a central role; however, these methods remain relatively slow and typically require per-scene tuning. In this work, we present Instant NuRec, a feed-forward neural reconstruction model that turns a short multi-view driving log into a fully simulatable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) world in a single forward pass. It reconstructs a 10-20-second multi-camera scene in roughly 1.5 seconds and achieves a PSNR on the Waymo Open Dataset that is 2.01 dB above the strongest evaluated baseline. Instant NuRec is deeply integrated into NuRec and is compatible with AlpaSim for closed-loop simulation.

DRS-VPT: Directly Relocalizing in a Scan with Vision Point Transformers 2026-11-10
📝 ECCV 2026. Code: https://github.com/ori-drs/drs-vpt Project page: https://ori-drs.github.io/drs-vpt-pages/
Show abstract

We present DRS-VPT, a feed-forward transformer architecture for foundational image-to-scan registration. Given query images and a reference 3D point cloud, the model predicts the scan pose and point map alongside the poses and point maps of each camera, all expressed in the first camera's frame. It additionally predicts a coarse-to- fine pyramid of per-point and per-pixel features for direct reprojective alignment of the scan to the first image. This formulation unifies downstream tasks such as camera-LiDAR calibration in autonomous driving and indoor camera-to-map relocalization. A single DRS-VPT model achieves state-of-the-art performance for image-to-LiDAR registration in autonomous driving, competitive indoor relocalization without training map-specific weights, and strong zero-shot transfer to unseen environments. We also show qualitatively that the model learns complex scan-to-image projection properties such as occlusion of back-facing points. Spatio-Temporal Calibration of a Vehicle-Mounted Microphone Array for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Submitted to IEEE ICRA 2027
Show abstract

Microphones let an autonomous vehicle sense into areas its cameras and LiDAR cannot see, by picking up the sound that other traffic participants emit. Locating those sources and fusing them with the visual perception requires the microphone array to be calibrated and registered relative to the vehicle's other sensors. Existing calibration methods assume small, unobstructed indoor rigs, whereas a vehicle body casts an acoustic shadow that straight-line propagation cannot represent, and to our knowledge no calibration of an array distributed around a vehicle has been reported. We propose a pipeline that uses a balloon burst as a single cross-modal target, which the LiDAR locates in metric coordinates, every microphone hears as a sharp onset and a camera times. Each observation is weighted by its own timing variance, which discounts shadowed arrivals instead of discarding them and needs no model of the vehicle. On a full-size vehicle with eight microphones over a 4.9 m aperture, the held-out range residual of 6.37 cm approaches the 6.07 cm floor set by the onset timing noise, and the calibrated array tracks a moving source to within a few degrees of the vehicle's own LiDAR. A dozen bursts spread around the vehicle suffice, and spreading them matters more than adding more.

RiSE-Drive: Risk-Sensitive Long-Horizon Candidate Evaluation for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 11 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables, including supplementary material. Published in ACM Multimedia 2026 (MM '26)
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving systems increasingly rely on generating multiple trajectory candidates and selecting one for execution. In this setting, a central challenge is no longer only candidate generation, but how to evaluate candidates according to their long-horizon consequences under uncertainty. Existing methods still mainly use deterministic point scores, heuristic subscores, or single-outcome evaluations, which makes it difficult to capture delayed failures, tail risk, and ranking instability under plausible future perturbations. We present RiSE-Drive, a framework for risk-sensitive long-horizon candidate evaluation in end-to-end autonomous driving. RiSE-Drive first performs candidate-conditioned latent future rollout, then estimates long-horizon outcome distributions instead of single scalar scores, and finally ranks candidates using a tail-risk-aware and perturbation-robust decision criterion. This reformulates candidate scoring from deterministic oracle-style evaluation into distributional outcome assessment with explicit risk control. As a result, the evaluator is encouraged to prefer candidates that are not only strong under nominal conditions, but also reliable under long-horizon rollout and safety-critical deviations. Experiments on the NAVSIM-v1 and NAVSIM-v2 benchmarks, as well as the closed-loop HUGSIM benchmark, show that RiSE-Drive achieves state-of-the-art performance and significantly improves the robustness of candidate selection.

MAPLE: Latent Multi-Agent Play for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are effective as end-to-end motion planners, but can be brittle when evaluated in closed-loop settings due to being trained under traditional imitation learning framework. Existing closed-loop supervision approaches lack scalability and fail to completely model a reactive environment. We propose MAPLE, a novel framework for reactive, multi-agent rollout of a dynamic driving scenario in the latent space of the VLA model. The ego vehicle and nearby traffic agents are independently controlled over multi-step horizons, while being reactive to other agents in the scene, enabling closed-loop training. MAPLE consists of two training stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on the latent rollouts based on ground-truth trajectories, followed by (2) reinforcement learning with global and agent -specific rewards that encourage safety, progress, and interaction realism. We further propose diversity rewards that encourage the model to generate planning behaviors that may not be present in logged driving data. Notably, our closed-loop training framework is scalable and does not require external simulators, which can be computationally expensive to run and have limited visual fidelity to the real-world. MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art driving performance on Bench2Drive and demonstrates scalable, closed-loop multi-agent play for robust E2E autonomous driving systems.

Route-Time Conflict Maps for Cooperative End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Existing cooperative end-to-end driving works at Class A (status-sharing) of the SAE J3216 classes of cooperative driving automation (CDA): vehicles exchange what they perceive, not what they intend, so each plans alone when to go, which limits cooperation and leads to deadlock when both vehicles yield or to collisions when both go. We present When2Go, a cooperative end-to-end driving system that learns when to go by sharing intent under CDA Class B or higher. Each vehicle broadcasts its LiDAR detections with forecast motion as instance tokens, together with its plan and intended trajectory. The receiver draws the fused detections, forecasts and partner plans into a route-time conflict map, the occupancy along its route over the planning horizon, on which a planner scores candidate speed profiles. Where two intended trajectories conflict, an order gate, a precedence network distilled from a right-of-way rule, decides who goes first; both vehicles evaluate it on the same messages and so reach the same order. When2Go imitates the rule-based expert of V2Xverse, which sees the simulator's ground truth; stops that When2Go cannot observe within its 2 s planning horizon are relabelled, so that it does not learn to wait without an observable cause. For our runs only, the benchmarks' PID controller steers by When2Go's waypoints instead of the route, and its forced push of long-waiting vehicles is off. With one model, When2Go sets the state of the art on three closed-loop CARLA benchmarks: 97.8 and 90.7 driving score on InterDrive without and with traffic (best published 90.1 and 78.9), 70.0 on the public V2Xverse protocol (best of sixteen rerun methods 59.1) and 88.2 on MDrive (best rerun 85.1), with 95.0% of its vehicles collision-free (87.7%).

Generative Scenario Rollouts for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
📝 Accepted NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are emerging as highly effective planning models for end-to-end autonomous driving systems. However, current works mostly rely on imitation learning from sparse trajectory annotations and underutilize their potential as generative models. We propose Generative Scenario Rollouts (GeRo), a plug-and-play framework for VLA models that jointly performs planning and generation of language-grounded future traffic scenes through an autoregressive rollout strategy. First, a VLA model is trained to encode ego vehicle and agent dynamics into latent tokens under supervision from planning, motion, and language tasks, facilitating text-aligned generation. Next, GeRo performs language-conditioned autoregressive generation. Given multi-view images, a scenario description, and ego-action questions, it generates future latent tokens and textual responses to guide long-horizon rollouts. A rollout-consistency loss stabilizes predictions using ground truth or pseudo-labels, mitigating drift and preserving text-action alignment. This design enables GeRo to perform temporally consistent, language-grounded rollouts that support long-horizon reasoning and multi-agent planning. On Bench2Drive, GeRo improves driving score and success rate by +15.7 and +26.2, respectively. By integrating reinforcement learning with generative rollouts, GeRo achieves state-of-the-art closed-loop and open-loop performance, demonstrating strong zero-shot robustness. These results highlight the promise of generative, language-conditioned reasoning as a foundation for safer and more interpretable end-to-end autonomous driving.

Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved strong image and video understanding, yet their visual-spatial representations remain geometrically fragile, leading to failures in spatial reasoning needed for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous driving. Prior approaches to geometry grounding either fine-tune VLMs on spatial question answering, which can perpetuate spurious visual representations, or fuse features from large geometry-grounded vision models, which substantially increases model size at inference. Knowledge distillation from geometry-grounded vision models offers an alternative, but directly matching multi-view teacher features can disrupt the pretrained alignment between visual and textual representations, degrading object- and language-semantic capabilities. We propose multi-view relational distillation (MVRD), which distills patch-wise cosine similarities across views instead of the teacher features themselves. These relations encode geometric correspondences adequate for spatial understanding, while leaving the student representation underdetermined, allowing it to remain close to its pretrained vision- language space. Across representative VLMs, MVRD improves visual-spatial reasoning, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and feature distillation while approaching feature fusion methods with considerably fewer added parameters and lower latency. We show that MVRD makes visual representations more geometric while retaining language alignment, and generalizes to 3D scene understanding tasks such as object grounding, dense captioning, and question answering. Code is available at https://github.com/kietngt00/MVRD

Distill to Think, Foresee to Act: Cognitive-Physical Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Current end-to-end autonomous driving models are fundamentally constrained by the behavioral cloning ceiling of imitation learning. While reinforcement learning offers a path to smarter autonomy, it demands two missing pieces of infrastructure: (1) a cognitive foundation that understands traffic semantics and driving intent, and (2) a foresighted physical environment that can anticipate the consequences of candidate actions. To this end, we propose CoPhy, a CognitivePhysical reinforcement learning framework for autonomous driving. To distill to think, we distill VLM knowledge into the BEV encoder and then discard the VLM entirely, retaining cognitive ability at zero inference cost while releasing the cognitive channel as a pluggable interface for optional human language commands. To foresee to act, we build an auto-regressive BEV world model that explicitly predicts future semantic maps conditioned on candidate actions, serving as an interpretable physical sandbox from which safety metrics are directly derived. Built upon this dual infrastructure, we optimize the driving policy via GRPO with a novel dual-reward mechanism: a physical reward derived from BEV rollouts enforces hard safety constraints, while a cognitive reward from a language-aligned scorer ensures intent compliance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CoPhy not only achieves state-of-the-art results on NAVSIM v1 and v2 benchmarks, but also enables safer driving via cognitively informed scene compliance and flexible intent control through user-defined language instructions. Project page: https://wuyang98.github.io/CoPhy/.

Efficient Spintronic Stochastic In-Sensor Computing Modules 2026-11-09
Show abstract

In-sensor processing (ISP) is used in various fields, including autonomous driving automotive systems, wearable healthcare devices, environmental monitoring, wireless sensor networks, smart cities, and industrial control systems. One of the main features of most of these applications is energy and hardware restriction. This restriction amplifies the importance of using high-performance and low-energy consumption computing systems and methods. This paper proposes efficient spintronic stochastic computing modules for in-sensor computing systems to address this challenge. The proposed design utilized the stochastic behavior of the magnetic tunnel junction in the subcritical switching regime to generate a probability-adjustable random stream. Probability-adjustable random stream generation is the most important step in any stochastic system because it determines the quality of the final output. The simulation results indicate that the proposed design offers almost the same peak signal-to-noise ratio and structural similarity while occupying at least 62\% lower area and consuming 60\% lower energy. These advantages made the proposed design a good choice for ISP applications with limited energy.

FogDrive: A Multi-Modal Synthetic Driving Dataset for Perception under Graded Fog 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Fog degrades autonomous-driving perception across modalities: it lowers image contrast, attenuates and scatters LiDAR returns, and shifts the input distribution of every learned module downstream. Choosing between restoring the degraded input and training on it directly requires the same scenes observed with and without fog at known severities, and enough foggy data to train on. Real-world capture cannot supply this: fog arrives entangled with lighting and traffic, at severities recorded rather than chosen; existing synthetic datasets narrow the sensor suite, the scale, or the severity range. We present FogDrive, a CARLA dataset of ~660 scenes (~133k frames), each released as a clear reference plus three calibrated fog densities, with physics-based fog on camera and LiDAR and 2D/3D annotations consistent across all variants. Every frame carries four cameras (RGB, depth, semantic), LiDAR, semantic LiDAR and radar -- spanning fog's response spectrum from heavily degraded cameras to largely unaffected radar. Experiments trained on FogDrive show that mixing fog densities improves 3D detection at no extra data cost, that PSNR and SSIM poorly predict a dehazer's detection benefit, and that radar improves camera-LiDAR fusion at every density. The dataset and its generation framework will be released.

Mitigating SM-Level Load Imbalance in 3D Gaussian Splatting on GPUs 2026-11-09
Show abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables photorealistic, real-time rendering on commodity GPUs and is increasingly adopted in generative world models and autonomous driving simulators. However, existing optimizations rely on algorithmic changes that often require retraining, or on specialized accelerators that lack flexibility across evolving 3DGS variants. In this paper, we identify a fundamental inefficiency in GPU-based 3DGS rendering: severe SM-level load imbalance caused by uneven workloads across screen tiles. This imbalance creates a long execution tail, leaving most SMs idle while a few process computationally intensive tiles, particularly in large-scale urban scenes. To address this, we present a software-defined GPU scheduling approach that mitigates the execution tail. Our approach uses a persistent kernel to schedule heavy tiles first and selectively chunks outlier tiles across multiple CTAs. A lightweight per-frame policy determines when and how much to chunk using tile statistics already available in the rendering pipeline. Our approach requires neither retraining nor specialized hardware and can be applied orthogonally to existing optimizations across diverse 3DGS variants. Averaged per scene, it accelerates alpha blending by up to 2.93x on a large-scale urban scene, and by up to 5.6x on its most imbalanced frames, while retaining the original alpha blending formulation.

PointForward: Feedforward Driving Reconstruction through Point-Aligned Representations 2026-11-09
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

High-fidelity reconstruction of driving scenes is crucial for autonomous driving. While recent feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods enable fast reconstruction, their per-pixel Gaussian prediction paradigm often suffers from multi-view inconsistency and layering artifacts. Moreover, existing methods often model dynamic instances via dense flow prediction, which lacks explicit cross-view correspondence and instance-level consistency. In this paper, we propose PointForward, a feedforward driving reconstruction framework through point-aligned representations. DeltaForge: A Post-hoc Mixture-of-Experts VLA for Multi-Domain Autonomous Driving 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Fleets of driving vision-language-action (VLA) models are developed one domain at a time: each operator finetunes a shared base model on private data, producing specialized experts that are costly to consolidate into a single model. Weight merging loses accuracy as heterogeneous domains accumulate, and dense mixture-of-experts routing is prohibitively expensive to combine them. We present DeltaForge, to our knowledge the first post-hoc mixture-of-experts VLA for driving, which consolidates N independently finetuned per-domain models into a single multi-domain model from their weights and a small proxy set alone. When different operators finetune the same base model, their weight updates end up sharing a lot of structure. We exploit this by decomposing all the updates together using singular value decomposition (SVD), splitting each one into a shared part (the same across all domains) and a tiny domain-specific part. The router then only needs to work with those tiny domain-specific pieces, not the full expert weights, which is what keeps the approach efficient. Then two cooperating clip-level blind routers, one over the VLM reasoning experts and one over the flow-matching dynamics experts, are trained from soft labels on the proxy dataset. Across six diverse domains spanning urban, off-road, and highway driving on cars, trucks, and all-terrain vehicles (ATVs), DeltaForge approaches the accuracy of the per-domain experts and surpasses every merging, routing, centralized, and federated baseline at close to single-model parameter and compute cost. Adding a new domain requires only projecting its weight update onto the shared basis with no end-to-end retraining needed. The resulting model also generalizes to domain combinations it was never explicitly trained on.

DriveFoundry: Co-Evolving Data and Model for Efficient Closed-Loop Iteration in Autonomous Driving 2026-11-08
📝 57 pages, 31 figures, Project page: https://Xiaomi-EV.github.io/Xiaomi-Drive-o1
Show abstract

A driving foundation model must learn reusable decision knowledge from rare critical events and apply it under strict runtime constraints. We introduce DriveFoundry, a closed-loop framework improving iteration efficiency under data and computation budgets by coupling structured causal data refinement with adaptive inference. The Data Engine builds traceable scene-candidate records linking scene evidence and factor-decision relations to candidate trajectories and outcome assessments. Candidate comparisons and counterfactual annotations clarify how scene factors affect behavioral feasibility and preference. Verified model failures guide VLM-assisted annotation and expert correction, refining training data and annotation systems. The model engine, Xiaomi-Drive-o1, unifies Perception, Causation, Action, World, and Value within a shared vision-language backbone to enable cross-task knowledge transfer. Block-wise latent refill passes candidate-specific evidence and predicted visual consequences between task blocks, connecting scene understanding, planning, and evaluation. A learned value-of-computation head selects tasks and allocates latent tokens based on expected benefit and cost, adapting inference to each decision. On PhysicalAI, the 9B model achieves the lowest ADE@1 across evaluated splits and horizons among tested models.
