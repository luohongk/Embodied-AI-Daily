AlignDrive: Aligned Lateral-Longitudinal Planning for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-22
📝 NeurIPS 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Practical autonomous driving requires models that generalize by reasoning through spatial-temporal possibilities to exclude unsafe outcomes. While state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods use parallel planning architectures, they fail to explicitly couple speed decisions with agent behavior along the driving path, leading to suboptimal coordination. To address this, we propose a cascaded framework that transforms longitudinal planning from an independent prediction task into a path-conditioned reasoning process. On the model side, we introduce an anchor-based regression design that conditions longitudinal prediction on the lateral drive path, and reformulate longitudinal planning as 1D displacement prediction along the path. This reduces geometric uncertainty and sharpens the model's focus on interaction-driven dynamics. On the data side, we introduce a planning-oriented data augmentation strategy that simulates rare safety-critical events by programmatically inserting agents and relabeling longitudinal targets to enforce collision avoidance. Evaluated on the challenging Bench2Drive benchmark, our method achieves SOTA performance with a driving score of 89.07 and a success rate of 73.18%, demonstrating significantly improved coordination and safety. Further evaluation on Fail2Drive confirms strong generalization to rare edge cases where parallel formulations typically fail. Project page:https://yanhaowu.github.io/AlignDrive/.

RaSFNet: Ray-aligned and Scan-aware Fusion Network for LiDAR Semantic Segmentation 2026-10-22
Show abstract

LiDAR point cloud semantic segmentation is a fundamental component of 3D perception for autonomous driving and robotics. Projection-based methods map 3D point clouds into 2D representations for feature extraction using 2D networks. However, aggregating multiple points during projection can obscure geometric differences within each frustum, while local feature extraction limits the modeling of spatial relationships across scanning regions. Moreover, proximity in the projected representation does not necessarily imply proximity in 3D space, and interactions between point and projected features may mix features from different ranges, reducing semantic discriminability. To address these challenges, we propose the Ray-aligned and Scan-aware Fusion Network (RaSFNet), which incorporates point cloud geometry and LiDAR scanning characteristics into projection-based semantic segmentation. Specifically, the Ray-Aligned Geometric Distribution Encoding (RAGDE) module characterizes point distributions within each frustum using a ray-aligned coordinate system. To model spatial context, a Scan-Topology-Aware Mamba (STAM) block organizes frustum features according to the LiDAR azimuth sampling pattern and captures long-range dependencies across scanning regions. In addition, a Range-Constrained Point-Frustum Fusion (RCPFF) mechanism uses shared range information to guide feature exchange between point and frustum representations while maintaining spatial correspondence. RaSFNet achieves mIoU scores of 70.2% and 77.7% on the SemanticKITTI and nuScenes validation sets, respectively, indicating competitive segmentation performance compared with the evaluated methods.

Learning to Drive in New Cities Without Human Demonstrations 2026-10-22

While autonomous vehicles have achieved reliable performance within specific operating regions, their deployment to new cities remains costly and slow. A key bottleneck is the need to collect many human demonstration trajectories when adapting driving policies to new cities that differ from those seen in training in terms of road geometry, traffic rules, and interaction patterns. In this paper, we show that self-play multi-agent reinforcement learning can adapt a driving policy to a substantially different target city using only the map and meta-information, without requiring any human demonstrations from that city. Using a simple reward function, NOMAD substantially improves both task success rate and trajectory realism in target cities, demonstrating an effective and scalable alternative to data-intensive city-transfer methods. AutoExpert: Automating 3D LiDAR Annotation from Expert-Crafted Guidelines 2026-10-22
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026. Code and benchmark: https://github.com/cc50121/AutoExpert-3D-benchmark
Show abstract

The contemporary paradigm of scaling data annotation, crucial for developing machine learning solutions, is to hire ordinary human annotators and instruct them with expert-crafted guidelines to label data. This paradigm is laborious, tedious, and costly, motivating us to study an open problem, auto-annotation with expert-crafted guidelines (dubbed AutoExpert). We develop benchmarks by redesigning the evaluation protocol and re-annotating data with nuScenes and PandaSet, two 3D detection datasets for autonomous driving research that provide expert-crafted annotation guidelines. Their guidelines define 18 and 25 object classes, respectively, using nuanced language descriptions and a few visual examples. Following the guidelines that require using 3D cuboids to label LiDAR data, AutoExpert requires algorithms to learn on few-shot labeled images and texts to perform the task of 3D detection on LiDAR data. Apparently, the challenges of AutoExpert lie in the data-modality and task discrepancy. Nevertheless, public foundation models (FMs) serve as promising tools to tackle these challenges. To address AutoExpert, we adopt a conceptually simple pipeline consisting of three components: (1) 2D object detection and segmentation in RGB images, (2) lifting 2D detections into 3D using known sensor poses, and (3) 3D cuboids generation for the 2D detections. Within this pipeline, we enhance and evaluate a variety of methods such as open-vocabulary detectors, few-shot detectors, and self-supervised learned detectors. We also develop novel techniques, leading to refined components that boost 3D detection mAP from 12.1 to 25.4 on the AutoExpert-nuScenes benchmark.

Measuring the Reasoning-Action Coupling in Driving Vision-Language-Action Models through Counterfactuals 2026-10-22
📝 11 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Driving vision-language-action (VLA) models bring an appealing form of explainability to a safety-critical domain, since a system that can state the reasons for its maneuvers offers the transparency that deployment and public trust in autonomous driving demand. These models read the world into language before they act on it, describing the scene and reasoning over it in text that ranges from chain-of-thought to multi-stage question answering on the way to a trajectory. That framing quietly assumes two separable properties, namely that the trajectory follows the generated reasoning and that the generated reasoning tracks the causes that actually produce the trajectory. To thoroughly investigate these properties, we apply causal, counterfactual interventions to four driving VLAs across different benchmarks. First, we perturb the models' own reasoning and show that altering its meaning produces small trajectory changes relative to blanking the same models' camera inputs, a pattern that persists across benchmarks and model architectures. We then construct scene-level counterfactuals through photorealistic removal of key objects named in the models' reasoning and analyze whether the generated reasoning and trajectories track these changes. Overall, our findings indicate that the reasoning these models present to account for their resulting driving behavior is weakly coupled to that behavior itself, pointing to limits on the explainability these systems are intended to provide.

Causal Analysis of Failures of Cyber-Physical Systems with Continuous Inputs 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Modern autonomous Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs), such as self-driving cars, operate in increasingly complex environments while expected to maintain high reliability. The black-box nature of many of these systems makes it impossible to fully verify their behavior prior to deployment. When a failure occurs, it is important to analyze it to guide improvements to system robustness. A recent work introduced the novel concept of leveraging the framework of actual causality for CPS failure explanation. However, that work considered only a discrete, binary causal model that cannot capture continuous changes in system inputs. In this paper, we address these limitations by extending the actual causality framework with the notion of distance to account for the continuous characteristics of the system. Specifically, we introduce the novel concepts of distance-weighted actual cause and distance-weighted degree of responsibility, enabling causal influence to account for the magnitude of changes required to avoid a failure, rather than only its cardinality. While motivated by the continuous nature of CPSs, this constitutes a general extension of the theory of actual causality and enables more fine-grained analysis of continuous inputs in any causal model. Building on these notions, we present an efficient, practical, system-agnostic explanation-derivation algorithm that uses distance-weighted responsibility to guide the explanation search. We experimentally evaluate our approach across a number of environments from two autonomous-driving simulators with black-box neural controllers. The results show that the proposed extension provides more effective guidance than the current discrete approach.

Hybrid-PCGC: Hybrid Point Cloud Geometry Compression Bridging Generalization-based Model and Implicit Neural Representation 2026-10-22
📝 8 pages, 10 figures
Show abstract

Efficient point cloud geometry compression (PCGC) is essential for 3D sensing applications such as autonomous driving and digital twins. Hybrid methods combine the generalization of learned models with the adaptability of Implicit Neural Representations (INRs), but existing schemes often use complex architectures, rigid update strategies, and post-training weight compression, limiting adaptation efficiency and increasing bitstream overhead. This paper proposes Hybrid-PCGC, which formulates hybrid compression as a joint geometry-and-model bitrate allocation problem and optimizes instance-specific weight offsets as rate-constrained transmitted variables. It separates dataset-agnostic prior modeling from instance-specific adaptation through four components: a Frozen General Module (FGM), a Dual Trainable Adaptive Module (DTAM), an Adaptive Scope Controller (ASC), and a Supervised Offset Compression Module (SOCM). FGM captures global geometric regularities, while the parameter-efficient DTAM adapts to individual point clouds. ASC selects the update scope based on coding difficulty, and SOCM jointly optimizes and compresses weight offsets through differentiable entropy supervision during training. Experiments show that Hybrid-PCGC balances fidelity and bitrate, reducing bitrate by 13.96% and 16.33% over G-PCC on lossless and lossy benchmarks, respectively, and saving up to 12.33% bitrate over the state-of-the-art hybrid method on challenging dense point clouds.

OEL-AD: An Online Evolutive Learning Framework for Cross-Region Adaptive Autonomous Driving 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Autonomous driving models have achieved great progress in recent years, yet most existing approaches remain static, following a train-then-deploy paradigm that lacks the ability to adjust internal parameters when encountering new environments. This limitation is particularly critical in cross-region scenarios, which are common in real-world deployments in autonomous driving where differences in road layouts, traffic patterns, and environmental conditions introduce distribution shifts. In this paper, we argue for a paradigm shift towards Online Evolutive Learning (OEL), a learning paradigm designed to enable adaptive, online model optimization through the interaction and coordination of intelligent components in dynamic environments. We introduce OEL-AD, a novel framework that instantiates this paradigm for autonomous driving. Specifically, OEL-AD leverages trajectory uncertainty from the planning module to determine whether adaptive updates are necessary. When triggered, the framework uses discrepancies between predicted agent behaviors and subsequent observations as a self-supervised learning signal to refine perception and prediction modules, thereby indirectly improving planning accuracy and safety. Experiments on the nuScenes dataset with a cross-region split between Singapore and Boston demonstrate that OEL-AD consistently improves perception and prediction performance. This enhancement in perception and prediction consequently results in improved planning capabilities, thereby confirming the promise of online evolutive learning as a fundamental approach for building adaptive and robust autonomous driving systems.

Integrated Longitudinal and Lateral Control for High-Speed Platooning of Articulated Vehicles 2026-10-21
📝 Original manuscript of an article published in Vehicle System Dynamics on September 20, 2026. Published version: https://doi.org/10.1080/00423114.2026.2733699
Show abstract

This paper presents an integrated control framework for high-speed platooning of articulated vehicles, combining advanced longitudinal and lateral control strategies to address the challenges of safe and efficient autonomous driving. A novel proof of string stability for the proposed longitudinal control algorithm, based on a PID framework, ensures robust inter-vehicle distance regulation even under dynamic operating conditions. Additionally, an arc-length-based longitudinal error formulation enhances the applicability of the control system for curved roads, making it realistic for diverse driving scenarios. For lateral control, significant improvements to the clothoid-based path tracking algorithm are introduced to mitigate oscillations and enhance stability at high speeds. These enhancements include a tangential criterion for oscillation reduction, trajectory segment classification for adaptive lookahead distance tuning, a dual-adaptive mechanism for regulating the rate of lookahead distance adjustments based on both cross-track error (CTE) and anticipated road curvature, and extensions for articulated vehicle dynamics. The proposed integrated control framework is validated through simulations, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining stability, minimizing tracking errors, and ensuring robust performance in high-speed and challenging platooning scenarios.

Closing the Navigation Compliance Gap in End-to-end Autonomous Driving 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Trajectory-scoring planners achieve high navigation compliance when following the expert's original command, yet they struggle at intersections when presented with alternative commands; over 30 percent of such commands are ignored. We attribute this navigation compliance gap to two root causes: (1) existing metrics like Ego Progress do not explicitly measure navigation adherence, diluting the gap between on-route and off-route trajectories; and (2) current datasets pair each scenario with a single command, preventing models from learning command-dependent behavior. We address the metric gap by introducing the binary Navigation Compliance metric (NAVI) and the derived Controllability Measure (CM), and the data gap with the NavControl dataset, 14,918 intersection scenarios augmented with all feasible alternative commands and routing annotations, yielding over 34,000 direction samples. Building on these, we propose NaviHydra, a trajectory-scoring planner incorporating NAVI distillation and Bird's Eye View (BEV)-based trajectory gathering for context-position-aware trajectory feature extraction. NaviHydra achieves 92.7 PDM score on NAVSIM navtest split and 77.5 CM on NavControl test split. Training with NavControl improves controllability across diverse architectures, confirming it as a broadly effective augmentation for navigation compliance. MeSS: City Mesh-Guided Outdoor Scene Generation with Cross-View Consistent Diffusion 2026-10-21
📝 accepted to GCPR2026
Show abstract

Mesh models have become increasingly accessible for numerous cities; however, the lack of realistic textures restricts their application in virtual urban navigation and autonomous driving. To address this, this paper proposes MeSS (Meshbased Scene Synthesis) for generating high-quality, styleconsistent outdoor scenes with city mesh models serving as the geometric prior. While image and video diffusion models can leverage spatial layouts (such as depth maps or HD maps) as control conditions to generate street-level perspective views, they are not directly applicable to 3D scene generation. Video diffusion models excel at synthesizing consistent view sequences that depict scenes but often struggle to adhere to predefined camera paths or align accurately with rendered control videos. In contrast, image diffusion models, though unable to guarantee cross-view visual consistency, can produce more geometry-aligned results when combined with ControlNet. Building on this insight, our approach enhances image diffusion models by improving cross-view consistency. Concurrently with generation, a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene is reconstructed by initializing Gaussian balls on the mesh surface. Our method outperforms existing approaches in both geometric alignment and generation quality. TerraTransfer++: Demonstration-Free End-to-End Planner 2026-10-21
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks and real-world deployments. Its standard training recipe, however, is expensive across all existing paradigms: imitation learning requires fleet-scale driving logs whose behavior distribution only thinly covers the safety-critical tail; closed-loop reinforcement learning in a photorealistic simulator, while offering diverse behaviors, is bottlenecked by sample-efficiency, per-step cost of rendering, and a forward pass through a large vision backbone. Self-play in vectorized simulators changes the economics: at millions of rollout steps per second, one can obtain a state distribution with natural diversity in collisions, near-misses, and recoveries that no driving log contains, and a highly reactive policy emerges from a basic driving-quality reward. Our approach exploits this asymmetry. We pretrain a single policy by self-play, distill its rollouts into a flow-matching trajectory teacher that also plans, and train lightweight adapters on a frozen pretrained vision backbone to reproduce the latent space of either teacher through the action KL divergence, a batch-relational low-rank structural loss, and, for the planner, a trajectory-matching loss. Every target traces back to the self-play policy, so alignment never supervises against a logged trajectory: a paired dataset of (image, scene-state) frames suffices, with no need for the curated expert demonstrations that imitation pretraining is built on. From this one self-play teacher we obtain two end-to-end students: a closed-loop policy that outperforms every published imitation-based end-to-end method on the photorealistic 3D Gaussian splatting closed-loop benchmark HUGSim, and a planner that reaches 59.4 EPDMS on NAVSIM navhard, above every published method including DriveZero-Scale (57.1), and 95.5 PDMS on navtest, without human demonstrations.

Reliable validation of autonomous driving cyber-physical systems requires testing not only nominal driving behavior but also failures arising from faults in sensing and embedded computation. Physical road testing provides realistic system behavior but is costly and difficult to reproduce, while simulation enables scalable evaluation but cannot fully capture hardware-dependent behavior and fault propagation. Hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) validation bridges this gap by executing the driving stack on real embedded hardware within a simulated environment. However, preparing fault-augmented scenarios for HIL testing remains largely a manual process, limiting test coverage, scalability, and efficiency. We present FaultGene, an LLM-assisted framework for automated generation of executable fault-augmented tests for HIL fault-resilience validation. The resulting generator then produces fault-augmented tests directly at deployment time without requiring manually engineered reference executions or repeated refinement loops.

DriveMLP: When Training Strategy Outperforms Model Complexity in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-21
📝 Accepted at 2026 Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL)
Show abstract

Learning motion planning policies that remain reliable under closed-loop execution is a central challenge in Autonomous Driving (AD). Imitation Learning (IL) scales well to large AD datasets but suffers from distribution shift, causal confusion, and compounding errors under closed-loop execution. In contrast, Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically requires handcrafted rule-based rewards and massive simulation. We study a simple closed-loop IL framework that uses RL purely as an optimization tool, guided by a trajectory-distance reward derived from logged expert data. We refer to this training strategy as Expert-Guided RL (EGRL). It achieves a score of 86.51 on the nuPlan Val14 Reactive benchmark, a +3.2 point improvement over the previous best IL planner (Flow Planner), with >15x faster inference. FogTTA: Online Test-Time Adaptation for Robust Transformer-based Object Detection in Foggy Weather 2026-10-21
📝 Published in Proceedings of the 39th Canadian Conference on Artificial Intelligence (Canadian AI 2026), PMLR 318:13-24. Available at https://proceedings.mlr.press/v318/abedi26a.html
Show abstract

Object detection models for autonomous driving commonly experience substantial performance drops when deployed under adverse weather due to the domain shift between training data and real-world operating conditions. This degradation is especially evident when models trained on clear-weather images encounter foggy environments with reduced visibility and contrast. To address this challenge, we introduce FogTTA, an online test-time adaptation framework designed to improve the robustness of Transformer-based object detectors in fog. Using RF-DETR as the underlying object detector, FogTTA enables real-time adaptation to the streaming target domain without requiring source data or retraining. The framework follows a teacher-student design, where the deployed model serves as the teacher and generates pseudo labels from weakly augmented target inputs. These predictions are subsequently refined through non-maximum suppression and confidence filtering. The student model then learns from strongly augmented target sample using the Varifocal loss to mitigate pseudo-label noise. The teacher is updated via exponential moving averaging to ensure stable and continuous adaptation. Experiments show that FogTTA outperforms prior baselines, delivering improved detection accuracy and stability while maintaining real-time performance.

NeuralSRender: G-Buffer Guided Video Diffusion for Sim-to-Real Rendering 2026-10-21

The scarcity of high-quality annotated visual data remains a critical bottleneck for autonomous-driving perception. Simulators provide training data at scale, but the limited realism of synthetic videos compromises perception performance in real-world applications. To address this limitation, we propose NeuralSRender, a sim-to-real video translation framework that enhances the photorealism of synthetic videos while maintaining geometric consistency with the source scenes. Zero-Shot Cross-City Generalization in End-to-End Autonomous Driving: Self-Supervised versus Supervised Representations 2026-10-20
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving models are typically trained on multi-city datasets using supervised ImageNet-pretrained backbones, yet their ability to generalize to unseen cities remains largely unexamined. When training and evaluation data are geographically mixed, models may implicitly rely on city-specific cues, masking failure modes that would occur under real-world domain shifts when generalizing to new locations. In this work, we formulate zero-shot cross-city transfer as a controlled representation-level stress test for end-to-end autonomous driving and ask how visual pretraining affects transfer behavior under geographic domain shift. We conduct a comprehensive study by integrating self-supervised backbones I-JEPA, DINOv2, and MAE into planning frameworks. In closed-loop evaluation, self-supervised pretraining improves average out-of-distribution PDMS in several single-city training settings. Our results provide empirical evidence that representation learning influences the robustness of cross-city planning and motivate zero-shot geographic transfer as an important stress test for evaluating end-to-end autonomous driving systems.

When applied to global driving-condition edits, general-purpose instruction-guided video editors frequently corrupt these semantics, leading to structural hallucinations and ``temporal shock.'' This work distills and protects traffic semantics during video editing at three levels. First, we construct a hybrid dataset by combining temporally aligned real-world videos with synthetic edits, balancing foundational scene structure with unambiguous appearance signals. Deep Learning-Based Road Lane Quality Assessment from Egocentric Dashcam Video 2026-10-20

Show abstract

Reliable lane markings are important for both human drivers and automated driving systems, but their condition is still commonly assessed through manual inspection. Existing autonomous-driving datasets primarily support lane detection and scene understanding and usually lack maintenance-oriented labels for lane-marking visibility, continuity, or degradation. In this study, we present a geo-referenced smartphone dashcam dataset and baseline framework for lane-marking quality assessment. Test-Time Trajectory Optimization for Autonomous Driving 2026-10-20
Show abstract
