HIVE-Diff: High Frame Rate HDR Video Reconstruction from a Single LDR Frame and Events 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027 (Applications Track). 8 pages main paper + 8 pages supplementary material (included as appendix), 12 figures, 5 tables. Code: https://github.com/caixinw4-afk/hive-diff
Show abstract

High frame rate HDR video is important in applications such as autonomous driving and scientific imaging, where rapid motion and extreme illumination often occur simultaneously. Conventional frame cameras face a trade-off between temporal resolution and dynamic range, while event cameras provide complementary high-speed brightness-change measurements over a wide illumination range. These properties have motivated event-based HDR reconstruction, with existing methods broadly following event-only and hybrid event-frame paradigms. Event-only HDR reconstruction, however, suffers from brightness and color drift due to the lack of absolute intensity references. Hybrid event-frame methods alleviate this ambiguity using low-dynamic-range (LDR) frames as anchors, but most perform one-to-one HDR reconstruction at available LDR timestamps. In this work, we investigate single-reference HDR temporal expansion, reconstructing N HDR frames from one LDR reference and events instead of N aligned LDR observations. We develop HIVE-Diff, an event-conditioned video diffusion framework that decomposes the task into event-guided temporal generation and HDR content completion. Because paired real HDR-event data are scarce, we train on synthetic data and adapt the model to real event domains using LDR-event sequences as proxy supervision with teacher-student distillation. Experiments on synthetic and multiple real-world datasets demonstrate strong reconstruction quality and robust real-data adaptation.

SV-WAM: An Efficient Surround-View World-Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-02
📝 23 pages, 16 figures. Project page: https://jinyangwang427.github.io/SV-WAM/
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

World models (WMs) have demonstrated strong potential for end-to-end autonomous driving by learning predictive representations of future scene dynamics. However, generating future videos during inference introduces substantial computational overhead, leading many recent driving WMs to adopt a single front camera as input for efficient deployment. This design restricts spatial coverage in safety-critical maneuvers such as lane changes, merges, and turns. To address this limitation, we propose SV-WAM, a surround-view world-action model (WAM) that preserves full six-camera observations while maintaining efficient inference. SV-WAM leverages future-video prediction as dense training supervision for action learning within a shared generative model, rather than as an inference-time output. At the core of this design is an action-centered causal mask that prevents action tokens from attending to future-video tokens during joint action-video denoising. Consequently, the video branch can be discarded at deployment, enabling efficient action-only planning. Furthermore, we introduce a differentiable drivable-area compliance regularizer that penalizes vehicle-footprint corners approaching or crossing drivable boundaries, improving planning safety and boundary awareness. Extensive experiments on the closed-loop NAVSIMv2 benchmark and the open-loop nuScenes benchmark demonstrate that SV-WAM achieves state-of-the-art planning performance with low inference latency and competitive zero-shot transfer capability.

CL3DOD: Understanding the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma in Outdoor Class-Incremental 3D Object Detection 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Class-incremental learning (CIL) for outdoor 3D object detection remains largely underexplored. Existing outdoor studies are limited to single-benchmark evaluations, with SDCoT++ considering KITTI and CL2OD considering nuScenes under different incremental settings. This makes it difficult to assess whether observed continual-learning behavior generalizes across autonomous-driving benchmarks. To the best of our knowledge, we provide the first unified cross-benchmark analysis of outdoor class-incremental 3D object detection across multiple autonomous-driving benchmarks, covering both two-task and multi-stage settings. We investigate: (1) how severely existing 3D detectors forget previously learned classes, (2) how incremental training exposes the stability-plasticity dilemma between knowledge retention and new-class acquisition, and (3) which forms of supervision are most effective for balancing these objectives. We introduce CL3DOD, a class-incremental 3D detection framework that restores missing supervision for previous classes using predictions from the previous-stage detector, preserves old-class responses through query-wise knowledge distillation, and employs EMA-based temporal consistency regularization. Experiments on KITTI and nuScenes reveal a consistent stability-plasticity dilemma across both benchmarks. CL3DOD generally achieves a more favorable stability-plasticity balance than controlled incremental baselines and improves upon published prior methods in the reported benchmark comparisons. This advantage persists across multiple incremental stages, demonstrating strong retention of previously learned knowledge while maintaining effective new-class acquisition. Our code, evaluation setup, and incremental task splits will be publicly released.

Scaling Action-Free Video Latent Prediction for Transferable Representations in End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-02
📝 23 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving policies require effective visual representations that capture both spatial scene structure and temporal dynamics. Video latent prediction has emerged as an effective paradigm for learning spatiotemporal visual representations, yet it remains unclear whether scaling this objective with action-free driving videos can yield representations that benefit end-to-end planning. We study action-free video latent prediction as a scalable pretraining objective and introduce ScaleDrive, a driving-oriented framework pretrained exclusively on large-scale internet open-world driving videos, without action annotations or benchmark-specific data. We keep the pretrained video encoder frozen during downstream end-to-end planning training to isolate the transferability of representations learned from internet driving videos. Ego-motion and depth probes show that scaling action-free video pretraining progressively strengthens the temporal and geometric information encoded in the frozen representation. We systematically study the visual representation transfer across different end-to-end driving policies and closed-loop benchmarks, where ScaleDrive consistently improves downstream performance and demonstrates that scaling action-free video latent prediction yields representations that generalize across both planning policy architectures and evaluation scenarios and protocols.

Towards Zero-Shot Transfer Across Embodiments For Driving VLAs 2026-11-02
📝 Paper accepted for WACV 2027

Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) have shown strong potential in autonomous driving by leveraging multimodal pretraining for instruction following, visual reasoning, and scene-level generalization. In robotic manipulation, scaling VLA fine-tuning across multiple robot setups--especially when unifying representations across embodiments--has been shown to improve in-dataset performance and cross-embodiment generalization; in autonomous driving, however, VLAs remain largely trained on individual datasets and are rarely evaluated for zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets and camera rigs; furthermore naively adding more datasets to the training data does not necessarily lead to better performance within seen embodiments. To address these problems, we study multi-dataset training for the driving task and BEV-Forcing, an auxiliary objective that transfers ground-plane object-layout information from a specialized Bird's-Eye-View model into the VLA backbone. By encouraging the model to represent object position through a shared BEV spatial interface, we show that an auxiliary task such as BEV-Forcing can improve both in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance when training on a small number of camera rigs. As the number of training embodiments increases, however, the benefits of the auxiliary task are reduced; we present this as evidence that new techniques in the literature may see their benefits diminish when simply scaling up training diversity, which motivates presenting results taking into account data scaling.

ReinSeg: Retrieval-Guided Bounded Multimodal Refinement for 3-D Road Anomaly Segmentation 2026-11-02
Show abstract

3-D road anomaly segmentation is essential for risk-aware perception in autonomous driving. Its central challenge is to rank sparsely observed hazards ahead of abundant normal road structures while preserving point-wise metric localization. LiDAR provides accurate 3-D point support, whereas surround-view imagery offers complementary semantic context for separating unexpected hazards from challenging normal structures. We propose ReinSeg, a retrieval-guided bounded multimodal refinement framework for point-wise road anomaly segmentation. Occupied LiDAR bird's-eye-view cells retrieve scene-specific context from a shared memory of eight surround-view images without supplying calibration variables to the retrieval module. A relative-norm-bounded residual returns this context to the LiDAR point features, so the frozen LIDO anomaly-scoring interface is preserved, and the refinement is optimized with in-distribution semantic labels only. On the Spotting the Unexpected (STU) benchmark, ReinSeg improves validation average precision from 27.54\% to 35.67\% and reduces the false-positive rate at 95\% anomaly recall from 34.85\% to 10.61\%, shrinking the high-recall screening set from 67.54 to 20.55 million points. Relative to REL, AP is 24.99 percentage points higher and FPR95 is 0.99 points lower. Matched interfaces and component isolation show that retrieved scene imagery and the relative-norm bound are both essential, and ReinSeg stays above LiDAR-only performance under missing, corrupted, or misaligned camera inputs while running at 21.8 frames per second. These results establish bounded retrieval of scene-specific visual evidence as an effective route to reliable 3-D road-hazard perception.

MERIS: Adaptive Real-Time Scheduling of Refined Inference DAGs in Dynamic Robotic Environments 2026-11-02
📝 Extension version of RTSS'23
Show abstract

Robots deployed in dynamic environments execute changing directed-acyclic-graph (DAG) workloads under limited compute and timing budgets. This article presents MERIS, a framework for model scheduling under real-time constraints that combines proportional intermediate deadlines, MIMONet-aware graph refinement, and event-driven synchronization for multi-task inference on resource-constrained robotic platforms. We distinguish three representations: the application DAG, each MIMONet's internal encoder/decoder graph, and the refined scheduling DAG, and make the scope of the timing argument explicit. If the profiled execution bounds, release assumptions, precedence barriers, and per-level capacity premises hold, satisfaction of the assigned sub-deadlines composes to the end-to-end deadline. For non-partitionable tasks, we define a profile-bounded admission rule; before interpreting that rule as empirical timing evidence, we require direct per-task completion checks. We report run-level QoE alongside the offered-node deadline missing rate, making explicit that run completion and successful service of every node are different outcomes. We propose a comprehensive minibenchmark for evaluating the performance of schedulers under various DNN-driven autonomous driving scenarios. Extensive experiments on Nano Orin, AGX Orin, and M3 Max demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method.

INTERACT: Learning Interaction-Aware World Dynamics from Driving Videos for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Safe autonomous driving requires more than recognizing the current scene: the ego vehicle must anticipate how surrounding traffic participants will evolve under its own motion. However, existing end-to-end driving models typically encode scenes into dense visual or BEV features, leaving object-level interactions implicit. We propose (INTERACT INTERaction-aware Action-Conditioned Trajectory learning), a three-stage framework that learns a slot-based object-centric world model from unlabeled driving videos and transfers it to trajectory planning. INTERACT first learns recurrent slots that group dense visual features into temporally structured scene-entity representations without object annotations. It then infers latent ego actions from slot context and video transitions, and uses them to condition future slot prediction, capturing how ego motion and surrounding entities co-evolve. Finally, the learned slots, latent actions, and future slot predictions are distilled into a trajectory planner to guide trajectory generation and selection. Extensive experiments on NAVSIM and nuScenes validate the effectiveness of INTERACT. In particular, it achieves state-of-the-art performance on NAVSIM, with improvements in safety-critical collision metrics.

Fast-Slow Evolutionary Occupancy Prediction via Controlled Dynamics 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

3D semantic occupancy prediction provides dense geometric and semantic scene representations for autonomous driving, where both high accuracy and low response latency are crucial for safe downstream forecasting and planning. Existing methods usually exhibit different accuracy-latency characteristics. Performance-oriented models provide stronger geometric and semantic predictions, while deployment-friendly models provide faster responses. This motivates EvoOcc, a fast-slow evolutionary occupancy prediction framework that jointly exploits their complementary strengths to improve the accuracy-latency balance without changing the upstream architectures. EvoOcc models evolutionary occupancy prediction as a continuous time controlled dynamical system, enabling flexible adaptation to variable slow system delays and irregular fast system arrivals. The framework further decomposes the evolution process into dual state evolution where ego state evolution compensates for the deterministic coordinate shifts and scene state evolution models the genuine geometric and semantic scene changes. Extensive experiments on three different fast-slow system pairs and irregular prediction arrivals show that EvoOcc consistently improves over fast system baselines while maintaining much lower latency than the slow system, indicating a favorable accuracy-latency balance and stable behavior under practical inference situations.

Towards a Theory for the Design of Rules in Multi-Agent Systems: the case of Homogeneous Fleets 2026-11-01
📝 30 pages; A first version of the RDP
Show abstract

The regulation of systems composed of many interacting decentralised agents is a long-standing problem. This problem has typically been resolved by the imposition of rules. Rules resolve the limitations of sub-optimal agents whilst simultaneously aligning the behaviour of the agents with the demands of society. Traffic laws, for example, let a driver cross a junction without seeing around the corner, and transport protocols such as TCP let applications share a network without knowing of one another. The problem is more pressing today, as autonomous systems are deployed in society. In this work, we establish the basis of a system-agnostic theory of rule design for multi-agent systems. We first state the rule design problem (RDP) in its generality. It relates a rule to the set of system performances, defined by the regulator, that the agents deployed under it can attain. However, it is not yet possible to solve the RDP in full generality. As such, we introduce the homogeneous rule design problem (HRDP), in which the agents in each environment are identical, and among other interesting properties, show that under worst-case aggregation the two problems have the same solution, bypassing the combinatorial complexity of the RDP. To solve the RDP under worst-case aggregation we propose Rule Branch and Bound (RuleBnB), an anytime branch-and-bound algorithm. Finally, we illustrate the value of solving the RDP on an autonomous driving case study.

Dynamic map (DM) is a core information infrastructure for vehicle-road-cloud (VRC) cooperative autonomous driving. Leveraging 2D Foundation Priors for Generalized Category Discovery under Test-Time Adaptation in 3D LiDAR Segmentation 2026-11-01
📝 Accepted by ACMMM 2026
Show abstract

In autonomous driving, 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation is expected to adapt to evolving environments and recognize previously unseen categories, making generalized category discovery under test-time adaptation (GCD-TTA) an important problem. However, existing works treat GCD and TTA separately, while the limited semantic information in 3D point clouds makes GCD-TTA under domain shift more prone to feature collapse when combining GCD with TTA. To address this issue, we propose a two-stage framework for GCD-TTA in 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation. Specifically, in the first stage, we adaptively aggregate local 3D features according to geometric density variations and obtain reliable pseudo labels under a global class-wise prototype consistency constraint. Meanwhile, semantic features from 2D vision foundation models are fused with 3D point cloud features to enrich 3D semantic information and promote a more separable feature space. Extensive experiments on Synth4D to SemanticKITTI, SynLiDAR to SemanticKITTI, and Synth4D to nuScenes demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework. Under a unified evaluation protocol, our method achieves the best overall mIoU on 9 out of 10 benchmark splits and improves the overall mIoU by up to 12.21 points over the strongest re-implemented baseline.

DriftingPlanner: Drifting Generative Models for One-Step Trajectory Planning in End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-31
Show abstract

End-to-end planners decode the ego trajectory either by coordinate regression, which is fast but blends plausible maneuvers into one trajectory that may follow none of them, or by diffusion and flow matching, which model the trajectory distribution but need several inference steps. Drifting models move this iteration into training: a one-step generator learns to follow an attraction--repulsion field between generated and ground-truth samples. Drifting was developed for class-conditional image generation, where every class supplies many positive samples. In planning, every scene is its own condition. Pooling scenes would attract the generated trajectories of one scene to the ground truth of unrelated scenes, whereas computing the field per scene leaves a single positive, the expert trajectory. Mutual repulsion then holds the generated trajectories on a shell around it, so any single generated trajectory deviates from the ground truth. We present DriftingPlanner, a trajectory planner trained with the drifting loss alone, without any coordinate regression term. It computes the field per scene and removes the shell at inference: it decodes every noise vector together with its negation in one batched pass, and averaging each antithetic pair cancels the linear part of the displacement from the shell center. On NAVSIM navtest, DriftingPlanner reaches a state-of-the-art 93.75 PDMS, and with single-step inference its drifting decoder comes within 0.13 PDMS of the best diffusion or flow matching setting, which needs ten inference steps.

Acceptance and Responsibility Attribution Toward Autonomous Ambulances Across Driving Conditions: A Preliminary Survey of Fire Service Personnel 2026-10-31
📝 The 2nd International Workshop on Trust and Blame in Multi-Person Multi-Agent Societies (TBSA_2nd) in conjunction with HAI 2026, 6 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Traffic incidents involving ambulances and fire engines during emergency-response driving can have serious consequences. Although autonomous driving technologies may reduce workload and improve safety, their introduction into emergency services requires consideration of first responders' trust, willingness to ride (WTR), and perceptions of responsibility. We surveyed 34 fire service personnel from a single fire station in Himeji, Japan, to examine their evaluations of autonomous ambulances and responsibility attribution under non-emergency and emergency driving conditions. Emergency driving was associated with lower perceived usefulness for improving safety, trust, and WTR, as well as greater anxiety. Open-ended responses revealed concerns about system reliability, the complexity of emergency driving, and the need for human judgment, as well as considerations regarding operator and organizational responsibility. These findings suggest that acceptance of autonomous ambulances is sensitive to driving conditions, while responsibility judgments may also reflect perceived professional roles and responsibilities.

Large Discrete Policy: Advancing Explicit Behavior Modeling with Stochastic Iterative Scoring 2026-10-31
Show abstract

Behavior policies are often formulated as continuous generative models, whose iterative denoising processes are expressive but difficult to interpret and prone to producing implausible actions. We propose the Large Discrete Policy (LDiP), a fully discrete behavior modeling framework that selects actions from a large vocabulary of physically plausible candidates. Rather than perturbing actions, LDiP improves expressivity through stochastic iterative scoring: it progressively re-scores and prunes candidates with score-space stochasticity, enabling fine-grained ranking and exploration among plausible actions while preserving an explicit decision process. Across end-to-end planning, closed-loop driving, robotic manipulation, and vision-language-action settings, LDiP consistently outperforms strong discrete and continuous baselines in autonomous driving, and exceeds or matches continuous generative policies in robotic manipulation. These results show that discrete policies, when equipped with effective scoring mechanisms, offer an expressive, plausible, and interpretable alternative for behavior modeling. STEER: Route-Aware Adaptive Reasoning for Autonomous Driving 2026-10-31
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Vision-language action (VLA) models excel for end-to-end autonomous driving, yet their tendency to over-invoke chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning mirrors a human cognitive pitfall: overthinking. Just as deliberate reasoning can slow or mislead human judgment in routine tasks, excessive CoT in VLA incurs unnecessary compute overhead and can paradoxically degrade planning performance. Recent research tackles this via adaptive reasoning, which dynamically modulates inference complexity by selectively triggering CoT for complex scenes while responding directly to routine ones. While prior adaptive methods rely on implicit adaptation, recent work shows that explicitly optimizing the route between CoT and direct responses yields superior performance, assuming scene-difficulty annotations which are labor-intensive, poorly scalable, and prone to error due to the subjectivity of judging driving complexity. We explore, for the first time, whether adaptive reasoning with explicit route optimization can be achieved without any scene-difficulty labeling. We introduce STEER, a generic over-reasoning mitigation strategy featuring two key components: (i) routing uncertainty-aware rollout, which calibrates sampling to align routing diversity with model uncertainty; and (ii) cross-route advantage credit assignment, which introduces a differential metric to reinforce optimal routing decisions based on environmental rewards rather than subjective labels. Extensive experiments on NAVSIM v1/v2 demonstrate that STEER is competitive with state-of-the-art methods in both planning quality and the Pareto front of inference efficiency.

Understanding not only where drivers look but also why their attention shifts is essential for interpretable human-AI collaboration in autonomous driving. Driver attention is not purely perceptual but semantically structured. Thus attention shifts can be learned through minimal semantic supervision rather than dense large-scale annotation. We present FSDAM (Few-Shot Driver Attention Modeling), a framework that achieves joint spatial attention prediction and structured explanation generation using 90 annotated examples. These results suggest that semantically grounded attention modeling enables data-efficient learning and provides a scalable path toward explainable driver attention systems in data-constrained environments.

Adaptive-WAM: Quality-Guided Early-Exit Planning from Intermediate Video-Diffusion Features 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Large video diffusion models provide rich spatiotemporal priors for autonomous driving, but existing world-action models often inherit the cost of iterative future-video generation even though deployment only requires an ego trajectory. We ask a more basic question: how much of a video diffusion model must be executed to make a reliable driving decision? Through a controlled study of video denoising timesteps and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) depth, we find that planning performance is largely insensitive to the tested video-noise levels, whereas strong trajectories can already be decoded from intermediate layers. Based on this observation, we introduce Adaptive-WAM, a quality-aware multi-exit planner built on a Wan2.2-5B backbone. Trajectory diffusion heads are attached to selected DiT blocks, and a lightweight trajectory-quality scorer terminates inference once the best trajectory decoded so far satisfies a quality threshold; otherwise, computation continues from the cached hidden state to a deeper exit. The deployed planner therefore avoids the iterative classifier-free denoising loop and VAE decoding required for future-video synthesis, while dynamically allocating backbone depth according to trajectory quality. On NAVSIM, the adaptive single-trajectory planner achieves 90.8 PDMS; a separate fixed-exit variant reaches 92.6 PDMS with 64 proposals. It further obtains 89.9 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, yielding the best reported results among the compared front-view video world-model planners. Without target-domain fine-tuning, Adaptive-WAM transfers to nuScenes with 0.88 m average L2 error and a 0.08\% collision rate. On an A100, adaptive routing improves PDMS from 90.62 to 90.79 while averaging 170 ms end-to-end planning latency, approximately 10\% below the 190 ms fixed block-15 planner and 47\% below the 320 ms fixed full-depth planner. Code will be released.

Mechanistic Interpretability for End-to-End Self-Driving 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted to the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) 2026. Project page: https://mgagvani.github.io/mechanistic-interpretability-for-e2e-driving/
Show abstract

End-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving models achieve strong planning performance but remain largely opaque, limiting observability and trust. We apply mechanistic interpretability to E2E driving by training Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) on the internal activations of a simple, single-camera end-to-end path planning model. Unsupervised clustering of SAE latents reveals interpretable structure aligned with route intent, vehicle speed, and braking behavior, recovering route intent with 98.3% accuracy without supervision. Through paired counterfactual image edits, we further show that specific SAE features are visually grounded and systematically respond to scene changes such as traffic light state and stop sign presence.
