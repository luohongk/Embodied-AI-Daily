To Own or Not to Own? Endogenous Ownership in Peer-to-Peer Sharing of Private Autonomous Vehicles 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Autonomous driving allows private owners to release their vehicles to an on-demand platform and earn income when they do not need them. This possibility changes vehicle acquisition incentives, as rental income can encourage purchases, while access to shared vehicles can reduce the need for personal ownership. Understanding these competing effects is essential to assessing the ownership implications of private AVs sharing. While existing models of private AV sharing often take ownership as given, in this paper, we develop a stationary equilibrium model in which vehicle ownership and listing decisions determine service availability and operational frictions, which in turn shape individuals' incentives to own and share. Based on the model, we first characterize individual choices by comparing payoffs of being an owner and a non-owner. We then analyze the resulting potential population partitions, establish conditions for the existence of an active sharing market, and categorize them by owner types. The equilibrium is presented by a system of equations and solved through a tailored algorithm. An illustrative numerical study on calibrated U.S. travel and vehicle cost data shows that sharing can either increase or decrease ownership, depending on the balance between purchases encouraged by rental income and ownership displaced by shared vehicles. The balance is shaped by market parameters, including market density, AV cost, and platform operating conditions. Platform pricing and municipal regulation can also influence ownership through their effects on acquisition incentives and the shared service that makes nonownership attractive. These findings highlight the range of possible ownership outcomes in private AV sharing markets, and the model provides a framework for evaluating how market conditions, platform decisions, and regulation shape these outcomes.

Mechanism Parity for Simulation-Based Validation of Autonomous-Driving Perception 2026-11-20
📝 15 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables. Extended version of the authors' papers at IEEE IV 2026 and IEEE ITSC 2026. This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication
Show abstract

Simulation is central to intelligent-vehicle validation, but visual realism or output agreement between a real scene and its synthetic counterpart does not prove that a perception system used the same evidence in both domains. This paper addresses that mechanism-parity gap for paired real-synthetic autonomous-driving perception. It unifies Decisive-Feature Fidelity, which compares decisive evidence maps in a frozen system under test (SUT), with Concept-Decisive Feature Fidelity, which projects decisive evidence into semantic scene concepts for diagnosis. The framework is a validation diagnostic rather than a standalone safety certificate: it asks whether output-consistent simulation also preserves the mechanism by which the perception model reaches that output. The empirical evidence comprises ten retrained diffusion ControlNet generators evaluated on a deterministic exact-geometry KITTI-Virtual KITTI 2 subset. Generated counterparts use a 1248-by-376-pixel padded inference canvas and are cropped back to the exact KITTI dimensions before evaluation. The final benchmark reports target-image, output-consistency, and mechanism metrics recomputed from a single audited output set, then adds metric-disagreement cases, bootstrap uncertainty, threshold sensitivity, and concept/cache integrity audits. Across the benchmark, output and appearance fidelity are at most weakly and inconsistently rank-correlated with decisive-mechanism distance for all three frozen SUTs, and a constructed positive control shows the estimator detects a known planted mechanism that output agreement misses, supporting decisive-evidence diagnostics for intelligent-vehicle simulation validation. All numerical claims are restricted to the declared exact-geometry benchmark, ten generators, and three frozen SUTs.

Trailblazer-F: Speculative Action-Flow Inference for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Driving world-action models generate planned trajectories through iterative action denoising, with each update depending on the preceding state. This sequential dependency incurs substantial inference latency, limiting timely planning. Speculative inference offers an opportunity to reduce this latency by predicting future denoising states for parallel target evaluation, but requires efficient proposal generation and suitable verification for continuous actions. We present Trailblazer-F, a speculative action-flow inference framework in which a lightweight drafter acts as a trailblazer, charting a candidate denoising path through the compact, structured waypoint space so that the target model can verify multiple steps in parallel and advance with fewer sequential updates. Our method maintains planning quality while keeping the pretrained target model frozen.Across all 12,146 NAVSIM v1 navtest scenes, Trailblazer-F maintains near-baseline planning performance, with PDMS changes of +0.04 and -0.08 points for SimWAM and DriveWAM, respectively. Paired inference measurements on an NVIDIA L20 GPU yield 2.31x and 1.55x speedups. UDA-Restoration: Bridging Unsupervised Domain Adaptation and Multi-Degradation Denoising for Enhanced AV Semantic Segmentation 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Autonomous vehicles rely heavily on vision-based perception systems for safe navigation and decision-making. Adverse weather conditions, sensor noise, moving artifacts and illumination changes, however, often degrade the image quality and hence reduce perception performance. This paper introduces a diffusion-guided unsupervised domain adaptation algorithm for autonomous driving environments, namely UDA-Restoration, which is designed to enhance and denoise images. The proposed method follows the paradigm of Noise-Space Domain Adaptation (Noise-DA), which is based on multi-source degradation learning and diffusion-guided adaptation that spans across synthetic and real-world degradations. The framework has been extensively tested and shown to restore image quality and to be robust to unseen degradation scenarios, such as snow, haze, sensor distortions, and more. The quantitative results indicate that Noise-DA outperforms the evaluated self-supervised and unsupervised baselines while matching the best supervised restoration performance. In addition, semantic segmentation experiments show that the performance of downstream perception tasks is directly improved by better image restoration. The outcomes show the promise of unsupervised domain adaptation based on diffusion as a powerful and scalable method for image restoration tasks in autonomous driving systems under various environments.

ReactDrive: Self-Reactive Driving World Model 2026-11-20
Show abstract

The world evolves through the dynamics of its constituent entities and their interactions, with changes in one entity influencing the behavior of others. To simulate this evolution under different actions, interactive world models need to capture the resulting agent responses as part of the world's own dynamics. In autonomous driving, errors in modeling these responses can misrepresent maneuver safety and undermine the reliability of generated videos used for policy evaluation and learning. To formalize this intrinsic requirement for driving world model, we define self-reactivity as the capability to model agent responses from observed history and a specified ego action, and generate visual futures that reflect those responses without prescribed future agent trajectories. To realize this capability, we introduce ReactDrive, a driving world model that jointly learns agent dynamics and visual generation through a shared agent state representation. Within this framework, dynamics supervision grounds a reactive dynamics prior in observed agent states, while the video objective jointly optimizes the self-reactivity and visual realism. Experiments show improved agent responses, ego controllability, and video quality, a 96.0% preference for agent reactivity in human comparisons across five baselines, and better downstream planning through augmentation with generated videos. We hope this work encourages the community to recognize self-reactivityas a foundational capability for developing and evaluating interactive world models. Composable Driving Style for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
📝 13 pages, including appendix
Show abstract

End-to-end driving policies can achieve strong task performance while offering limited control over their driving style. Driving style describes how a vehicle accelerates, brakes, and maintains distance. Policies trained without explicit preference conditioning lack a direct mechanism for adjusting these behaviors at inference. We propose YOUDrive, a vision--language--action planner for continuous and composable driving-style control. Each teacher's behavior is distilled into a task vector, a learned parameter update that induces a style direction in the planner's representation. These directions can be scaled and combined at inference without retraining the backbone. Conditioned on these directions, a flow-matching decoder models a multimodal trajectory distribution and generates kinematically feasible motion alternatives. We introduce the Style Alignment Score (SAS) to describe human-referenced kinematic behavior after safety and task gating. On NAVSIM navtest, a single coefficient monotonically controls assertiveness across six teacher-derived personas, while PDMS remains above 0.90 across the tested individual and blended configurations, reaching 0.954. Further analyses reveal systematic policy-specific kinematics after controlling for scene context, whereas the evaluated discrete style labels provide limited additional explanatory power. Code, checkpoints, and the metric will be released.

GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives 2026-11-19

Precise grounding matters. It specifies which object is the target and where that object is, even in clutter and for tiny objects, and it has to be fast enough for closed-loop control. Yet vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) take perception from general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones, which still fail in these settings. We introduce GroundingPI, a 4B grounding foundation model that generates points and boxes as quantized coordinates in a shared vocabulary. Training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, using supervision from public datasets and dedicated data engines. Against 44 baselines across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities, GroundingPI establishes a new state of the art, averaging 73.68%, above the larger GPT-6 Astra (71.54%). As a downstream visual backbone, GroundingPI improves performance on robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. Across RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboCasa-GR1, it outperforms all evaluated backbones in all four out-of-distribution settings, with relative gains of up to 24.8% over the strongest baseline. On RoboCasa-GR1, GroundingPI trained with 50% of the demonstrations outperforms those baselines trained with 75%. Its mean open-loop L2 error on nuScenes is 0.296 m. We systematically analyze GroundingPI's pretraining in scale and data composition. Grounding quality and downstream manipulation improve with pretraining scale, with generalization tracking perceptual gains most closely. Analyzing the data recipe across these 11 perceptual capabilities shows dense grounding's substantial benefits for both, and OCR's potential as a catalyst for perceptual learning. These results support grounding as a perceptual foundation, and dedicated perceptual pretraining as a promising direction for foundation models of physical intelligence.

FastRoverVLA: Fast Vision-Language-Action Execution for Off-Road Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
📝 14 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Off-road vision-language-action (VLA) models generate scene-conditioned plans, but frequent trajectory updates can repeatedly invoke expensive multimodal inference. Reusing a prediction reduces this demand while leaving its commands exposed to changes in vehicle motion. FastRoverVLA addresses this mismatch by decoupling visual plan generation from motion-conditioned execution. Its training-free interface maintains decoded plans between visual predictions. A speed-curvature action buffer is aligned with measured motion at each update, integrated into a local trajectory, and advanced by consuming the current action. The corrected remainder is written back to the buffer, preserving motion corrections across successive updates. A bounded refresh rule normalizes speed and curvature variations within a newly decoded plan and assigns a short or long reuse budget according to the resulting plan-roughness score. The interface requires neither backbone features nor an additional learned driving policy. Over a 3 s horizon, independent real-request evaluation achieves average displacement error (ADE) and final displacement error (FDE) of 0.328/0.596 m on ORAD-3D and 0.266/0.526 m on RELLIS-3D, reducing FDE by 18.0% and 36.7% at request rates of 6.0% and 4.8%, respectively. These runs retain lower errors across four backbones and both datasets and reduce Qwen3-VL request-processing cost by factors of 16.6 and 20.8 under serial replay, while mechanism and motion-only controls identify persistent state correction as the main source of the observed accuracy benefit. The source code and evaluation materials are publicly available at https://github.com/ADEPT-ORG/FastRoverVLA.

Business World Model 2026-11-19
📝 Under peer review

Show abstract

World model has emerged as a powerful paradigm in artificial intelligence, enabling agents to represent their environments, predict future states, and evaluate possible actions before acting. However, existing world models have largely been developed for visual or physical domains such as computer vision, robotics, gaming, and autonomous driving, which are governed by relatively stable dynamics. These formulations are not directly applicable to business practice, where the relevant environment is semantic, organizational, and market driven. Outcomes depend on context-sensitive factors such as customer behavior, pricing, competition, and regulation. This paper introduces the concept and architecture of a Business World Model, a world model specialized for business and organizational environments. We propose a business-semantics-centric formulation in which states, dynamics, and feasible actions are linked to business entities, their attributes, and their relationships. Within this framework, intelligent agents can simulate alternative action sequences, estimate their effects on future business outcomes, and evaluate trade-offs under uncertainty and business constraints. We present a reference design and open-source implementation in which LLM agents interpret natural-language business goals and explain results, while a deterministic, metadata-driven simulation engine integrating machine learning models, business rules, and action spaces performs all calculations. A churn case study of a digital subscription business demonstrates that the system recommends options meeting the churn-reduction target within the revenue constraint. This work establishes a conceptual foundation for autonomous business systems capable of moving from instruction-based execution toward goal-driven planning, optimization, and execution.

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables agents to learn optimal behaviors through interaction with their environment and has been increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications, including autonomous driving. PAD: Policy-Aware Data Valuation for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-19
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving increasingly depends on scaling training demonstrations, yet data growth does not guarantee proportional gains. However, additional data do not guarantee better policies: a demonstration that benefits one planner may be redundant or harmful to another. Existing curation methods typically infer data value from intrinsic sample properties or training-dependent proxies that do not measure downstream policy utility. To address this, we introduce \textbf{Policy-Aware Data Valuation (PAD)}, a framework that assigns each candidate demonstration a utility specific to the target driving policy. PAD combines scene-level calibration feedback with projected policy gradients and a regularized approximation of local training geometry, producing scalable influence-based values for the policy. A controlled group-removal study establishes the directional fidelity of these values, while cross-planner analysis shows that the same candidate pool induces markedly different rankings across planner families. On DiffusionDrive, PAD improves EPDMS over real-only training by $3.5$ points on NavTest and $5.0$ points on NavHard. Policy-specific curation consistently outperforms real-only and full-pool training across diffusion-, regression-, and flow-matching-based planners while substantially reducing scene-level regressions. These results establish policy-aware data valuation as a basis for replacing indiscriminate data accumulation with targeted policy improvement, enabling more efficient and reliable scaling of autonomous-driving systems.

Efficient World-Model Planning via Physics-Guided Search Distillation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

World models support autonomous driving by evaluating candidate trajectories through predicted future scene evolution. However, accurate future prediction does not ensure road-compliant execution; when all candidates violate road boundaries, re-ranking cannot select a feasible trajectory. We propose Physics-Guided Search Distillation (PGSD), a single-view planning framework that combines future representations and execution feedback for verified beyond-pool repair through three components: (1) Execution-Aware Selection checks ego-vehicle road compliance under simulated execution and selects the highest-scoring feasible trajectory; (2) a Clearance-Aware Search Teacher generates beyond-pool corrections and selects demonstrations with larger minimum road-boundary clearance during execution to buffer imitation errors; and (3) a Future-Conditioned Repair Student predicts one correction from rewarder features and execution feedback for single-rollout verification. On NAVSIM v2 navhard, it reaches 38.96 EPDMS, improving on WorldDrive by 11.24 points (40.5%). The student replaces up to ten search rollouts with a single verification rollout per repair trigger. HUGSIM Hard examples provide qualitative evidence of zero-shot transfer.

Does the Action Matter? Probing Action-Conditioned Semantic Forecasting as a World Model for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-18
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables. Accepted at the WAM-AD Workshop, WACV 2027. Code: https://github.com/binayksingh/does-action-matter
Show abstract

World models are increasingly used in autonomous driving to predict how a scene evolves under the ego vehicle's actions, supporting counterfactual reasoning and model-based planning. However, existing evaluations focus on prediction fidelity and planning performance, and rarely verify that the predictions depend on the conditioning action. In this paper, we study action dependence in action-conditioned semantic forecasting, which predicts future semantic segmentation from past segmentations and the ego action. We compare a forecaster with feature-wise and input-level action conditioning against an identical model without action input, and measure action dependence with test-time action interventions scored by standard segmentation metrics. The evaluation is validated in a synthetic environment with known action-dependent dynamics and applied to Cityscapes and BDD100K, both within each dataset and across the two vehicle platforms. Experiments show that the executed action improves mid-term mIoU by at most 2.5 points on Cityscapes and 0.6 points on BDD100K, whereas a wrong action reduces it by up to 23.2 and 7.0 points, below the model without action input. On real data, feature-wise conditioning depends 1.6 times more on the action than input-level conditioning at similar accuracy, whereas both depend on it equally in the synthetic environment, where the history does not determine the action. These results show that an accuracy gain indicates that a driving world model uses its action, but not how strongly its forecasts depend on it. Teaching VLMs What to Say, Not How to Reason: Rethinking Counterfactual Reasoning in Autonomous Driving 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 Main Track (41 pages, 14 figures, 14 tables). Project page: https://ophd-hahao.github.io/projects/Rethink-CF/
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving systems have been shown to suffer from shortcut learning and causal confusion, limiting their ability to generalize robustly under distribution shift. To mitigate these limitations, recent works based on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have explored incorporating linguistic reasoning capabilities into driving decision-making, reporting improvements in driving performance. In particular, counterfactual (CF) reasoning has been proposed to enable self-reflective, high-level causal understanding. However, it remains unclear whether the language-based reasoning mitigates shortcut learning or merely shifts it from visual representations to linguistic patterns, creating an illusion of causal understanding. Moreover, existing approaches often conflate intervention-based reasoning with CF reasoning, leaving the effects of genuine CF reasoning unexplored. In this work, we examine what CF reasoning actually induces in VLM-based autonomous driving and observe that it shifts the action distribution toward more conservative behaviors while increasing uncertainty in reasoning and decision-making. This effect persists under reinforcement fine-tuning, suggesting that it is not merely a transient artifact. Furthermore, visual attention analysis shows that CF reasoning does not alter visual grounding, while causal intervention experiments reveal that masking causally relevant and non-causal factors in accident scenarios results in comparable decision change rates. Overall, these results suggest that CF reasoning does not induce genuine causal reasoning grounded in visual evidence. Instead, it acts as a linguistic shortcut that reshapes the action distribution, teaching models what to say but not how to reason, highlighting a gap between language-based reasoning and causal understanding in autonomous driving.

TrajWAM: Trajectory-Conditioned Future Learning for Vision-Language Driving 2026-11-18
📝 22 pages, including supplementary material. Code and model weights: https://github.com/FangWD520/TrajWAM
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) connect future scene prediction with action planning for autonomous driving. Each driving record pairs an executed ego trajectory with its observed future, providing a factual outcome for learning this connection. We present TrajWAM, a trajectory-conditioned WAM that improves vision-language driving through auxiliary bird's-eye-view (BEV) future learning. An ego-trajectory query conditions DINOv3-aligned BEV predictions from shared planner representations. Training combines factual reconstruction under the executed trajectory with one-sided feature separation under an unlabeled perturbed trajectory.
