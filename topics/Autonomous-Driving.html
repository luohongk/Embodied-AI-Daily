Structured Representation Probing for Risk Anticipation in End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-27
Show abstract

End-to-end driving policies form structured internal representations of traffic, ego behavior, and road constraints. These representations offer a monitoring interface for anticipating failures, complementing risk signals from trajectories, controls, and manually designed features. We introduce the Structured Representation Probe (SRP), a lightweight monitor that reads actor, ego, and map representations from a frozen policy. SRP learns a separate linear risk direction for each source and aggregates element scores through cardinality-normalized log-mean-exp pooling, retaining sensitivity to salient evidence while reducing dependence on token count. Event-window supervision links the readout to upcoming hard failures, and auxiliary event type heads jointly train the source directions across failure modes. Online alerting uses a single generic score without modifying the driving policy. Bench2Drive experiments on DriveTransformer, UniAD, and VAD show higher frame-level PR-AUC and ROC-AUC than the evaluated runtime baselines. On DriveTransformer at a horizon of 2 seconds, ROC-AUC reaches 0.833 versus 0.715 for temporal consistency, with 2.67% readout runtime overhead. The trained probes also retain the ability to distinguish impending failures on new routes.

FastBEVFusion: Simple Multi-Modal Fusion for 3D Object Detection at 50 FPS 2026-10-27
📝 Accepted at Iberian Robotics Conference (ROBOT2026)
Show abstract

Reliable real-time 3D object detection based on LiDAR and camera data is critical for safety in autonomous driving, where systems must accurately perceive their surroundings with minimal latency. Many state-of-the-art systems achieve high-quality detection results but fail to meet real-time constraints, limiting their practical use. To address this challenge, we propose FastBEVFusion, a simple, fast, and robust fusion system for 3D object detection. Based on modality-specific features, FastBEVFusion projects these features into semi-aligned modality-specific BEV spaces, without relying on a computationally intensive and potentially error-prone depth estimation. Given these BEV spaces, we propose a light-weight, soft, and dynamic fusion module based on cross-attention, which adapts dynamically to the scene context and enables feature fusion across the entire BEV spaces. Experiments on the complex nuScenes dataset show that FastBEVFusion outperforms state-of-the-art real-time systems, and achieves over 50 FPS without deployment libraries. Additionally, our system shows strong robustness to calibration errors, making FastBEVFusion a powerful 3D object detection system for real-world applications.

Hyperparameter and Neural Architecture Search via Evolutionary Optimization for Reinforcement Learning: HPNAS-RL 2026-10-27
Show abstract

In this study, we propose a dual-branch evolutionary framework that simultaneously performs Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) for Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Autonomous Driving (AD) tasks. While NAS has shown strong potential in automating architecture design for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), its application to RL remains limited, especially in scenarios that require joint optimization of architectural and training dynamics. Building on our prior work on Evolutionary Multi-Objective NAS (EMNAS), the proposed framework evolves discrete network architectures and continuous hyperparameters in parallel under a unified multi-objective search strategy. In this setup, NAS targets the convolutional layers of the RL policy network - responsible for visual feature extraction, while HPO focuses on optimizing critical RL hyperparameters. To accelerate convergence and improve generalization, we incorporate parallel evaluation and a teacher-student policy transfer mechanism. Experiments in a high-fidelity driving simulator show that the joint NAS-HPO framework consistently outperforms standalone NAS, yielding policies with higher rewards, fewer parameters, and improved stability. These results highlight the benefits of jointly optimizing model structure and training dynamics for scalable and effective RL agent design.

The Boreas Road Trip (Boreas-RT) dataset extends the multi-season Boreas dataset to new and diverse locations that pose challenges for modern autonomous driving algorithms. Boreas-RT comprises 60 sequences collected over 9 real-world routes, totalling 643 km of driving. Each route is traversed multiple times, enabling evaluation in identical environments under varying traffic and, in some cases, weather conditions. The data collection platform includes a 5MP FLIR Blackfly S camera, a 360 degree Navtech RAS6 Doppler-enabled spinning radar, a 128-channel 360 degree Velodyne Alpha Prime lidar, an Aeva Aeries II FMCW Doppler-enabled lidar, a Silicon Sensing DMU41 inertial measurement unit, and a Dynapar wheel encoder. Centimetre-level ground truth is provided via post-processed Applanix POS LV GNSS-INS data. The dataset includes precise extrinsic and intrinsic calibrations, a publicly available development kit, and a live leaderboard for odometry and metric localization. Benchmark results show that many state-of-the-art odometry and localization algorithms overfit to simple driving environments and degrade significantly on the more challenging Boreas-RT routes. Boreas-RT provides a unified dataset for evaluating multi-modal algorithms across diverse road conditions. The dataset, leaderboard, and development kit are available at www.boreas.utias.utoronto.ca.

Show abstract

Coding agents can adapt robotic software by revising executable programs, but successful responses to an evolving opponent may not remain effective. We present a framework for program-level adversarial co-adaptive self-play in which coding agents generate attacks and defenses from tested opponent code and mission feedback, without model fine-tuning. Environment-specific interfaces support external programs for autonomous driving and SDK-level defenses for humanoid locomotion. Closed-Loop Aware Planning via Imitation-Free Speed Scoring for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027. 17 pages (8-page main paper, references, and supplementary material), 5 figures, 7 tables. Project page: https://clap-ad.github.io. \c{opyright} 2027 IEEE. Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving has predominantly relied on open-loop imitation learning for training, which presents two limitations. First, expert demonstrations show only one behavior per scenario, so the model cannot learn alternative valid behaviors and, in turn, cannot adapt to user-specified preferences at test time. Second, noise or ambiguity in these demonstrations can produce inconsistent, unreliable supervision for the end-to-end models. Driving is inherently multi-modal: multiple different behaviors can be valid responses to the same scene, and this diversity arises largely from speed rather than path. We introduce a planning paradigm that learns the path through imitation while scoring a set of candidate speeds across multiple criteria with a learned function supervised by physically grounded safety constraints. We realize this paradigm as Closed-Loop Aware Planning for Autonomous Driving (CLAP-AD), a plug-in planning head that integrates with existing end-to-end driving models and, to our knowledge, is the first to enable explicit test-time control over their speed selection without speed-conditioned training. On Bench2Drive-Speed, CLAP-AD sets a new state of the art Speed-Adherence Score of 82.53, topping TFv6, the strongest baseline on this metric, by 12.9 points while retaining strong closed-loop driving performance. On Bench2Drive, it improves driving score by +28.5 and +32.7 over UniAD and VAD, and on NAVSIM v2, built on real-world driving data, EPDMS by +6.1 over its backbone. OSWAM: An Effective World Action Model with One-Step Action Generation 2026-10-26
📝 18 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Direct-policy World Action Models (WAM) have demonstrated the benefits of decoupling action generation from explicit future visual prediction for efficient inference in autonomous driving and embodied control. However, they still require tens of denoising steps to achieve competitive action quality, incurring substantial latency and hindering real-time deployment. Simply reducing the number of denoising steps will collapse the nonlinear multi-step denoising trajectory into coarse updates, substantially degrading action quality. To reduce the denoising steps while enabling real-time action generation, we present an effective world action model with one-step action generation, namely OSWAM, for autonomous driving and embodied control. We argue that it is the absence of structured action priors in random noise initialization that forces direct-policy WAMs to recover coherent action sequences through iterative denoising. Hence, we construct imagination tokens to initialize the diffusion process, providing physical priors and action-conditioned representations. We further introduce privileged information distillation with future conditioning to enhance future-aware action generation. As a result, OSWAM can efficiently and effectively generate high quality actions in just one diffusion step. Our experiments demonstrate that OSWAM achieves comparable or even better results in both autonomous driving and embodied control than direct-policy WAM and Joint-WAM that require tens of steps. It obtains approximately $3\times$ speedup over direct-policy WAM baselines and supports real-time compiled inference with latency as low as 40ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. The project page is available at https://gwenzhang.github.io/OSWAM/

Revisiting Direct Control: A Field Evaluation of Sensor Fusion Efficacy in Teleoperating Full-Scale Autonomous Vehicles via Bird's Eye View 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted at 2026 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)
Show abstract

In autonomous driving, robot teleoperation serves as a critical safety fallback mechanism, requiring operators to assume direct control in the event of system failure. While recent research has pivoted toward high-level supervisory control or simulation-based studies, these approaches often fail to capture the physical dynamics and sensory demands of driving a full-scale vehicle. To address this gap, we evaluated the impact of spatial data representation on operator performance using a real car-sized vehicle in a field experiment with $N = 18$ participants. We compared three visualization interfaces: (i) a standard camera feed baseline, with two bird's-eye views (BEV) from (ii) a raw LiDAR point cloud, and (iii) a sensor fusion of LiDAR and camera data streams. Contrary to prior passive observation studies suggesting that sensor-fusion BEV may hinder awareness, our active-control results demonstrate that such representation significantly outperforms the standard LiDAR BEV in perceived usability ($p \leq 0.0001$) and cognitive task load ($p = 0.01$, NASA-TLX score: 34.58/100) while also being significantly better than the Camera-only view ($p = 0.043$). The fused interface also led to statistically significant reductions in task completion time ($p = 0.0461$) and superior usability (SUS: 77.9/100) compared to those of the Camera-only and LiDAR-only views. By synthesizing objective performance data with comprehensive subjective assessments, we demonstrate that BEV fusion maximizes safety margins while minimizing the operator's mental demand. These findings challenge existing teleoperation conventions and provide an empirically validated approach for designing robust remote piloting systems.

Camera-based bird's-eye view (BEV) perception methods such as Fast-BEV have demonstrated promising results with fast inference speed and competitive accuracy. However, their robustness to real-world corruptions remains insufficiently explored, posing risks for autonomous driving deployment where environmental variations are inevitable. In this paper, we propose Sparse Recovery Bird's-Eye View Model Soup (SR-BEVS), a sparse recovery learning approach that simultaneously improves clean accuracy and corruption robustness without requiring additional data or architectural modifications. Our method identifies pruning candidates based on layer-wise weight magnitudes, applies random pruning to create diverse sparse models, and retrains each model with a randomly sampled learning rate. Our simple yet effective approach may be applicable to other BEV models.

Adaptive Probe Design for Auditing AI Agents 2026-10-26
Show abstract

How can we audit an AI agent before it is safe and practical to deploy? A central task is to estimate its performance under the intended deployment policy (the target policy). However, historical observations may provide insufficient coverage of relevant states and actions, limiting evaluation reliability. We propose CAD (Flow-Constrained Audit Design), a framework that combines historical logs with a limited budget of targeted audit trials (probes). The key idea is to design probes that minimize the mismatch between the combined state-action visitation distribution (occupancy) of the logs and probes and that of the target policy. We formulate this design as a constrained convex optimization problem, directing probes toward regions important to the target policy but underrepresented in historical logs. We develop an adaptive algorithm that iteratively estimates environment dynamics and occupancies to update the probe policy. We link occupancy mismatch to bounds on mean squared error and asymptotic variance, and establish error bounds for CAD's adaptive estimator in finite and continuous state spaces. Experiments in Gridworld, LunarLander, and autonomous driving using real-world logs from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset show improved estimation accuracy over alternative data collection and probe designs.

STATIC: Surface-normal-guided Two-branch Alignment and Temporal Integration for Consistent Video Depth Estimation 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted by Pattern Recognition
Show abstract

Video depth estimation is essential for applications such as autonomous driving, AR/VR, and robotics. Recent transformer-based single-image monocular depth estimation models perform well on a single image but struggle with depth consistency across video frames. Traditional methods aim to improve temporal consistency using multi-frame temporal modules or explicit priors like optical flow and camera parameters. However, these approaches face issues such as high memory use, reduced performance with dynamic or irregular motion, and a heavy reliance on the accuracy of external motion estimators. We propose STATIC, a novel model that independently learns temporal consistency in geometrically stable and unstable regions using only two adjacent frames. By leveraging intrinsic geometric cues rather than external motion priors, STATIC generates a depth-derived geometry-aware difference mask from surface normals to identify regions with inter-frame geometric variation by measuring directional variance. For geometrically stable regions, the Masked Stable (MS) module enhances temporal consistency by focusing on structurally stable regions. For geometrically unstable regions, the Surface Normal Similarity (SNS) module models inter-frame correspondences and ensures temporal consistency by measuring feature similarities between frames. A final refinement integrates the independently learned stable and unstable features, enabling STATIC to improve consistent depth prediction across consecutive frames. Experiments on KITTI, NYUv2, and Sintel demonstrate strong depth accuracy and temporal consistency, as well as stable performance across the tested frame-rate and camera-motion conditions.

Show abstract

Simulation with realistic traffic agents is essential for validating autonomous driving systems. Existing data-driven simulators learn agent behavior from higher-level abstractions such as 3D bounding boxes and polylines, inferred by upstream perception pipelines. These lossy abstractions discard sensory context that directly shapes agent behavior, limiting the distributional realism that simulation aims to reproduce. To address this limitation, we propose AutoWorld, a traffic simulation framework that grounds agent behavior in raw sensor observations through a self-supervised world model trained on LiDAR occupancy data. Given world model samples, AutoWorld constructs a coarse-to-fine predictive scene context as input to a multi-agent motion generation model. Furthermore, we designed a motion-aware latent supervision objective that enriches AutoWorld's latent representation of scene dynamics. Experiments on nuPlan and Waymo demonstrate that AutoWorld improves generation realism, with larger gains in partially observed scenarios where trajectory abstractions are most limited, reducing collision rates by 36%. AutoWorld also remains physically plausible under planner-controlled ego behavior and supports controllable, diverse safety-critical scenario generation.

RSKD: Grounding Semantic Consistency in Driving VLMs via Distillation 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Knowledge distillation can transfer large multimodal models to compact models for autonomous driving, but uniform token-level matching can dilute planning-relevant semantics and amplify teacher errors. We propose RSKD, a semantic-consistency-guided distillation framework that routes supervision according to both the task importance of an answer phrase and its semantic consistency with the teacher prediction. Task-critical semantic phrases are localized in the ground-truth answer, and a phrase-level consistency score controls the balance between Jensen--Shannon divergence (JSD) distillation and ground-truth supervision. While reasoning technology like Chain of Thought (CoT) has been widely adopted in Vision Language Action (VLA) models, it demonstrates promising capabilities in end to end autonomous driving. However, recent efforts to integrate CoT reasoning often fall short in simple scenarios, introducing unnecessary computational overhead without improving decision quality. To address this, we propose AdaThinkDrive, a novel VLA framework with a dual mode reasoning mechanism inspired by fast and slow thinking. In the Driver's Seat: A Multi-Company Study on the Reality of Autonomous Driving System Testing 2026-10-25
📝 11 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Autonomous driving systems (ADS) are increasingly deployed in complex real-world environments, creating strong demands for rigorous testing to establish their functionality and safety. However, ADS testing remains difficult because practitioners must determine not only what and how to test, but also whether the resulting evidence is sufficient to support progression and release decisions. To understand how this is handled in practice, we conducted semi-structured interviews with experts involved in ADS development and testing across nine companies in six countries. Through thematic analysis, we examined how practitioners organize ADS testing to produce and assess evidence, and what limits confidence in that evidence. Our findings show that industrial ADS testing can be understood as an evidence-building process involving three recurring activities: planning what evidence is needed, producing and evaluating evidence across complementary testing environments, and deciding when testing is sufficient. We further identified recurring limitations concerning scenario adequacy, simulation and deployment fidelity, test execution and observability, and assurance and decision sufficiency. Practitioners reported several approaches for addressing these limitations, although important gaps remain. Based on these findings, we propose an evidence-centered closed-loop testing framework that connects testing intent, scenario selection, evidence production, acceptance decisions, and operational feedback. The study provides an industry-grounded account of how ADS testing is organized, where confidence in current testing breaks down, and how testing processes can be structured more systematically.

C-DFF: Concept-Level Diagnostics for Mechanism Parity in Simulation-Based Validation of Autonomous Driving Perception 2026-10-24
📝 7 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables. Accepted and presented at the 2026 IEEE 29th International Conference on Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITSC), Naples, Italy, September 15-18, 2026
Show abstract

Simulation is widely used in autonomous-driving validation, but matching task outputs between real and synthetic images does not guarantee that a system relies on the same visual evidence. This creates a validation gap for transfer, especially in safety-critical perception functions. We present Concept-Decisive Feature Fidelity (C-DFF), a concept-level mechanism-parity diagnostic for paired real-synthetic evaluation. Starting from decisive evidence extracted from a frozen system-under-test (SUT), C-DFF projects that evidence into a shared semantic concept space using off-the-shelf segmentation and compares real and synthetic concept-importance vectors. The result is not only a scalar mechanism-parity score, but also a concept-shift diagnostic that identifies which scene concepts become more or less decisive in simulation. We evaluate C-DFF on KITTI $\leftrightarrow$ Virtual KITTI 2 with 2126 matched pairs across three frozen SUTs (steering regression, YOLOP drivable-area, YOLOP lane-line). Across these tasks, C-DFF is SUT-discriminative and yields interpretable concept-level summaries that support validation triage and simulator debugging. When reported alongside task-specific output-value context metrics, C-DFF shows that output agreement does not by itself establish mechanism parity. These results position C-DFF as a practical semantic diagnostic layer for validation and verification workflows in simulation-based AV testing.

Show abstract

Automotive millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar has attracted increasing attention for semantic segmentation in autonomous driving, owing to its robustness under adverse weather and lighting conditions. Recent works have introduced radar-specific neural operators such as PeakConv (PKC), inspired by constant false alarm rate (CFAR) detection, to better model the peak-dominated characteristics of radar images. However, existing methods typically treat the guard bandwidth as learnable parameters, without explicitly characterizing the frequency response of the PKC operator. In this work, we revisit PKC from a frequency-domain perspective and propose Frequency-Aware PeakConv (FreqPKC), which explicitly models and exploits its underlying spectral characteristics. By analyzing the frequency response of PKC, FreqPKC enables the network to capture a broader range of frequency components and adaptively adjust its effective bandwidth. With the proposed Frequency-Aware (FA) module, the network can infer appropriate guard bandwidths to balance high- and low-frequency information: it enhances high-frequency components in target regions for more precise segmentation while suppressing noise through low-frequency filtering in background areas. Building on FreqPKC, we further develop FreqPKC-Net, a radar semantic segmentation (RSS) framework that integrates frequency-guided adaptive modulation with cross-view fusion for multi-view inputs, and can also be adapted to single-view data. Experiments on the public datasets CARRADA, CARRADA-RAC, and a self-built dataset named RS-SAR demonstrate that FreqPKC-Net consistently outperforms existing SoTA methods on both range-Doppler and range-angle views.

DriveOSS: Dataset and Benchmark for 3D Anomaly Segmentation and Open-set Semantic Segmentation in Autonomous Driving 2026-10-23
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027
Show abstract

Recent advances in 3D semantic segmentation have significantly improved scene understanding in autonomous driving. However, existing methods are typically developed under a closed-set assumption and evaluated under standard driving conditions, where all categories are known in advance. In real-world driving, autonomous vehicles inevitably encounter previously unseen objects, making closed-set perception insufficient for safe deployment. To address this challenge, we introduce DriveOSS, which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first real-world multimodal dataset and benchmark specifically designed for 3D anomaly segmentation and open-set semantic segmentation. It contains 12,661 synchronized and annotated frames captured using six surround-view RGB cameras, a 128-beam LiDAR, and a GPS/IMU unit. The dataset comprises 52 real-world driving scenes collected under clear, nighttime, cloudy, rainy, and snowy conditions and is organized into ID (in-distribution) and OOD (out-of-distribution) subsets. The ID scenes contain only known classes and are used for training, whereas the OOD scenes additionally contain unknown objects annotated as the outlier class and are used for validation and testing.
