Benchmarking Evidence Sufficiency in Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Reliable driving VLMs must do more than answer correctly or localize relevant scene actors: they must determine whether the available visual evidence is sufficient to support an answer. Existing evaluations largely treat grounding and answerability as separate measurements, making it difficult to distinguish well-supported predictions from those localized to relevant but insufficient evidence. We introduce DriveAnswer, a benchmark of 20,151 questions across seven reasoning families, that operationalizes this distinction using matched strong- and weak-evidence configurations. Each pair preserves the underlying scene fact and semantic answer while changing whether the observations support answering, making abstention the appropriate response under weak evidence. On the six-family evaluation, the strongest zero-shot baseline reaches 67.31% strong-evidence accuracy, yet only 21.55% of matched questions satisfy both answer-abstain behavior and complete grounding. With controlled adaptation, Gemma-4-12B reaches 63.29% on this joint capability, nearly tripling its zero-shot performance. Qwen3-VL-8B exhibits the same broad improvement in evidence-sensitive answering and grounding. These results show that semantic correctness, evidence sufficiency, and grounding are distinct capabilities that should be evaluated jointly.

MemAnchorWorld: A Sparse World Model with Horizon-Conditioned Memory Retrieval for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
📝 11 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Sparse world models for 4D semantic occupancy forecasting represent scenes with a small set of evolving queries to efficiently predict future geometry and semantics for autonomous driving. However, existing sparse world models face a bottleneck in using historical temporal evidence for long-horizon forecasting. They rely on scene states to carry historical information implicitly, allowing errors to propagate and accumulate across successive forecasting steps and causing geometric and semantic deviations. To address this limitation, we present MemAnchorWorld, a sparse occupancy world model that explicitly retrieves relevant historical geometric and semantic evidence for 4D semantic occupancy forecasting. Inspired by the von Restorff effect, we introduce a forecast-time-conditioned hierarchical dual-path memory-anchor retrieval mechanism. Guided by the effective age and semantic compatibility of historical information, it combines query-level candidate selection with point-level memory-anchor retrieval to provide historical evidence for sparse points at different forecast times. To better use memory anchors and existing features, a point-level residual adapter jointly gated by the task and forecast time separately controls residual corrections to semantics, occupancy confidence, and spatial positions. On Occ3D-nuScenes, MemAnchorWorld achieves an average mIoU of 13.55% and an average IoU of 22.84% across the 1-3 s forecasting horizons, attaining state-of-the-art performance on both metrics.Project page: https://memanchorworld.github.io/

Topological maps provide essential road structure and connectivity information for autonomous driving. However, accurate centerline modeling remains challenging due to the lack of explicit visual markings and the insufficient exploitation of geometric regularities in existing methods. To address these challenges, we propose HGeo-TopoMap, a hierarchical geometric prior learning framework that integrates road-structure guidance and geometric consistency into centerline instance decoding. Specifically, a Geometric Adaptive Learning (GAL) module introduces spatially adaptive cross-attention to incorporate road structure priors derived from inverse perspective mapping, where distance-aware attention biases guide instance queries toward spatially relevant structural information. Furthermore, we develop a hierarchical centerline decoding strategy that progressively predicts instance centers and their constituent points, facilitating the learning of geometric orientations and spatial configurations. Building upon this structured representation, a Geometric Consistency Learning (GCL) module imposes layer-wise contrastive supervision on matched instance queries using geometric groupings, exploiting geometric relationships among centerline instances to regularize instance representations and improve centerline detection. By jointly exploiting spatial structural guidance and instance-level geometric regularities, HGeo-TopoMap enhances centerline detection and topology reasoning. Extensive experiments on the OpenLane-V2 centerline and lane segment benchmarks demonstrate improved topological mapping accuracy. Meanwhile, the proposed method demonstrates stable performance under the robustness benchmark. The source code and model weights will be made publicly available at https://github.com/lynn-yu/HGeo-TopoMap.

GeoVLDrive: Geometry-Grounded Vision-Language Reasoning for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) planners inherit semantic knowledge from pretrained vision-language models, but their reasoning is rarely grounded in 3D scene structure and its evolution over time. We introduce GeoVLDrive, a geometry-grounded VLA framework for 3D spatiotemporal reasoning and planning. Multi-layer features from a 3D foundation model augment the vision-language backbone through gated cross-attention. A structured latent chain of thought (CoT) organizes future scene geometry, key-agent motion, and meta-actions into ordered, separately supervised latent states. An action expert generates trajectory proposals from these states and multi-layer scene features, and ranks them using scene-grounded, interacting score tokens. We also introduce NAVSIM 3DQA to train and evaluate metric 3D spatial and motion understanding. GeoVLDrive achieves 91.8 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 90.9 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2. On NAVSIM 3DQA, the geometry-grounded backbone scores 89.2% overall, 4.6 percentage points above the fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B baseline. On LingoQA, the backbone achieves 68.6% Lingo-Judge accuracy.

CodeWAM: A Code World Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Driving world models anticipate action consequences through representations of how the world evolves. For autonomous driving, these representations must capture both the surrounding scene and the ego vehicle's motion under a candidate plan. We propose CodeWAM, a driving world model that incorporates executable code as part of its world representation. Code represents ego dynamics by expressing how the vehicle state evolves under plan execution, while a learned visual model captures scene-dependent consequences. Executing the code produces a candidate-conditioned motion sequence, which is evaluated relative to the prior plan and recorded motion history. The resulting execution consequences and learned scene outcomes jointly represent the candidate's future, enabling the planner to assess both what a plan may lead to and how the vehicle transitions into that future. The selected plan becomes part of the next decision's execution context, linking successive choices within the model. CodeWAM achieves 93.6 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 navtest, 92.1 EPDMS on v2 navtest, and 50.6 EPDMS on NAVHARD. Further experiments demonstrate improved pairwise execution consistency and gains on Hydra-MDP and DrivoR without additional training, supporting executable code as a complementary representation for driving world models.

OmniPersist: Persistent Dynamic World Modeling via Coupled Panoramic Evolution 2026-11-24

Show abstract

Action-conditioned world models play a vital role in robotics and autonomous driving, yet existing video diffusion models struggle to maintain dynamic object persistence. When the camera turns away and returns, out-of-view moving entities fail to evolve plausibly in latent space, often mutating or vanishing entirely due to bounded perspective views. To resolve this issue, we propose OmniPersist, a unified framework that couples perspective video generation with panoramic evolution within a single-stage diffusion model, without relying on auxiliary models or initial panoramic inputs. To maintain efficiency, we model the panoramic stream at a lower frame rate, bridging both views across space and time via Geometric Anchoring (GeoAnch) for spatial correspondence and Temporal Compensation (TemComp) for temporal aggregation. OmniPersist is agnostic to camera-control architectures, supporting both latent-level and attention-level conditioning. Extensive experiments on a newly constructed dynamic benchmark across image-to-video and video continuation tasks demonstrate consistent gains in visual quality, camera controllability, revisit consistency, and dynamic persistence.

Enabling Mixed criticality applications for the Versal AI-Engines 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Mixed-criticality systems (MCSs) are used in autonomous driving, aviation and medical systems. They require hardware platforms, which are reconfigurable at runtime to fully utilize performance while maintaining critical deadlines in emergency scenarios. However, Systems-on-Chip (SoC) components, like the Versal AI-Engine (AIE), only support static task mapping, limiting its utilization. In this work, we propose a dynamic task dispatching infrastructure that enables task switching on the AIE at runtime. Based on this infrastructure, we present an MCS design that dynamically assigns tasks of different criticality to AIE tiles, depending on the criticality mode of the system. Our approach overcomes the limitations of static dataflow graph mappings and, for the first time, exploits the parallel processing capabilities of the AIE for MCSs. We provide a timing analysis of the overhead introduced by the task dispatching infrastructure, which demonstrates that it is negligible. Finally, we evaluate the proposed infrastructure using an autonomous driving workload with tasks that have variable execution times and different criticality levels. In this case study, we maximized AIE utilization, reducing idle time by 65.5\%, while measuring an execution time overhead of less than 0.8\%, and doubling the throughput of low-criticality tasks.

NativeDrive: Native Multimodal Foundations for Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
📝 16 pages, 8 figures, 7 tables, including supplementary material
Show abstract

Autonomous driving requires semantic understanding and fine-grained scene perception for precise action prediction. Vision-language-action models (VLAs) inherit semantic priors, but low-dimensional action labels provide limited structural supervision. Some studies extend VLAs with visual generation tasks or adopt video-based world-action models (WAMs), but both approaches introduce appearance-reconstruction overhead, which is less relevant to planning. Native multimodal models offer an alternative to unify understanding and generation within one backbone. On this basis, we propose NativeDrive, a native encoder-free multimodal driving framework adapting the unified backbone for policy learning and introduce a dense supervision task tailored to driving. The Native Action Interface reuses the understanding expert's token-prediction interface for the discrete policy and adapts the generation expert for continuous policy. Driving Semantic Editing reuses the generation expert's editing capability for dense scene-structure supervision during training, while inference runs only the selected planning policy. NativeDrive achieves competitive planning performance among unified understanding-and-generation models on NAVSIM v1 and v2, with ablations validating its components' effectiveness. XMind: Thinking Twice via Cross-Memory Reasoning for LLM-Based Autonomous Driving 2026-11-24
📝 9 pages, 3 figures
Show abstract

Memory-augmented large language model (LLM) driving agents can reuse past experience, yet previously learned corrections often fail to transfer when traffic conditions change. A key limitation is that existing memories mainly record observed outcomes, providing little evidence about alternative actions. We introduce XMind, a training-free framework that enables LLM-based driving to "Think Twice" through cross-memory reasoning. XMind integrates working memory of recent traffic states with episodic memory of critical risk events. The agent first makes an initial decision from the current context and then cross-checks it against retrieved historical experience. If a conflict arises, it makes a second decision guided by the retrieved evidence. To construct reliable episodic memory, XMind evaluates alternative actions through counterfactual rollouts under perturbed traffic conditions and stores the verified outcomes as structured risk capsules. These capsules are retrieved by jointly matching spatial risk patterns and scenario semantics. Across closed-loop highway simulations, XMind achieves success rates of 83.3-93.3%, exceeding the strong baseline by 6.6 percentage points on average. Evaluation on K-Risk further demonstrates lower decision risk on unseen real-world high-risk interactions. Ablation studies confirm the complementary benefits of working memory, risk-text retrieval, and counterfactual verification. Code is available at https://github.com/xiangxiangliuwo/XMind

Instant LiDAR: Feed-Forward Gaussian Reconstruction for LiDAR Simulation 2026-11-24
Show abstract

LiDAR reconstruction supports closed-loop autonomous driving simulation, but hours of per-scene optimization make large asset libraries costly to build and maintain. We present Instant LiDAR, a feed-forward pipeline that reconstructs multiple LiDAR scans as reusable Gaussian assets in minutes without per-scene optimization. LiDAR measurements provide strong geometric priors, but directly initializing Gaussians from them yields poor reconstruction due to nonuniform sampling and a limited field of view. Sparse returns leave surface gaps, while combining scans expands coverage but introduces redundant primitives. To address these challenges, Instant LiDAR combines measurement-anchored Gaussian prediction, sampling-aware Gaussian scales, and multi-frame consolidation. The predictor refines attributes anchored to recorded returns, while the scales adapt spatial support to LiDAR sampling. Multi-frame consolidation merges overlapping predictions into a compact asset spanning the driving log. Compared with NuRec, Instant LiDAR reconstructs scenes approximately 46 times faster on Physical AI (PAI) and 70 times faster on Waymo. It also achieves comparable downstream 3D detection accuracy on Waymo.

ReJEPA: Bounded Predictive Regulation for Latent World Models 2026-11-24
📝 19 pages, 12 figures, 24 tables
Show abstract

Latent world models learn representations by predicting future states in an abstract embedding space rather than reconstructing raw pixels. When training across multi-frame sequences, however, not all frames and spatial regions are equally informative or predictable. We argue that forcing a world model to minimize every prediction error may be unnecessary and harmful. Under standard $L_2$ loss, unbounded gradients force the encoder and predictor to overfit noisy or transient frames at the expense of core physical structure, while amplifying gradient conflict with representation regularizers. Conversely, $L_1$ loss bounds large gradients, but its constant gradient magnitude near zero disrupts the local smoothness required for planning. We resolve this tension with \textbf{ReJEPA}, a bounded predictive regulation principle that combines bounded gradients for large errors with smooth quadratic behavior near zero, instantiated simply via the element-wise $\log\cosh$ loss. As a drop-in training replacement across Temporal Straightening, LeWorldModel, and V-JEPA~2.1, $\log\cosh$ consistently outperforms $L_2$, a curvature-matched quadratic control, $L_1$, and Smooth-$L_1$ baselines without altering architectures or inference procedures. Notably, the gains grow with environmental complexity, scaling from simple navigation to contact-rich manipulation and real-world autonomous driving on NAVSIM. Probing and geometric analyses confirm that relaxing the penalty on extreme errors yields representations that better capture physical states and align with the true planning cost landscape.

Beyond the Benchmark: Localizing Released Autonomous Driving Models to Real Vehicles 2026-11-24
📝 14 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

Released autonomous-driving models achieve strong benchmark performance, yet deploying them on a new real vehicle remains challenging. We study this deployment gap and find that released models can remain coupled to assumptions from their source sensing, data, and execution processes even after conventional adaptation. We introduce Released-to-Real (R2R), a deployment-localization methodology that identifies such source-system coupling and corrects only the affected components while preserving the released model. For data and behavior adaptation, we find that the mismatch between trajectory-level optimization and receding-horizon execution enters through the ego state rather than the observation, and introduce Human-Anchored Reinforcement Learning (HA-RL), which corrects it on recorded target driving without the costly rollouts of generative closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). For physical sensor adaptation, R2R localizes and corrects source-dependent spatial and temporal representations with frozen perception weights, requiring neither target annotations nor retraining. HA-RL improves the closed-loop success rate of a released diffusion-based planner from 82.2% to 95.2%, while sensor adaptation across multiple released detectors and three real sensor rigs improves the vehicle mean average precision (mAP) of a released multi-camera detector from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera target rig. These results provide a practical path for preparing released models before deployment on new real vehicles. Our project page is publicly available at https://released-to-real.github.io.

Collision Risk Estimation via Loss Prediction in End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Collision risk estimation and avoidance play central roles in the safety of autonomous driving (AD) systems. Recently emerged end-to-end AD systems gain collision avoidance ability by minimizing losses to penalize planning trajectories that are too close to other objects. Despite a significant collision rate during testing, most end-to-end planners do not explicitly quantify the collision risk in their outputs. To address this, we introduce RiskMonitor, an efficient plug-and-play module that interprets planning and motion tokens from state-of-the-art end-to-end planners to estimate collision risk. Inspired by loss prediction based uncertainty quantification, RiskMonitor predicts whether the collision loss -- commonly adopted to train end-to-end planners -- is positive along planned waypoints, framing collision risk estimation as a binary classification task. We evaluate RiskMonitor on the real-world nuScenes dataset (open-loop) and the neural-rendering based simulator, NeuroNCAP (closed-loop). Our token-driven method outperforms prediction-driven approaches, including deterministic rules, Gaussian mixture models, and Monte Carlo Dropout. When integrated with a simple braking policy, RiskMonitor improves collision avoidance ability by $66.5\%$ in a closed-loop test on safety-critical scenarios. These results demonstrate that monitoring collision risk using plan and motion tokens enhances the safety of end-to-end AD without retraining it.

Pruning Autonomous Driving VLAs: What to Preserve for Reasoning and Action 2026-11-23
📝 45 pages. Project page: https://cvlab-kaist.github.io/MoOP
Show abstract

Reasoning vision-language-action (VLA) models for autonomous driving use chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to guide action generation, but incur substantial memory and computational costs. We study structured pruning for these models, focusing on preserving both scene understanding and trajectory prediction. Our analysis of Taylor importance in Alpamayo-1.5-10B shows that reasoning and trajectory objectives assign different importance to the same vision-language model (VLM) structures. In the action expert, MLP channels exhibit more stable importance rankings across denoising steps than query heads, and a small fraction of channels accounts for most of the measured importance. Based on these findings, we propose MoOP, which combines importance ranks from both objectives for VLM pruning and aggregates stepwise importance to select a fixed subset of expert MLP channels. At 24.0% total parameter reduction, VLM-only pruning with MoOP performs comparably to or slightly better than the strongest baselines in driving, while retaining 94.0% of the dense model's LingoQA score, far above all other methods. At a higher reduction of 30.0%, MoOP degrades much less than every other method, as it is the only one whose reasoning does not collapse. Removing 93.75% of expert MLP channels increases total parameter reduction to 39.4%, with nearly unchanged open-loop errors relative to VLM-only pruning and competitive closed-loop performance. The resulting model achieves a 1.37x end-to-end inference speedup and 36.6% lower peak memory than the dense model, without post-pruning fine-tuning.

Can Multimodal Fusion MakeSense for Embodied Vision in Degraded Sensing? 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Embodied vision systems rely on complementary sensors to perceive and act as conditions change. Even models that accept arbitrary sensor subsets can exhibit negative fusion: adding a sensor increases prediction error. We propose MakeSense, a multimodal fusion framework that preserves sensor-specific evidence in private streams and combines information through shared bottleneck tokens. Training pairs full and subset views of the same degraded observations, penalizing higher error with more sensors. Where geometry permits, the distance readout uses range measurements alone, while the rest of the prediction draws on all available inputs. Coordinating Perception and Planning for Test-Time Adaptation in End-to-End Driving under Distribution Shifts 2026-11-23
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved significant milestones, yet its reliance on the i.i.d. assumption renders it vulnerable to real-world distribution shifts. While Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm for on-the-fly enhancement, its application to driving is hindered by two barriers: (i) multi-task adaptation dependency, where updates to upstream perception also affect downstream planning, while perception confidence alone does not directly assess these downstream effects; and (ii) continuous adaptation instability, where recursive online updates over long horizons cause compounding parameter drift. To address these challenges, we propose P2-TTA, a plug-and-play TTA framework for the continual adaptation of end-to-end driving models. P2-TTA coordinates adaptation through a joint objective combining perception entropy minimization with a retrospective temporal planning consistency objective derived from observed ego motion. To stabilize online updates, we use an EMA teacher as a moving parameter reference and reject candidate updates that increase historical planning loss. Extensive experiments across multiple models demonstrate that, by updating an average of 0.11 per mille of parameters with an average adaptation time of 19.9 ms on different backbones, P2-TTA achieves average recovery rates of 36.4%, 30.0%, and 9.3% for L2, collision rate, and PDMS, respectively, under severe distribution shifts. Under a shared perception-planning objective, P2-TTA achieves the highest L2 and collision recovery among the compared TTA baselines on UniAD.

AgentMoT: An Agentic Mixture-of-Transformers for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Driving models need to choose which capabilities to use and when to reconsider their decisions. We introduce AgentMoT, a Mixture-of-Transformers with built-in agent reasoning and system scheduling. Combinations of agent, world, and action experts form three jointly trained pathways for direct-action, vision-language-action, and world-action computation. The Router selects a native pathway, while the agent expert supplies reasoning and selects skills. Execution feedback raises wake-up requests, and the agent self-scheduler learns whether to reconsider the task decision or keep it. Mandatory requests invoke the agent directly. AdvMTO: Multimodal and Temporal Adversarial Object Attacks via 2D Gaussian Splatting for BEV-Based 3D Object Detection 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Adversarial object attacks pose a threat to BEV-based 3D object detectors in autonomous driving. Existing attacks typically target a single modality, coordinate camera and LiDAR observations at the current timestamp, or model temporal persistence mainly in camera streams. Without a unified construction of current and historical observations, attacks can leave part of the detector's accumulated evidence unsuppressed, limiting their effectiveness. In this paper, we propose AdvMTO, a 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) framework for suppressing detection of persistent adversarial objects. Its oriented surfels encode appearance, geometry, and opacity in one object state, from which camera images and scanline LiDAR returns are generated. AdvMTO then constructs current and historical inputs through detector-native processing, regenerating LiDAR returns in each native source sweep for fusion detectors. We optimize bounded appearance perturbations for each detector, propagating gradients through historical BEV memory for temporal camera detectors. For fusion detectors, bounded geometry refinement along local normals further perturbs both sensing modalities. Experiments on nuScenes demonstrate effective suppression of traffic-cone detection across three BEV detectors. Temporal ablations further highlight the importance of detector-native history modeling for both attack construction and optimization.

Show abstract
