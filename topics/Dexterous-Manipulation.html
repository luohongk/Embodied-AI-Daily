LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning 2026-11-10
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

Learning Dexterous Manipulation Using Contact Wrench Guidance From Human Demonstration 2026-11-10
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Dexterous robot manipulation can benefit from the abundance of human demonstrations, but transferring such demonstrations to robot policies remains challenging. We present Contact Wrench Guidance from Human Demonstration in Robotic Dexterous Manipulation (CHORD), a framework for long-horizon manipulation of rigid and articulated objects with reinforcement learning. The key idea is object-centric contact wrench space guidance: we represent human and robot motions by the forces and torques they can induce on the object, enabling similarity to be measured by the induced instantaneous motions. This guidance makes reinforcement learning more scalable for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. We further introduce a large-scale simulation benchmark with 4,739 bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, constructed from motion-capture datasets and reconstructed in-house videos. Evaluated on 1,831 benchmark tasks, CHORD achieves an average success rate of 82.12%, demonstrating strong scalability. CHORD also generalizes to whole-body manipulation from hand-only and third-person demonstrations, achieving a 90.77% success rate, and the learned policies transfer to the real world in both open-loop and closed-loop settings.

DexForesight: Grounding Dexterous Policies with Implicit Spatial Foresight 2026-11-10
📝 13 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. We then freeze the world model and align policy queries with its predictive world queries, transferring the learned spatial dynamics into policy latents and using them to condition action generation. At deployment, only the policy is retained, operating on RGB observations, language, and robot state without depth input or explicit future prediction. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. On three real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, DexForesight achieves 58.3% success, compared with 50.0% for the strongest baseline. Project page https://dexforesight.github.io/

Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Tactile sensing is an indispensable modality for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, a capability that hints at the ultimate frontiers of embodied intelligence. However, commercially available tactile sensors are still at a relatively early stage of development, with each type of sensor offering irreplaceable strengths alongside its own drawbacks. A key challenge is thus to free tactile understanding from sensor-specific physics, so that embodied models can effectively leverage heterogeneous tactile data. To this end, we develop LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model characterized by the following properties. (i) Our model yields a unified representation across different tactile sensors (i.e., piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile sensors), and is easily extensible to new sensor types. (ii) The resulting tactile tokens are plug-and-play: they can be seamlessly integrated into policy models across paradigms, from diffusion policy to vision-language-action (VLA) models such as $π_{0.5}$ and LingBot-VLA, consistently enhancing manipulation dexterity. (iii) Extensive experiments, spanning simulation and real-robot deployments across multiple hardware platforms, demonstrate that our tactile representation substantially benefits complex manipulation tasks while maintaining strong computational efficiency. We believe the findings in this work demonstrate the potential of leveraging cross-sensor data to scale tactile learning, and offer encouraging insights for the design of future hardware and embodied models.

PCTac: Mechanochromic Photonic-Crystal Tactile Sensor for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-09
📝 9 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Modern visuotactile sensors primarily sense contact by imaging the deformation of a compliant interface. Using techniques such as photometric stereo, they can recover detailed contact geometry, often represented as surface normals or depth. However, force-related information typically still needs to be inferred indirectly through calibration, geometric reconstruction, or learned mappings. Mechanochromic photonic-crystal (PC) materials offer an alternative physical encoding mechanism: contact loading changes their periodic microstructure and spectral response, directly transforming local stress into a spatially resolved structural-color field. Despite this potential, PC materials have rarely been explored as visuotactile sensors for closed-loop robot learning. We present PCTac, an open-source mechanochromic PC fingertip that uses a dense hue field as its tactile observation. Loading characterization demonstrates a repeatable relationship between the applied normal load and the observed hue response. We further integrate this representation directly into a Diffusion Policy and evaluate it on three real-robot manipulation tasks. Across 90 physical rollouts, PCTac Hue achieves 68 successes (75.6\%), compared with 60 (66.7\%) for PCTac RGB, 57 (63.3\%) for Wedge RGB, and 29 (32.2\%) for vision-only. PCTac Hue also achieves the highest observed success rate on each evaluated task, including Perturbed Test-Tube Insertion, where it achieves 16/30 successes versus 13/30 for tactile RGB baselines and 1/30 for vision-only. These results demonstrate the potential of mechanochromic structural color as a compact, load-sensitive tactile representation for robot learning and closed-loop manipulation. Project page: https://bey0ndt1me.github.io/PCTac-page/.

Embodied First Exam 2026-11-09
📝 Preprint version
Show abstract

Early successes on agentic robotics point to a potential paradigm shift toward using coding agents to control robots. Existing robotics benchmarks are built around visuomotor policy learning, leaving it open whether coding agents can reliably control diverse robots under physical-world constraints. We therefore introduce the Embodied First Exam, a unified, comprehensive, and agent-native evaluation framework where coding agents can choose their own control approach: direct control, code to control, or train a policy to control. We hope it serves as a first exam for coding agents before they enter the physical world, with 1,156 tasks in 35 suites on 47 robots, including 9 new suites that go beyond pick-and-place to spatial puzzles, dexterous manipulation, multi-robot interaction, dynamic whole-body control, and sim-to-sim engineering. Each task can be run in up to three evaluation settings. The Standard Setting mimics the real world: the agent receives only sensor observations and, by default, cannot reset the environment. Its Realtime variant keeps the environment running while the agent reasons. The Privileged Setting gives the agent access to the full simulator state and unlimited resets, allowing it to search for an oracle solution for training purposes. Success rates in this setting serve as an approximate upper bound. On the 456 core tasks, the strongest of eight agents, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, achieves a success rate of 34% in the Standard Setting, leaving much room for improvement. To see where agents fail, we label the stage at which failed attempts go wrong and compare failures across agents, settings, and stage categories.

HandMimicX: Learning Human-Like Dexterous Manipulation via an Object-Centric Policy from Egocentric Videos 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Learning dexterous manipulation from human videos remains an open challenge, particularly in the context of transferring a single demonstration into a spatially generalizable robotic skill and maintaining physically plausible human-like interactions. Unlike prior works that emphasize hand-motion imitation, we introduce an object-centric framework for acquiring dexterous skills from egocentric human RGB-D videos. Within our framework, we formulate the reinforcement learning (RL) objective in terms of object motion to decouple the underlying skill from the specific hand trajectory and impose constraints on hand-object interaction states to promote more anatomically natural hand configurations. Extensive experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate that our method enables efficient learning from human videos and generalizable skill transfer across novel spatial configurations.

Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids (DASH): Latency-Agnostic Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models for Humanoid Manipulation 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models pair a vision-language backbone with an action head, but in closed-loop control the backbone sits on the control-critical path: while it computes, the robot keeps moving, so each executed action is conditioned on an increasingly stale observation. A larger backbone therefore directly blocks reactivity, especially in humanoid dexterous manipulation. We present DASH, a Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids that removes this coupling. DASH splits the policy into a slow vision-language model (System 2) and a fast flow-matching action head (System 1) that run as concurrent processes over the LCM communication protocol, so the control rate is set by the action head alone, independent of backbone size. The action head fuses stale semantics from a frozen backbone with fresh vision from its own ResNet encoder, desynchronized training exposes it to the staleness it meets at deployment, and an adaptive temporal aggregator aligns overlapping action chunks under variable inference latency. On an asynchronous RoboTwin benchmark that charges inference latency in simulated time, DASH holds 20 Hz control rate from 0.8B to 8B backbones while the synchronous rate falls from 9.7 to 4.1 Hz, with higher success at every size. On a real humanoid with dexterous hands, DASH achieves clearly higher pick-and-place success than the synchronous pipeline.

BAT-WAM: Bridging Action and Touch in World-Action Models for Dexterous Hands 2026-11-07
Show abstract

Dexterous manipulation with world action models (WAMs) relies on hand configuration and contact information, yet joint-state and whole-hand tactile observations may be partially or entirely missing. RGB images provide scene context and hand-object geometry but are insufficient to reliably recover these missing modalities. We further observe a correlation between hand state and tactile information. Motivated by this observation, we introduce BAT-WAM, a framework that bridges visual, joint-state, and tactile representations for WAMs. BAT-WAM learns separate codebooks and decoders for the tactile and action streams. An RGB-conditioned continuous latent bridge connects the two streams for bidirectional learning, while visual information serves as continuous context. Under the HTBench evaluation protocol, BAT-WAM reduces the root mean squared error (RMSE) of tactile reconstruction by 34.73% under fully masked conditions and 38.84% under partially masked conditions, relative to an image-conditioned HandTouch checkpoint. For future tactile prediction conditioned on target-time RGB and historical actions, it reduces mean RMSE by 85.56% relative to OpenWAM with HandTouch. Real-robot experiments further show that BAT-WAM consistently outperforms related WAM baselines in dexterous manipulation. Generalization experiments on unseen objects and scenes also demonstrate substantial improvements over baseline models. These findings support joint modeling of visual context, hand state, and touch as a representation approach for multimodal WAMs.

Which Taxels, Not How Many: Tactile Sensor Placement for Dexterous Policies 2026-11-07
📝 Accepted at the Workshop on Everything Beneath the Policy, CoRL 2026. 8 pages, 5 figures, 12 tables
Show abstract

Policies for dexterous manipulation depend on design choices made before training: which sensors enter the observation, where they are placed, and how their readings are arranged for the encoder. These choices are rarely examined. For tactile sensing, simulation studies place taxels uniformly over the hand, and this placement has become a default without being tested. We test this default under a single controlled protocol. The protocol holds the teacher, the training procedure, and the candidate taxels fixed and varies only the sensor configuration and the representation. We evaluate it on in-palm rotation and grasping with three robot hands. A small set of taxels selected from contact statistics matches a far larger uniform set. Taxels at rarely contacted sites are equivalent to no tactile input. With all candidate taxels, their layout in the input tensor does not affect performance, even for a convolutional encoder. Uniform placement therefore overestimates the number of taxels a task requires. We distill these results into lessons for designing tactile policies and into a minimal set of design choices that a tactile policy study should report.

SimDuoReal: Object-Centric Bimanual Policy for Emergent Dexterity 2026-11-07
📝 Website: https://simduoreal.github.io
Show abstract

Bimanual dexterous manipulation requires coordinated arm and hand control, yet specifying such coordination manually is challenging and often task-specific. In this work, we explore whether complex bimanual dexterity can emerge from a simple object-centric learning objective. We train reinforcement learning policies under a shared object-centric reward, varying their training environments rather than tuning separate rewards for each behavior. We find that the policies discover pre-grasp manipulation, grasping and regrasping, in-hand adjustment and handover to reach object-pose goals without hand-designed motion sequences. We then refine the experts on transition states and use DAgger and reinforcement learning to distill them into a single policy that composes these behaviors for diverse bimanual tasks. On real hardware, the same policy succeeds in 61 of 80 trials (76.3\%) across four tasks with unseen objects and no task-specific fine-tuning. In simulated barcode scanning, our policy achieves 85.0\% success, compared with 60.4\% for two single-arm policies without handover. These results suggest that this objective provides a simple and scalable interface for learning general bimanual dexterity, shifting the burden from manually designing robot coordination to specifying what should happen to the object.

GUARD: Anomaly-Informed Q-Steering for Generative Robot Policies in Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-07
📝 8 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Generative robot policies based on diffusion and flow matching can model complex, multimodal behaviors, but remain susceptible to covariate shift during closed-loop execution. Existing approaches mitigate such failures through additional demonstrations, policy fine-tuning, or test-time guidance, but often require human intervention, engineered rewards, or other task-specific signals. We introduce GUARD, an anomaly-informed Q-steering framework that leverages the policy's training distribution to construct a dense, failure-anticipatory steering signal. GUARD pairs sparse task rewards with dense anomaly scores learned from policy training data and propagates both through a learned Q-function. Its gradients then steer the denoising process toward actions that improve task return while preventing trajectories from entering poorly supported regions, without directly modifying the pretrained policy. Across four Adroit dexterous manipulation tasks, GUARD achieves average success rates of $96.0\%$ and $80.0\%$ under nominal and perturbed dynamics, outperforming the strongest baseline by $21.0$ and $20.0$ percentage points while maintaining the lowest anomaly score. GUARD further demonstrates direct sim-to-real transfer on in-hand screwdriver rotation and reorientation tasks, achieving $40$ and $25$ percentage-point gains in success rate. These results demonstrate that anomaly signals can serve not only for failure monitoring but also to complement sparse task rewards as effective supervision for robust policy steering.

NoContactNoWorries: Estimating Contact through Vision and Proprioception for In-Hand Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-06
📝 Accepted to IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems(IROS) 2026
Show abstract

Perceiving physical contact is fundamental to dexterous manipulation. While robots often rely on dedicated hardware tactile sensors, humans exhibit a remarkable ability to infer contact by integrating visual information with an innate sense of their body's pose and movement. Inspired by this embodied perceptual skill, we investigate whether a robot can learn to infer contact from vision, an approach that also offers a scalable alternative to tactile hardware specifically for binary contact estimation, which faces practical challenges in cost, fragility, and integration. We present NoContactNoWorries, a transformer-based multimodal framework that fuses RGB-D vision with the robot's proprioception to infer binary contact states as a pseudo-tactile signal for hand-object interactions. DexHOT: Learning Hand-Object Configuration Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-06
📝 9 pages, 4 figures
Show abstract

Dexterous manipulation involves not only moving objects, but also arranging the hand around them to maintain useful contacts and enable subsequent actions. Yet many learning-based systems specify goals primarily through object poses or trajectories, leaving hand-object configurations to emerge implicitly. We introduce DexHOT, which formulates dexterous manipulation as hand-object configuration transfer: moving between feasible configurations that jointly specify hand articulation and object pose relative to the wrist. DexHOT learns a reusable relation-conditioned transfer primitive from synthetic feasible configuration pairs, without human reference motions, and composes long-horizon behaviors through closed-loop tracking of configuration subgoals. On DexHOTBench with 120 objects, a single policy achieves 80.5% transfer success on seen objects and 69.4% on unseen objects; on a real dexterous hand, it achieves 61.5% success over 200 real-robot transfers and 90% task success across six demonstration-guided tasks. These results show that explicit hand-object goals provide an expressive and composable interface for dexterous manipulation. More details and videos are available at https://dex-hot.github.io/.

Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-05
Show abstract

Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging because policies must acquire both perceptual grounding and precise control, while reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation can acquire rich control behaviors from large-scale interaction with privileged information. We introduce \textbf{Latent RL Prior}, a two-stage framework that reuses this control knowledge for data-efficient visual policy learning. During privileged pretraining, we distill large-scale RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context. During visual adaptation, we freeze the decoder and learn a visual interface that maps images to its latent context using action supervision alone, without privileged labels or latent targets. The same decoder can further be shared across arms by learning coordinated visual contexts for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated tasks, our method outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime; on door lifting and bimanual assembly, it surpasses full-data baselines using $10\times$ and $5\times$ fewer demonstrations, respectively. Further analysis shows that direct visual-to-latent conditioning improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing visual predictions through the pretrained state encoder. Robustness to unseen mass variation and greater linear accessibility of contact-force information in decoder representations further provide evidence of retained RL-acquired control knowledge. DexTeleop-0: Force-Aware Bimanual Dexterous Teleoperation with Ego-Centric Perception towards Shared Autonomy 2026-11-05
📝 16 pages, 9 figures, 6 tables
Show abstract

Fine-grained, bimanual dexterous manipulation remains a foundational challenge in robotics. Embodiment gaps hinder accurate kinematic mapping, and visual tracking alone does not regulate contact forces. To address these limitations, we propose a tactile-driven adaptation strategy designed to enable fine-grained manipulation on top of teleoperation pipelines. Instantiated within our bimanual dexterous framework, DexTeleop-0, this strategy introduces a real-time optimization loop that adds robot-side contact corrections to human tracking commands using fingertip feedback. By estimating contact positions and forces, the system dynamically computes localized corrections using a contact-stiffness approximation and the kinematic Jacobian, with per-contact vector force tracking, soft predicted-contact-moment regularization, and bounded hand-joint residuals. We evaluate this tactile-driven adaptation strategy across both simulated environments and real-world hardware. Controlled simulation replay yields final-stage success rates of 91% for Ball Assembly and 62% for Stir in Cup, versus 88% and 49% for force tracking alone. Fixed-input replay on two hand types, seven-operator teleoperation, and external-force traces further assess contact assistance. The open-sourced project page can be found at https://henryhcliu.github.io/dexteleop-0.

Evo-DexBench: Benchmarking Generalization across Related Tasks for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-05
📝 v2: Extended experiments to all 30 tasks in six families with an added QwenOFT baseline (best average SR updated to 24.4%); added a target-data efficiency study; extended tactile ablation to all 30 tasks; updated abstract, figures, and appendix
Show abstract

General-purpose dexterous manipulation requires policies to transfer knowledge from related tasks to new tasks with only a few target demonstrations. Existing benchmarks often provide too many target demonstrations and leave the differences between source and target tasks uncontrolled. As a result, they cannot clearly distinguish knowledge transfer from task memorization. Our key insight is the Core-Gen principle. Core tasks are related tasks that share a functional interaction and provide source knowledge, while a Gen task is a novel target within the same functional family that has only a few demonstrations. This setup tests whether a policy can reuse functional interaction knowledge. Based on this principle, we propose Evo-DexBench, which controls both source-task relatedness and target-task exposure. It comprises 30 tasks in six functional families, three everyday scenes per task, and 1,248 multimodal demonstrations collected via VR teleoperation with simulated Shadow Hands equipped with 123 taxels. Experiments show that, among six representative policies, even the best-performing method achieves an average success rate of only 24.4%, highlighting the challenges existing methods face in transferring functional interaction knowledge. Further analysis shows that, across the two tasks, joint Core + Gen training improves performance by 12% and 38%, respectively, over training on Core followed by fine-tuning on Gen, indicating that joint training makes better use of Core knowledge. With matched Core data, 8 Gen demonstrations achieve performance comparable to training on 16--32 Gen demonstrations alone. On all three tested Gen targets, functionally matched Core data yields greater gains in success rate than mismatched data. We release the Evo-DexBench dataset and benchmark to support the evaluation of dexterous manipulation policies' generalization across related tasks.

Vortex-Induced Torque in Grasping Soft Objects: Influence of Geometry and Pneumatic Parameters 2026-11-04
📝 Accepted for the SII
Show abstract

Vortex grippers are commonly integrated into automated systems for handling objects with complex geometries in packaging, sorting, and assembling. However, their interaction with compliant objects remains difficult to predict, particularly with respect to the vortex-induced torque transmitted during grasping. This study investigates vortex-induced torque during the grasping of compliant objects and quantifies the coupled effects of supply pressure, tangential nozzle diameter, surface geometry, and curvature. The results show that increasing the tangential nozzle diameter primarily enhances the sensitivity of the generated torque to supply pressure rather than producing a uniformly higher torque. Surface geometry also strongly influences the rotational interaction, with dome-concave surfaces generally producing substantially greater torque than corresponding dome-convex surfaces, whereas the difference between cylindrical concave and convex surfaces is comparatively small. The strongest and most repeatable response was observed for a dome-concave surface exhibiting favorable object-gripper geometric matching. Among the evaluated machine-learning regression methods, Gaussian Process Regression provided the best overall predictive performance under repeated cross-validation, demonstrating that vortex-induced torque can be estimated from combined pneumatic and geometric parameters. These findings provide a basis for predicting and utilizing vortex-induced torque in the dexterous manipulation of soft objects.

Bridging the Morphology Gap: Adapting VLA Models to Dexterous Manipulation via Intent-Conditioned Fine-Tuning 2026-11-04
Show abstract

Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models offer semantic and spatial priors for robot manipulation, but their parallel-gripper interfaces leave multi-finger contact coordination implicit. Adapting these models requires both timely grasp transitions and embodiment-specific hand trajectories. We introduce InDex, an intent-conditioned framework that connects these two requirements. A normalized aperture signal, derived from retargeted demonstrations, supervises a scalar grasp intent while preserving full hand-action supervision. Stage 1 learns synchronized end-effector-intent chunks; Stage 2 conditions a diffusion hand decoder on these predictions, visual-language context, and proprioception. Sequential optimization establishes the upstream interface before learning dexterous control with low-rate co-adaptation. Component ablations and timing interventions support the value of explicit grasp-intent conditioning and expressive hand generation. These results identify arm-hand coordination as an actionable bottleneck in adapting parallel-gripper priors to dexterous manipulation.

Tactus: Tactile & Pose Sensing Glove for Natural Dexterity Data Collection 2026-11-04
📝 9 pages
Show abstract

Collecting naturalistic human manipulation demonstrations with accurate contact force and hand pose is essential for robot dexterous manipulation policy and foundational world model learning - yet no existing wearable platform provides both calibrated fingertip force sensing and occlusion-resilient hand tracking in a form factor that preserves natural manipulation behavior. We present Tactus, a textile-integrated sensing glove that co-optimizes three design pillars: (1) a thin, conformal 3D-knit form factor (1.5 mm fingertip stackup) designed to minimize interference with natural manipulation, (2) strategically placed capacitive tactile sensors at manipulation-relevant regions informed by empirical contact studies, and (3) IMU-fused pose tracking that maintains accuracy through the hand-object occlusions where manipulation data is most valuable. Tactus includes a documented calibration pipeline for cross-glove consistency and per-session calibration for subject-dependent correction. We show that these combined signals support object property estimation, slip detection, and representative recording of hand-object interaction - yielding demonstration data with the fidelity required for contact-aware manipulation policies and world models.

GRASP: Gradient-Guided Sampling for Contact-Rich Physics-Based Motion Retargeting 2026-11-03
📝 26 pages, including references and appendices. Project website: https://computationalrobotics.seas.harvard.edu/GRASP/
Show abstract

We study physics-based retargeting of human demonstrations for robot learning: finding robot controls that reproduce reference motions under contact dynamics. We propose GRASP (Gradient Recovery from Archived Samples for Proposals), which combines sampling-based exploration with gradient-guided refinement without requiring a differentiable simulator. GRASP-TO extends tempered sequential Monte Carlo by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts and using their gradients to guide Markov chain Monte Carlo rejuvenation. Metropolis-Hastings correction preserves the target distribution for each fixed proposal kernel, and a population analysis connects the recovered gradients to Gaussian smoothing. General-purpose manipulation requires reliable behavior across embodiments, objects, and task demands. We introduce HandsOn, a composable simulation platform and benchmark for cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation. It supports five robotic arms, six dexterous hands, and approximately 200 rigid, articulated, and deformable assets in randomized scenes. The benchmark pairs 18,000 curated human demonstrations with 66 human-verified tasks: Core-50 supports demonstration-based learning and execution tests, while Transfer-16 evaluates asset and relation transfer, skill composition, and deformable manipulation without target-task training demonstrations. Qualitative real-robot rollouts illustrate sim-to-real transfer. Across diffusion, vision-language-action, world-action, and prompted VLM-based methods, the best trained multi-task policy achieves 29.1% Core-50 success. Without benchmark finetuning, GPT-6 using move_eef for palm-and-finger control achieves 5.2% on Core-50 and 1.9% on Transfer-16 over 660 trials. Trained-policy stage diagnostics expose failures in contact establishment, action coordination, and maintaining achieved goals, with additional sequencing errors in composed tasks. HandsOn provides a shared basis for investigating manipulation generalization and the limits of reliable physical interaction.

Gen2Dex: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Simulation-Initialized Generated Video Demonstrations 2026-10-17
Show abstract

Human video demonstrations provide rich manipulation strategies for dexterous manipulation learning. We study a setting in which a fully specified simulated scene is used to generate videos that specify the demonstrated manipulation behavior. This setup provides access to scene information such as object geometry, articulation, and camera parameters, while allowing direct control over task and scene configuration. However, generated videos may contain geometric distortions, inaccurate contacts, and other physical inconsistencies, making the recovered motion difficult to execute directly on a robot. We present an end-to-end framework for converting such simulation-grounded videos into physically executable dexterous robot demonstrations. Our perception pipeline leverages the known scene through model-based object tracking with dense foundation-model features and depth-based hand-object alignment. The recovered human motion is then retargeted and physically grounded using residual reinforcement learning, where a backward curriculum and a cache of physically valid reset states enable the policy to correct imperfect video-derived references while preserving the demonstrated manipulation strategy. On clean ARCTIC demonstrations, our method achieves competitive performance with prior dexterous retargeting approaches and shows greater robustness when applied to references extracted from generated videos. We further demonstrate diverse rigid and articulated manipulation tasks in simulation and successful open-loop transfer of the optimized trajectories to a real dexterous robot.

Achieving human-level dexterity in complex, unstructured environments requires the seamless integration of whole-body scene interaction and dexterous object manipulation skills. While existing physics-based controllers generate physically plausible behaviors in each domain, they largely address these two capabilities independently. In this paper, we present Co${}^{2}$Skill that integrates scene interaction and dexterous manipulation through a unified policy formulation. Built on a pretrained motion prior, the policy uses task and phase dependent observation masks to select information relevant to the current interaction goals. We introduce a goal-conditioned loco-manipulation curriculum that combines partial reference guidance for precision with exploration from varied initial states while allowing goal-directed execution beyond the demonstrated trajectories. We further introduce a cross-task curriculum that jointly trains individual skills and selected task sequences, preserving physical states across task boundaries and maintaining grasps during subsequent scene interactions. Together, these support sequential task execution and simultaneous scene interaction with object manipulation. We evaluate sitting, standing, climbing, stair traversal, and goal-directed manipulation, together with sequential execution and with random different conditions. Additionally, we demonstrate skill compositions in indoor environments, illustrating their integration within the same control formulation.

Automating biological laboratories requires robots to perform dexterous tool interactions under realistic visual conditions while handling dynamic experimental phenomena, presenting significant challenges for robotic manipulation. To enable systematic evaluation under these conditions, we introduce DexBioLab, a simulation benchmark and framework for dexterous manipulation in realistic biological laboratory environments. DexBioLab comprises 14 tasks across 4 robot embodiments, spanning functional tool use, precision manipulation, language-conditioned visual grounding, and instrument operation. We introduce controllable dynamic experimental phenomena, including flames, vapor, and bubbles, to capture the evolving visual conditions of laboratory procedures. To further enhance environmental realism, we develop a hybrid rendering pipeline that integrates interactive simulated foreground objects with Gaussian-splatting backgrounds reconstructed from 6 real laboratory scenes. For efficient large-scale data generation and evaluation, we further build a parallel simulation framework on top of mjlab, proposing Parallel Finite-State Machine (PFSM) and integrating batched rendering with decoupled trajectory replay for demonstration generation and model-independent closed-loop policy evaluation. Our evaluation reveals substantial variation in policy performance across tasks, exposing distinct strengths and weaknesses of current manipulation policies. Real-world experiments further demonstrate a reduced sim-to-real gap, supporting the validity of AutoBio+ for realistic manipulation evaluation. Touch is fundamental to dexterous manipulation, yet most egocentric human data increasingly used for robot learning lacks tactile information. Directly collecting large-scale tactile data is challenging due to sensor limitations, while human video data is abundant, contact-rich, and easily scalable. This motivates a natural question: can tactile signals be inferred purely from vision? To address this, we introduce EgoTac, a generalizable model that predicts rich tactile information directly from egocentric human videos. EgoTac is trained on a unified corpus of over 5.7M image-tactile pairs, covering both continuous force measurements and binary contacts. By learning from this diverse dataset, EgoTac captures nuanced touch dynamics across varied interactions. Experiments demonstrate strong performance: in-domain prediction achieves an average force error below 0.06N. On out-of-domain contact prediction benchmarks, EgoTac consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art contact estimator. It also captures the rise and fall patterns of real tactile data and enables zero-shot predictions on unconstrained real-world videos. Scaling analyses further reveal that both data diversity and volume improve performance steadily. Overall, EgoTac provides a scalable pathway to extract tactile priors from egocentric human videos, enabling broadly applicable tactile-aware robot learning. Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs), such as wires and cables, are central to industrial assembly. Unlike rigid objects, whose state is captured by a 6-DoF pose, DLOs have an infinite-dimensional configuration space and deform continuously under contact with grippers, fixtures, and the workspace, making them a demanding benchmark for general dexterous manipulation. Despite their importance, policy development and comparison remain difficult: existing benchmarks are often tied to specific hardware setups, lack modular and customizable task assets, or study generic deformable-object tasks without the fixtures relevant to real-world industrial wire manipulation. Few benchmarks align simulation, real-world data, and shared evaluation protocols. To bridge this gap, we introduce WireCraft, a simulation benchmark for industrial DLO manipulation with configurable difficulty and assets, spanning three task families: connector insertion, clip routing, and channel seating. It supports two complementary DLO physics models, articulated and deformable, and the released trajectories come from both simulation and a physical UR5. We benchmark reinforcement learning (RL), imitation learning (IL), and vision-language-action (VLA) policies under shared metrics. Privileged state-based RL solves a representative setting in each task family with over 82% success, confirming the tasks are well-posed. Image-based policies remain less successful across the evaluated tasks, with ACT and DiT VLA falling below privileged State PPO. On a physical UR5, supplementing real demonstrations with simulated data improves success by 15-65 percentage points in the best-performing mixture for each task. These results indicate that industrial DLO manipulation, though tractable under privileged state, remains an open challenge for current vision-based learning.

Generalist robot policies benefit from large-scale visual pretraining but often struggle with contact-rich dexterous manipulation, where force, compliance, and incipient slip are hard to observe from images. We present Spark-0, a spatially aligned vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) model that uses touch in two complementary ways. During pretraining, image-space contact maps derived from tactile gloves, together with features from a frozen 3D geometry teacher, supervise the visual tokens; these objectives are removed at inference. In parallel, a tactile pathway encodes regional force histories and 3D taxel states to condition the action expert. A single checkpoint therefore supports both vision-only and tactile-conditioned deployment. To scale this supervision, we build HumanTouch, a 1,000-hour dataset of full-hand tactile human manipulation, and pretrain Spark-0 on it together with egocentric video, robot data, and simulation. We also release SparkArena, which pairs vision-only simulated tasks with tactile-conditioned real-robot tasks on one dexterous embodiment. Spark-0 achieves the highest mean success rate on both SparkArena tracks and ranks first on all eight aggregate metrics of the Ego Humanoid Manipulation Benchmark. Compared with vision-only pretraining on the same data and compute with touch discarded, retaining the touch of 1,000 hours of HumanTouch raises vision-only success on Ego Humanoid from 52.5% to 71.7%, and success on SparkArena-Real, where touch is also used at deployment, from 44.0% to 52.0%, indicating that touch serves not only as a policy input but also as scalable supervision for dexterous visuomotor pretraining. Whole-Body Dexterity 2026-10-15
📝 20 pages, 13 figures
Show abstract

Current humanoid dexterous manipulation remains largely confined to end-effectors and simple grasps, falling short of humans' ability to engage the entire body by bracing and sliding bulky objects against the arms and torso. We introduce Whole-Body Dexterity (WBD), a general framework that uses the robot's body and appendages as contact surfaces to grasp and manipulate objects. Our sim-to-real pipeline first generates a dataset of feasible whole-body grasps, then trains reinforcement-learning experts on a general any-to-any repositioning objective by sampling initial and goal grasps from the dataset, and distills these experts into a single policy that uses proprioception and object geometry. We show that the distilled policy transfers zero-shot to a real Unitree G1, where it repositions a basketball and a cardboard box to regions around the torso and completes a long-horizon task that stows a box under one arm, carries it from a shelf to a table, and places it. The controller operates interactively, steered by coarse motion references from a human teleoperator. Across real-world whole-body manipulation tasks, the distilled controller completes 90% of trials, compared with 18% for direct retargeting and 4% for a generalist whole-body controller.

Achieving human-like dexterous manipulation remains a central challenge in robotics. Learning from human hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets offers a promising pathway to diverse dexterous skills, yet existing physics-based approaches train specialist policies for individual trajectories, thereby limiting skill reuse at scale. We introduce DexMimic, a generalist dexterous hand-object tracking policy jointly trained across diverse HOI trajectories. Efficient joint training requires allocating each rollout to both an undertrained phase and a starting state that supports continued tracking. DexMimic realizes this insight through Value-Guided Reset Allocation, which adapts the initialization distribution online to prioritize difficult phases and select reset states with higher expected tracking returns. We further introduce a benchmark spanning training, fine-tuning, and generalization. Experiments show improved tracking success and training efficiency, faster fine-tuning, and the potential for zero-shot generalization to unseen trajectories and objects; larger training sets further improve efficiency and generalization. Finally, we validate DexMimic on a real-world dexterous manipulation platform.

EgoJEPA: Beyond Retargeting for Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Videos 2026-10-14
Show abstract

Egocentric human videos offer a scalable alternative to costly robot demonstra- tions, yet transferring their manipulation priors to robot control remains chal- lenging. The commonly adopted retargeting-based approaches convert human wrist and finger poses into embodiment-specific action commands through hand- crafted geometric mappings. However, such mappings require meticulous recal- ibration across different embodiments and risk propagating hand-tracking errors into downstream robot control pipelines. To this end, we propose EgoJEPA, a three-stage framework that learns embodiment-agnostic representations from ego- centric human videos without rule-based human-to-robot retargeting: 1) Human- action pre-training learns effect-aware latent actions through JEPA-style visual prediction conditioned on hand-motion differences; 2) Latent-action-based world modeling jointly predicts future visual dynamics and latent actions, which decou- ples human prior pre-training from robot-specific control parameterizations; 3) Embodiment-specific tuning adapts the pretrained model to diverse robots through lightweight action adapters. Experiments on five real-world manipulation tasks across three robot embodiments demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of EgoJEPA. It achieves a 74% average success rate under the out-of-domain evalu- ation, compared with 65% for the retargeting baseline, while reducing cumulative cross-embodiment wall-clock cost by 47.2%, from 743.2 to 392.7 hours.

VICON: Visual-Inertial-Contact based Hand-Object Tracking for Manipulation Datasets 2026-10-14
📝 9 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Learning dexterous manipulation benefits from human demonstration datasets that capture diverse and natural hand-object interactions. In particular, contact points and forces provide supervision on where and how strongly to interact, which cannot be fully captured by motion trajectories alone. However, methods for jointly capturing hand and object motion, contact points, and forces remain limited. Moreover, severe occlusion from hand-object interaction challenges accurate tracking of both hands and objects. To address these limitations, we present a Visual-Inertial-CONtact based hand-object tracking (VICON) framework. It holistically captures both hand and object motion along with contact information during manipulation, even under severe occlusion. First, we adopt a visual-inertial glove and an RGB-D camera for accurate hand tracking, and redesign the glove to incorporate contact sensing. Specifically, force-sensitive resistors (FSRs) are placed on the glove based on human grasp frequency to synchronously record contact states and calibrated normal forces. Second, without requiring pre-existing CAD models, we estimate object poses using RGB-D images and a mesh reconstructed from a monocular video. We propose factor-graph-based object trajectory estimation that fuses object-pose estimates weighted by visibility under hand-object occlusion, FSR measurements, and a hand-motion prior. Across 40 motion-capture sessions with five objects, VICON achieves a 2.5% failed-frame rate compared with 50.9-64.6% for the baselines, with median errors of 3.9 mm and 3.0 degrees under occlusion. Using VICON, we construct a dataset containing synchronized hand-object motion, contact points, and normal forces, and will publicly release an expanded dataset covering 10 object categories at https://github.com/VICON-dataset/dataset.
