PyRUA-Dex: Learning Programmatic Policies for Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation with Coding Agents 2026-11-24
📝 23 pages, 9 figures, 7 tables. Project page: https://vme0522.github.io/PyRUA-Dex/
Show abstract

Learning for dexterous manipulation is limited by the scarcity of high-quality demonstrations: retargeting from human video is imprecise, teleoperation gives no force feedback to the operator, and exoskeleton-based collection is hard to scale up. We present PyRUA-Dex, a framework that enables coding agents to develop dexterous manipulation through programmatic policy, without the need of demonstrations. The programmatic policy is developed in two stages: during exploration, multiple agents explore in parallel for task skills and ways to compose them, and a distilling agent condenses the successful attempts into a task library and one program. During refinement, this program is improved through randomized layouts for enhanced robustness and execution efficiency. In simulated environments, PyRUA-Dex achieves SOTA on Bench2Dex with a score of 72.9 and reaches 81.0% success on DexJoCo under full randomization, ahead of every trained policy on both benchmarks. In real world experiments on a bimanual platform with two 22-DoF hands, PyRUA-Dex learns to play piano, write calligraphy, and other tasks efficiently: learning a new piece from sheet music takes as little as 30 minutes. These results suggest that PyRUA-Dex is an effective framework for developing dexterous manipulation policies with coding agents in the absence of high quality dexterous manipulation demonstrations.

DiffAnchor: Anchor-Space Diffusion for Monocular 3D Hand Pose Estimation 2026-11-24
📝 10 pages. Accepted to the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Embodied AI
Show abstract

Accurate and efficient monocular 3D hand pose estimation is an important perception capability for embodied human interaction and dexterous manipulation. Recent diffusion-based methods model pose uncertainty by iteratively denoising 3D joint coordinates. However, directly diffusing the final joints requires the generative process to simultaneously explore plausible poses and recover precise coordinates, often relying on additional denoising steps or multiple hypotheses and consequently increasing inference cost. In this work, we propose DiffAnchor, a few-step anchor-space diffusion framework that diffuses anchors rather than joints. Our key insight is that stochastic exploration does not need to operate directly on the final output. Instead, diffusion searches for informative intermediate anchors that serve as local 3D regressors, while anchor-to-joint offset prediction and weighted aggregation complete precise joint localization. Specifically, fixed context anchors provide stable global references, whereas noisy dynamic anchors are iteratively refined toward informative joint regions. An importance-guided anchor selection and replenishment mechanism further retains useful anchors, removes ineffective ones, and explores insufficiently covered regions. This design reduces the precision burden of the diffusion process and enables accurate prediction using only a few denoising steps and a single hypothesis. Extensive experiments on HO-3D V2, NYU, ICVL, and InterHand2.6M demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on the first three benchmarks and competitive performance on challenging interacting-hand estimation, while maintaining inference efficiency comparable to deterministic regression baselines.

Ego2Real: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Visuo-Tactile Demonstrations 2026-11-24
📝 19 pages, 9 figures
Show abstract

Learning dexterous manipulation from egocentric human demonstrations offers a scalable path toward robot skill acquisition. Vision alone, however, provides limited information about physical contacts and the demonstrator's force intent, leading to ambiguities in interpreting hand-object interactions. Capturing human touch via tactile sensing is therefore crucial for reliable human-to-robot dexterous skill transfer. We present Ego2Real, a framework that leverages egocentric demonstrations and tactile gloves to transfer bimanual dexterous manipulation skills to robotic hands. Ego2Real incorporates tactile supervision at two complementary stages: (1) tactile-guided hand-object interaction reconstruction, which uses measured tactile signals to refine hand poses and contact geometry; and (2) contact-informed reinforcement learning, which translates human contact patterns into robotic contact rewards. By combining refined motion references with physical contact rewards during training, Ego2Real guides anthropomorphic robotic hands to emulate demonstrated contact behaviors while adapting to their own morphology and actuation. Experiments show that incorporating tactile supervision into both reconstruction and policy learning yields higher manipulation success rates, underscoring the importance of enriching egocentric demonstrations with tactile observations to improve their quality and transferability.

Many Senses, One World: Learning Hierarchical Multimodal World Representations for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Bimanual dexterous manipulation relies on multimodal inputs that capture visual context and non-visual physical interaction cues for precise and coordinated control. To address the challenge of effectively integrating these modalities for policy learning, we propose HiMoDex, a two-stage framework centered on hierarchical multimodal world-state representations. In Stage 1, we pretrain a hierarchical representation network on the collected dataset of 1.19 million synchronized multimodal time steps. The network integrates visual context with tactile, force-torque, and audio cues into unified world-state embeddings. In Stage 2, we freeze the pretrained network and use its embeddings to condition a shared world-action transformer. The transformer jointly learns action generation and action-conditioned future-state prediction. Experiments across five real-world tasks show that HiMoDex achieves an average success rate of 78.7\%, representing an absolute improvement of 32.0\% over the strongest evaluated baseline. Extensive ablations validate the benefits of hierarchical representation learning and two-stage training. Failure analysis helps explain performance differences across variants. Code, data, and demos will be released at https://anonymous.4open.science/w/research-website-BAB4/.

OVOGO: Offline Value-Guided On-Policy Generative Optimization for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Flow-based vision-language-action models can benefit from robot experience collected during deployment, but repeatedly acquiring new physical interactions for policy improvement is costly. Offline reinforcement learning provides a natural way to reuse collected experience, while flow-based policies offer an additional opportunity because fresh generation trajectories can be cheaply resampled from the current policy at replay observations. This motivates separating offline value learning from on-policy optimization of the generative process. We propose OVOGO, an offline reinforcement learning framework that realizes this separation for flow-based VLAs. A central challenge in this formulation is to learn a critic that remains grounded in logged robot experience while providing useful value signals for newly generated actions. OVOGO introduces a Return-Anchored Ranking Critic that combines in-sample distributional value learning with guarded local ranking, improving discrimination among generated actions without assigning them artificial return targets. The frozen critic then provides group-relative value signals for selected-transition optimization of fresh action candidates sampled from the current flow policy. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, DexJoCo, and LIBERO, as well as a real-robot dexterous manipulation task, show that OVOGO improves the initial VLA across all four settings and achieves higher success rates than the evaluated offline adaptation and value-guided baselines. These results suggest that offline robot experience can be repeatedly reused for value learning while inexpensive generative sampling supports policy optimization, providing an effective approach to experience-based post-training of flow-based VLAs.

YUBI: Yielding Universal Bidigital Interface for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation at Scale 2026-11-23
📝 Accepted to CoRL 2026. Project page: https://yubi.airoa.io/

Show abstract

We introduce Yielding Universal Bidigital Interface (YUBI), a finger-aligned gripper designed to enable intuitive, ergonomic, and scalable data collection for bimanual dexterous manipulation. While handheld data collection systems such as Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) enable affordable data collection, their bulky pistol-grip designs can pose ergonomic and usability challenges for fine-grained, dexterous manipulation tasks. To address this, YUBI presents a distinct design principle: yielding, finger-driven actuation that directly maps human finger movements to gripper jaw motion. Using the YUBI devices, we set up a data collection system with integrated VR-based 6 DoF tracking of the gripper, ensuring high-fidelity trajectory data acquisition. We curate a UMI-based dataset of unprecedented scale: 8,434 hours across 1.20M episodes and 119 tasks. Experiments show that YUBI offers advantages over the UMI gripper in versatility for complex bimanual tasks, dexterity, and operational efficiency. A single policy trained on the YUBI dataset transfers across multiple bimanual robots (UR, Franka, and ELEY) simply by mounting the gripper on each platform, confirming that the collected data are directly executable as policy supervision. We release the gripper hardware, data-collection software, and dataset as one integrated stack, offering the open community a reproducible path to large-scale data acquisition for advancing robotic foundation models.

CHAMP: Coordinated Humanoid Loco-Manipulation with Whole-Body Motion Priors 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Coordinating locomotion and dexterous manipulation is fundamental to humanoid robots operating in human environments. A key challenge is learning motion priors from diverse data and using them effectively for coordinated robot control. We present CHAMP, a unified framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that combines whole-body motion pretraining with adaptive feature extraction. CHAMP pretrains a vision-language model to predict future body and hand motion from approximately 2,298 hours of heterogeneous human and robot data. A shared motion representation enables unified whole-body motion learning across embodiments from datasets with body annotations, hand annotations, or both. To ground these motion priors in robot control, we introduce a Coordination-oriented Action Expert (CAE) and train it on replay data generated using the target controller. Through sparse routing, CAE adaptively selects, transforms, and fuses features from multiple layers of the pretrained model to jointly generate body and hand actions. In experiments, CHAMP achieves overall success rates of 84.8% on seven simulated tasks and 91.4% on seven real-world tasks with a Unitree G1, outperforming the evaluated baselines in both settings. In particular, CHAMP achieves 70.0% success on the long-horizon popcorn-serving task, compared with 40.0% for the best-performing baseline.

Skill-Adaptive Noise Scheduling for Diffusion Policies 2026-11-23
📝 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)
Show abstract

Recent advances in diffusion policies have demonstrated strong effectiveness when integrated into hierarchical skill-based learning frameworks, leveraging offline data to capture multimodal and temporally abstracted behaviors. However, prior approaches have rarely considered the diffusion process as a learnable component, adopting fixed noise schedules applied uniformly across skills, regardless of their distinct behavioral contexts. Such a design fundamentally limits the achievable performance, particularly for contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks where different skills exhibit distinct couplings between action dimensions. In this paper, we present a skill-adaptive noise diffusion framework (SaND) which introduces a learnable, skill-conditioned noise schedule that modulates the diffusion process per action dimension, adapting the noising dynamics to each skill. To prevent the learned noise schedule from collapsing into trivial solutions, we employ an auxiliary inverse objective that reconstructs the skill embedding from the Legendre coefficients of the derivative of the noise schedule, encouraging the schedules to remain skill-discriminative. This formulation naturally enables adaptive control over the denoising steps for each skill, where noise schedules are clustered in the Legendre coefficient space, and the appropriate number of denoising steps is determined at the point where reconstruction error abruptly increases. At inference, we identify each skill embedding's cluster from its noise schedule and apply the corresponding number of denoising steps, dedicating more compute to skills requiring fine-grained control and less to simpler ones. In simulation and on real robot hardware, SaND outperforms existing skill learning and planning baselines on contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks, achieving higher success rates while requiring fewer denoising steps.

RoboColiseum: Co-evolution of Simulation, Evaluation, and Models 2026-11-23
📝 11 pages, 8 figures. The first eight authors contributed equally. Maoqing Yao and Qian Wang are the corresponding authors
Show abstract

Simulation-based evaluation of embodied models requires calibrated interactions, diverse tasks, and explicit execution constraints. We present RoboColiseum, a platform combining system identification, composable physics and rendering, and multidimensional benchmarking. Recorded-command replay characterizes robot dynamics and object interactions, while a shared interface connects rigid and deformable simulation to rendering. Across 22 paired real--simulation robot--task configurations, task scores have a reported Pearson correlation of 0.9686. Eight evaluation tracks span instruction following, spatial reasoning, general manipulation, robustness, dexterous manipulation, precision manipulation, whole-body coordination, and execution across robot embodiments. Budget-aware scoring combines task progress, resource use, and completion rewards. We report 12 selected Season~I policies and all 11 systems on the public Season~II leaderboard, comprising eight Agent systems and three VLA policies. Agent2Act connects language models to robot-control execution; the VLA study uses mixed-task supervised fine-tuning. The results reveal substantial variation across tracks within each evaluation category. RoboColiseum connects simulation development with capability-oriented evaluation and provides a foundation for extending robot interactions and model assessment.

TipDex-VTLA: Learning to Refine Actions with Visual Reliability and Tactile Feedback for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-22
📝 10 pages, 5 figures. Title updated; main text and results unchanged
Show abstract

Changes in illumination can induce action drift in dexterous manipulation even when the physical state remains unchanged. Although existing vision--tactile--language--action (VTLA) policies incorporate tactile feedback, when and how touch refines actions under changing visual conditions remains challenging. We introduce \textbf{TipDex-VTLA}, a VTLA policy that combines estimated visual action reliability and tactile forecast verification to regulate execution-time refinement. Specifically, Visual Action Sensitivity Estimation (VASE) learns visual reliability from a frozen reference policy's action sensitivity to illumination perturbations. Tactile Anticipation and Correction during Execution (TACE) compares predicted and measured touch at matched times and combines the resulting discrepancy with visual reliability to gate bounded feature corrections for unexecuted actions. This effectively helps to refine actions under challenging unseen visual conditions. We evaluate TipDex-VTLA on four real-world dexterous manipulation tasks and the UniVTAC simulation benchmark. TipDex-VTLA trained on data collected under normal visual conditions, achieves average full-task success rates of 87.5% (resp. 73.8%) under normal (resp. challenging unseen) lighting in real-world tasks, significantly outperforming some state-of-the-art methods. We will release the collected data and source code.

SiMDex: Mining Similar Egocentric Videos for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-20
📝 38 pages, 12 figures, 16 tables. Project page: https://lin-nie.github.io/SiMDex/
Show abstract

Recent years have witnessed an explosive trend of scaling ego-centric human videos for robot manipulation, yet it remains unclear which data actually benefits dexterous manipulation. We present SiMDex, a similarity-based data mining framework that casts human data selection for VLA post-training in dexterous manipulation as a recommendation problem. For each robot demonstration, SiMDex runs a recall-ranking-re-ranking cascade over ~32M egocentric human samples: two stages, recall and ranking, select a task-relevant subset in a morphology-agnostic action space, and re-ranking verifies it on pixels, with no change to VLA architecture or training. Against a strong baseline trained with an equal amount of randomly sampled human data, SiMDex uses only ~1.49M mined samples (<5% of the pool) yet improves the overall success rate from 47.7% to 61.7%, showing that selective curation outperforms indiscriminate data mixing.

Phoenix: Learning Scene Structure Representations for Generalizable World-Action Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet reconstruction-oriented visual representations do not explicitly emphasize the spatial relationships and object-state changes needed for manipulation. We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling. Specifically, Phoenix Encoder enriches DINOv3's general visual semantics through dual-teacher distillation of geometric and spatiotemporal semantic knowledge. Next, CS-VAE causally aggregates and compresses Phoenix Encoder features into generative latents while preserving scene information and spatial layout. Finally, Phoenix jointly predicts these latents, complementary appearance latents, and robot actions, linking scene evolution with action generation. To study pretraining data efficiency, we curate approximately 39,000 hours of egocentric and robot manipulation data for representation learning and use the robot demonstration subset for WAM pretraining. Pretraining-scale analysis shows larger performance gains from data scaling than a video-VAE-only baseline, indicating stronger scaling benefits and more effective data utilization. Moreover, Phoenix maintains high success rates when adapted with limited downstream demonstrations, demonstrating strong few-shot adaptation. Our code, pretraining data, and models are available on the project page to help the community reproduce our results.

Coherent Moment Propagation: Epistemic Uncertainty for Pre-trained Robot Policies 2026-11-19
📝 Webpage: https://comp-uq.github.io/comp-uq/
Show abstract

Flow matching policies underpin the state-of-the-art in robot learning, with impressive results ranging from long-horizon planning to dexterous manipulation. However, they provide no measure of whether their predicted commands can be trusted, particularly in novel situations outside their training set. Epistemic uncertainty reflects uncertainty about the model's weights and quantifies this ignorance, but calculating it at inference time from a flow matching policy is not trivial. Actions are produced by an ODE solver, requiring uncertainty to be propagated through the entire integration process. BayesDiff addresses this problem for diffusion models by propagating the mean and variance of the state through the denoising chain under a last-layer Laplace posterior approximation, but it severely underestimates the epistemic uncertainty. In this paper we: i) prove that, for flow matching and diffusion ODEs, BayesDiff's total variance decays at rate $O(1/T)$ as the number of Euler steps $T$ used by the solver increases; ii) introduce Coherent Moment Propagation (CoMP), two recursions inspired by the extended and unscented Kalman filters that fix this problem by carrying the cross-covariance between state and weights; and iii) widen the last-layer posterior with observation embeddings the policy already computes, improving uncertainty estimates without retraining for CoMP and several test-time estimators alike. We demonstrate these benefits on three manipulation benchmarks with applications in failure detection and demonstration selection for fine-tuning, where CoMP corrects BayesDiff's variance collapse while matching or outperforming several uncertainty estimators.

Evo-DexBench: Benchmarking Generalization across Related Tasks for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

General-purpose dexterous manipulation requires policies to transfer knowledge from related tasks to new tasks with only a few target demonstrations. Existing benchmarks often provide too many target demonstrations and leave the differences between source and target tasks uncontrolled. As a result, they cannot clearly distinguish knowledge transfer from task memorization. Our key insight is the Core-Gen principle. Core tasks are related tasks that share a functional interaction and provide source knowledge, while a Gen task is a novel target within the same functional family that has only a few demonstrations. This setup tests whether a policy can reuse functional interaction knowledge. Based on this principle, we propose Evo-DexBench, which controls both source-task relatedness and target-task exposure. It comprises 30 tasks in six functional families, three everyday layouts per task, and 1,248 multimodal demonstrations collected via VR teleoperation with simulated Shadow Hands equipped with 123 taxels. We first compare few-shot adaptation strategies and find that joint Core + Gen training makes the most effective use of Core knowledge. Under this setting, we evaluate six representative policies. The best-performing policy, $π_{0.5}$, achieves a mean success rate of only 24.4%, revealing the limitations of existing methods in transferring functional interaction knowledge. Further analysis shows that on the tested Gen targets, functionally matched Core data yields larger success-rate gains than mismatched data, and that with matched Core data, 8 Gen demonstrations reach the performance of training on 16--32 Gen demonstrations alone. These results indicate that Evo-DexBench separates knowledge transfer from task memorization under controlled conditions and effectively evaluates the generalization of dexterous manipulation policies across related tasks.

SHAPE: Discrete Supervision and Parallel Continuous Streams for Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Human Data 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://li-yaping.github.io/SHAPE/
Show abstract

Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. We address both in SHAPE (Supervision from Human Action tokens with Parallel Executable streams), a vision-language-action (VLA) model whose backbone predicts discrete action tokens of EEF chunks as an auxiliary task, while a shared flow-matching expert outputs two executable streams, EEF and joint. On a bimanual robot with 22-DoF hands, a matched study shows that discrete supervision helps learn from human data, driving backbone gradients and shaping action-informative representations across domains. Under standard control pipelines at deployment, direct joint readout excels at fine-grained control with robot demonstrations, scoring 0.80 across six tasks against 0.63 for π0.5, whereas IK-based EEF readout excels at human-to-robot transfer. Pretrained with human data and discrete tokens, SHAPE learns new tasks from human demonstrations without task-specific robot data.

3DTouch: A Tactile-aware VLA Model with 3D Geometric Relations for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-18
Show abstract

In contact-rich dexterous manipulation, occlusion by the fingers and interacting objects obscures local geometry, preventing visual observations from fully capturing contact locations. Visually similar scenes may also differ subtly in the relative positions of interacting objects, leading to different contact states. We propose 3DTouch, a tactile-geometric conditioning approach for vision-language-action (VLA) models. 3DTouch constructs a shared 3D visuo-tactile representation by expressing RGB-D scene points and dense five-finger tactile measurements in the robot-base frame, preserving each taxel's spatial location and force vector. Hand-object and object-object relations derived from hand landmarks and pose-aligned reconstructed surfaces explicitly describe the relative arrangement of interacting entities. Together, these representations condition the VLA action expert on spatially localized contact forces and relative surface geometry, providing explicit cues for contact adjustment and object alignment. We evaluate 3DTouch on five real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks using a UR7e robot arm equipped with an XHand dexterous hand. 3DTouch improves the average success rate by 42 percentage points over the corresponding VLA baseline and by 20 percentage points over the strongest evaluated alternative. Ablation studies reveal the complementary benefits of scene geometry and spatial tactile feedback, with further gains from shared spatial grounding and explicit geometric relations.

Finetuning VLAs for Dexterous Manipulation: The Right Checkpoint Is All You Need 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted to the Workshop on Dexterous Manipulation at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action models have demonstrated strong manipulation capabilities. A limitation of VLA models is that most manipulation datasets focus on parallel-jaw gripper systems, making transfer to multi-finger hands challenging, particularly as teleoperation datasets are harder to collect with multi-fingered robots. In this work, we study how to best adapt VLAs to dexterous hands by pairing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with diffusion-steering reinforcement learning (DSRL). Using $π_{0.5}$ and a LEAP hand, we evaluate five fine-tuning recipes across two simulation tasks using only a handful of demonstrations. Our empirical analysis yields three key insights: (1) SFT success is poorly correlated with post-DSRL success, meaning the best SFT checkpoint is rarely the most effective to steer; (2) freezing the vision-language model (VLM) and tuning only the action expert produces the most steerable policy for both training and out-of-distribution placements; and (3) low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is the least steerable approach. Consequently, we recommend an adaptation recipe that freezes the VLM, fully trains the action expert, and eschews SFT rollout success as a checkpoint selection metric.

T-WLA: Bridging Slow Vision and Fast Touch with a World-Language-Action Model 2026-11-17
Show abstract

In contact-rich manipulation, contact conditions can change before a visual policy updates its action plan. Recomputing the plan through the visual policy adds latency, while tactile feedback alone does not specify how the ongoing motion should be revised for the task. We introduce T-WLA, a world-language-action architecture that uses shared visual memory to guide both action generation and tactile correction. At each visual update, the visual branch integrates the current image, task instruction, and retrieved history into a visual memory that conditions base-action generation. The same memory is cached for a lightweight tactile editor, which combines it with the active action chunk and incoming touch to predict residual corrections to unexecuted actions without rerunning the visual branch. Future-image and future-pressure prediction provide training-only supervision, encouraging the visual memory and tactile features to capture interaction dynamics relevant to action generation and correction. We evaluate T-WLA on tactile-conditioned hand-motion prediction using EgoTouch and Tachin, and on four real-world dexterous manipulation tasks. On EgoTouch, T-WLA reduces finger-joint MPJPE by 30% relative to the strongest evaluated baseline. Across four real-robot tasks, T-WLA achieves 51.0% mean success versus 23.0% for the baseline, a gain of 28.0 percentage points. Videos and code are available at https://t-wla.github.io/.

TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Human demonstrations offer an efficient and scalable source of data for dexterous robot manipulation, but differences in sensing and hand morphology complicate the transfer of contact-rich interactions. We present TouchBridge, a hardware--software co-designed framework for human-to-robot data transformation. A wearable human demonstration interface and a corresponding robot interface establish cross-embodiment correspondence in visual and fingertip tactile sensing. We introduce Tac-Retarget, a tactile-conditioned retargeting method that uses measured contact to align fingertip contact patches and preserve their spatial relations within and across hands. Unlike object-aware or simulation-based transfer pipelines, Tac-Retarget requires no object mesh, object-pose estimate, or physics simulation for retargeting, enabling direct and scalable conversion of human demonstrations into robot-domain training data. Combined with visual synthesis and camera-anchored action conversion, TouchBridge produces robot-domain demonstrations for fine-tuning a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model. Across four real-world contact-rich tasks, policies fine-tuned solely on TouchBridge-converted human demonstrations achieve a 72.5% average success rate, compared with 77.5% using an equal number of teleoperated robot demonstrations, while human demonstration collection is 7.0x faster. Under the same policy and training setup, Tac-Retarget outperforms DexPilot and UniDex by 25.0 and 42.5 percentage points, respectively.

Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation: A Survey 2026-11-17
📝 Awesome Embodied Data Pyramid; Project Page at https://jasper-aaa.github.io/embodied-data-pyramid/ GitHub Repo at https://github.com/worldbench/awesome-embodied-data-pyramid
Show abstract

Multimodal foundation models learned to see and to speak by consuming the whole internet. Embodied agents admit no such shortcut, since they require data that couple observations with physical states and actions. These signals can be provided, to varying degrees, by multiple data sources. In this work, we organize the embodied data ecosystem as a "pyramid'' spanning five complementary sources: real-robot data, UMI-style data, egocentric and exocentric data, simulation data, and general vision-language data. We organize the pyramid around the tension between scalability and robot alignment, and further characterize each source in terms of data quality, diversity, reusability, and physical fidelity. We then analyze recent embodied foundation models through the lens of data recipes, examining how different sources are selected, aligned, and mixed during pretraining. For embodied brain models, vision-language-action models, and world-action models alike, we relate data composition to capabilities in perception, reasoning, planning, action generation, and world prediction. To facilitate community use, we additionally consolidate over 10,000 hours of trajectories from widely used robot datasets into a unified LeRobot format, with transparent frame-level quality annotations for filtering. We close by discussing six open challenges: building large-scale tactile datasets, collecting failure and recovery data, developing scalable data-collection pipelines, aligning actions across embodiments, leveraging egocentric data for dexterous manipulation, and designing principled data recipes for robot learning. We hope this work lays the foundation for the design of next-generation embodied systems.

From Human Videos to Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Data: Confidence-Guided RL Refinement of Video Priors 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Collecting demonstrations for bimanual dexterous manipulation is costly, motivating the use of human videos as a source of robot training data. However, uncertainty in video-derived motion estimates and differences between human and robot embodiments make direct motion transfer challenging. In this work, we introduce PriDex, a framework that transforms human-video Priors into executable robot Dexterous manipulation demonstrations through reinforcement learning in simulation.
