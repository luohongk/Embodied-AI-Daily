EfficientVPR: Toward Efficient Visual Place Recognition via Scene-Aware Prompt Tuning and Adaptive Feature Enhancement 2026-11-17
📝 18 pages, 11 figures, accepted at CVPR 2026
Show abstract

Visual place recognition (VPR) faces critical challenges in handling extreme environmental variations while meeting the computational constraints of practical applications. Current methods predominantly address these challenges by either scaling up model capacity or employing computationally intensive reranking stages, creating a significant efficiency bottleneck. To overcome this limitation, we propose EfficientVPR, a lightweight one-stage framework that achieves speed-accuracy trade-offs through two key innovations: i) a scene-aware visual prompt tuning method which adapts pretrained features with less parameters while dynamically adjusting to sample-specific characteristics, and ii) an instance-dependent key local feature enhancement module that further reinforces discriminative regions. Comprehensive evaluations on Pitts250k, MSLS, Eynsham, AmsterTime and SVOX demonstrate that our method establishes a new SOTA for DINOv2-small models by outperforming all same-scale competitors, and delivers a $73\times$ speedup with 60\% lower-dimensional features while maintaining competitive (within 2.5% average R@1 gap) against the SOTA DINOv2-large-based two-stage method. Our code will be available at https://github.com/WiniTang/EfficientVPR.

Beyond Appearance: Where Forest Visual Place Recognition Actually Fails 2026-11-16
📝 10 pages, 6 figures, accepted to the Australasian Conference on Robotics and Automation (ACRA)
Show abstract

Visual place recognition (VPR) in forest environments remains challenging because repetitive vegetation, and changes in illumination, season, and viewpoint can make distinct places appear similar and revisited places appear substantially different. In this paper, we investigate whether temporal, linguistic, structural, and geometric context can improve on an appearance-only descriptor, and what limits each representation when it does not. Using MegaLoc as the appearance-based baseline on the WildCross benchmark, we evaluate score-space and descriptor-space sequence matching, vision-language captions and reranking, scene-graph representations, and depth-derived stem graphs across same-direction revisits, extended routes, and reverse traversals. On a same-direction revisit, both sequence matching strategies reach similar Recall@1 (94.1% vs. 91.5%), but recall at 100% precision rises from 50.9% (single-frame MegaLoc) to 71.6% in score space and falls to 36.5% in descriptor space. The remaining context representations provide no consistent improvement over the appearance-based baseline. Same-place captions show limited place-specific discrimination, reranking is constrained by the initial candidate shortlist, and stem-graph matching remains weak even with LiDAR depth.

Illumination-Robust Localization around Unknown Space Objects using Multispectral Image Fusion 2026-11-16
📝 37 pages, 17 figures. Submitted to AIAA Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics
Show abstract

As on-orbit operations grow, so does the need for precise navigation around unknown resident space objects (RSOs), such as spacecraft, debris, and asteroids. Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms are often studied for mapping an RSO and estimating the inspector's relative pose, but conventional cameras struggle during eclipse, while lidar, though illumination-robust, has higher SWaP. Thermal-infrared cameras track targets through difficult lighting but lack the resolution and feature-richness of visible cameras. Images of a target satellite in low Earth orbit are simulated in both visible and thermal bands, and fused at the pixel level to combine each sensor's strengths for front-end tracking. Thermal imagery is further exploited by a novel silhouette-based visual place recognition (VPR) method that detects loop closures without training, runs entirely on CPU, and outperforms all baselines. Integrated SLAM performance is compared using visible, thermal-infrared, and fused imagery across lighting conditions, trajectories, and place recognition methods; using fused imagery for front-end tracking and our thermal-silhouette place recognition method performs best. The results show that visible and thermal modalities each provide unique information that is exploited differently at various stages of navigation. These low-SWaP sensors could ultimately enable reliable proximity operations on smaller, lower-cost spacecraft.

Long-Term Multi-Session 3D Reconstruction Under Substantial Appearance Change 2026-11-16
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Long-term environmental monitoring requires the ability to reconstruct and align 3D models across repeated site visits separated by months or years to enable the detection of structural change and the assessment of ecosystem health. However, existing Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines are generally designed for imagery captured within a limited time window and consequently fail in long-term monitoring scenarios such as repeated coral reef surveys with substantial visual and structural change. In this paper, we show that the primary limitation of current approaches lies in their inability to maintain visual correspondences under substantial temporal appearance change. We address this limitation by enforcing cross-session correspondences within a joint SfM reconstruction. Our approach combines handcrafted and learned visual features to establish correspondences across large temporal gaps, enabling the reconstruction of a single coherent 3D model from imagery captured years apart, where standard SfM fails to connect sessions. To ensure scalability and further improve alignment robustness on large datasets, we restrict expensive learned feature matching to a small set of likely cross-session image pairs identified via visual place recognition, which reduces computational cost while improving the reliability of cross-session correspondence establishment. We evaluate our method on long-term underwater datasets exhibiting significant real-world change, and demonstrate consistent joint reconstruction across sessions in cases where existing methods fail to connect the sessions or align them only coarsely.

Breaking Déjà Vu: Independent Auditing of Visual Place Recognition through Vision-Language Reasoning 2026-11-14
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Visual place recognition (VPR) is a key enabler of accurate localization and long-term autonomous navigation in robotics applications, such as loop closure detection for simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM). However, real-world VPR deployment relies on selecting an image matching threshold that balances precision and recall. These thresholds are typically tuned using labeled validation data and fixed during deployment, making them unreliable under environmental changes where ground truth is unavailable. This is particularly problematic in safety-critical robotics, where accepting a false loop closure can corrupt the estimated trajectory and map. In this work, we introduce Visual Place Recognition Auditing, an independent post-retrieval verification framework that leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to assess retrieved matches by reasoning jointly over query and candidate images. Unlike conventional verification methods, our approach performs instance-level verification without requiring architecture-specific confidence measures, dataset-dependent thresholds, or prior knowledge of the deployment environment. We evaluate our method on six benchmark datasets using five state-of-the-art VPR methods and four VLMs. Results show that VLM-based auditing improves recall@1 by 13.6% on average as compared to state-of-the-art methods while reducing false acceptance rates to 12%, maintaining precision above 95% and coverage above 75%.

Anchored Rank-Adversarial Fine-Tuning of Pretrained Visual Place Recognition Models 2026-11-11
📝 15 pages, 3 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Visual place recognition (VPR) localises a camera by retrieving similar geo-tagged images, and current foundation-model VPR models are accurate across cities, seasons and illumination, but not robust to adversarial perturbations: under a white-box attack on the ranking at an imperceptible budget of 1/255, SuperVLAD and BoQ keep at most 0.2% top-1 recall (R@1) on four benchmarks. We propose anchored rank-adversarial fine-tuning (ARAFT), which fine-tunes a released model with a listwise rank loss on each adversarial query's hardest positive and an anchor to the clean descriptor of the frozen pretrained model. Checkpoints are selected under a clean-accuracy budget fixed in advance, and the full trade-off is reported. Within five validation R@1 points of clean cost, plain adversarial training has no admissible checkpoint, whereas ARAFT raises robust R@1 from zero to 47.3 on SuperVLAD and 55.3 on BoQ; plain adversarial training exceeds this only at about three times the clean cost. Against unbudgeted plain adversarial training on the test sets, ARAFT matches its robust R@1 at 2/255 with about ten more clean R@1 points. At 4/255, plain adversarial training is 6-9 robust R@1 points ahead, while ARAFT still keeps its ten-point clean advantage. Developed on SuperVLAD, ARAFT transfers to BoQ without re-tuning, and its robustness holds against stronger optimisers, with no sign of gradient masking.

Altitude-Adaptive UAV-to-Map Image Matching for Aerial Geo-Localization 2026-11-10
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Matching downward-looking unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) images to georeferenced satellite or aerial map tiles supports map-based geo-localization when direct positioning signals are degraded or unavailable. Variations in UAV altitude change the observed ground footprint and create a scale mismatch between query images and fixed-scale map references. We address this problem with an altitude-adaptive aerial geo-localization framework. The framework estimates relative altitude from a single image using a frequency-domain representation and regression-as-classification. The predicted altitude-class center defines a scale prior that guides query normalization toward the canonical map scale before full-gallery visual place recognition. A footprint-adaptive margin classifier (FAMC) adjusts training margins according to footprint overlap. Experiments on two synthetic and two real-flight datasets show that, at the 100 m localization threshold, altitude adaptation improves average R@1 and R@5 by 50.68 and 47.20 percentage points, respectively, for our visual place recognition module compared with the same retrieval pipeline without altitude normalization. These results show that a relative-altitude-derived scale prior improves cross-altitude UAV-to-map image matching in the evaluated regions and camera settings.

Sparse Anchors for Visual Multi-Session Registration in Changing Environments 2026-11-03
📝 8 pages, 4 figures, 2 tables
Show abstract

Repeated robotic monitoring requires localization across visits to the same environment, even if the environment changes. Such changes can substantially reduce persistent visual content, and repetitive structures can lead to incorrect cross-session correspondences. In this paper, we address visual multi-session registration of a new data recording (the query session) to a previously reconstructed reference map. Under substantial visual and structural changes, cross-session localization may be possible only for parts of the trajectory. In our approach, we therefore rely on a sparse set of reliable absolute pose constraints and combine them with within-session relative-pose information to register the complete trajectory to the reference map. We first estimate absolute alignment hypotheses through visual place recognition, local feature matching, and geometric verification. We then use sequence consensus to reject hypotheses that are inconsistent with a common query-to-reference alignment and retain the remaining poses as absolute anchors. These anchors are refined through featuremetric alignment against a Gaussian feature field and combined with within-session relative-pose constraints in a robust pose-graph optimization. We evaluate our approach on 15 cross-session experiments from the challenging HILTI26 construction-site dataset, covering substantial scene changes and difficult-to-match environments. Our method achieves 94.4% recall within 1m and 100.0% within 5m, outperforming independent image localization, which peaks at 65.3% and 70.7%, respectively, as well as direct registration of independently reconstructed Structure-from-Motion geometry. Our results show that sparse, reliable absolute anchors can provide accurate and complete trajectory registration even when reliable cross-session correspondences are available only for part of the query session.

SAFEVPR: Patch-Based Verification with Empirical FDR Control for Cross-Condition Sequence Visual Place Recognition 2026-11-03
📝 accepted by RA-L
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Sequence-based visual place recognition (VPR) for loop closure and robot relocalization must decide whether the retrieved top-1 candidate should be accepted. Conformal calibration can set this accept/reject threshold, but its finite-sample guarantees require exchangeability between calibration and deployment, a requirement that cross-condition deployment violates. We therefore claim no formal guarantee under condition shift: this paper instead asks which verification score and calibration design keep a target false-discovery rate (FDR) empirically satisfied across realistic appearance change. We present SAFEVPR, a non-trainable pipeline that replaces the backbone cosine similarity with a mutual-nearest-neighbour (MNN) patch score from frozen DINOv2 features, and flat Learn-Then-Test (LTT) calibration with per-bin Clopper-Pearson thresholds. Across 23 cross-condition ground-vehicle setups (Oxford RobotCar, NCLT, St Lucia; three frozen backbones), SAFEVPR meets the FDR target on 23/23 setups at alpha = 0.10 (mean accepted FDR 0.014, mean TPR 0.75) and on 22/23 at alpha = 0.05 with 22 retaining non-trivial coverage (best cosine variant, cosine+LTT: 22/23 valid but 17 non-trivial); under leave-one-dataset-out calibration, validity drops to 20/23 with 14 retaining useful coverage (strongest cosine baseline: 16/23 and 10), and on texture-poor scenery (Nordland) the verifier is uninformative and the system abstains entirely, as it does everywhere at alpha = 0.01; we report these as limitations. In a pose-graph relocalization study, gating loop-closure constraints reduces erroneous constraints by 29.6x and cuts the median trajectory error from 95.5 m to 9.7 m. Code is available at https://github.com/TIERS/SafeVPR.

VPR-Space: A Benchmark for Visual Place Recognition in Space Analog Domains 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is an important means for loop closure detection in visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). While state-of-the-art VPR and SLAM approaches excel in urban and feature-rich natural environments, the vast and feature-poor environments of extraterrestrial surfaces like on the Moon or Mars pose severe challenges for existing approaches. This paper presents a novel benchmark, VPR-Space, to evaluate VPR capabilities on extraterrestrial surfaces. There are three contributions: (1) We collected sixteen VPR database-query datasets based on existing data from Space Analog Domains (SAD), terrestrial environments that resemble properties of the Moon or Mars. The collection includes a range of challenges regarding environment, viewpoint change, and illumination conditions. (2) To address the ill-posed nature of VPR, we propose a novel approach to generate the ground-truth information in VPR benchmarks. Instead of defining that a query and datbase image show the same place if and only if their camera poses are within fixed position and viewing direction thresholds, we propose to make the decision dependent on whether the match would be valuable in the downstream SLAM task. Under this GT we require a VPR method to identify exactly those image pairs for which a subsequently applied RPE is expected to provide a valuable pose estimate in a SLAM context. (3) We evaluated nine state-of-the-art VPR approaches. The results show considerable room for improvement and indicate directions for future research. We provide a pre-release version of the dataset on github: $\texttt{https://github.com/rfechner/VPR-Space}$

FlatVPR: Plug-and-play Geo-linear Residual Adapter for Geometric Rectification of Foundation Model Feature Manifolds 2026-10-26
📝 25 pages, 7 figures, technical report; v3: Added supplementary materials for downstream tasks and included a supplementary roadmap in Section I
Show abstract

This paper proposes ``FlatVPR,'' a novel geometric rectification paradigm that effectively bridges the trade-off between map lightweightness and localization accuracy in visual place recognition (VPR) by enforcing a feature manifold structure where any descriptor between two adjacent anchors $\mathbf{z}_A$ and $\mathbf{z}_B$ can be accurately reconstructed via linear interpolation $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{pseudo} = (1-t)\mathbf{z}_A + t\mathbf{z}_B$, where $t \in [0,1]$ denotes the relative position. While state-of-the-art foundation models such as DINOv2-ViT-S/14 provide robust semantic features, their latent manifolds exhibit prominent curvature, projecting uniform linear motion in physical space onto highly non-linear trajectories in the feature space, which hinders reliable reconstruction under sparse anchor conditions. To enable the aforementioned interpolation-based reconstruction, we introduce a residual transformation $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{z} + \text{Res}(\mathbf{z})$ to the raw foundation features $\mathbf{z}$, where $\text{Res}(\cdot)$ represents a learnable adapter. Our method explicitly suppresses manifold curvature using a mathematically grounded Pullback Flatness Loss that minimizes the deviation of intermediate features from the linear segment connecting adjacent anchors, thereby minimizing the intrinsic curvature of the manifold. Through this spatial flattening, map construction is formulated within an Expectation-Maximization (EM) framework, decoupled into a continuous M-step for manifold adaptation and a conceptual E-step for optimal anchor selection guidelines. Experiments on the NCLT dataset demonstrate that the application of our adapter leads to significant performance improvements even under extremely sparse anchor conditions with 100m intervals and extreme seasonal changes.

Joint Multi-Condition Representation Modelling via Matrix Factorisation for Visual Place Recognition 2026-10-17
📝 20 pages - preprint
Show abstract

We address multi-reference visual place recognition (VPR), where reference sets captured under varying conditions are used to improve localization performance. While deep learning with large-scale training improves robustness, increasing data diversity and model complexity incur extensive computational cost during training and deployment. Descriptor-level fusion via voting or aggregation avoids training, but often targets multi-sensor setups or relies on heuristics with limited gains under appearance and viewpoint change. We propose a training-free, descriptor-agnostic approach that jointly models places using multiple reference descriptors via matrix decomposition into basis representations, enabling projection-based residual matching. We also introduce SotonMV, a benchmark for multi-viewpoint VPR captured over time. Our method improves Recall@1 by up to $\approx$18% over conventional single-reference set matching on appearance-change datasets, and matches or outperforms existing multi-reference baselines across most appearance-change settings. For viewpoint-change datasets, our method supports matching when viewpoint is distributed among the references, with the benefit depending on reference set coverage of the query conditions. On unconstrained reference sets, we additionally observe gains of $\approx$5%, demonstrating strong generalization while remaining computationally lightweight.

Removing the Compute Bottleneck for Event-based Visual Place Recognition with Spiking Neural Networks 2026-10-12
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, under review
Show abstract

Neuromorphic sensing and computing promise low latency and high energy efficiency for robotics, yet most event-based visual place recognition (VPR) pipelines reintroduce dense, frame-like computation downstream of an inherently sparse sensor, forfeiting the sensor's latency advantage. In this paper, we address the problem of resolving an event-based VPR query in tens of microseconds on a commodity CPU. We train a two-layer spiking neural network (SNN) with additive spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), which drives the learned weight matrix towards a strongly bimodal distribution, amenable to aggressive magnitude pruning. At inference, our pipeline exploits this sparsity by reading only the pruned weights corresponding to the query's active pixels. As surviving weights share a near-uniform magnitude, each neuron's score reduces to an integer count of matching synapses instead of a weighted sum, lowering per-query latency. This computation is small enough to remain resident in a CPU's fast on-chip cache. On three event-based VPR benchmarks, our sparse SNN exceeds the recall of LENS, an existing event-based SNN, while reducing per-query latency by 5.5-6.2x to tens of microseconds. It matches the recall of a dense variant of our network and of the sum-of-absolute-differences (SAD) baseline, while reading 10x fewer weights than the dense variant. We further characterise how recall and latency scale with the number of active pixels and reference places. These results suggest that the sparsity of both event-based input and learned weights can already be exploited for low-latency inference on conventional processors.

Change-Robust Online Topological Memory for Long-Term Relocalization and Semantic Navigation 2026-10-02
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Long-term semantic navigation requires a robot to reuse past observations after appearance and scene change, but semantic memories are only useful if the robot can relocalize into the memory without corrupting it with false visual matches. We propose CROSS, a change-robust topological memory that introduces a pre-commitment localization layer between visual place recognition and map update. Instead of treating a retrieved keyframe as an immediate place association or loop-closure factor, CROSS lifts each RGB-D retrieval into a candidate global SE(3) pose mode using relative pose estimation. A bounded Gaussian-mixture filter then propagates competing continuous trajectory branches with odometry, rejects branches that are physically inconsistent, and promotes only persistent branches to loop closures. This moves ambiguity handling from discrete place IDs or post-hoc graph-factor rejection to continuous pose-space validation before map commitment. Across public long-term relocalization benchmarks and real quadruped object-navigation experiments, CROSS improves reuse of a single sparse RGB-D memory under illumination, seasonal, dynamic-scene, and object-level change. Geometry-Conditioned Visual Place Recognition in Natural Environments 2026-09-23
📝 Accepted at the 28th International Conference on Digital Image Computing: Techniques and Applications (DICTA 2026). 8 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Visual Place Recognition (VPR) in natural environments remains challenging due to repetitive vegetation, sparse distinctive landmarks, and substantial appearance and viewpoint variation across traversals. While visual observations of the same place can change considerably, their underlying spatial structure is often more persistent. We exploit this complementary geometric consistency through Depth-Aware Distillation (DAD), which conditions the token representations of a pretrained Vision Foundation Model (VFM) on geometry inferred by a Geometric Foundation Model (GFM), without any depth sensor. Rather than treating geometry as an additional input modality, DAD projects image-aligned depth into the VFM token space and selectively modulates visual representations through channel-wise geometric conditioning. A two-stage teacher-guided learning strategy first anchors the geometry-conditioned representation to the pretrained appearance space, before refining it for place discrimination. These results show that GFM-derived geometry can provide a persistent structural prior for VPR when visual appearance becomes unreliable.

TM-APR: Thermal Temporal-Memory Localization via Analytic Online Adaptation 2026-09-22
📝 8 pages, 6 figures, technical report
Show abstract

Thermal Visual Place Recognition (Thermal VPR) maps camera observations to metric poses within a mapped environment, serving as a prerequisite for autonomous navigation. However, thermal VPR suffers from severe environmental dependence, heavy online retraining overheads, and an inability to model dynamic non-linear shifts, causing existing frameworks to fail during online deployment. To achieve robust domain-invariant place recognition, we bridge Analytic Class-Incremental Learning (ACIL) with domain-invariant VPR for the first time, revealing that its gradient-free matrix updates construct a surprisingly strong baseline that outperforms conventional fine-tuning. Nevertheless, standard ACIL exhibits a critical vulnerability to extreme non-linear thermal fluctuations due to its structural linear assumptions. Our formulation guarantees exact closed-form matrix updates within $\mathcal{O}(1)$ computational complexity, bypassing backpropagation to ensure that the online update latency ($Δt_{\mathrm{learn}}$) remains strictly bounded below the sensor acquisition interval ($Δt_{\mathrm{acquire}}$), thereby eliminating trajectory jumps in real-time SLAM pipelines.

📝 8 pages, 3 figures, technical report
Show abstract

Strong mapped-region thermal visual place recognition (VPR) does not ensure safe rejection of unmapped queries. We identify and quantify this gap in AnyThermal: high Map-In retrieval accuracy coexists with confident false loop closures in Map-Out. Closed-form $\text{SE}(2)$ SVD verifies candidates, while frozen visual features, nine-dimensional SVD residuals, and motion proxies enter a masked multi-head transformer (MHT). Event-based localization research and datasets are a rapidly growing area of interest, with a tenfold increase in the cumulative total number of published papers on this topic over the past 10 years. Whilst the rapid expansion in the field is exciting, it brings with it an associated challenge: a growth in the variety of required code and package dependencies as well as data formats, making comparisons difficult and cumbersome for researchers to implement reliably. To address this challenge, we present Event-LAB: a new and unified framework for running several event-based localization methodologies across multiple datasets. Event-LAB is implemented using the Pixi package and dependency manager, that enables a single command-line installation and invocation for combinations of localization methods and datasets. To demonstrate the capabilities of the framework, we implement two common event-based localization pipelines: Visual Place Recognition (VPR) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). We demonstrate the ability of the framework to systematically visualize and analyze the results of multiple methods and datasets, revealing key insights such as the association of parameters that control event collection counts and window sizes for frame generation to large variations in performance. The results and analysis demonstrate the importance of fairly comparing methodologies with consistent event image generation parameters. Our Event-LAB framework provides this ability for the research community, by contributing a streamlined workflow for easily setting up multiple conditions.

Event stream-based Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is an emerging research direction that offers a compelling solution to the instability of conventional visible-light cameras under challenging conditions such as low illumination, overexposure, and high-speed motion. Recognizing the current scarcity of dedicated datasets in this domain, we introduce EPRBench, a high-quality benchmark specifically designed for event stream-based VPR. EPRBench comprises 10K event sequences and 65K event frames, collected using both handheld and vehicle-mounted setups to comprehensively capture real-world challenges across diverse viewpoints, weather conditions, and lighting scenarios. To support semantic-aware and language-integrated VPR research, we provide LLM-generated scene descriptions, subsequently refined through human annotation, establishing a solid foundation for integrating LLMs into event-based perception pipelines. Furthermore, we propose a novel multi-modal fusion paradigm for VPR: leveraging LLMs to generate textual scene descriptions from raw event streams, which then guide spatially attentive token selection, cross-modal feature fusion, and multi-scale representation learning. This framework not only achieves highly accurate place recognition but also produces interpretable reasoning processes alongside its predictions, significantly enhancing model transparency and explainability. In emerging multi-robot societies, heterogeneous agents must continually extract and integrate local knowledge from one another through communication, even when their internal models are completely opaque. Existing approaches to continual or collaborative learning for visual place recognition (VPR) largely assume white-box access to model parameters or shared training datasets, which is unrealistic when robots encounter unknown peers in the wild. This paper introduces \emph{Continual Communicative Learning (CCL)}, a data-free multi-robot framework in which a traveler robot (student) continually improves its VPR capability by communicating with black-box teacher models via a constrained query--response channel. We repurpose Membership Inference Attacks (MIA), originally developed as privacy attacks on machine learning models, as a constructive communication primitive to reconstruct pseudo-training sets from black-box VPR teachers without accessing their parameters or raw data. To overcome the intrinsic communication bottleneck caused by the low sampling efficiency of black-box MIA, we propose a prior-based query strategy that leverages the student's own VPR prior to focus queries on informative regions of the embedding space, thereby reducing the knowledge transfer (KT) cost. Experimental results on a standard multi-session VPR benchmark demonstrate that the proposed CCL framework yields substantial performance gains for low-performing robots under modest communication budgets, highlighting CCL as a promising building block for scalable and fault-tolerant multi-robot systems. Furthermore, we propose a Distributed Statistic Integration (DSI) framework that theoretically eliminates catastrophic forgetting by efficiently aggregating sufficient statistics from black-box VPR models while maintaining data privacy and reducing communication overhead to a sample-invariant constant complexity.

Loop closure detection (LCD) is a core component of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM): it identifies revisited places and enables pose-graph constraints that correct accumulated drift. Classic bag-of-words approaches such as DBoW are efficient but often degrade under appearance change and perceptual aliasing. In parallel, deep learning-based visual place recognition (VPR) descriptors (e.g., NetVLAD and Transformer-based models) offer stronger robustness, but their computational cost is often viewed as a barrier to real-time SLAM. Visual Place Recognition (VPR) aims to match query images against a database using visual cues. State-of-the-art methods aggregate features from deep backbones to form global descriptors. Optimal transport-based aggregation methods reformulate feature-to-cluster assignment as a transport problem, but the standard Sinkhorn algorithm symmetrically treats source and target marginals, limiting effectiveness when image features and cluster centers exhibit substantially different distributions. We propose an asymmetric aggregation VPR method with geometric constraints for locally aggregated descriptors, called $A^2$GC-VPR. Our method employs row-column normalization averaging with separate marginal calibration, enabling asymmetric matching that adapts to distributional discrepancies in visual place recognition. Geometric constraints are incorporated through learnable coordinate embeddings, computing compatibility scores fused with feature similarities, thereby promoting spatially proximal features to the same cluster and enhancing spatial awareness. Experimental results on MSLS, NordLand, and Pittsburgh datasets demonstrate superior performance, validating the effectiveness of our approach in improving matching accuracy and robustness.

One of the central challenges in visual place recognition (VPR) is learning a robust global representation that remains discriminative under large viewpoint changes, illumination variations, and severe domain shifts. While visual foundation models (VFMs) provide strong local features, most existing methods rely on a single model, overlooking the complementary cues offered by different VFMs. However, exploiting such complementary information inevitably alters token distributions, which challenges the stability of existing query-based global aggregation schemes. To address these challenges, we propose DC-VLAQ, a representation-centric framework that integrates the fusion of complementary VFMs and robust global aggregation. Specifically, we first introduce a lightweight residual-guided complementary fusion that anchors representations in the DINOv2 feature space while injecting complementary semantics from CLIP through a learned residual correction. In addition, we propose the Vector of Local Aggregated Queries (VLAQ), a query--residual global aggregation scheme that encodes local tokens by their residual responses to learnable queries, resulting in improved stability and the preservation of fine-grained discriminative cues. Extensive experiments on standard VPR benchmarks, including Pitts30k, Tokyo24/7, MSLS, Nordland, SPED, and AmsterTime, demonstrate that DC-VLAQ consistently outperforms strong baselines and achieves state-of-the-art performance, particularly under challenging domain shifts and long-term appearance changes.

Towards Implicit Aggregation: Robust Image Representation for Place Recognition in the Transformer Era 2026-01-16
📝 Accepted by NeurIPS 2025
Show abstract

Visual place recognition (VPR) is typically regarded as a specific image retrieval task, whose core lies in representing images as global descriptors. Over the past decade, dominant VPR methods (e.g., NetVLAD) have followed a paradigm that first extracts the patch features/tokens of the input image using a backbone, and then aggregates these patch features into a global descriptor via an aggregator. This backbone-plus-aggregator paradigm has achieved overwhelming dominance in the CNN era and remains widely used in transformer-based models. In this paper, however, we argue that a dedicated aggregator is not necessary in the transformer era, that is, we can obtain robust global descriptors only with the backbone. Specifically, we introduce some learnable aggregation tokens, which are prepended to the patch tokens before a particular transformer block. All these tokens will be jointly processed and interact globally via the intrinsic self-attention mechanism, implicitly aggregating useful information within the patch tokens to the aggregation tokens. Finally, we only take these aggregation tokens from the last output tokens and concatenate them as the global representation. Although implicit aggregation can provide robust global descriptors in an extremely simple manner, where and how to insert additional tokens, as well as the initialization of tokens, remains an open issue worthy of further exploration. To this end, we also propose the optimal token insertion strategy and token initialization method derived from empirical studies. Experimental results show that our method outperforms state-of-the-art methods on several VPR datasets with higher efficiency and ranks 1st on the MSLS challenge leaderboard. The code is available at https://github.com/lu-feng/image.

Compared to conventional cameras, event cameras provide a high dynamic range and low latency, offering greater robustness to rapid motion and challenging lighting conditions. Although the potential of event cameras for visual place recognition (VPR) has been established, developing robust VPR frameworks under severe illumination changes remains an open research problem. In this paper, we introduce an ensemble-based approach to event camera place recognition that combines sequence-matched results from multiple event-to-frame reconstructions, VPR feature extractors, and temporal resolutions. Unlike previous event-based ensemble methods, which only utilise temporal resolution, our broader fusion strategy delivers significantly improved robustness under varied lighting conditions (e.g., afternoon, sunset, night), achieving a 57% relative improvement in Recall@1 across day-night transitions. Additionally, we propose a modification to the standard sequence matching framework that enhances performance at longer sequence lengths.
