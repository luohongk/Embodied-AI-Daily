Temporal Conditioning for Human Behavioral Cloning in Chess 2026-11-24
📝 6 pages, 2 figures, 3 tables. Accepted manuscript of the paper published at the 2026 IEEE Gaming, Entertainment, and Media Conference (GEM). Code: https://github.com/Prof-it/temporal-chess-model; weights: https://huggingface.co/holymolyyy/temporal-chess-model
Show abstract

Behavioral cloning models in chess typically predict human moves from board states while ignoring temporal inputs such as the remaining clock. However, time pressure is a major determinant of human decision-making quality, and temporal state is a missing dimension of human behavioral modeling. This paper introduces a time-aware transformer that extends a Chessformer-style architecture with learned temporal conditioning tokens encoding player clock state, time control, and recent tempo, together with behavioral heads for thinking time and resignation. The model is trained on large-scale online chess data and evaluated against time-agnostic baselines and the state of the art. Results show that temporal conditioning yields modest overall accuracy improvements but substantially stronger gains under time pressure. Beyond move choice, it also produces more accurate thinking-time and resignation predictions. More importantly, the model better matches human move-quality degradation in critical time regimes, reducing the systematic over-strength observed in time-agnostic behavioral cloning models. These results suggest that temporal state is a previously underutilized but important signal for human behavioral cloning in chess.

A Few Teacher Steps Go a Long Way: Efficient SFT Data Collection for LLM Agents 2026-11-24
📝 Longer version of the NeurIPS 2026 main track paper
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

DAgger-style supervised finetuning (SFT) combines full teacher demonstrations with teacher continuations of partial student rollouts, providing supervision on contexts the student encounters. We study how to allocate the teacher-inference budget on these on-policy (OP) continuations after initial behavior cloning (BC). One existing collection recipe runs the teacher to termination and retains only successful continuations (OP-Success). Since rejected outputs still incur teacher-inference costs, we compare this recipe with shorter continuations without outcome filtering (OP-Short), and with continuing only failed learner rollouts while retaining successful continuations (OP-Critical). We evaluate these choices against acquiring additional full demonstrations, tracking total teacher-inference cost separately from retained training tokens. Across HotpotQA, ALFWorld, and OpenThoughts-TB-Dev, teacher continuations improve the accuracy-cost tradeoff over scaling full demonstrations. On HotpotQA and OpenThoughts-TB-Dev, OP-Short surpasses the strongest tested BC baseline while reducing estimated teacher-inference cost by 20% and 83%, respectively, and on ALFWorld five-step continuations cut teacher tokens by 22% relative to full continuations with little accuracy loss. Controlled HotpotQA ablations show that by focusing on failed student rollouts, OP-Critical achieves comparable accuracy to OP-Success with 30% lower estimated teacher-inference cost and 36% fewer retained training tokens. Compute-matched comparisons with reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation further support brief teacher continuations as an efficient and lightweight post-training recipe.

RetraQ: Manifold-Constrained Critic Guidance for Flow Policies in RL 2026-11-24
📝 32 pages, 11 figures, including appendix
Show abstract

A learned critic can improve a pretrained flow-matching policy by steering sampled actions toward higher predicted value. However, even a critic that fits the behavior data well can provide misleading action gradients. Gradient components pointing away from the data-supported region are weakly constrained by behavior data and can lead guidance to exploit critic extrapolation errors. We introduce RetraQ (Retraction-based Q-guidance), which uses geometry encoded by the pretrained flow to constrain both the direction and the destination of critic guidance. A single velocity evaluation of the frozen flow defines a denoising map. RetraQ filters the critic gradient with the map's Jacobian and applies the map itself to correct the updated action. Our analysis characterizes when these operations approximate tangent filtering and retraction onto the behavior manifold. The same update guides actions at test time and provides targets for policy distillation and online reinforcement learning. In controlled studies, RetraQ improves true return while keeping refined actions close to the data-supported region, and its distillation targets remain more stable than those of raw critic ascent. In offline-to-online training on LIBERO and OGBench, RetraQ achieves higher mean final success than the evaluated critic-guidance baselines. On a real-world plug-insertion task, test-time RetraQ improves the success rate of a behavior-cloned $π_{0.5}$ policy from 55.0% to 76.7%. Online training further increases success to 98.3% in only 30 minutes and 100% within one hour. Project page: https://2027ICLR.github.io/retraq/

PAC-ACT: Post-training Actor-Critic for Action Chunking Transformers 2026-11-23
📝 Revised version with clarified methodology, updated experimental analysis, and improved presentation

Show abstract

Precision industrial contact manipulation requires reliable robot policies under pose perturbations and contact-force constraints. Vision-language-action models offer broad generalization but often introduce high inference latency and GPU-memory cost, while vision-action chunking policies are more suitable for real-time industrial control. However, these policies are usually trained by behavior cloning and suffer from distribution shift in contact-rich tasks. This paper proposes PAC-ACT, a reinforcement-learning post-training framework for pretrained Action Chunking Transformer policies. PAC-ACT reformulates policy optimization at the chunk level, constructs an ACT-transferred actor-critic architecture, and introduces a hybrid behavior-prior constraint to preserve the pretrained action distribution during online fine-tuning. Experiments on industrial precision-contact benchmarks show that PAC-ACT improves task success, contact stability, and force safety while retaining low latency and low GPU-memory usage. On the Contour task, PAC-ACT significantly reduces peak contact force and decreases the proportion of force readings above 60 N by 46 times. Sparse-reward ablations further show that the proposed behavior-prior constraint enables effective exploration under randomized initial poses.

ABI: Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations for Zero-Shot Test-Time Guidance 2026-11-23
📝 Project page: https://securitycfs.github.io/ABI-demo/
Show abstract

Behavior-cloning policies show strong ability in sequential control by generating coherent multi-step action chunks, yet their purely imitative objective limits zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks. While offline reinforcement learning offers principled policy improvement, existing methods either require task-specific reward supervision or remain tied to a specific actor during critic training or policy deployment, inflating training cost and restricting cross-policy generalization. We observe that evaluating actions under the data-collecting behavior largely preserves their relative value ordering across target policies, offering a way around actor coupling. Combining this property with reward-free successor prediction, we propose Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations (ABI) to learn multi-horizon action-chunk representations from unlabeled trajectories without reward supervision or actor training. At test time, pairing the frozen representation with lightweight task readouts forms a chunk critic that guides target policies without parameter updates and transfers directly across distinct unseen policies. Our analysis establishes when the value ordering is preserved across policies and when selecting among proposed chunks improves closed-loop return. Across 65 locomotion, navigation, and manipulation tasks, ABI's chunk critic guides weak imitation policies to match or surpass zero-shot RL baselines with high sample efficiency, outperforms existing zero-shot critics on identical proposals, and improves externally trained RL actors without retraining. From raw pixels, ABI delivers substantial guidance gains and surpasses prior zero-shot RL baselines on visual manipulation, highlighting its potential for visuomotor control.

EgoLAM: Learning Latent Actions from Hand and Head Motion 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Latent action models let a robot policy learn from video by turning the change between two frames into a training target. In egocentric video, a cup can move across the image because a hand pushes it or because the wearer turns. Both changes help predict the next frame, but only the first describes manipulation. During training, EgoLAM supervises the action code with measured wrist motion and supplies camera motion separately to the forward model. Even when the wearer turns while pushing a cup, the training target still describes how the hand pushed it. The action encoder pools object slots around each hand, and wrist translation and rotation can be decoded from the code in a common reference frame. Extracting a code then requires only two RGB frames. Alongside LARYBench classification and regression, we measure how much wrist motion a frozen code retains and how strongly it responds to a synthetic view or lighting change. EgoLAM has the highest hand-motion decoding score among eight evaluated models. Across three LIBERO suites, policies pretrained on its codes and fine-tuned on expert demonstrations achieve 79.38% mean success, 28.73 points above expert behavior cloning and 9.76 points above the strongest other latent action model in each suite on average.

Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning 2026-11-19
📝 9 pages, 5 figures. Code, videos and results: https://zhanghutony.github.io/ibmarl/. A preliminary version appeared at the PhysCollaBot Workshop at IROS 2026
Show abstract

Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.

Latent Action Policies from Suboptimal Data 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Latent Action Models (LAMs) learn control representations from action-free video, but existing pipelines typically train latent policies with behavioral cloning, without an explicit policy improvement objective. Although offline RL offers a natural approach to policy improvement, standard methods do not transfer directly to learned latent action spaces. We find that the structure of learned latent actions makes naive offline RL unreliable: directly maximizing value over latent actions or fitting a unimodal policy can produce latent actions that cannot be realized by physical execution, while the latent representation can merge physical controls that receive different rewards. To understand when latent-space optimization remains valid, we derive conditions under which policy improvement transfers to decoded execution and under which latent and physical optima align. Building on these insights, we propose LAPIS for goal-conditioned latent policy improvement, grounding value-based policy improvement in observed state-latent transitions and using a conditional flow policy to capture their multimodal structure. LAPIS outperforms baselines on most tasks, with the clearest gains in visual manipulation, and remains effective even with only 1% action labels, where competing methods largely collapse.

Latent Goal Prediction from Language for Model-Based Planning 2026-11-17
📝 10 pages. Preprint under review
Show abstract

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) enable agents to plan in latent space by imagining the outcomes of candidate actions, yet task specification remains a bottleneck. Visual targets provide precise local gradients but poor distant guidance, while language is flexible yet limited by noisy cross-modal alignment or dependence on distinct large generative models. We introduce LAGO (Latent Goal Prediction from Language), a hierarchical world model in which a single predictor both forecasts action-conditioned dynamics and grounds language instructions as sequences of intermediate latent subgoals, training both modes with a single regression objective over a shared latent space. At each planning step, LAGO predicts a sequence of latent subgoals from a language instruction and optimizes an action sequence using a soft-minimum alignment cost that rewards subgoal proximity without enforcing a rigid path. Subgoals are repredicted as the agent's state evolves, turning a single long-horizon instruction into a sequence of locally tractable objectives. Across environments spanning navigation and manipulation, LAGO outperforms flat and hierarchical image-goal planners, with the largest gains on long-horizon navigation with curved, hazard-constrained paths, where it more than doubles the success rate of a goal-conditioned behavior cloning policy trained on the same demonstrations. Grounding language in the world model's own latent space also outperforms vision-language reward models, including one that plans with ground-truth dynamics.

Progress Predicts Policy 2026-11-17
Show abstract

A task specifies how the world should change, while a policy specifies what the robot should do. We introduce Progress Predicts Policy (PPP), which learns a language-conditioned scalar field through the commands induced by its differential. We obtain action conditioning by composing a latent progress field with a shared world model and read its action gradient at a fixed reference command. Continuous commands scale this gradient by a learned positive gain, while discrete commands use finite differences of the same scalar. This yields a hybrid policy without a separate action head or iterative search. Fitting demonstration progress values alone does not ensure that action gradients align with expert commands. PPP trains the induced commands by imitation and uses double backpropagation for the continuous gradient readout to shape the field's local control geometry. Demonstration timing calibrates field values on observed states without additional annotation. The continuous readout remains learnable without progress labels but these timing-derived targets support progress monitoring during execution. Experiments in simulated and real robot manipulation show that PPP outperforms behavioral cloning under a shared architecture.

Autonomous monitoring and intervention in semi-structured agricultural environments, such as orchards and vineyards, require mobile robots to navigate to locations across diverse field configurations. Learning and Verifying Progress: Closed-Loop Agentic Planning with Progress-Enhanced Execution for Long-Horizon Vision-Language Navigation 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Long-horizon vision-language navigation (VLN) requires agents to track instruction progress and recover from local errors before they propagate across subtasks. However, executors trained on expert trajectories encounter policy-induced state shifts during deployment, and their stop signals do not reliably indicate subtask completion. We introduce Learning and Verifying Progress for VLN (LeaP-VLN), an agentic VLN system that couples progress-aware execution with independent progress verification through plan--execute--verify--recover cycles. LeaP-VLN leverages progress both explicitly, through a progress verifier that verifies subtask completion before the agent advances and triggers dual-phase recovery upon route deviation, and implicitly, by co-training the executor on progress reasoning and action prediction to enhance the executor's progress awareness and corrective control. To adapt the executor to this closed-loop interface, we propose a two-stage progress-enhanced curriculum: behavior cloning on expert demonstrations, followed by joint training on demonstrations, expert action corrections, and progress-reasoning supervision. Policy-generated trajectories expose the executor to both intra-subtask drift and inter-subtask state shifts, aligning training with the deviations encountered during closed-loop execution. Extensive experiments on R2R-CE and RxR-CE demonstrate state-of-the-art success rates among monocular methods trained without additional navigation datasets, while real-world experiments further validate the effectiveness of LeaP-VLN in long-horizon navigation.

Fuzzy Explainable Continual Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Navigation 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Continual robot navigation requires reinforcement learning policies to continuously adapt to new environments while retaining previously acquired knowledge. Meanwhile, explainable reinforcement learning aims to learn meaningful behavioral representations through policy interaction. However, these objectives are inherently conflicting, as continual learning emphasizes policy stability whereas explainable behavior learning requires adaptive exploration. Existing methods rarely address both simultaneously. To resolve this issue, this paper proposes a Fuzzy Explainable Continual Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (FECHRL) method. A two-stage behavior cloning strategy is introduced to balance rapid policy adaptation and explainable behavior evolution by progressively transitioning from strong imitation guidance to autonomous reinforcement learning. Furthermore, a fuzzy critic based on a Takagi--Sugeno fuzzy inference system is proposed to alleviate value extrapolation errors during the transition between imitation-guided learning and autonomous exploration, thereby improving continual training stability. Experimental results on sequential multi-scenario navigation tasks demonstrate that the proposed method effectively unifies continual learning capability and policy explainability within a single reinforcement learning framework, achieving stable knowledge retention and meaningful behavioral explanation.

When Does On-Policy Interaction Help? Representational Tradeoffs in Value-Based Imitation Learning 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Imitation learning (IL)---training an agent to replicate expert behavior from demonstrations---underpins applications from robotics to language model training. Standard approaches such as Behavior Cloning (BC) are known to suffer from compounding errors and performance plateaus, particularly when the learner cannot perfectly represent the expert's policy (as is typical, e.g., in distillation). Two interventions are widely understood empirically to improve performance: querying the expert interactively along the learner's own trajectories, and using value function estimation en route to generating a policy. We investigate the nature of these improvements and their potentially surprising interplay. Our main finding is that expert interaction relaxes the representational demands on the learner: one only needs a model capable of realizing the expert's value function, bypassing the (often stricter) requirement of realizing the expert's policy itself. Concretely, we introduce OVI, an interactive on-policy IL algorithm that is statistically and computationally efficient whenever the learner can represent the expert's value function. We complement this with a negative result showing that interaction is necessary. Namely, without stronger assumptions beyond expert-value realizability alone, any offline IL algorithm must scale with the complexity of the expert policy class. Our findings bear out empirically. OVI outperforms offline policy-based (BC), interactive policy-based (DAgger), and offline value-based IL methods, with the largest gains when the learner network is substantially less expressive than the expert's.

Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Robots that learn from demonstrations should extrapolate their policy despite state perturbations such as sensor noise, scene variation, and compounding errors. One approach to this robustness is regularization. Prior regularizers consider each state on its own and limit how sensitive the policy is to any perturbation at that state. By contrast, we explore how the policy's output shifts when given the same perturbation at different states. Our insight is that, locally, the robot should respond to state perturbations in a consistent manner: e.g., if changing an irrelevant object position does not impact the policy at one state, that same change should not shift the policy output at a nearby state. Applying this insight, we develop inner product regularization (InneR). We show that the inner product on action space induces an inner product on state space through the policy Jacobian (the Fisher information for stochastic policies), and that the null space of this inner product governs which state perturbations have a first-order effect on the policy. By clustering states whose inner products are aligned, averaging the inner products within each cluster, and minimizing the smallest eigenvalues of that average, InneR shapes the learned policy so that nearby states share a common null space. InneR enforces consistent responses to perturbations without limiting policy expressiveness, and can be appended to existing imitation learning pipelines for deterministic or stochastic policies. In simulated experiments, InneR makes learned policies more robust to irrelevant state dimensions and shifts between training and test distributions, whereas norm penalties often perform worse than behavior cloning. On a real robot arm performing three manipulation tasks, InneR raises the average success rate of a diffusion policy from 18% to 57% without additional data.

Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. The Relative Value of Demonstration Data: Execution Time Limits and Operator Mixtures in Behavior Cloning 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Robot demonstration datasets combine demonstrations from teleoperators of uneven experience, so collection teams need to know how many demonstrations from one group match those from another. We measure this with a data multiple: the ratio of the numbers of demonstrations two groups need for their policies to reach the same success rate. On robomimic's simulated Square and Can tasks, we train behavior-cloning policies on demonstrations from the two teleoperators robomimic labels Better and the two it labels Worse, varying dataset size and the share of Worse demonstrations. Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. Offline navigation demonstrations are multimodal, because one observation often allows several valid actions, such as passing an obstacle on either side. Behavior cloning averages these actions, potentially leading to unsafe routes. Multimodal policies must discover the alternatives from the data, but similar states can lead to different routes whose current actions overlap. In navigation, the alternatives have a geometric cause and can be computed from one observation. We propose a hierarchical neural-network policy that computes candidate modes from local geometry and learns from demonstrations which mode to follow and how. A high-level policy selects a mode using the goal and its previous choice among a set of candidate modes, and a low-level policy turns it into an action.
