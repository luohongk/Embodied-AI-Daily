Tilt-Rotor Quadcopter with Learned Residual Control and Mode-Dependent State Estimation 2026-10-22
📝 5 figures
Show abstract

Tilt-rotor quadcopters redirect thrust for translation without banking, but complicate modeling, state estimation, and disturbance rejection. This paper presents a simulation-based flight stack for a \SI{2.00}{\kilo\gram} tilt-rotor quadcopter. The system uses a six-degree-of-freedom nonlinear model with first-order motor dynamics and two-axis rotor tilt, parameterized using a 3D CAD model. A cascaded PD controller commands forward and lateral tilt angles. A 14-state EKF fuses sensor data across hover, early-transition, and transition modes using a hysteretic (Schmitt-trigger) classifier to prevent covariance discontinuities. Because the proportional position loop leaves a steady-state offset under constant wind, a small bounded neural residual is added on top of the unmodified PD controller. It is trained by behavioral cloning from a teacher with privileged access to the true wind, while the deployed network never observes wind. In simulation, the EKF achieves a post-settling position root mean square error (RMSE) of \SI{0.226}{\metre} (horizontal) and \SI{0.03}{\metre} (vertical) and an attitude RMSE below \SI{0.8}{\degree}, and it estimates a wind velocity of $(\num{2.0}, \num{-1.2})\,\unit{\metre\per\second}$ to within \SI{0.15}{\metre\per\second} per axis. The residual reduces steady-state station-keeping error under wind by approximately \SI{49}{\percent}, from \SI{0.117}{\metre} to \SI{0.060}{\metre} at \SI{1.5}{\metre\per\second} and from \SI{0.319}{\metre} to \SI{0.162}{\metre} at an out-of-distribution \SI{3.9}{\metre\per\second}. The framework provides a basis for hardware-in-the-loop and flight-test studies.

EBV-CAN: Vision--Language Grounding for Contact-Aided Endoscope Navigation 2026-10-22
Show abstract

Flexible endoscopes exploit structural compliance to traverse narrow lumens, pipes, and cavities, where navigation involves distributed environmental contact. This compliance makes tip motion depend on the evolving support configuration. We present external bird's-eye-view contact-aided navigation (EBV-CAN), a vision-language policy that combines one initial global static bird's-eye image with evolving endoscopic observations and motor state. Three supervised semantic queries locate the tip, next waypoint, and instructed target on the cached global image; their residual representations predict bending and insertion commands. We introduce the Vision-Language Contact-Aided (VLCA) dataset, linking 517 demonstrations and 75k action samples to 958k spatial entries and 93k remaining-route records. On 50 held-out episodes, tip-coordinate and waypoint localization reach 97.5% and 94.8%. Swapping the target instruction under identical observations changes target attention and motor output across 17 dual-target episodes. Across 1,920 physical trials of eight policies trained under a shared protocol, EBV-CAN achieves 183/240 successes (76.25%) under automatic blue-area scoring. Gated recurrent unit behavior cloning (GRU-BC) achieves the lowest matched-task offline mean absolute error (MAE), 0.056, and 1/240 physical successes, establishing distinct rankings for action imitation and task completion. Successful multi-guide executions and representative failures connect spatial progress, steering transitions, and visibility to physical navigation.

What Does It Take For A Generalist Robot Policy To Adapt Using Minimal Data? 2026-10-21
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

A central goal in robot learning is to move beyond task-specific human data collection toward robots that improve through autonomous interaction. Yet fully autonomous learning remains difficult with current policies: sparse rewards and weak zero-shot exploration often prevent online learning from discovering successful behavior. As a first step towards fully autonomous learning, we propose the minimal-data adaptation regime, in which a pretrained robot policy must learn a new task from as little as one demonstration followed by autonomous online interaction. We establish that, although pretrained policies fail zero-shot on many new tasks, a single demonstration suffices to direct their behavioral prior to coarse task-following behavior, albeit with unreliable performance. From this insight, we isolate the key design principles required to convert coarse task-following into reliable task success. These include (1) the surprising efficacy of pretrained VLA representations to support sample-efficient value learning from online interaction (2) the sufficiency of residual policy learning to learn corrective behavior and (3) the need for careful data-balancing to prevent exploration collapse. As a representative of these principles, we propose MiDAS, a simple offline-to-online RL recipe that combines few-demonstration behavior cloning with value-based residual learning over frozen VLA representations. Across LIBERO and RoboCasa tasks, MiDAS substantially improves performance from single-demonstration initializations, including policies that succeed only rarely, validating the principles highlighted above. On a bimanual YAM platform, starting from a brittle initial policy, MiDAS improves task success after approximately six hours of online interaction.

DONIC: Generalist Dexterous Bimanual Tracking Control from Human Demonstrations 2026-10-21
Show abstract

Despite recent progress in dexterous reference tracking, existing models focus primarily on single-hand manipulation and a limited range of interactions, leaving the bimanual dexterous behaviors common in real life unaddressed. The scarcity of contact-rich bimanual manipulation data and the increased complexity of coordinating contacts make developing a generalist bimanual tracker particularly challenging. Therefore, we present 1) DONIC, the first generalist bimanual tracker jointly trained across operation categories and human-motion sources, together with 2) BiHOI, a multi-view dataset of 1,575 sequences spanning 229 objects and emphasizing sustained bimanual interaction, where reconstructed interactions are retargeted into robot references. DONIC combines proximal policy optimization with behavioral cloning in a feedback loop that recycles verified successful executions into training demonstrations. An object-centered per-hand reward maintains object-motion feedback while supervising each hand according to its reference contact role. Clip-Centered Advantages remove reference-dependent advantage offsets while preserving within-trajectory differences. Together, these designs enable one single policy to learn coordinated manipulation across heterogeneous references. Without finetuning, a single checkpoint achieves 86.79% success on unseen trajectories of training objects and 57.32% on held-out objects, surpassing the state-of-the-art bimanual tracking baselines by 77.36 and 45.73 percentage points.

Learning to Absorb Corrections: Critic-Weighted Residual Flow for Vision-Based Insertion 2026-10-21
📝 9 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Vision-based insertion requires correcting alignment errors while retaining useful behavior learned from demonstrations. We present ACT--Flow, an online learning framework that couples an adaptive Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) with a conditional residual flow policy. Following anchor-guided warmup, ACT absorbs successful executed actions through behavioral cloning, while residual targets are recomputed relative to the updated prior. A chunk-level critic learns from successful and failed interactions and evaluates final composed actions. Its estimates weight generated residual candidates for flow matching, complemented by supervision from successful real executions. Candidate scoring is confined to training; deployment uses RGB observations and samples one residual per action chunk. We evaluate the framework on power-plug, USB, and key insertion, together with cable routing and needle threading. Results show a five-task mean success rate of 66% at 20,000 post-initialization interaction steps, exceeding the strongest baseline by 18 percentage points at this checkpoint. Evaluations at matched checkpoints with the residual disabled show ACT's mean success increasing from 16% to 76%. Component ablations further support the complementary roles of behavior absorption, value weighting, and successful-action supervision.

AFFORD2ACT: How Far Can Sparse Keypoints Go for Robot Manipulation? 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Vision-based manipulation policies are commonly learned from rich visual observations, typically using dense visual features extracted from full resolution images. It remains unclear how much of this visual information is really needed for effective policy learning. In this paper, we investigate the opposite end of the spectrum: how effective can manipulation policies be if they use as input only a compact visual representation. Sparse 2D keypoints provide a deliberately compact representation, giving the policy spatial information while removing direct access to appearance, texture, and background. We design AFFORD2ACT (Affordance-Guided Keypoints to Act) to study how much manipulation capability can be retained under this representation constraint. An off-the-shelf language-conditioned affordance model identifies the task-relevant region, from which up to 15 object keypoints are selected, together with 4 end-effector keypoints. The policy input is therefore reduced to only 19 2D keypoints (38 coordinates). A lightweight gating further decides which keypoints to pay attention to during execution. Across six real-robot manipulation tasks, this compact state outperforms the evaluated RGB, RGB-D, and dense-affordance behavior cloning baselines, degrades less as the number of demonstrations is reduced, and achieves 82% average success in held-out settings. Replacing learned gating with uniform weights reduces success from 92% to 52%. At the same time, the representation depends on reliable affordance localization and keypoint tracking, and remains vulnerable to incorrect masks, occlusion, tracking drift, and distractors.

ASH: Agents that Self-Hone in Long-Horizon Worlds 2026-10-20
📝 NeurIPS 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Long-horizon visuomotor tasks remain a fundamental challenge in AI, as current methods rely on hand-engineered rewards or action-labeled demonstrations, neither of which scales. We introduce ASH, an agentic system that learns a long-horizon policy from unlabeled, noisy internet video, without reward shaping or expert annotation. ASH follows a self-improvement loop; when it gets stuck, ASH learns an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) from its own trajectories, and uses its IDM to extract supervision from relevant internet video. ASH uses unsupervised learning to identify key moments from large-scale internet video and retains them as long-term memory - allowing it to tackle long-horizon problems. We evaluate ASH on two complementary environments demanding multi-hour planning: Pokemon Emerald, a turn-based RPG, and The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap, a real-time action-adventure game. In both games, behavioral cloning, retrieval-augmented and zero-shot foundation-model baselines plateau, while ASH sustains progression across our 8-hour evaluation. ASH reaches an average of 11.2/12 milestones in Pokemon Emerald and 9.9/12 in Legend of Zelda, while the strongest baseline gets stuck in both environments at an average of 9.3/12 and 7.8/12 milestones, respectively. We demonstrate that self-improving agents are a scalable recipe for learning in long-horizon worlds.

AWLS: Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering for One-Step Generative Robotic Manipulation Policies 2026-10-20
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026
Show abstract

Human-collected real-world robotic manipulation demonstrations often contain mixed-quality trajectories, including hesitant, indirect, or inefficient action segments. While diffusion- and flow-based policies can model such multimodal data, standard behavior cloning (BC) assigns equal likelihood to all demonstrated actions and may therefore reproduce suboptimal modes. Offline RL value estimates offer a natural signal for biasing policies toward higher-value behavioral modes, but directly fine-tuning the generative policy or using critic gradients for policy steering can amplify critic errors and induce prior drift. We propose Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering (AWLS), an offline post-training method for improving frozen one-step generative manipulation policies without updating their decoders. AWLS learns a residual noise shaper, an observation-conditioned residual correction to the policy's latent noise, and trains it with an advantage-weighted action reconstruction objective. The advantages are detached, scale-normalized, and used only as supervised weights on demonstrated actions, avoiding critic-gradient policy updates while preserving fast single-step inference. We further introduce a rollout-free decoder-sensitivity criterion for selecting pretrained checkpoints that remain responsive to latent-noise perturbations. Experiments on simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks with clean and mixed-quality demonstrations show that AWLS improves over the frozen base policy while limiting drift from the pretrained behavior prior. Projected Distillation: On Learning to Solve Hard Problems from Close, Correct Targets 2026-10-20
Show abstract

On-policy learning is central to post-training: training on a model's own generations limits distribution shift and helps preserve existing capabilities. On hard problems, however, on-policy RL provides no reward-based learning signal when all attempts fail. On-policy distillation provides next-token supervision from a teacher, but on hard problems, if a rollout goes wrong early, subsequent supervision is conditioned on prefixes that already contain the mistake. Correct behavior can instead come from a gold or teacher solution, but such off-policy targets can differ substantially from the model's generations and, as we show, may erode existing capabilities. We therefore treat on-policyness as a continuum and propose projected distillation, which minimally edits the student's failed attempts into correct targets. The method uses contrastive updates to favor corrections over failed attempts and refreshes these corrections as the student evolves, enabling near-on-policy learning even when all student attempts fail. Theoretically, we prove that the student's learning regret grows with the edit size: it scales linearly with the horizon under minimal correction, as in DAgger, and quadratically under full-answer replacement, as in behavior cloning. Empirically, across two students (Qwen3-8B and OLMo-2-7B) and five domains spanning math, code, science, and tool use, projected distillation gives the highest area under the specialization-retention frontier (best on 8 of 9 cells against GRPO and OPD, and 7 of 9 against SPOT and SODA). Our ablations highlight the importance of online corrections, the contrastive loss, and on-policyness: moving targets farther off-policy steadily worsens the specialization-retention trade-off, with retention falling from 0.916 to 0.744 when minimal corrections are replaced by student-agnostic gold solutions.

VaDar: Implicit Goal Conditioning via Value Disaggregation 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Long-horizon tasks are important for modern AI and robotics, yet pose a challenge for reinforcement learning. Historically, a popular approach has been to decompose a task into subgoals, and learn a subgoal-conditioned policy to execute each subtask in sequence. We introduce an alternative called Value Disaggregation (VaDar), which trains a single subgoal-independent policy to maximize a linear combination of per-subgoal critic values. Because subgoal information is needed only at training-time, VaDar removes the need for possibly costly or cumbersome subgoal-selection during policy deployment. Moreover, through careful experiments, we show that VaDar (1) is always competitive with, and frequently outperforms, natural baselines like goal-conditioning and (2) succeeds on tasks with non-sequential goal structure and multiple optimization objectives. In particular, VaDar is the first algorithm to solve challenging, long-horizon tasks in the RoboCasa suite, even when starting from a policy pretrained from behavior cloning with near-zero initial task success. Taken together, VaDar's successes suggest that subgoal information benefits actor-critic reinforcement learning by enhancing the efficacy of critic learning, whereas policy goal-conditioning is often unnecessary.

ALIGN: Aligning Condition Estimation and Expression in Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Human-collected robot manipulation data often mix successful behavior with retries, pauses, recoveries, and failures. Advantage-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (AC-BC) can exploit such mixed-quality data, but only if the conditioning signal is both accurately constructed from offline trajectories and effectively incorporated into the policy's action-vector-field prediction. Existing pipelines can weaken this signal by thresholding estimator-derived advantage scores into noisy conditions, and by routing conditions through prompts that weakly affect the action vector field. We introduce ALIGN, a condition-aligned AC-BC framework for vision-language-action policies. ALIGN directly predicts categorical pairwise conditions in the policy-facing label space and injects them into a DiT action expert through AdaRMS modulation fused with flow time. We evaluate ALIGN on deliberately mixed real-robot datasets for ARX X5 bowl stacking and Franka key hanging, each combining standard demonstrations with retry-heavy trajectories. ALIGN improves condition fidelity, strengthens conditional vector-field separability, and achieves 90.0% and 56.7% closed-loop success, compared with 56.7% and 33.3% for the strongest AC-BC baseline. These results indicate that aligning conditions from estimation to action prediction is an effective way to extract robust manipulation policies from data in which success, recovery and error coexist.

Long-Horizon Multi-Object Rearrangement via Distilled Tree Search over Continuous Actions 2026-10-19
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 (Oral). Project page: https://distilled-search.github.io
Show abstract

Despite recent advances in policy learning, long-horizon manipulation remains difficult because learned policies must avoid compounding errors while preserving future feasibility. While search-based planning can explicitly reason over such future consequences, it quickly becomes expensive in high-dimensional continuous action spaces. This raises a central question: how can long-horizon reasoning from search be transferred into policies in continuous action spaces? To this end, we propose DISCO: Distilled Tree Search over Continuous Actions. DISCO uses progressive-widening MCTS in physics simulation to generate long-horizon plans. During search, a learned policy proposes continuous actions, while a learned value function scores actions by their expected progress toward task completion. Using GPU-parallelized simulation, DISCO periodically distills MCTS-generated plans into the policy-value model, improving the action proposals and value estimates used in subsequent searches. Finally, the learned policy-value model can be directly deployed without the need for simulator-based search at test time. Across simulated and real-world rearrangement tasks, DISCO outperforms behavior cloning, reinforcement learning, and search-based baselines.

TILDE: Teacher-in-the-Loop Distillation from Embodied Foundation Models 2026-10-18
📝 Project page: https://hku-sail.github.io/TILDE
Show abstract

Large-scale action pretraining equips Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs) with strong manipulation capabilities, with particular strengths in generalization and closed-loop robustness, but reproducing such pretraining requires substantial robot data and computation. To transfer these capabilities from pretrained EFMs to policies without action pretraining, we introduce TILDE (Teacher-In-the-Loop Distillation from EFMs), which synergizes on-policy distillation at student-induced states with learning from teacher-executed recovery trajectories. Scene2Demo: Self-Evolving Embodied Data Generation via Object-Action Graph 2026-10-17
Show abstract

We present Scene2Demo, an automated framework for offline embodied data generation. Given a single RGB scene image and a user query, Scene2Demo constructs an interactive simulated scene and generates executable task configurations, primitive action flows, multi-view execution videos, and offline robot-learning datasets. Its five role-specialized generation modules perform task understanding, symbolic decomposition, BDDL configuration generation, object-size adjustment, and action-flow planning. The resulting object-centric task specifications and action transitions are organized as path search over a scene-conditioned object-action graph. Failed or incomplete executions are refined by two feedback agents: a Robotic Safety Inspector that produces step-wise visual critiques and a Robotic Task Supervisor that revises action sequences or parameters using the rollout and previous evolution history. Across 102 automatically generated primitive scene-task pairs, Scene2Demo achieves a 71.6% execution success rate; on four representative long-horizon tasks, feedback-driven refinement improves both task success and subtask-level execution quality over primitive-only execution. Comparisons with RoboGen and GenSim2 show stronger execution performance under automated data-generation settings. Finally, behavior cloning policies achieve 96.0% and 92.0% success on two representative tasks, showing that the generated data supports downstream policy learning. Our project page is available at https://scene2demo-anon.github.io/.

ADEPT: Accelerating Dexterity via Pre-Training and Post-Training using Reinforcement Learning 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026 | Project page: https://adept-dexterity.github.io/
Show abstract

We introduce Accelerating Dexterity via Pre-Training (ADEPT), a large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) framework for learning sim-to-real transferable dexterity across high degree-of-freedom (DoF) robot embodiments that can solve long-horizon tasks directly from raw visuo-tactile perception. ADEPT pretrains a dexterous policy on a generic object reposing task, then post-trains downstream policies with this pretrained behavior as a prior. ADEPT enables learning new behaviors that are otherwise difficult to discover from scratch on multi-fingered robots and avoids learning the same set of skills over again for every new downstream task. The pretrained policy zero-shots the reposing phase of downstream tasks, but naïve RL fine-tuning rapidly degrades this capability during transfer. We address this with a stable post-training recipe combining behavior-cloning distillation, critic warm-up, and conservative on-policy updates. To safely exploit the full kinematic dexterity, we introduce a joint-space Geometric Fabric that mediates between the RL policy and the robot. We distill post-trained teachers into perceptive students that zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on two embodiments: a 23 DoF Kuka-Allegro with two RGB cameras, and a 29 DoF Flexiv-Sharpa with two RGB cameras and five vision-based tactile sensors, and can solve long-horizon tasks from challenging initial states with dexterity at human-level speed.

Dexterity from Supervised Play 2026-10-15
Show abstract

How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures 2026-10-15
Show abstract

When an LLM agent misbehaves, a human steps in, and that intervention is usually discarded or replayed as training data. We treat it instead as a measurement: a label pointing at the internal computation that failed. We study it in a controlled agent environment, on one failure (asking a clarifying question when the request was already unambiguous), with labels from a rule-based overseer validated against blind human judgment. Claims cover eligible models, which both over-ask and sometimes act on matched unambiguous requests: five of thirteen qualified (Qwen2.5-3B, gemma-2-2b, OLMo-3-7B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, Phi-4). Four findings. First, the ask-vs-act decision is causally localizable in every eligible family: a direction learned from intervention labels flips 72-100% of held-out over-asks at a definite residual-stream depth, with site and dose chosen on training data, and random directions under the same procedure flip at most one episode. Second, over-asking has two mechanistic signatures: in four families the model demonstrably knows the request is unambiguous (the belief is decodable, causally inert and orthogonal to the decision) and asks anyway; in the fifth, the rare over-asks track different world-representations. Third, steering gated on a probe readout removes most of the simulated oversight burden while legitimate questions survive (bootstrap 95% intervals exclude zero); a prompt instruction makes it worse. Fourth, consolidating the fix into the weights yields a map, not a rule: on one model a LoRA confined to the causal band passes every criterion fixed in advance while behavior cloning on identical data breaks retention; on three families the reverse holds; on the fifth the outcome crosses the boundary across seeds. A geometric rule written down in advance to predict the regime failed on two out-of-family tests; we report the failure and stop rather than fit a replacement.

X-Fly: Learning to Reach and Stop in Full-Chunk Aerial Vision-Language Navigation 2026-10-15

Aerial vision-language navigation (VLN) agents built on large vision-language models are expensive to query. Executing a complete predicted action chunk before replanning amortizes this cost, reducing model inference from 195 to 42 ms per executed waypoint for our policy. LASER: Latent Space Adjoint Matching for Support-Constrained Entropy-Regularized Offline RL 2026-10-14
📝 29 pages, 16 figures. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

While offline reinforcement learning (RL) enables policy optimization from static datasets without costly online interaction, it remains bottlenecked by the risk of executing out-of-distribution (OOD) actions. Recent approaches mitigate this by learning a behavior-cloning policy through flow matching and then performing RL within its constrained latent space. However, naively optimizing the latent policy can easily cause the policy to collapse into a brittle mode or exploit sharp artifacts of the learned critic. In this work, we find that entropy regularization is essential in latent-space RL for addressing these challenges. Through comprehensive experiments on 40 challenging OGBench tasks with varying dataset qualities, we show that LASER achieves state-of-the-art performance. Notably, LASER uses fixed method-specific hyperparameters across all tasks and outperforms the evaluated baselines, including those with task- and dataset-specific tuning, which highlights the robust applicability of LASER. Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-14
📝 Accepted at CoRL 2026. 25 pages, 16 figures, including appendix. Project page: https://gr-bc.github.io/
