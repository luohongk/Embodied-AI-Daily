ZeroVLN: Learning to Navigate without Supervised Fine-Tuning of the VLM Brain 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Representative vision-language navigation (VLN) methods typically couple a vision-language model (VLM) brain with a learnable action head and fine-tune the VLM brain on navigation-domain data to predict geometric goals. However, such adaptation can substantially alter base VLM weights and degrade general visual capabilities. We introduce ZeroVLN, a slow-fast navigation framework that achieves effective navigation with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain. Rather than teaching the brain to predict geometric goals, we equip it with a semantic tool-calling interface for conveying movement decisions to an action expert in natural language. We first adapt the action expert to carry out these movement requests, then jointly improve the brain's movement decisions and the action expert's continuous execution through closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). Trained on the same navigation episodes, ZeroVLN substantially outperforms representative SFT-based hierarchical VLN methods on standard VLN-CE (continuous environments) benchmarks, while inducing only minor changes to the brain's weights and largely preserving its general capabilities. To our knowledge, ZeroVLN is the first slow-fast VLN framework with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain, and the first to jointly optimize its slow and fast policies through closed-loop agentic RL in VLN-CE.

EmbodyAgent: Post-Training Embodied VLM Agents via Distillation, SFT, and RL 2026-11-19
📝 11 pages, 4 figures. Code-as-policy robot-use agent post-training
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) can act as closed-loop robot-use agents (RUAs) by writing and executing control code through tool interfaces. However, turning an open-weight VLM into a high-performing embodied agent requires a complete post-training stack: out-of-the-box open VLMs fail completely at interactive tool contracts, strong frontier teachers are accessible only as black-box endpoints without exposed weights or token probabilities, and supervised imitation alone plateaus under rollout distribution shift. We present EmbodyAgent, an end-to-end post-training framework with three tightly coupled pipelines: (1) a black-box data synthesis pipeline that queries a strong teacher under a forced think-then-code contract and filters trajectories by task success; (2) an SFT pipeline that distills the explanation-and-program targets into an open-weight 9B VLM with both vision and language encoders trainable; and (3) an online RL pipeline that optimizes student-generated episodes via group-relative advantage with a runtime-error-penalized outcome reward. All three pipelines share one primitive-free Python execution tool exposing only raw visual observation and seven-dimensional end-effector control, with no action decoder and no interface shift across stages. Under a strictly separated evaluation protocol on ten LIBERO Spatial tasks (disjoint state splits, greedy test-time decoding, training-only safeguards), held-out success on 100 test states rises from 0/100 (base 9B VLM) to 14/100 (after SFT) and 84/100 (after RL), while training rollout success climbs from 8.4% to 87.7% and median model calls per episode drop from 36 to 11. We further identify a self-reinforcing runtime-error mode induced by outcome-based RL on executable code, and establish a failure-category monitoring and sampling-side curation protocol that stabilizes training.

SpatialScout-V2: Can VLM Agents Actively Explore Real-World 3D Scenes and Localize Objects? 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Spatial intelligence requires agents to acquire visual evidence and integrate partial observations into coherent three-dimensional representations. We introduce SpatialScout-V2, which extends SpatialScout's active mapping task from simulation to real residential scenes through three advances: real visual observations, preservation of original scene content, and interactive exploration of recorded views. Built from 100 homes in Realsee3D, the benchmark covers 3,171 target objects across 29 categories. Given a fixed target inventory, agents autonomously select recorded viewpoints and viewing directions, integrate calibrated RGB observations, and iteratively estimate and revise object centers within a fixed interaction budget. We evaluate the localization accuracy and completeness of object-center maps using mean IoU, IoU-threshold hit rates, and target coverage. Evaluating seven frontier vision-language models (VLMs), we find that six achieve an all-target mean IoU below 20\%. Even the best-performing model, Claude Opus 5.5, reaches only 22.52\%, with just 18.36\% of targets exceeding an IoU of 0.5. Further analysis shows that broad submission coverage can coexist with limited localization accuracy, while target omissions further reduce overall scores. These results highlight substantial challenges in constructing accurate and complete object-center maps through active exploration of real scenes.

CAG-VLM: Fine-Tuning of a Large-Scale Model to Recognize Angiographic Images for Next-Generation Diagnostic Systems 2026-11-19
📝 The 4th Workshop on Computer Vision in the Wild
Show abstract

Coronary angiography (CAG) is the gold-standard imaging modality for evaluating coronary artery disease, but its interpretation and subsequent treatment planning rely heavily on expert cardiologists. To enable AI-based decision support, we introduce a two-stage, physician-curated pipeline and a bilingual (Japanese/English) CAG image-report dataset. First, we sample 14,686 frames from 539 exams and annotate them for key-frame detection and left/right laterality; a ConvNeXt-Base CNN trained on this data achieves 0.96 F1 on laterality classification, even on low-contrast frames. Second, we apply the CNN to 243 independent exams, extract 1,114 key frames, and pair each with its pre-procedure report and expert-validated diagnostic and treatment summary, yielding a parallel corpus. We then fine-tune three open-source VLMs (PaliGemma2, Gemma3, and ConceptCLIP-enhanced Gemma3) via LoRA and evaluate them using VLScore and cardiologist review. Although PaliGemma2 w/LoRA attains the highest VLScore, Gemma3 w/LoRA achieves the top clinician rating (mean 7.20/10); we designate this best-performing model as CAG-VLM. These results demonstrate that specialized, fine-tuned VLMs can effectively assist cardiologists in generating clinical reports and treatment recommendations from CAG images.

Rank2Replace: VLM-Ranked Slot Replacement for Out-of-Distribution Generalization 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Spurious correlations can give image predictors high in-distribution accuracy but poor generalization under distribution shift. We introduce $\textbf{Rank2Replace}$, which transfers knowledge of task-relevant evidence from pretrained vision--language models (VLMs) to small predictors through training-time augmentation for out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. The VLM ranks object-centric slots by task relevance and guides rank-dependent replacement with same-rank slot embeddings from another image. This augmentation retains the original image's label, encouraging reliance on preserved task evidence as incidental cues change, and supports combination with existing OOD methods. Across four datasets and six splits under shared frozen slot embeddings, the default cross-entropy configuration reaches macro-averaged average-group and worst-group accuracies of $80.43\%$ and $65.28\%$ without environment labels or dataset-specific replacement tuning. Slot corruption improves both macro-averaged metrics for all fourteen OOD training methods. Combining it with slot ranking and IRM reaches $81.96\%$ and $72.68\%$. Controlled ablations support the ranking and replacement design, with gains across five ranking VLMs and six extractor--predictor combinations. Permutation-invariant predictors further improve both macro-averaged metrics over their unaugmented counterparts while using VLM guidance only during training for efficient inference.

O-RAN enables a disaggregated baseband stack with programmable functions that communicate over standardized open interfaces. The same openness that enables multi-vendor composition also expands the attack surface across logically decoupled tiers that make up the compute continuum. Among these threats, Denial-of-Service and performance-degradation attacks, which account for the majority of catalogued O-RAN threats, are particularly difficult to detect. Traditional Time-Series Anomaly Detection (TSAD) methods fail in this new regime where labelled baselines are scarce, threats evolve faster than detectors can be retrained, and the high-dimensional multivariate telemetry overwhelms monolithic inference models. To address these challenges, we present DAST, a zero-shot multi-agent framework for cross-interface anomaly detection in O-RAN that chains a three-stage VLM $\rightarrow$ LLM $\rightarrow$ VLM pipeline. DAST converts multivariate KPI streams into visual representations, scores textual per-interface descriptions against O-RAN domain knowledge, and verifies suspects on high-resolution heatmaps to output the problematic interfaces, the anomalous time intervals, an indicative O-RAN WG11-aligned operational impact rating and the decision rationale. We evaluate DAST on real network traces collected from an O-RAN testbed under representative performance degradation scenarios, achieving 0.910 F1-Score and 0.843 Accuracy, outperforming state-of-the-art TSAD baselines.

Do Thinking VLMs Need to Keep Looking at Long Videos? Write It Down, Then Look Away 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) reason about long videos over hundreds to thousands of tokens. Every decoding step re-reads the key-value (KV) cache of the entire video. Must the model keep looking while it reasons? In LookAway, the model first writes timestamped notes on what the question needs and then puts the video away. Once the notes close, the inference engine hides the video's KV cache for the rest of the response. Across both hybrid and pure-attention VLMs, dependence on the video is concentrated early in the response. On Qwen3.6-27B, writing notes preserves accuracy better than free reasoning at the same withdrawal step. Replacing them with another video's notes harms accuracy, whereas keeping the video visible alongside the model's own notes adds no significant benefit. Without additional training, LookAway nearly matches full-video thinking accuracy at 0.12-0.13 times its modeled decode cost. To shorten this initial look, we fine-tune the model on its own correct LookAway responses. Reinforcement learning then rewards correct answers while penalizing KV reads. The trained model puts the video away after a median of 66-68 tokens. Across three held-out benchmarks, it writes more tokens than an identically trained full-video model yet reads only 27-35% as many KV positions at matched accuracy.

Eyes on VLM: Benchmarking Gaze Following and Social Gaze Prediction in Vision Language Models 2026-11-18
📝 NeurIPS ED 2026
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) have rapidly evolved into general-purpose multimodal reasoners with strong zero-shot generalization. In this context, VLMs could greatly benefit the analysis of human gaze and attention, a central task in human behavior understanding that requires reasoning about the physical scene as well as the activity, interactions, and social context. However, the extent to which VLMs can reliably understand human gaze and related attentional behaviors remains largely unexplored. In this work, we present EyeVLM, a systematic evaluation framework for gaze understanding in VLMs across two complementary dimensions: tasks and models. To assess gaze understanding capabilities, we focus on two core tasks. The first, gaze following, i.e., predicting the 2D location where a person is looking, has a geometric and visual processing focus, requiring a precise understanding of the human face, attention direction, 3D scene structure, and spatial grounding of attended targets. The second, social gaze prediction, requires social and relational reasoning over multi-person interactions (e.g., mutual gaze and shared attention), and may benefit more from the LLM semantic reasoning capabilities within VLMs. Regarding models, EyeVLM evaluates these tasks in two ways: a zero-shot setting with a diverse set of state-of-the-art open- and closed-source VLMs, exploring different prompting strategies; and a fine-tuning approach based on task-specific QA pairs, studying the impact of model scale and data scale. As benchmarks, we rely on existing gaze understanding datasets and perform a systematic comparison with state-of-the-art purely visual models. Overall, our results show that current VLMs lack precise gaze understanding capabilities. While standard training helps reduce the gap with visual models, significant improvements are still needed. Code available at https://github.com/idiap/EyeVLM.

EgoJev: Finding Evidence in Egocentric Video by Reading VLM Decisions 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Questions about long egocentric video often depend on one brief moment, such as the second a hand puts an object down. A vision-language model (VLM) that sees a few evenly spaced frames usually misses it. Training-free methods rank frames with a separate image-text model or ask the VLM to write a time. In a controlled test, the VLM itself locates the moment better when it chooses among labeled frames, and we read this choice from its output distribution. With the same candidates, this choice lands on the evidence more often than image-text scores or a written time span, and it compares all candidates in one decision. Based on this finding, we propose EgoJev, a training-free frame selector for wearable assistants. EgoJev reads where the evidence is from labeled thumbnails and looks again at the windows it points to. It places its few answer frames at the quantiles of the resulting distribution. One frozen VLM selects, answers and returns the evidence time, with no second model. A wearable device can encode its coarse thumbnails while it records. With Qwen3.5-4B and 8 answer frames, EgoJev raises QAEgo4D accuracy from 51.2% with uniform sampling to 62.0%, and it outperforms recent training-free frame selectors on QAEgo4D and MyEgo. A single-level version runs on a Mac and an iPhone.

Aura: Consistent Multi-Subject Video Generation via VLM-Grounded Semantic Alignment 2026-11-18
📝 Project page: https://aura-project-page.github.io/ Code: https://github.com/Camellia997/Aura
Show abstract

Subject-driven and multi-element video generation are central to controllable video synthesis, but existing methods still struggle to preserve identity consistency and model complex relationships among multiple subjects. In this paper, we propose Aura, a unified framework for high-fidelity and identity-consistent video generation. To better capture scene dynamics and subject interactions, we introduce AI director-level captions that provide dense and structured descriptions of video content. We further leverage a vision-language model (VLM) with learnable queries to extract multimodal semantic features from textual and visual references, covering both global semantics and fine-grained visual cues. To bridge the representational gap between the VLM and the Diffusion Transformer (DiT), we design a two-stage alignment strategy that progressively maps VLM features into the DiT feature space. For visual conditioning, we adopt token concatenation to inject reference information directly into the generation process. To distinguish heterogeneous subject types and reduce common copy-paste artifacts, we develop a subject-aware RoPE-Shift mechanism. To further differentiate reference images of different categories, we introduce subject-aware learnable tokens. In addition, we introduce Memory Tokens to balance the training signal across examples with different numbers of reference subjects. During inference, Progressive-APG (Adaptive Prompt Guidance) further alleviates oversaturation and improves semantic alignment with user prompts. Finally, we build a high-quality video-subject image dataset through a dedicated data construction pipeline. Extensive experiments show that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on both single-subject generation and more challenging multi-element scenarios.

Teaching VLMs What to Say, Not How to Reason: Rethinking Counterfactual Reasoning in Autonomous Driving 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 Main Track (41 pages, 14 figures, 14 tables). Project page: https://ophd-hahao.github.io/projects/Rethink-CF/
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving systems have been shown to suffer from shortcut learning and causal confusion, limiting their ability to generalize robustly under distribution shift. To mitigate these limitations, recent works based on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have explored incorporating linguistic reasoning capabilities into driving decision-making, reporting improvements in driving performance. In particular, counterfactual (CF) reasoning has been proposed to enable self-reflective, high-level causal understanding. However, it remains unclear whether the language-based reasoning mitigates shortcut learning or merely shifts it from visual representations to linguistic patterns, creating an illusion of causal understanding. Moreover, existing approaches often conflate intervention-based reasoning with CF reasoning, leaving the effects of genuine CF reasoning unexplored. In this work, we examine what CF reasoning actually induces in VLM-based autonomous driving and observe that it shifts the action distribution toward more conservative behaviors while increasing uncertainty in reasoning and decision-making. This effect persists under reinforcement fine-tuning, suggesting that it is not merely a transient artifact. Furthermore, visual attention analysis shows that CF reasoning does not alter visual grounding, while causal intervention experiments reveal that masking causally relevant and non-causal factors in accident scenarios results in comparable decision change rates. Overall, these results suggest that CF reasoning does not induce genuine causal reasoning grounded in visual evidence. Instead, it acts as a linguistic shortcut that reshapes the action distribution, teaching models what to say but not how to reason, highlighting a gap between language-based reasoning and causal understanding in autonomous driving.

Learning When to Think: Dual-Reference Offline Optimization for Adaptive VLM Reasoning 2026-11-18
📝 Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Reasoning traces improve vision-language models on complex multimodal tasks, but forcing every input to follow a long reasoning process leads to overthinking and unnecessary inference cost. We study adaptive fast/slow thinking for VLMs, where one model answers directly for simple inputs and reasons step by step when needed. Fast answers and slow traces often come from distinct behavior policies, making a single-reference offline objective misweight one mode. We propose DUAL-REFERENCE BOLT (DR-BOLT), which distinguishes the desired fast/slow mixture reference from the sampling distribution and corrects their mismatch in reward-weighted training. Fast/slow partition competition explains routing in the optimal target; an efficient source-wise correction approximates the exact objective when response formats are well separated. Matched Qwen3-VL comparisons use identical candidate banks to isolate the objective from slow-teacher quality. DR-BOLT improves score-length trade-offs over the evaluated offline and online baselines on ScienceQA and three held-out transfer benchmarks, with a 24.1% cross-benchmark slow ratio and a 2.90-fold end-to-end training speedup over reproduced GRPO. These results support adaptive reasoning in the studied two-reference setting, while broader robustness remains open. Our code is available at https://github.com/HongbinLin3589/DR-BOLT.

SEE: Unlocking the Hidden Perception of Frozen VLMs 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) grasp the overall content of an image but often miss the fine details a question hinges on, such as a small object in a large photo or a number in a dense infographic. Yet even when their answer is wrong, the region they point to often contains the evidence. We introduce Self-Elicited Evidence (SEE), a training-free method that lets a frozen VLM look again where it points. When the initial answer has low confidence, SEE prompts the model to propose regions where the details may appear, answers the question on these enlarged crops, and aggregates the answers with the original prediction through confidence-weighted voting. The model also answers with its own transcript, which helps on text-rich images. SEE needs no detector or OCR engine. Across five backbones, SEE improves every small-object benchmark by 2.3-13.1 points and outperforms five training-free baselines on small objects. With SEE, Qwen3.5-4B surpasses the direct answer of Qwen3.5-9B and matches the 6 times larger Qwen3.5-27B.

Visual Harness Scales General VLMs to Medical Images 2026-11-18
Show abstract

We show that an appropriate visual harness can substantially improve the performance of general vision-language models (VLMs) across diverse medical imaging tasks. We introduce VERA, a medical visual harness that equips general VLMs with interactive visual tools and systematic diagnostic evidence management. With VERA, general VLMs interactively explore medical images and progressively acquire diagnostic evidence, which is preserved in a lossless memory for subsequent retrieval and use. After submitting an initial answer, the VLM receives an assessment of potential omissions and the reliability of identified findings. This assessment guides further image inspection and measurement, followed by answer revision in an iterative agentic loop until sufficient evidence is obtained. Across 11 tasks and four imaging modalities, VERA improves performance on all tasks with Qwen3.8-27B, with notable gains in spleen measurement (63.3% to 98.3%), renal search (57.5% to 71.2%), and pulmonary nodule detection (0.0% to 28.2%). High-capability API-based VLMs achieve even higher performance with VERA. For example, GPT-6.1 Sol improves pulmonary nodule detection from 10.8% to 69.2% with VERA, demonstrating the complementary benefits of stronger model capabilities and an effective medical visual harness. Our results highlight the potential of visual harnesses as a new paradigm for scaling general VLMs to medical imaging tasks.

Keep the Reasoning, Read the Image: On-Demand Visual KV for Multi-Turn Reasoning VLMs 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Reasoning vision-language models (VLMs) think for thousands of tokens before they answer, and retention policies keep the KV cache affordable by pruning it as the reasoning grows. We find that this pruning sacrifices the image in multi-turn conversations: the image and the reasoning share one budget, so the growing reasoning crowds the visual entries out. A chart question that loses 8 points to the full cache when asked first loses 24 points when asked after a round of reasoning. Protecting a small visual reserve from eviction recovers most of this loss, while adding the same slots to the shared budget recovers considerably less. The image therefore lacks protection, not capacity. Based on this finding, we propose ShelfKV, a training-free method that works on top of existing retention policies. It keeps every visual entry in CPU memory and, at each decoding step, swaps the visual entries that the current query scores highest into the attention slots of the text entries it scores lowest. The image is never evicted, yet it occupies attention slots only while it is read. We evaluate ShelfKV on four benchmarks, three of them conversational, with two retention policies and two reasoning VLMs. In conversations, it improves the strongest existing policy, DBTrimKV, by up to 20.3 points at matched GPU memory, and still leads by 8.7 points when DBTrimKV uses twice the memory. It also consistently outperforms a fixed visual reserve of matched GPU memory in these settings, without hurting accuracy on standalone questions. These results show that the image should be protected from the reasoning but read only on demand.

Bits, Not Boxes: Label-Free Layer Selection for Visual Localization in VLMs 2026-11-17
📝 35 pages, 9 figures, 56 tables
Show abstract

Attention maps cheaply show where a vision-language model (VLM) looks, but attention sinks make them unreliable for localization. Contrasting the question with a generic prompt suppresses sinks, yet only some layers localize the queried object. Existing methods find these layers with box annotations or fixed rules. We show that attention itself can identify them. A question changes how much a head attends to the image and where this attention goes. The two effects separate exactly, and the second, measured in bits on unlabeled questions, indicates which layers localize. WhereBits turns these where-to-look bits into a layer prior and combines it with per-head contrastive scores. Its three design constants were fixed once on labeled COCO data, and a new model or resolution needs only unlabeled questions. Across eight VLMs, WhereBits matches the pointing accuracy of box-tuned layers on COCO and RefCOCO and ranks target tokens better. This advantage holds on a held-out model family and at higher resolutions. Its COCO pointing accuracy is 0.72, against 0.22 for mean attention. In training-free zoom-in, its crops raise V* accuracy by 6.0 points under forced-choice evaluation.

Uncovering Faithfulness Gaps in VLMs via Explanation-Driven Counterfactual Testing 2026-11-17
📝 Accepted to WACV 2027. 16 pages, including appendix
Show abstract

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can produce Natural Language Explanations (NLEs) that sound plausible yet remain inconsistent with the visual evidence they cite. We present Explanation-Driven Counterfactual Testing (EDCT), an intervention-based protocol that extracts visual concepts cited in a model's explanation, applies verified minimal edits to them, and tests whether the resulting answer and explanation remain consistent with the edited image. Using this protocol, we create EDCT-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark spanning three complementary domains: knowledge-intensive visual question answering (OK-VQA), safety-critical driving (DriveLM), and 3D spatial reasoning (3DSRBench). Across the evaluated VLMs, EDCT reveals substantial faithfulness gaps, with models frequently producing responses inconsistent with verified visual changes. Finally, our fine-tuning study suggests that EDCT-generated counterfactuals provide high-impact training signals.

Explore, Map, Remember, Decide: A Benchmark for VLM Readiness in Safety-Critical Scenarios 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are often deployed as embodied controllers for robotics, yet it remains unclear how well they can explore an environment, maintain an internal map, and ground safety-critical decisions in long-horizon sequential trajectories. In this work, we introduce EMRD, an evaluation framework that decouples spatial reasoning into four stages: Explore (what the agent observes), Map (what it records and where), Remember (what it retains), and Decide (whether its final choice is justified), scored across 18 stage-specific metrics. We use EMRD to evaluate eleven open- and closed-weight VLMs across 16 buildings under nominal conditions and three environmental stresses (darkness, smoke, and motion noise), where an embodied VLM explores unknown rooms, maps observed objects, and selects the safest gathering place. Open-weight models leave rooms unvisited in 95% of episodes and detect at most 51% of objects; closed-weight models explore more, though with an external memory (notepad), two of the three stop prematurely. Without external memory, most VLMs keep fewer of the objects they saw, especially in larger buildings. The start limits which rooms are explored, and open-weight models started in different rooms agree on the final room not significantly more often than chance. Moreover, these choices are biased by semantic priors: swapping a room's label is often enough to change the final choice. Finally, motion noise increases object position error (by 12-60% for closed-weight models), strong darkness lowers map recall by 28-31 percentage points, and dense smoke reduces seen objects by 25-39 points, while the choice among the rooms entered does not change significantly.

VLM-guided Grounding Expert for Generalised Referring Expression Comprehension 2026-11-17
📝 13 pages, 5 tables, 5 figures
Show abstract

Generalised referring expression comprehension (GREC) aims to identify and localise the zero, one or multiple objects referred to by a language expression. In robotic manipulation, grounding also needs to remain accurate when requests for the same objects are paraphrased or embedded in additional context in robotics. However, grounding expert models are sensitive to instruction variation, while generative vision--language models (VLMs) struggle with complete target recovery and rejection of absent targets. These complementary limitations motivate combining the contextual representations of VLMs with the direct spatial prediction capabilities of grounding experts. We therefore introduce VLM-guided Grounding Expert (VGE), which combines a frozen VLM with a trainable grounding expert through layerwise joint attention and text-conditioned instance scoring. These interfaces leverage multimodal understanding of the VLM to guide object-query refinement and candidate selection while retaining direct bounding-box prediction. Across three datasets, VGE achieves the highest averaged F1 score among the evaluated methods on all five test splits, with differences of at most 1.2 percentage points across instruction conditions within each split. On the dataset with robotic scenes, VGE also achieves the highest F1 across instruction variants and no-target, single-target and multiple-target cases. VGE provides a $5.3$--$13.0\times$ inference speedup over the baseline with the same VLM base model. These results support combining VLM context with specialised spatial prediction to address both grounding challenges, with particularly strong performance in robotic scenes. Code will be released upon publication.

Disagree to Prune: Reallocating Visual Tokens Where a Small VLM Dissents 2026-11-17
📝 45 pages, 8 in the main text. Code and data: https://github.com/LiZaiyuan0619/PRUDENT
Show abstract

Visual token pruning speeds up large vision-language models (VLMs) by keeping only a part of their visual tokens. Most existing methods improve the ranking of the tokens and then answer every question once with the same budget. With strong rankings, pruning hurts only a few questions, and signals computed on the kept tokens see little of what it removed. We find that, on most of these questions, a small VLM given the full image disagrees with the pruned answer, since it still sees the missing evidence. We propose PRUDENT, which first answers with about half the budget and spends the saved tokens on re-answering the disagreeing questions at twice the budget. We show when such re-answering pays and why the small VLM should read the full image. On 18 benchmarks, PRUDENT outperforms eight existing pruning methods at the same average budget of visual tokens. Its lead widens as the budget tightens, and holds across five InternVL sizes. PRUDENT also has the highest average in two more model families. The disagreement of the small VLM flags most pruning failures of all eight methods, and re-answering on it also improves the two strongest with their own rankings. Other checkers give similar gains, and the extra tokens go where the first pass hurts the answer. These results suggest that identifying which pruned answers are trustworthy provides an additional lever for visual token pruning beyond the ranking of tokens.
