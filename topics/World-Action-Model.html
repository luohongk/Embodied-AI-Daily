SilentWAM: Planning in the Masked State of a Diffusion World Model 2026-11-22
Show abstract

World-action models learn from future prediction, but accessing their future representations directly at test time can require costly frame generation. Methods that generate future frames before planning incur additional forward passes, while future co-training methods omit explicit future predictions at inference. We introduce \method, a planner that reads the hidden states of masked future tokens without decoding future frames. We pretrain MMaDA-Parallel-M on driving video using frame-level masked world modeling, which predicts a masked suffix of future frames and explicitly includes inputs with all future frames masked. Learned planning queries attend to these masked positions and the observed context, and a continuous head predicts the trajectory in the same forward pass. With a single front camera, \method reaches 92.0 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 89.3 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, with an end-to-end latency of 60.3\,ms.

ThinkWAM: Rethinking Latent Futures for Action Refinement 2026-11-21
📝 21 pages, 12 figures, 15 tables, including references and appendix. Project page: https://thinkwam-project.github.io/
Show abstract

World-action models (WAMs) couple action generation with predictions of future states. An initial action chunk may be flawed. A future prediction that is not conditioned on the chunk is identical for every proposal and thus carries no proposal-specific signal, limiting its usefulness for refinement before execution. We introduce ThinkWAM, a latent WAM that establishes bidirectional feedback before execution through a proposal-rethink-refine loop: the policy first proposes a complete action chunk, the proposal corrects multi-horizon future latents through a learned residual, and the corrected latents are injected into a second pass of the same action head, which generates the executable chunk. An affordance-guided observation-action memory and a near-fine, far-coarse multi-horizon prediction scheme, both motivated by predictive coding, support this loop, and all prediction stays in latent space. ThinkWAM achieves 98.70% success on LIBERO, 84.76% on LIBERO-Plus without OOD fine-tuning, 85.75%/65.85% on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean/Random, and 65.17% on RoboCasa24. ThinkWAM achieves 64.4% mean success across three real-world OOD conditions. The mean two-pass inference latency per action chunk is 153.48 ms on LIBERO, 164.61 ms on RoboTwin, and 101.2 ms on the Galaxea A1Z. Ablations show complementary gains from rethinking and affordance guidance. Diagnostics indicate that proposal-conditioned corrections change both predicted futures and action trajectories, showing that the corrected future representations improve the final action.

Probing Invisible Actuation Shifts for Test-time Calibration of Visuomotor Policies 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Visuomotor policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs) are increasingly deployed on robots whose cameras and controllers differ from those used to collect their training data. Such deployment introduces two kinds of shifts. Visual shifts, such as a moved camera, change the observed images; actuation shifts, such as a retuned controller, latency or an added payload, change only how commanded actions translate into robot motion. Invisible to the policy, actuation shifts sharply degrade its success, even under domain randomization. In this work, we propose PEA, a test-time calibration method that identifies actuation shifts through active probing before task execution. Specifically, before each episode, the robot executes a short probe that moves one arm axis at a time with known commands, so that gain, delay and drift leave separable traces. A closed-form identifier estimates them from the response, and PEA compensates every subsequent command so that the robot executes the motion the policy intends. The identifier requires no policy training and thus applies to any frozen visuomotor policy. Since visual shifts remain visible to the policy, PEA further adapts the frozen policy with a removable low-rank adapter, addressing coupled visual and actuation shifts. Experiments on LIBERO with a WAM and a VLA show that, without any policy training, the identifier of PEA recovers most of the success lost to actuation shifts and generalizes to shifts unseen during training (e.g., from 61.0% to 89.7% on LIBERO-Spatial with Cosmos-Policy).

La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling 2026-11-20
📝 31 pages, 6 figures. v2: full-RoboTwin results with scaling to 2.1B parameters, leave-one-out ablation, revised figures and text. Code: https://github.com/mt-clemente/lajepa
Show abstract

World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B. Its S and B policies run two to five times faster than Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM while outperforming both on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, and La-JEPA outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to $π_{0.5}$. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction improves the policy even when skipped at inference. Tailoring the policy's design, including its latent space, to control can thus matter more than the knowledge transferred from a pretrained generative backbone, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

MAWAM: A Multi-Agent World-Action Model for Coordinated Manipulation 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Every move in a robot team changes the world in which its teammates must act. Coordinated manipulation therefore calls for predicting the team's actions together with the physical future they jointly create. We introduce MAWAM, a multi-agent world-action model that jointly generates each robot's future views and executable action chunks in a single sample. Built from a pretrained single-robot world-action backbone, MAWAM jointly denoises all robots' action and future-view tokens through cross-agent attention, while preserving a separate control interface for each robot. MAWAM achieves the highest success on all three real-robot tasks (laundry-bag collection, bath-towel folding, and umbrella handover), with 67.8% mean success, 18.9 percentage points above MA-VLA trained on the same demonstrations and cameras. In simulation, MAWAM reaches 90.3% mean success on eight tasks with teams of up to four arms. Ablations show that cross-agent attention and team-stage future-video supervision both improve coordinated control: without attention, a four-arm relay falls from 95.0% to 39.0%, and without this supervision, Place Food falls from 78.0% to 47.0%.

Beyond Linear Decodability: Understanding and Improving Instruction Following in World Action Models 2026-11-20
📝 38 pages, including appendices. Project page: https://ruflect-intelligence.github.io/LDS-Homepage/
Show abstract

World action models (WAMs) exhibit strong manipulation capabilities yet often fail to follow test-time language instructions. Prior work shows that non-following (NF) WAMs encode information about the instructed target, but this information does not control their behavior. By comparing NF WAM with its instruction-following (IF) counterpart, we find that target information is substantially more linearly decodable in IF WAMs, despite being encoded in both. Motivated by this observation, we introduce linear decodability supervision (LDS), a simple yet effective auxiliary training objective that promotes the linear decodability of the instructed target information. On LIBERO-CF, InternW0-$Δ$ with LDS achieves a success rate of 74.6% and a grounding rate of 89.2%, surpassing the strongest baseline by 26.8 and 42.9 percentage points, respectively. The effectiveness of LDS challenges the prevailing interpretation that encoded target information does not contribute to WAMs' behavior. To reconcile this apparent contradiction, we propose a prior-instruction competition mechanism, which explains NF and IF behavior as arising from the dominance of prior and instruction signals, respectively. We provide causal evidence for this mechanism by evaluating two training-free interventions derived from it. We reinterpret LDS through this mechanism, showing that it acts as a form of instruction signal enhancement. Finally, more comprehensive evaluations across diverse manipulation tasks and visual domains demonstrate the consistent effectiveness of LDS.

ReActWAM: Action-Consistent Caching for Efficient World-Action Models 2026-11-20
📝 10 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

World-action models (WAMs) often generate future visual states alongside robot actions, introducing substantial computational overhead during inference. During closed-loop execution, consecutive inference cycles often predict similar action sequences, leading to redundant computation. However, reducing this redundancy requires distinguishing consistent actions from changes that demand fresh computation. To address these challenges, we propose ReActWAM, a training-free framework that combines action-intent-guided prefix skipping with inner-chunk prediction reuse. A coarse-to-fine assessment evaluates motion changes, visual consistency, and action prediction conflicts. For eligible chunks, we draw on first-step differences to adapt the cached latent and network features to the current condition, providing an estimate at a later denoising step that bypasses the redundant prefix. Subsequent inference refines this estimate while reusing stable denoising predictions within the chunk. Experiments across LingBot-VA, FastWAM and the Cosmos family on LIBERO-Long and RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrate $1.41\times$--$5.44\times$ GPU inference speedup, with minimal degradation in task success rates. Real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 (FR3) robot further achieve $1.46\times$ end-to-end speedup while maintaining success rate.

DIDO: Distilling Interaction-Centric Dynamics into One-Step Denoising for World Action Models 2026-11-20
📝 Preprint. 22 pages, 8 figures, 5 tables

Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) use video generation models to predict future visual dynamics for robotic manipulation, but iterative denoising introduces additional latency for closed-loop control. We empirically find that visual content converges at different rates during denoising. Static background structure forms early, whereas the gripper and manipulated object remain blurry after the first step, with their interaction dynamics emerging only through subsequent denoising. Consequently, naively truncating a multi-step video model to one step preserves scene structure but loses the interaction-centric dynamics most critical for manipulation. To address this issue, we propose DIDO, which distills the converged dynamics of a multi-step video model into a single denoising step. DIDO combines distribution matching distillation with interaction-centric representation guidance. Beyond compressing multi-step generation into one forward pass, DIDO explicitly models the gripper, manipulated object, and their interaction using supervised bounding-box visual reasoning tokens. Additionally, DIDO aligns the target object's representations across multiple model layers with features from a pretrained DINOv3 encoder. This interaction-centric guidance helps the distilled model preserve both the relevant entities and their future dynamics in a single step, while substantially reducing inference latency. DIDO achieves an average success rate of 99.0\% on LIBERO, 76.6\% on LIBERO-Plus, and 92.0\% on RoboTwin, while also demonstrating effective transfer to long-horizon and generalization tasks in real-world robotic manipulation.

Driving world-action models generate planned trajectories through iterative action denoising, with each update depending on the preceding state. This sequential dependency incurs substantial inference latency, limiting timely planning. Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.

Phoenix: Learning Scene Structure Representations for Generalizable World-Action Models 2026-11-20
Show abstract

World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet reconstruction-oriented visual representations do not explicitly emphasize the spatial relationships and object-state changes needed for manipulation. We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling. Specifically, Phoenix Encoder enriches DINOv3's general visual semantics through dual-teacher distillation of geometric and spatiotemporal semantic knowledge. Next, CS-VAE causally aggregates and compresses Phoenix Encoder features into generative latents while preserving scene information and spatial layout. Finally, Phoenix jointly predicts these latents, complementary appearance latents, and robot actions, linking scene evolution with action generation. To study pretraining data efficiency, we curate approximately 39,000 hours of egocentric and robot manipulation data for representation learning and use the robot demonstration subset for WAM pretraining. Following benchmark-specific post-training, Phoenix achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulation and real-robot benchmarks spanning single-arm, bimanual, and dexterous manipulation. It demonstrates strong robustness to perturbations, achieving success rates of 94.8% on LIBERO-Plus and 72.4% on RoboTwin 2.0 C2R, alongside strong cross-embodiment transfer and real-world manipulation performance on Bench2Dex and RoboDojo-RealWorld, respectively. Pretraining-scale analysis shows larger performance gains from data scaling than a video-VAE-only baseline, indicating stronger scaling benefits and more effective data utilization. Moreover, Phoenix maintains high success rates when adapted with limited downstream demonstrations, demonstrating strong few-shot adaptation. Our code, pretraining data, and models are available on the project page to help the community reproduce our results.

ViewDreamer: A Robot World Model beyond Fixed Camera Setups 2026-11-20
📝 29 pages, 13 figures, 21 tables. Project page: https://matchlab-imperial.github.io/ViewDreamer/
Show abstract

Video generation models are increasingly important for robot learning, yet most generate each camera view separately or tile all views in a fixed layout. Robot demonstrations, however, are captured by synchronized cameras whose number, placement, and order vary across embodiments. We present ViewDreamer, a single world model that handles variable sets of cameras by disentangling per-view generation from cross-view interaction. Each view follows the generation path of a pretrained single-view model, and views interact only through shared slot tokens. We stabilize multi-view training with a curriculum that gradually increases the number of views. These gains carry over to our world-action model (WAM) and action-conditioned world model (AC-WM). Our WAM outperforms the FastWAM-Joint baseline on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboDojo, and on a real Franka robot. Both variants improve out-of-domain generalization to various camera settings and scenes.

RoboDyna: A Benchmark for Evaluating Robotic Manipulation under Physically Grounded Dynamics 2026-11-20
📝 8 pages, 9 figures, 2 tables. Project page: https://robodyna.github.io/robodyna/

Object manipulation in the dynamic real world requires understanding how the environment functions and evolves, and acting accordingly. Existing benchmarks, however, do not adequately address these needs. Physics-oriented benchmarks evaluate predictive fidelity but not closed-loop action. Dynamic robotic manipulation benchmarks often use simplified, physically implausible object motions, limiting evaluation of reasoning and action under realistic dynamics. Evaluation is also typically synchronized, freezing the environment during inference and making computation time irrelevant to task outcomes. To this end, we propose RoboDyna, a benchmark for evaluating the physical understanding and action capabilities of manipulation policies in dynamic scenes. LoCo-WAM: Test-Time Training as Long Context for World-Action Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

World-action models guide robot actions by predicting what happens next, but typically condition on a short observation history. Extending this history increases computation at every decision and can require a separately trained model. We introduce LoCo-WAM, which learns from the outcomes of its actions through test-time training while keeping the same short history length. It uses newly observed frames to update a low-rank adapter in the video expert, whose adapted predictions guide subsequent actions. The updates accumulate within each episode and are reset between episodes, allowing past observations to influence decisions after they leave the input window. LoCo-WAM thus learns from a longer stream of experience without expert action labels, additional data or meta-training. Experiments on two world-action models show improved task success across four simulation benchmarks. On RoboCasa GR-1, a model with 2.4,s of history matches one trained with 9.6,s, while a model with 4.8,s exceeds it by 4.8 percentage points. Our analysis shows that the updates improve prediction of upcoming frames, affect the action mainly through adaptation of the imagined future, and have an increasing effect on actions as the episode unfolds.

Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment 2026-11-19
📝 44 pages, 10 figures, 11 tables
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under four years the field has grown from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. VLA research joins robotics, computer vision and language modelling, so this survey is written for researchers and practitioners from each of these fields. It reviews 89 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. Seven design axes follow a VLA's life cycle: model design (inputs and memory, architecture, action output, intermediate predictions), training (pretraining data, post-training) and deployment (real-time execution). We compare 22 models on these axes, with their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge. (i) Training data has broadened beyond teleoperated robot data (about 10,000 hours for $π_0$) to community-collected episodes and egocentric human video (20,000 hours for GR00T N1.7). (ii) Model sizes have split into two groups: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters; a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs. (iii) Generalist VLAs handling both manipulation and navigation are emerging, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions, and review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail after small scene changes. A practitioner's guide gives a default for each design decision, and a glossary supports readers new to the field.

GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives 2026-11-19
📝 64 pages, including supplementary material. Project page: https://groundingpi.github.io/ Code: https://github.com/groundingpi/GroundingPI Model: https://huggingface.co/GroundingPI/GroundingPI
Show abstract

Precise grounding matters. It specifies which object is the target and where that object is, even in clutter and for tiny objects, and it has to be fast enough for closed-loop control. Yet vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) take perception from general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones, which still fail in these settings. We introduce GroundingPI, a 4B grounding foundation model that generates points and boxes as quantized coordinates in a shared vocabulary. Training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, using supervision from public datasets and dedicated data engines. Against 44 baselines across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities, GroundingPI establishes a new state of the art, averaging 73.68%, above the larger GPT-6 Astra (71.54%). As a downstream visual backbone, GroundingPI improves performance on robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. Across RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboCasa-GR1, it outperforms all evaluated backbones in all four out-of-distribution settings, with relative gains of up to 24.8% over the strongest baseline. On RoboCasa-GR1, GroundingPI trained with 50% of the demonstrations outperforms those baselines trained with 75%. Its mean open-loop L2 error on nuScenes is 0.296 m. We systematically analyze GroundingPI's pretraining in scale and data composition. Grounding quality and downstream manipulation improve with pretraining scale, with generalization tracking perceptual gains most closely. Analyzing the data recipe across these 11 perceptual capabilities shows dense grounding's substantial benefits for both, and OCR's potential as a catalyst for perceptual learning. These results support grounding as a perceptual foundation, and dedicated perceptual pretraining as a promising direction for foundation models of physical intelligence.

📝 Code and videos at: https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-amp/
Show abstract

Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation. Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Comparing the three views shows whether a system lacks a capability or fails to compose capabilities it already has. A diagnosis harness further attributes the failed episodes of three representative systems to Mind, Act, or their composition and summarizes each system in a profile card. Our evaluation of multimodal agents, vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world-action models (WAMs) shows that competence in a single view does not carry over to Mind2Act tasks. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem.

SteerTrace: Tracing How Target Attacks Steer World Action Models 2026-11-19
📝 10 pages, 5 figures, 5 tables
Show abstract

World action models (WAMs) predict future videos and actions with one backbone, and their imagined future is often seen as a safety signal. We introduce SteerTrace, a targeted universal attack on WAMs that also traces its effect inside the model. One $\ell_\infty$-bounded (4/255) perturbation per model, shared across frames, tasks, and camera views, pushes the executed action toward an attacker-chosen target. On eight WAMs from four families (FastWAM, Faster-WAM, OpenWAM and LingBot-VA) on LIBERO and RoboTwin, it drives task success close to zero and moves the attacked action toward the target. Same-budget noise and BadWAM produce little action shift in the target direction. We use the signed targeted effect to measure how much of the effect each intervention removes or reproduces. Under the standard attack, restoring future tokens removes 61-94% of the targeted effect in the five models whose action experts attend to them. When the attack is constrained to keep the predicted future close to the clean prediction, the targeted effect can become more dependent on current-frame tokens. In three of these five models, restoring current-frame tokens removes 87-99% of the effect, compared with 9-15% for future tokens. In six models, restoring just 0.36% of the FFN neurons in layers 10-29 of the video expert removes 72-96% of the targeted effect. Residual directions 2-16 hold only 11-18% of the shift energy, yet removing them cancels most of the targeted effect.

Don't Lose the Future: Latent-Aware 4-Bit Quantization of World Action Models 2026-11-19
📝 10 pages, 4 figures, 6 tables
Show abstract

World action models (WAMs) predict how a scene will evolve and condition their actions on this predicted future. Deploying them on a robot's onboard computer calls for 4-bit weights, activations and key-value caches (W4A4KV4). Existing 4-bit quantizers for WAMs preserve the generated action, and estimate sensitivity one denoising step at a time.
