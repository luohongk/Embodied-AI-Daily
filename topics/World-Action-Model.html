WAM-TTT: Steering World-Action Models by Watching Human Play at Test Time 2026-10-23
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Steering robot foundation models (RFMs) toward new task variants or user-preferred behaviors remains challenging, often requiring additional robot demonstrations, task-specific fine-tuning, or long-context conditioning. We present WAM-TTT, a test-time training framework for steering world action models from raw human videos. Rather than treating human videos as trajectories to imitate, WAM-TTT absorbs them into a lightweight adaptive memory inside a frozen WAM through self-supervised video prediction. To make this memory useful for control, we introduce a meta-training stage that aligns human demonstrations with robot behaviors using paired human-robot data and a key--value memory reconstruction objective. At test time, only unlabeled human videos are required to adapt the memory, while the pretrained WAM remains frozen. This enables efficient and reusable steering without robot actions, human-side annotations, or task-specific fine-tuning, while preserving the generalization ability of the foundation model. Extensive experiments show that WAM-TTT consistently outperforms in-context human-video conditioning baselines across diverse manipulation tasks and generalization settings.

Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success 2026-10-23
📝 28 pages. Project page and code available at https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/
Show abstract

Robot foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, motivating low-cost offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To test this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to robotics and make sensorimotor alignment measurable, offering a low-cost, representation-level view of robot foundation models that closed-loop success rates alone cannot reveal.

PhysTrace: Interpretable Temporal Evaluation of Physical Consistency in Video Generative Models 2026-10-22
📝 17 pages, 7 figures (including supplementary material). Code: https://github.com/PhysTrace/PhysTrace
Show abstract

World action models increasingly rely on pretrained video models assumed to capture physical dynamics for prediction, planning, and action generation. Existing evaluations assess plausibility, probe representations, or measure predictive surprise, but rarely identify which history-conditioned physical expectations conflict with observations. We introduce PhysTrace, a training-free framework aligning MLLM-generated, history-conditioned expectations with observed video windows to temporally localize physical violations and provide interpretable descriptions without task-specific probes or classifiers. We also introduce IntPhys2-Timing, adding violation intervals to IntPhys2. PhysTrace achieves a Hit Rate of up to 70.7% across physical principles on IntPhys2-Timing and outperforms the compared methods in overall possible-impossible discrimination accuracy on IntPhys2. Applied to reconstructed videos along the trajectories of pretrained diffusion/flow-based video models, PhysTrace reveals attribute- and noise-dependent patterns in physical violation localization, enabling interpretable physical consistency analysis across timesteps.

UniFlowWAM: A Unified World-Action Model with Visual Semantics and 4D Geometry 2026-10-22
📝 14 pages, 5 figures, 4 tables. Project page: https://kksham111.github.io/UniFlowWAM_ProjectPage/
Show abstract

Robot demonstrations record what the robot saw and what it did. They do not specify how the 3D world changed in between. Scene-level 3D flow describes it by tracking 3D scene points, providing the world representation with 4D geometry. Prior work has used flow to improve robot policies. Flow served as an intermediate prediction that a separate model converts into actions, as a prior injected at inference time, or as an extra output learned together with the action. In all of these designs, flow stays outside the policy, as a separate module or as an extra prediction head. We introduce UniFlowWAM, a Unified World-Action Model that makes 3D scene flow a third modality on par with vision and action. Its world representation therefore covers both visual semantics, through future visual features, and 4D geometry, through 3D flow, and flow receives its own supervision. UniFlowWAM is a diffusion transformer with one expert per modality, and the three experts exchange information through joint attention. We train UniFlowWAM under a ten-objective role grid, where each objective assigns every modality exactly one of three roles, namely target, condition, or absent. The same model thus serves as a policy, as forward and inverse dynamics, and as a predictor of future visual features or 3D flow. We evaluate UniFlowWAM on RoboCasa-GR1, LIBERO, and LIBERO-Plus in simulation, and on four real-world bimanual manipulation tasks. On RoboCasa-GR1, UniFlowWAM raises the success rate of its base model without flow from 52.1% to 56.4%. It preserves in-distribution success on LIBERO at 98.1% and reaches 87.7% zero-shot on LIBERO-Plus. On the real robot, it achieves 87.0% average success, compared with 68.5% for the strongest baseline.

Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models 2026-10-21
📝 27 pages; Project page: https://huiwon-jang.github.io/FRED/
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

Recent world action models provide visual foresight and future prediction for robot manipulation, but lack explicit mechanisms for compositional skill reuse. Agentic robotics combines code-based planning and reasoning with reusable skills, but often lacks predictive grounding in physical dynamics. CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion 2026-10-21

Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components. First, we diagnose how precise ego motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we strengthen ego-motion state in VGM by aligning the representations at the identified layers to a 3D foundation model through delta-token space. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields noticeable gains in embodied models.

Relational Geometry Forcing for World Action Models 2026-10-21
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have shown strong potential for robot manipulation by jointly learning visual dynamics and executable actions. However, their video and action objectives do not explicitly constrain the 3D geometry that is crucial for precise manipulation. Recently, geometry-aware WAMs introduce depth or 4D prediction branches, whereas representation alignment methods supervise intermediate WAM features with pretrained 3D foundation models. Yet we find that such pointwise alignment benefits the video features but degrades action learning, since dense geometric feature targets overly restrict compact action features. To address this limitation, we propose Relational Geometry Forcing (RGF), which transfers geometric priors from a frozen 3D foundation model by aligning pairwise feature relations instead of pointwise features. Specifically, RGF applies three complementary relational objectives, where intra-frame and cross-view relations transfer scene geometry and multi-view correspondence to the video features, while action-temporal relations couple action dynamics to geometric scene evolution. Applied only during training, RGF requires no explicit geometry branch or extra inference module. Extensive experiments in simulation and the real world demonstrate the effectiveness of Relational Geometry Forcing, showing consistent gains across diverse manipulation scenarios and WAM paradigms.

How Representational Priors Shape Generalization in World Action Models 2026-10-21
Show abstract

World action models (WAMs) are shown to substantially diverge under generalization performance based on their generative backbones. We investigate this gap through the representational geometry that WAMs inherit from their backbones and how policy training reorganizes it. Our central hypothesis is that a useful representational prior would structure geometry that separates nuisance information from the in-distribution manifold in advance of training as a policy. We quantify this with nuisance orthogonality; orthogonality of the feature under visual variation outside the empirical in-distribution manifold. Across various image and video backbones, we show that a higher orthogonality from the representational prior is associated with stronger robustness to visual variations after WAM training, enabling policy generalization measurable prospectively for world models. In addition, analyzing the resulting robust WAMs reveals nuisance suppression; relative suppression of feature displacement caused by such variations and robust action generation of the policy. Together, these findings provide a geometric perspective on how the representational prior of the world model shapes the generalization performance of WAMs.

TAPDreamer: Transferable Adversarial Patches for World Action Models 2026-10-20
📝 Project Page: https://tapdreamer.github.io
Show abstract

World models learn to predict how their environment will evolve, making them an important foundation for general-purpose robotic control. Yet world action models depend on camera inputs whose manipulation can corrupt the visual representations used across tasks and action policies. Existing attacks on these models optimize against the victim's actions or predicted futures and therefore require access to target-model outputs. In this paper, we propose an attack, TAPDreamer, against world action models that instead uses a public encoder alone to construct a fixed local perturbation that transfers across tasks and action architectures. TAPDreamer requires no target-policy queries. Our key insight is that interactions between patch-induced changes in attention weights and value vectors broadcast a nearly identical representation shift far beyond the patch footprint, and this shift remains stable across task observations. Guided by this insight, TAPDreamer uses six frames from one source task to maximize the global L1 distance between clean and patched encoder representations. The same patches reduce success to 1.45% and 1.00% on two DreamWAM configurations and to 10.60% on Motus. These results show that protecting downstream action generation alone is insufficient: defenses for world action models must also secure shared visual encoders against persistent local perturbations.

De-Diffusing WAMs: Action-Sufficient Direct Prediction for Robot Control 2026-10-20
Show abstract

Cascade world-action models (WAMs) use predicted visual futures to condition robot actions, but inherit costly iterative inference from diffusion-pretrained experts. We ask what a single-step predicted future must preserve to support accurate single-step action prediction. In the evaluated WAM, resampling video noise has little effect on downstream actions, yet naively truncating both streams to one step substantially reduces task success. Video-space supervision only partially closes this gap, and a local sensitivity analysis shows that vision prediction errors have highly unequal consequences for action accuracy. These findings motivate DirectWAM, which converts pretrained video and action experts into single-step predictors without stochastic sampling through action-sufficient distillation, while retaining the explicit future-to-action cascade. After learning a one-step action model, we train the future predictor by decoding actions from its own predicted futures and backpropagating demonstration action error through the expert, with an optional future-latent reconstruction loss. The resulting objective evaluates the student-generated future through its downstream control consequences rather than relying on representation matching alone. DirectWAM achieves 97.1% average success across four LIBERO suites and 85.4% on RoboTwin 2.0, outperforming Flash-WAM at the same one-evaluation-per-stream budget. This advantage has also extended to three real-world tasks that we designed. Compared with the full iterative teacher, it reduces inference latency by $28.0\times$.

PICA-WAM: Visual Perturbation Invariance via Cross-Appearance Self-Alignment in World Action Models 2026-10-19
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) jointly predict future video and generate actions, yet remain sensitive to task-irrelevant visual perturbations, i.e., changes in visual appearance that should not affect robot behavior. We identify a gap in conventional WAM training: video prediction encourages representations to preserve appearance information, while action generation does not explicitly enforce invariance to task-irrelevant appearance. Data augmentation exposes WAMs to such perturbations but does not require perturbed and nominal observations to produce consistent action representations, and alignment with external pretrained features does not guarantee the desired invariance. To address this gap, we propose PICA-WAM, a self-supervised training framework that casts perturbation invariance as an auxiliary cross-appearance self-alignment task for WAMs. An exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher processes the nominal observation to provide a deeper-layer target, while the online WAM processes a perturbed observation and aligns its shallower-layer action representation to this target through a lightweight projection head. Across four strong WAMs evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Plus, EBench, and a real robot, PICA-WAM consistently improves robustness under visual perturbations, preserves nominal performance in simulation, and raises the real robot success rate. PICA-WAM further reduces the sensitivity of video features to task-irrelevant appearance and its propagation into actions, as shown by paired layer-wise diagnostics. PrismWAM: Decoupling State Representations in World Action Models 2026-10-19
📝 Project Page: https://prism-wam.github.io/
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) typically represent the world through latent embeddings from pretrained video models, which entangle heterogeneous aspects of the world, from how it looks (e.g., texture and color) to how it is physically configured (e.g., occupancy and pose). This raises an underexplored question: $\textbf{what should constitute the state of a WAM?}$ We argue that the world state should separate how the world appears from how the world evolves under action: the former captures semantic context, while the latter is governed by physical state. To this end, we propose $\textbf{PrismWAM}$, which decouples physics from semantics while jointly modeling state and action. PrismWAM factorizes an embodied task into a $\mathrm{Semantic\ Planning}\rightarrow\mathrm{Physical\ Rollout}\rightarrow\mathrm{Executable\ Action}$ pipeline: given only the initial RGB observation, semantic planning learns to predict the final goal image, while physical rollout predicts the fine-grained evolution toward this goal in depth space; executable actions are then generated from the depth rollout. Without any embodied action pretraining or additional data augmentation, PrismWAM achieves strong manipulation performance in both simulation and the real world. Notably, PrismWAM even surpasses a broad range of models with large-scale embodied action pretraining (e.g., OpenWAM-$α$). More broadly, we reframe World Action Models from modeling how the world looks to how it responds to action, paving the way for action-centric world models grounded in occupancy, flow, tactile sensing, proprioception, and beyond.

RIFAR: Reliability-Informed and Forgetting-Aware Replay for Continual Robot Learning with World-Action Models 2026-10-19
📝 16 pages, 8 figures, 11 tables, including appendices
Show abstract

Genuine embodied agency requires robots to turn continuous real-world experience into lasting, transferable skills. This demands continual learning that integrates new capabilities while preserving prior knowledge in evolving environments. Experience replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting, but storing complete demonstrations becomes costly as tasks accumulate. World-action models provide an alternative through generative replay. However, visually coherent rollouts may contain actions that cannot realize the predicted transitions, while new-task adaptation can disrupt learned behavior. To address these problems, we propose RIFAR (Reliability-Informed and Forgetting-Aware Replay), a reliability-informed and drift-aware generative replay framework. It reconstructs trajectories from compact historical demonstration prefixes and uses a trainable inverse-dynamics model to assess action-visual consistency.
