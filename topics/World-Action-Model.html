Bind-WAM: Binding-Uncertainty-Guided Selective Future Imagination in World Action Models 2026-11-11
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) can improve robot learning by jointly predicting future visual states and actions, but generating a visual future at every replanning step is expensive and sometimes unnecessary. This raises a critical question: when should a robot policy imagine the future? In this paper, we propose Bind-WAM, a binding-uncertainty-guided WAM for selective future imagination. Bind-WAM introduces language-object binding uncertainty, selectively invoking joint future-action generation when the current observation does not support stable task-relevant action generation. An offline labeling pipeline uses complete demonstrations and action context to identify task-relevant objects and detect reference mismatch, invisibility, and identity drift. The resulting pseudo-labels supervise a lightweight router that predicts binding uncertainty from the current observation and language instruction. At inference, a shared WAM policy invokes selective future imagination according to the predicted uncertainty and its change between consecutive replanning steps. Simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that Bind-WAM can establish binding uncertainty as a strong, interpretable signal for selective future imagination.

SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment 2026-11-11
📝 Project page (https://char-lotte-xia.github.io/swam-official/). Paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12870). Code (https://github.com/shaaaaron/SWAM). Hugging Face paper page and discussion (https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12870)
Show abstract

World action models jointly predict video and actions conditioned on language. Existing studies focus primarily on architectures and training mechanisms, typically treating language-video alignment acquired through video pretraining as an established foundation. However, introducing action learning may impair this alignment. Principled guidance on data and supervision design for maintaining language-video alignment during action learning remains limited. To investigate this, we curate a pool of approximately 47,000 hours of general videos, egocentric videos, UMI demonstrations, and robot demonstrations. Building on this, we systematically study how language-video supervision, language-video-action supervision, and the joint use of these two forms affect WAM performance. In particular, we examine how language annotation quality affects pretrained language-video capabilities, and how action supervision from UMI and robot demonstrations extends these capabilities to language-video-action modeling. To better scale the model, we further conduct detailed studies on joint training recipe and model architecture. Under a fixed training budget, mixing egocentric videos and UMI demonstrations improves control generalization. Model architecture affects how effectively these experiences are used, while scaling pretraining under the selected configuration brings further gains. Native Action-Prior Learning from Videos for World Action Models 2026-11-10
📝 Project Page: https://zhaochongan.github.io/projects/NAVA-WAM
Show abstract

World action models integrate future visual dynamics with robot action prediction, but their scalability remains limited by the need for action-annotated robot trajectories. Observation-only videos contain rich evidence about interaction dynamics, but existing approaches typically use them either to pretrain visual representations that must later be adapted for control, or to infer latent actions that are subsequently grounded to robot commands. We present NAVA-WAM, which introduces native action-prior learning by directly pretraining the action policy from observation-only videos, avoiding indirect representation-to-control transfer or a separate latent-action model. Our training consists of two stages. First, we pretrain on observation-only videos, where future-video flow-matching supervision over visual transitions is propagated through transition-structured joint attention to optimize the Action-DiT and learn action-relevant priors. Second, we use action-labeled demonstrations to post-train the Action-DiT for robot control through joint video--action flow matching, while asymmetric attention decouples the visual branch from iterative action denoising and enables efficient action-only inference. Extensive experiments show that NAVA-WAM consistently outperforms prior approaches under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings, while demonstrating strong action-label efficiency and effective real-robot generalization. These results establish native action-prior learning as an effective approach to directly pretrain action policies from observation-only videos, providing a scalable path beyond action-labeled robot data.

From Video Frames to Robot Meta-Frames: A Unified Latent Interface for World Action Models 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) offer a powerful foundation for World Action Models (WAMs), which jointly predict robot actions and future observations. Building such models requires injecting low-dimensional robot signals (e.g. proprioception, actions, and values) into a latent space shaped by natural videos, and a key design choice is the interface through which these signals enter: existing schemes treat it as a formatting detail, spending a separate latent frame on each signal or bolting on dedicated encoders and decoders. We argue that signal injection is instead a distribution-alignment problem: how well a representation matches the prior of the pretrained VDMs during denoising. Using the relative reconstruction error of a frozen VDM denoiser as a probe, we find that conventional spatial encodings sit far off this prior, whereas frequency-domain packing yields latents that the denoiser corrects far less. Motivated by this diagnostic result, we propose MetaWAM, which folds all robot signals into a single unified meta-frame: each signal is assigned to a disjoint frequency-domain channel band and lifted to the spatial latent by an inverse DCT, yielding a decodable interface aligned with the pretrained VDM prior without any added module or architectural change. A fixed band layout together with slot-aware routing lets the same meta-frame support policy, world-model, and value prediction over a shared set of robot variables. On RoboCasa, MetaWAM reaches 68.9% success while collapsing the per-signal frames into one and cutting inference latency by 39%, and attains 85.0% success on three real-world Franka tasks. Code and models are available at https://github.com/showlab/MetaWAM

Elastic-WAM: Adaptive Spatiotemporal Imagination for Efficient Robot Control 2026-11-10
📝 14 pages. Project page: https://lihaitian.com/Elastic-WAM/
Show abstract

World-Action Models improve robot control by predicting future visual dynamics, but iterative video denoising incurs substantial inference overhead. Recent approaches reduce this cost through partial future denoising, yet typically rely on fixed refinement budgets. Our empirical observations reveal that preferred refinement depths vary across tasks and states, while different future regions exhibit varying relevance to action generation. Motivated by these findings, we propose Elastic-WAM, which adaptively allocates world refinement computation across temporal denoising time and spatial future space. A unified spatiotemporal refinement demand field governs when to stop world refinement, how far to advance each denoising step, and which regions to refresh. We parameterize this field with a shared temporal basis and spatial amplitudes, and learn it through a three-stage action-grounded supervised procedure. At inference, the learned field guides recurrent sparse world refinement to improve task success while reducing inference overhead. Evaluations across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world long-horizon manipulation demonstrate state-of-the-art control performance with reduced inference latency.

LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning 2026-11-10
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.

ForeAct: Geometry-Guided Action Learning from World Representations 2026-11-08
Show abstract

Robotic manipulation requires representations that capture both scene geometry and its evolution during interaction. Existing approaches have largely treated these two aspects separately. World action models capture such dynamics by coupling action prediction with future video generation, but their reliance on iterative diffusion or rectified-flow inference leads to high computational cost, while their representations lack explicit spatial structure. Explicitly modeling scene geometry typically requires auxiliary modules that increase both parameter count and computational cost. Conversely, geometry-centric policies primarily recover observed spatial structure and often lack explicit supervision for anticipating future geometric changes during interaction. To address these limitations, we introduce ForeAct, which repurposes a pretrained video world model as a single-pass representation backbone for low-latency control. ForeAct directly processes the clean latent of the current observation in one forward pass, eliminating future-frame synthesis and iterative denoising while retaining the backbone's pretrained spatiotemporal priors. Rather than introducing a separate geometry backbone, ForeAct uses learnable tokens supervised by next-frame relative depth to shape the shared video representation through bidirectional self-attention. This compact interface turns the spatial knowledge already encoded in the backbone into a future-aware representation for action generation, with little parameter overhead. The lightweight geometry readout is used only during training and removed at deployment, enabling low-latency, geometry-aware control without depth input or an external geometry backbone. Without additional large-scale pretraining, ForeAct achieves 96.6% average success on LIBERO, 63.5% on SimplerEnv-Bridge, and a 74.4% success rate across 90 real-world rollouts.

Next Forcing: World Action Modeling with Multi-Chunk Prediction 2026-11-08
📝 NeurIPS 2026; Project page: https://gangweix.github.io/next-forcing/
Show abstract

Autoregressive video generation has emerged as a powerful paradigm for World Action Models (WAMs). However, existing approaches suffer from slow training convergence and limited converged accuracy, particularly at high frame rates, as the training supervision is confined to the current chunk without explicit signals about future dynamics; they also suffer from slow inference due to iterative video denoising. In this paper, we present Next Forcing, a multi-chunk prediction (MCP) framework for world action modeling that enables faster training, higher accuracy, and accelerated inference. Inspired by multi-token prediction in large language models, Next Forcing introduces an MCP training objective that augments the main model with lightweight auxiliary MCP modules to simultaneously denoise video chunks at multiple future temporal horizons (next$^1$, next$^2$, next$^3$ chunks). These MCP modules form a causal chain across prediction depths, where intermediate features fused from multiple layers of the main model are leveraged to predict future dynamics, allowing near-future predictions to inform farther-future ones and providing dense multi-scale temporal supervision back to the main model. During training, the MCP modules significantly accelerate convergence and improve converged accuracy, especially at high frame rates: at 50 fps, Next Forcing achieves a 93.1% relative improvement over LingBot-VA at 5k training steps and 2.3$\times$ faster convergence, and establishes new state-of-the-art results on the RoboTwin benchmark (94.1/93.5% on Clean/Random). At inference, the MCP modules can be retained to predict the next video chunk in parallel with the current one, achieving 2$\times$ inference acceleration. Beyond World Action Models, Next Forcing also improves physical-law adherence on PhyWorld and reduces FVD by over 50\% in action-free general video pretraining.

FlowPilot: Real-Time World-Action Modeling for Agile UAV Navigation 2026-11-08
📝 8 pages, 8 figures, 4 tables. Accepted for publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L)
Show abstract

We present FlowPilot, the first world-action model for real-time onboard UAV navigation from depth. Unlike map-then-optimize pipelines that require local reconstruction or end-to-end policies that lack explicit scene prediction, FlowPilot jointly denoises future depth observations and executable trajectories with flow matching. A dual-stream mixture-of-transformers couples video and action experts through shared attention, allowing future-scene prediction and trajectory generation to inform each other. At deployment, the model runs action-centrically and outputs only a trajectory. To generate smooth, state-consistent references, actions are parameterized as degree-7 Bernstein polynomials: the current state constrains the initial control points, and the network predicts five free control points, yielding C^2-continuous references with closed-form velocity, acceleration and jerk. FlowPilot is trained on a three-level depth pyramid spanning high-throughput simulation, photorealistic simulation, and real onboard data. In closed-loop simulation, it outperforms learning- and optimization-based baselines under increasing clutter and commanded speeds up to 10 m/s. On a physical quadrotor, FlowPilot achieves a mean inference latency of 10.2 ms on a Jetson Orin NX and reaches a peak speed of 10 m/s during outdoor flight using only onboard sensing and computation.

These findings support joint modeling of visual context, hand state, and touch as a representation approach for multimodal WAMs.

WorldBridge: Bridging Video and Action for Task Generalization in World Action Models 2026-11-06
📝 Authors are listed in alphabetical order. Project page: https://worldbridge-jd.github.io/
Show abstract

Achieving the broad task generalization of embodied models in physical environments remains a central challenge in robot learning. We study this challenge in World Action Models (WAMs) through two complementary capabilities: video generalization, which predicts the visual evolution of new tasks, and action generalization, which translates these predictions into executable behavior through a video-to-action bridge. By varying video and action supervision for tasks outside an initial training set, we examine their respective contributions to task transfer. On RoboTwin 2.0, using only in-distribution video-action supervision yields a 25.07% success rate on out-of-distribution tasks.
