Ropedia W0: Scaling Loco-Manipulation World Models across Embodiments with Human Experience 2026-10-19
📝 Project page: https://ropedia.github.io/ropedia-w0/ ; Code: https://github.com/Ropedia/ropedia-w0
Show abstract

World models enable embodied agents to anticipate action consequences, yet jointly modeling locomotion and manipulation across diverse embodiments remains challenging due to heterogeneous action spaces and limited multimodal supervision. We present Ropedia W0, the first egocentric loco-manipulation world foundation model spanning humans and robots with diverse embodiments. We curate Xp-Data, comprising 10k hours of egocentric human data and 3k hours of robot data with unified multimodal annotations and systematic quality control. A unified kinematic representation encodes locomotion through camera poses and manipulation through projected skeletons, with robot skeletons combining embodiment-specific forward kinematics with extensive manual keypoint annotation and correction. We also introduce Xp-Teleop, a long-horizon loco-manipulation benchmark based on high-quality dexterous humanoid teleoperation data. Evaluations demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across five cross-embodiment benchmarks (DreamDojo, $Ψ_0$, $ω$-HOME, Xp-Teleop, and AgiBot) and on held-out human egocentric video, from zero-shot inference to few-shot and full-data post-training. Scaling human pre-training data improves performance on both human and robot evaluations. After distillation, our model supports real-time, minute-long rollouts, enabling live VR interaction. Applications in policy evaluation, model-based planning, and world-action modeling further demonstrate the model's utility for embodied decision-making.

PICA-WAM: Visual Perturbation Invariance via Cross-Appearance Self-Alignment in World Action Models 2026-10-19
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) jointly predict future video and generate actions, yet remain sensitive to task-irrelevant visual perturbations, i.e., changes in visual appearance that should not affect robot behavior. We identify a gap in conventional WAM training: video prediction encourages representations to preserve appearance information, while action generation does not explicitly enforce invariance to task-irrelevant appearance. Data augmentation exposes WAMs to such perturbations but does not require perturbed and nominal observations to produce consistent action representations, and alignment with external pretrained features does not guarantee the desired invariance. To address this gap, we propose PICA-WAM, a self-supervised training framework that casts perturbation invariance as an auxiliary cross-appearance self-alignment task for WAMs. An exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher processes the nominal observation to provide a deeper-layer target, while the online WAM processes a perturbed observation and aligns its shallower-layer action representation to this target through a lightweight projection head. Across four strong WAMs evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Plus, EBench, and a real robot, PICA-WAM consistently improves robustness under visual perturbations, preserves nominal performance in simulation, and raises the real robot success rate. PICA-WAM further reduces the sensitivity of video features to task-irrelevant appearance and its propagation into actions, as shown by paired layer-wise diagnostics. Code: https://github.com/YudeZou/PICA-WAM-release

PrismWAM: Decoupling State Representations in World Action Models 2026-10-19
📝 Project Page: https://prism-wam.github.io/
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) typically represent the world through latent embeddings from pretrained video models, which entangle heterogeneous aspects of the world, from how it looks (e.g., texture and color) to how it is physically configured (e.g., occupancy and pose). This raises an underexplored question: $\textbf{what should constitute the state of a WAM?}$ We argue that the world state should separate how the world appears from how the world evolves under action: the former captures semantic context, while the latter is governed by physical state. To this end, we propose $\textbf{PrismWAM}$, which decouples physics from semantics while jointly modeling state and action. PrismWAM factorizes an embodied task into a $\mathrm{Semantic\ Planning}\rightarrow\mathrm{Physical\ Rollout}\rightarrow\mathrm{Executable\ Action}$ pipeline: given only the initial RGB observation, semantic planning learns to predict the final goal image, while physical rollout predicts the fine-grained evolution toward this goal in depth space; executable actions are then generated from the depth rollout. Without any embodied action pretraining or additional data augmentation, PrismWAM achieves strong manipulation performance in both simulation and the real world. Notably, PrismWAM even surpasses a broad range of models with large-scale embodied action pretraining (e.g., OpenWAM-$α$). More broadly, we reframe World Action Models from modeling how the world looks to how it responds to action, paving the way for action-centric world models grounded in occupancy, flow, tactile sensing, proprioception, and beyond.

UniW(A)M: A Unified Mixture-of-Experts Framework for World and World-Action Models 2026-10-19
📝 34 pages, 19 figures, 15 tables
Show abstract

World models (WMs) and world-action models (WAMs) focus on future world prediction and executable action generation, respectively. Although these models learn shared physical dynamics and interaction semantics from embodied trajectories, they are typically trained in isolation, which restricts knowledge reuse between prediction and control. The central challenge is therefore to develop a unified learning framework that shares these dynamics while preserving the distinct modeling capabilities required by each paradigm. We propose UniW(A)M, a mixture-of-experts world-(action) model built upon diffusion Transformers that unifies action-conditioned world modeling and diverse WAM variants. Shared experts capture common dynamics, while atomic capability experts are reused across interfaces so that each interface keeps its own conditional pathway. At deployment, a plug-in adaptive interface router (PAIR) learns from a small subset of target-task chunk scenarios to select a suitable WAM interface for each execution chunk, balancing task success and execution efficiency while keeping the backbone frozen. We evaluate UniW(A)M on LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and three real-world manipulation tasks, where it achieves 88.3% average real-world success versus 76.7% for the strongest baseline. On WorldArena, it leads action-conditioned world modeling on all three tasks. AC-WM co-training contributes to action generation on every action interface and PAIR raises OOD success from 63.3% to 76.7%.

RIFAR: Reliability-Informed and Forgetting-Aware Replay for Continual Robot Learning with World-Action Models 2026-10-19
📝 16 pages, 8 figures, 11 tables, including appendices
Show abstract

Genuine embodied agency requires robots to turn continuous real-world experience into lasting, transferable skills. This demands continual learning that integrates new capabilities while preserving prior knowledge in evolving environments. Experience replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting, but storing complete demonstrations becomes costly as tasks accumulate. World-action models provide an alternative through generative replay. However, visually coherent rollouts may contain actions that cannot realize the predicted transitions, while new-task adaptation can disrupt learned behavior. To address these problems, we propose RIFAR (Reliability-Informed and Forgetting-Aware Replay), a reliability-informed and drift-aware generative replay framework. It reconstructs trajectories from compact historical demonstration prefixes and uses a trainable inverse-dynamics model to assess action-visual consistency. It first adapts to new tasks using current-task demonstrations and high-reliability replay, then evaluates how this adaptation changes action predictions on historical inputs and prioritizes reliable trajectories with greater action drift for further training. Across three LIBERO suites and real-world experiments, RIFAR outperforms the previous state of the art in WAM-based generative replay. On LIBERO-Goal, it achieves an AUC of 90.97 while retaining only 320 historical time steps per task, about 4.9% of those retained by 50-demonstration replay.

Vision-Language-Delta-Action: Learning Compact Deltas as Actionable Transition Interfaces for Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-19
📝 37 pages, including appendix. Code framework: https://github.com/baidu-baige/LoongForge/tree/master/cookbook/loongforge-vlda
Show abstract

Robotic manipulation is not merely observation-to-action generation, but the realization of task-specified state changes in the physical world. Existing vision-language-action (VLA) methods directly learn observation-to-action mappings, imposing a substantial learning burden due to the gap between semantic understanding and action execution. Although world-action models (WAMs) improve control through future prediction, modeling complete future states wastes capacity on static appearance and action-irrelevant changes. We propose Vision-Language-Delta-Action (VLDA), a paradigm that reformulates full future prediction as compact actionable transition modeling. Its core is a compact Delta variable that captures latent world-state changes, serving as an interface between vision-language understanding and action generation. VLDA learns Delta through a unified objective composed of the Local Transition Filter (LT-Filter) and Global Transition Anchor (GT-Anchor). LT-Filter captures task-relevant manipulation-centric changes while suppressing static reconstruction biases. GT-Anchor provides coarse future-level regularization to preserve the overall direction of state changes. Delta-Driven Action Refinement (DDAR) further grounds Delta in the action manifold and injects it as a structured seed to guide action denoising. We implement VLDA within LoongForge, an open-source training framework for large-scale foundation and embodied models, using its modular and distributed infrastructure for unified two-stage optimization. Experiments show that VLDA improves long-horizon consistency, robustness, and generalization, validating actionable Delta as an alternative to direct action prediction and full future-state modeling.

TrajWAM: An Efficient Trajectory-Guided World Action Model for Dynamic Robotic Manipulation 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Dynamic object manipulation requires robots to anticipate target motion while replanning fast enough to avoid executing outdated actions. Existing approaches either rely on fast reactive control without an explicit future target state or encode anticipated motion only implicitly. World Action Models (WAMs) offer a promising alternative by coupling future visual prediction with action generation, but scene-level video objectives may underrepresent target dynamics that are critical for manipulation, while iterative video-action denoising introduces substantial latency. We present TrajWAM, a trajectory-guided world action model for efficient dynamic robotic manipulation. Our key insight is to explicitly ground future visual imagination in predicted target-object motion and use the resulting future representation to condition action generation, while preserving this anticipation pathway after acceleration. Given a short history of object masks, a history-conditioned motion predictor forecasts future masks encoding the target object's trajectory. A lightweight Trajectory ControlNet then steers a pretrained video model toward trajectory-consistent futures. The resulting future representation conditions an action expert to predict executable action chunks. To enable one-step inference without breaking this interface, we introduce a decoupled distillation scheme that combines distribution matching for the video backbone, Trajectory ControlNet realignment, and action rollout distillation to support accurate one-step action prediction conditioned on the compressed future representation. Comprehensive evaluations in simulation and the real world show that TrajWAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on dynamic object manipulation tasks while enabling efficient one-step video-action inference. Project page: https://kevingeng3.github.io/TrajWAM/.

CoEvolve: Self-Evolving Code Policies from Skills to Strategies for Humanoid Robots 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Recent advances in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs) have expanded the manipulation and whole body capabilities of individual robots. Yet a single robot remains limited by its embodiment, and many real world tasks require capabilities that can only emerge through cooperation. Existing multi robot frameworks largely emphasize task decomposition, allocation, and replanning, while providing limited support for adapting executable coordination when physical execution deviates from the intended behavior. We present CoEvolve, a framework that represents team coordination as executable code over fixed robot motor skills. CoEvolve introduces Verifier Guided Coordination Evolution, where a multimodal foundation model instantiates coordination strategies through motion primitives, semantic objectives, temporary robot roles, and bounded execution parameters. Physical verification evaluates each executed transition, rejected behaviors trigger structured coordination revision from an accepted checkpoint, and verified experience is retained to guide subsequent decisions. We evaluate CoEvolve with two simulated Unitree G1 humanoids transporting a freely supported rigid payload through constrained corridors. Across 15 base task runs with three multimodal backbone families, CoEvolve completes 8 tasks, with every backbone achieving end to end success. Additional evaluations yield verified placement on two additional corridor layouts and after both same-backbone and cross-backbone experience initialization, while exposing persistent limitations in motion precision and contact stability. These results support executable coordination as a practical interface for adapting long horizon multi robot collaboration under shared physical constraints.

AffordanceWAM: Affordance-Aware Joint World-Action Modeling for Robot Manipulation 2026-10-18
📝 20 pages, 4 figures, including supplementary material. Updated references and formatting. Project page: https://skywalker-yqz.github.io/AffordanceWAM/ Code: https://github.com/Skywalker-yqz/AffordanceWAM
Show abstract

Generalizable robot manipulation requires predicting how a scene will evolve, identifying where interactions are feasible, and determining how to act. Action-labeled robot videos directly supervise control but are costly and limited in diversity, whereas egocentric human videos capture diverse interactions but lack robot actions and differ in embodiment and appearance. We introduce AffordanceWAM, an affordance-aware generative World Action Model that represents object-centric spatiotemporal affordance through Scalar Affordance and Affordance Heatmap, within the generated future World. This representation grounds visual prediction in task-relevantant objects and interaction regions for action generation, and provides shared interaction targets across human and robot videos. Built on a pretrained video diffusion Transformer, AffordanceWAM uses separately parameterized World and Action Experts, coupled through Masked Joint Self-Attention, to jointly predict future RGB observations, Scalar Affordance fields, Affordance Heatmaps, and continuous robot actions under a unified flow-matching objective. Human videos supervise all three future-World streams, whereas robot trajectories additionally provide action supervision, enabling transfer without human action labels or retargeting. Experiments on RoboCasa, CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D, and real-world manipulation demonstrate consistent gains over RGB-only and robot-data-only baselines. Under fixed robot supervision, RoboCasa performance improves monotonically as affordance-annotated human video scales. These results support affordance as an effective interface for both vision-language-action learning and human-to-robot transfer.

Latent-WAM: Latent World Action Modeling for End-to-End Autonomous Driving 2026-10-18

We introduce Latent-WAM, an efficient end-to-end autonomous driving framework that achieves strong trajectory planning through spatially-aware and dynamics-informed latent world representations. Existing world-model-based planners suffer from inadequately compressed representations, limited spatial understanding, and underutilized temporal dynamics, resulting in sub-optimal planning under constrained data and compute budgets. Latent-WAM addresses these limitations with two core modules: a Spatial-Aware Compressive World Encoder (SCWE) that distills geometric knowledge from a foundation model and compresses multi-view images into compact scene tokens via learnable queries, and a Dynamic Latent World Model (DLWM) that employs a causal Transformer to autoregressively predict future world status conditioned on historical visual and ego status representations. LIBERO-Pro2.0: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Robustness of Embodied Foundation Models 2026-10-17
📝 39 pages
Show abstract

LIBERO has emerged as a widely adopted benchmark for evaluating robotic manipulation policies. However, strong performance on LIBERO does not necessarily indicate robust embodied capabilities, as models may exploit benchmark-specific regularities rather than generalize to diverse operating conditions. In this work, we introduce LIBERO-Pro2.0, a comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating the robustness of embodied foundation models under distribution shifts. LIBERO-Pro2.0 encompasses 22 static perturbations and 20 runtime interventions across six dimensions: Condition, Environment, Observation, Execution, Robot State Configuration, and Language, yielding 1,648 validated shifted task instances. To support robustness-oriented learning, we further collect 39,390 training trajectories through model-generated and human demonstrations. All shifted task instances undergo six rounds of iterative human review and correction to ensure physical plausibility and task validity. We evaluate 17 representative robot policies spanning vision-language-action models, world-action models, and robustness-oriented policies. Our results reveal substantial robustness gaps across all evaluated paradigms: under runtime distribution shifts, the evaluated models exhibit an average performance drop of 26.5 percentage points, with individual drops reaching 32.5 points. These findings highlight the gap between nominal benchmark performance and robustness under distribution shifts, underscoring the need for systematic robustness evaluation of embodied foundation models.

FlashRT-PAI: Fast Kernels Are Not Fast Pipelines for Physical AI Inference 2026-10-17
📝 source code https://github.com/flashrt-project/FlashRT
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA), video-action (VA), and world action models (WAMs) form the backbone of modern physical AI systems, yet their real-time single-batch inference remains a defining bottleneck. Latency is dominated not by individual kernel computation, but by system-level overhead: thousands of short kernel launches, intermediate data movement across operator boundaries, and redundant recomputation across consecutive decisions. General-purpose graph compilers cannot address these costs, as they optimize individual calls in isolation and lack visibility into cross-call state semantics and global pipeline structure. We present FLASHRT-PAI, a low-latency inference engine built around model-hardware pipeline co-design rather than isolated kernel optimization. FLASHRT-PAI combines three core mechanisms: deep kernel fusion with launch overlap eliminates inter-operator boundaries and launch gaps; an execution contract formalizes the pipeline's stage DAG, update rates, and named buffers to enable cross-decision state reuse and selective refresh; and execution-state capsules enable fast snapshot, restore, and fork of persistent pipeline state. The system also supports drop-in acceleration of unmodified PyTorch models via parity- and latency-gated kernel replacement. Evaluation covers six policies on RTX 5090 and four on Jetson AGX Thor. At matched 16-bit precision, FLASHRT-PAI is 1.3-3.7x faster than official compiled paths. Combining pipeline co-design with reduced precision delivers 3.38-12.66x speedup over official eager implementations. In LIBERO-10, four-bit pi0.5 achieves 94.04% success on Thor and 93.32% on RTX 5090, compared with 92.4% for the measured official FP16 reference. FLASHRT-PAI is open source, distributed via Hugging Face Kernel Hub, and its opt-in pi0.5 kernels have been adopted into the LeRobot framework.

AWAM: Active Trajectory-Conditioned View Imagination for Robust Robot Manipulation 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Rapid progress in embodied intelligence has enabled increasingly capable robots to follow language instructions and manipulate diverse objects. However, strong results in structured environments do not automatically transfer to open-world conditions, where clutter, occlusion, and changing viewpoints can obscure the spatial relationships required for action. To address this perceptual gap, a virtual active-perception paradigm is introduced. AWAM, an Active World-Action Model actively plans where to observe and imagines what will be seen along the planned trajectory before acting. Task-related virtual camera motion is predicted and used as a structured query into a geometry-conditioned world model, producing a reusable spatial prior for manipulation, which is later compressed into a fixed memory and fused with live observations through cross-attention for diffusion-based action prediction. These results support improved robustness to the evaluated occlusions and distribution shifts, addressing part of the gap between structured manipulation and less controlled environments.

GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives 2026-10-16

Precise grounding matters. It specifies which object is the target and where that object is, even in clutter and for tiny objects, and it has to be fast enough for closed-loop control. Yet vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) take perception from general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones, which still fail in these settings. We introduce GroundingPI, a 4B grounding foundation model that generates points and boxes as quantized coordinates in a shared vocabulary. Training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, using supervision from public datasets and dedicated data engines. Against 44 baselines across 34 grounding benchmarks spanning 11 perceptual capabilities, GroundingPI establishes a new state of the art, averaging 73.68%, above the larger GPT-6 Astra (71.54%). As a downstream visual backbone, GroundingPI improves performance on robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. On RoboTwin 2.0, it outperforms every mainstream backbone we evaluate in all four out-of-distribution settings, by up to 24.8% relative to the strongest backbone. On RoboCasa-GR1, GroundingPI trained with 50% of the demonstrations outperforms those baselines trained with 75%. On nuScenes, used as the visual backbone, GroundingPI attains an average open-loop L2 error of 0.296 m. We systematically analyze GroundingPI's pretraining in scale and data composition. Downstream autonomous driving and robotic manipulation improve as the pretraining is scaled. Analyzing the data recipe across these 11 perceptual capabilities shows dense grounding's substantial benefits for both, and OCR's potential as a catalyst for perceptual learning. These results support grounding as a perceptual foundation, and dedicated perceptual pretraining as a promising direction for foundation models of physical intelligence.

RealtimeWAM: One-Step Asynchronous World Action Models 2026-10-16
📝 The code and checkpoints are available at https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/tree/main/scripts/realtimewam
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) incorporate visual representations from video generation backbones to guide action prediction. Recent efficient WAMs adopt Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architectures and compute video representations once for reuse by the action expert. However, intra-expert iteration (\ie, multi-step action denoising) and inter-expert waiting (\ie, sequential execution of the video and action experts) still limit inference efficiency. To this end, we present RealtimeWAM, an extremely efficient WAM variant with one-step action generation and asynchronous inference, addressing these two bottlenecks. To reduce intra-expert iteration, we propose Teacher-Anchored Consistency Distillation (TACD) to address a local-global error gap: low local consistency error alone does not guarantee accurate final actions. TACD supplements local consistency with explicit supervision from the frozen teacher's multi-step rollout endpoint, enabling accurate one-step action generation. Additionally, we propose Cross-Expert Wavefront Pipelining (CEWP) to eliminate unnecessary expert-level waiting. It overlaps the two experts through block-wise sharing of the video KV cache, synchronizing only immediately before the corresponding action attention consumes it. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks (\eg, LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin) and model variants (\eg, Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM) demonstrate the superiority of RealtimeWAM. Notably, RealtimeWAM maintains near-lossless performance (\ie, $<1\%$ drop) across these benchmarks while delivering significant end-to-end speedup (\eg, $\sim25\times$ on H100). Our code and checkpoints are available via this \href{https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/tree/main/scripts/realtimewam}{link}.

Same Goal, Different Words: Spatial Instruction Sensitivity in World-Action Models 2026-10-15
📝 18 pages, including appendices. Restored the four-page main-text layout and corrected the General Motors affiliation. Institutional disclaimer retained; scientific content unchanged
Show abstract

World-action models should preserve the intended manipulation across equivalent descriptions of a spatial goal. We evaluate this consistency in 864 rollouts from three models across four RoboLab scenes, holding initial states and task goals fixed. Describing relations through the reference object rather than the object-to-move lowers the rate of reaching a stable placement by 12.2 percentage points on average. The effect of instruction rephrasing varies across spatial relations, so aggregate scores can hide sensitivity to wording. Standalone tests of the policies' language understanding identify relation reversals and show that adding images can disrupt otherwise correct text-only interpretations. These findings motivate a more thorough evaluation of world-action models' steerability, language understanding, and grounding. Evaluations should test consistency across equivalent instructions, verify that the intended object moved, and check whether the requested arrangement persists to the end of the rollout.

TS-WAM: Temporal Latent Actions and World-Action Coupling for Vision-Language Robot Control 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Many vision-language-action policies rely on action-labeled robot demonstrations and lack an explicit mechanism for predicting future visual states. These limitations hinder learning from action-free videos and can encourage overfitting to demonstration conditions, weakening generalization and robustness. We present TS-WAM, a lightweight Teacher-Student World-Action Model that addresses these challenges through self-supervised temporal learning and explicit future prediction. Its vlm-jepa framework learns from heterogeneous data, including robot demonstrations and action-free human videos, using an EMA target branch without requiring a separately trained external teacher. By coupling vlm-jepa with delta-lwm, TS-WAM learns to predict latent actions from the current observation and instruction and uses them to predict future visual features that guide action generation at inference.
