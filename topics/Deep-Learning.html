Quantifying the added value of human forecaster discussions for tropical cyclone intensity prediction 2026-11-16
📝 74 pages, 24 figures, 35 tables
Show abstract

The National Hurricane Center routinely releases Tropical Cyclone Discussions that offer insights into its tropical cyclone forecasts. This study examines the added value of these discussions for intensity forecasting beyond a set of numerical predictors. For 637 Atlantic and eastern North Pacific tropical cyclones from the 2002-2023 hurricane seasons, we analyze the discussion text and its combination with 13 hurricane environmental predictors from SHIPS and ERA5 10-meter wind fields, for forecasting intensity changes at lead times of 24 to 120 hours using three modeling methods. In one approach, an end-to-end deep learning framework for the text shows that the discussions alone offer comparable value for 24-hour intensity forecasts to the SHIPS predictors. Adding discussion information to the SHIPS predictors in an ensemble of five such models reduces the mean absolute error of 24-hour maximum sustained wind forecasts for 2022-2023 cyclones from 9.89 to 8.90 kt, with a confidence interval that excludes zero when whole storms are resampled. The ERA5 wind fields have a minor effect on mean absolute error in wind predictions, and model rankings differ across predicted variables, lead times and model types. We provide prediction intervals for these forecasts, whose coverage falls below the 90% target during rapid intensification. Analysis of the text shows that words related to intensity and intensity change (such as intensify, weaken, eyewall and category) are important for the model predictions. This retrospective study shows the added value of the forecasters' discussions and suggests that multi-modal learning can help understand the predictive contribution of expert human judgment. The reanalysis data differ from real-time analyses, and the discussions are issued at least three hours after the valid time of the other inputs, so these results should not be interpreted operationally.

Beyond Job Titles: Interpreting Early-Career Job Advertisements with Sparse Autoencoders and Semantic Clustering 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Job titles and job descriptions alone provide limited insight into how technical roles differ in a changing job market. To assess how such roles differ in their work and industry applications, we design and compare two methods based on deep learning and transformer representations using 36,081 early-career job postings collected from LinkedIn in the United States between December 2025 and October 2026. We first apply PERC, short for Preprocessing (P), Embedding (E), Dimensionality Reduction (R) and Clustering (C). We employ GPT-6 Luna to classify job description passages into Team context, Role task and Qualifications. We then create combinations of these sections and examine whether embedding and clustering the segmented passages produce meaningful semantic clusters about different job roles. We use Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to rank observed differences between embedding distributions and employ a pretrained sparse autoencoder (SAE) to extract features from layer-19 representations of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Lastly, we employ GPT-6 Luna to come up with hypotheses about job roles' feature patterns, and GPT-6.1 Terra to investigate whether those patterns exist in held-out job descriptions from unseen companies. Some example findings include Data Scientist advertisements placing more emphasis on machine learning model development, Data Analyst advertisements on reporting business performance, and Machine Learning Engineer advertisements on applying developed models in real-world settings. We also observe differences in the applications described by Data Analyst and Business Analyst advertisements across selected months, although our dataset's scale and uneven coverage limit their interpretation. Overall, we present a realistic application of SAEs and PERC to unstructured textual data and examine how automated insight extraction could support manual analysis of job descriptions.

Symmetry Detection with Patch Embedding Models by Means of a Scalar Product Transform 2026-11-16
📝 8 pages, 7 figures, accepted for VISAPP 2027
Show abstract

We present a transformation of vector embeddings representing image patches into scores for point reflection symmetry at the crossing points of patches. The transformation is heavily inspired by the Gradient Product Transform, but it uses the vector embeddings instead of the grayscale gradients. We therefore call it the "Scalar Product Transform" (SPT). Unlike deep learning models for symmetry detection, the method does not require a specific training step tailored for symmetry detection, but it works with arbitrary pre-trained patch embedding models. We have tested the method with DINOv3 and Franca. In our tests, the method outperformed the Gradient Product Transform and was even better than EquiSym, a convolutional neural network specifically designed and trained for symmetry detection. Although the runtime of the SPT is greater than that of EquiSym, the SPT has the advantage that it directly yields a bounding box for the symmetric region.

Layer-wise Geometric Approximation Rates for Deep Networks 2026-11-16
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Depth is widely viewed as a central contributor to the success of deep neural networks, yet standard approximation results typically give error bounds only for the final output of a network. Inspired by classical multiscale expansions and multigrade deep learning, we give depth a scale-dependent interpretation by constructing a single network whose intermediate readouts approximate the target at successively finer scales. For any $f\in L^p([0,1]^d)$ with $1\le p <\infty$ and any integer $N\ge2$, we construct a single network of width $3dN+2d+4$ with the same activation pattern in every hidden layer, using sine in the first two neurons and $\mathtt{ReLU}$ in the rest. For every $\ell\ge0$, the $L^p$ approximation error of the readout $Φ_\ell$ at layer $\ell+4$ is bounded by $(2d+1)ω_{f,p}(N^{-\ell})$, where $ω_{f,p}$ denotes the $L^p$ translation modulus of $f$. Under the Hölder condition $ω_{f,p}(t)\le Ct^α$ with $C>0$ and $0< α\le 1$, this estimate yields the geometric bound $(2d+1)C N^{-α\ell}$. More generally, if $ω_{f,p}(t)$ has a known upper bound that tends to zero as $t\downarrow0$, we obtain a stopping rule for adaptive depth selection. We can stop at the first depth where the error bound meets the desired tolerance and later append layers to meet a tighter tolerance while keeping all earlier maps and readouts fixed.

The substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) is a small midbrain nucleus that degenerates early in Parkinson's disease (PD). T1-weighted FLASH MRI enables its visualization and may support early detection of neurodegeneration. However, manual segmentation is difficult and time-consuming, and deep learning models often suffer from the limited availability of labeled data. Transfer learning (TL) may help by adapting pre-trained models to small datasets. We investigate whether TL from a model trained on the locus coeruleus (LC), a nucleus with a similar appearance in this sequence, improves SNpc segmentation. SemiNovo: Learning Beyond Search-Identified Spectra for De Novo Peptide Sequencing 2026-11-16
📝 13 pages. Accepted as an oral presentation at KDD 2027, AI for Sciences Track
Show abstract

Sensitive peptide identification is fundamental to protein characterization and novel protein discovery, for which de novo peptide sequencing (DNPS) from tandem mass spectra provides an important database-independent approach. Although deep learning has substantially advanced de novo sequencing, existing models are predominantly trained on high-confidence peptide-spectrum matches obtained through database search. This search-conditioned supervision excludes a large fraction of unidentified spectra, creating a distribution mismatch between the selectively labeled training data and the complete spectrum stream encountered at inference time, thereby limiting peptide identification performance. To address this challenge, we propose SemiNovo, a semi-supervised framework that learns jointly from identified and unlabeled spectra. SemiNovo first constructs DarkSpec, a large-scale quality-controlled corpus of unlabeled spectra collected from diverse public proteomics projects, thereby extending training beyond search-identified spectra and mitigating the resulting shift in DNPS supervision. It then combines stepwise mass-constrained autoregressive decoding with cumulative path confidence to derive reliable supervision from unlabeled spectra. Moreover, SemiNovo introduces head-wise chemical-evidence gating to improve spectral evidence aggregation under noisy and incomplete fragmentation. Experiments show that SemiNovo performs favorably against state-of-the-art methods on multiple benchmarks. Code and dataset are publicly available.

Purpose: To develop and validate a fully automated deep learning method for segmenting retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) loss, ellipsoid zone (EZ) loss, and EZ thickness in geographic atrophy (GA) on optical coherence tomography (OCT). Methods: Three convolutional neural networks segment Bruch's membrane, the EZ boundaries, and RPE loss in Spectralis OCT volumes; EZ loss and thickness are derived from the EZ boundaries. The networks were developed on 298 volumes (GA, intermediate and neovascular age-related macular degeneration, healthy) and evaluated against double-graded expert annotations in an independent real-world set of 43 eyes with GA. Further analyses assessed inter-reader reliability (25 eyes), test-retest repeatability between screening and baseline visits of two phase 3 trials (68 eyes), and the effect of B-scan density (61 eyes). Results: Mean Dice similarity coefficients were 0.88 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.84-0.90) for RPE loss and 0.87 (95% CI, 0.83-0.89) for EZ loss; the mean absolute EZ thickness error was 2.08 $μ$m. Lower B-scan densities had little effect on lesion area but markedly increased the error in measuring 3-month change. Conclusions: On real-world OCT, the fully automated method was accurate, with agreement comparable to human readers, and reliable, with high test-retest repeatability. Translational Relevance: Fully automated, reproducible quantification of RPE and photoreceptor loss on routine OCT could support standardized structural endpoints in GA clinical trials and objective assessment of GA in routine ophthalmic care.

When Is the Expensive Method Worth It? A Content-Driven Analysis of Classical Bayer Demosaicing 2026-11-16
📝 Preprint. 14 pages
Show abstract

Demosaicing reconstructs a full-colour image from a single-sensor colour-filter-array (CFA) mosaic, where only one colour is measured per pixel. Classical methods make different assumptions about images, from simple averaging to directional edge-aware interpolation, and the directional methods cost markedly more compute. We ask a practical question that a per-image quality ranking does not answer: for a given image, is the expensive directional method worth its cost? Using a controlled simulate-reconstruct pipeline on 80 benchmark images from four public datasets (Kodak, McMaster, BSD100, Urban100), six classical methods, and image-content descriptors fixed before any results were seen, we show that the quality gain of the edge-aware method (Menon 2007) over the best cheap linear method is not constant. It ranges from $-6.6$ to $+6.8$ dB, and on 31% of images it is zero or negative, meaning a cheap method matches or beats the expensive one. The gain is also predictable from content: it correlates with mean image saturation at $r=-0.86$ ($p<10^{-6}$). We then build an interpretable depth-2 decision tree that recommends the best cheap method from image descriptors, beating an always-pick-the-most-common baseline by 20.0 percentage points under leave-one-image-out validation. The analysis uses no deep learning, and every step can be inspected.

SOAP-Bubbles: Structured Weight Uncertainty for Neural Networks 2026-11-16
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code available at: https://github.com/team-approx-bayes/evon
Show abstract

Structured weight-uncertainty can improve many aspects of deep learning, but it remains costly to estimate and difficult to implement. Here, we show that these issues can be addressed by adapting the SOAP optimizer. Our key idea is to run IVON, an existing diagonal-covariance variational method, in the eigenspace of SOAP's preconditioner and then use the preconditioner to transform the diagonal estimate into a non-diagonal covariance. The resulting method has costs similar to those of SOAP and requires no drastic changes to training pipelines. We call the posteriors obtained in this way SOAP-Bubbles and our new optimizer Eigenspace-VON (EVON). We show that, for logistic regression, EVON can match the full Gaussian covariance and that, for language model pretraining, it yields significantly better results than existing diagonal-covariance methods. Our work makes it easier to estimate more expressive posterior distributions for deep learning at scale.

📝 18 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Hybrid quantum-classical convolutional neural networks have been proposed as an alternative to classical deep learning models. However, executing network components on quantum hardware imposes fundamental limitations, such as gradient estimation through repeated measurements, and increasing the number of qubits can lead to trainability problems such as barren plateaus. Moreover, the reported success of these hybrid networks has been linked to quantum architectures that can be efficiently simulated on classical computers. We investigate whether small, classically simulated quantum circuits can serve as useful components within larger models, as a substitute for purely classical layers. We present a systematic comparison between a Hybrid Quantum-inspired CNN (HQiCNN) and standard CNN architectures, where the models differ only in a single intermediate layer: an emulated quantum circuit in the HQiCNN and classical counterparts in the CNN. The models are evaluated on two medical imaging datasets while varying training set size, learning rate, batch size, circuit entanglement and observable se. Under a strict parameter budget, the HQiCNN outperforms the classical baselines. When the classical layer is given more parameters, overall performance becomes comparable, with the HQiCNN showing improvements only in the intermediate-data regime. Removing entanglement yields comparable performance and richer observable sets yield no performance improvement. Finally, we introduce two SHAP-based metrics for comparing model explanations, $|$SHAP$|$IoU and EMD$_{\mathrm{pos}}$. They show that all architectures attend to anatomically plausible regions, and that the spatial attention agreement between them increases with training set size. These results indicate that, under specific data regimes, quantum-inspired hybrid models are a competitive alternative to classical CNNs for medical image classification.

iPINN for Broadband CARS Phase Retrieval: A Framework for Function Approximation and Inverse Modeling Problems in Nonlinear Spectroscopy 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Broadband coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering (BCARS) records chemically specific vibrational spectra, but the measured signal mixes the resonant, Raman-like response of the sample with a non-resonant background that changes from one measurement to the next. Recovering the resonant response, known as phase retrieval, is an ill-posed inverse problem. We present iPINN, an inverse physics-informed neural network that predicts the positions, amplitudes, and widths of Lorentzian vibrational bands directly from a BCARS spectrum. A transformer network estimates these band parameters, and a differentiable analytical model of the resonant susceptibility rebuilds the Raman-like spectrum from them as a sum of physically defined bands. We train the network on simulated spectra in which the bands of each reference compound stay fixed while the shape and strength of the background and the noise vary. Training also encourages similar band predictions for the same compound across these variations. On a public benchmark of toluene and dimethyl sulfoxide spectra, the network reached a mean absolute error about one third of that of the best of five deep-learning baselines. Its error for dimethyl sulfoxide stayed nearly constant across three noise levels. We also used 28 spectra of seven solvents, recorded at four focal depths with different background strengths. For five of the seven solvents, the mean absolute error changed little with depth, while it varied more for the other two. These results indicate that this approach keeps phase retrieval stable for most of the tested solvents when the background changes.

Kähler landscapes for complex neural network descents and guarantees including a search and destroy of the Calabi-Yau manifold 2026-11-16
Show abstract

We study landscapes for complex-parameterized networks. Our approach is motivated with an information-theoretic manifold perspective of the parameter and via classical optimization guarantees although of complex geometric variety such as through Dolbeault asymptotics. The descent path admits a Kähler information metric under a cross-entropy via the Wirtinger Hessian on the log-likelihood potential. We descend according to a differentiated loss scaled by the inverse metric, so the descent path remains in the holomorphic tangent bundle. We emphasize information manifolds which profane theoretical guarantees via ill-curvature-conditioned, or lack thereof, landscapes. We focus on Calabi-Yau metrics specifically in a non-compact setting with a global potential, so defined geometrically rather than invoking the topological requirements of the Calabi conjecture. In non-compact settings, we can write the metric determinant with respect to a background in terms of a function, pluriharmonic or real. Using Yau's Liouville theorem, we get a small eigenvalue blow-up effect under nontrivial background interaction, geometric invariance of the endomorphism constant, and almost low rank assumptions. In an empirical setting, a Ricci-flat metric will not form, but large eigenvalues can be a local condition and hold empirically on open sets even in generalized Kähler cases due to lower bounds on the Ricci potential. Moreover, it has been discovered that negative curvature subverts the loss landscape, specifically sectional curvature, so we expand on this and draw interconnections to negative-definite Ricci curvature. Our arguments primarily exist via geometric modality, although we establish roots in deep learning theory such as through asymptotics at initialization and connections through failure modes of neural network guarantees under vanishing and negative Ricci curvature.
