What Survival Benchmarks Don't Tell You: Impact of Model Selection and Dataset Regimes 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Despite the proliferation of survival analysis methods, current benchmarks often operate under narrow conditions: they heavily rely on the concordance index (C-index) for evaluation and model selection, and frequently either exclude deep learning (DL) methods or underrepresent challenging dataset regimes. To address these limitations, we conduct a large-scale, neutral benchmark comparing 10 classical, tree-based, and DL methods across 49 diverse datasets. Crucially, we treat the model selection criterion (optimizing for C-index, Integrated Brier Score (IBS), or Mean Absolute Error (MAE-PO)) as an independent experimental variable. Using Bayesian mixed-effects modeling, we demonstrate that evaluation design heavily dictates conclusions. For complex DL and tree-based architectures, selecting hyperparameters to optimize for IBS instead of C-index yields substantial improvements in probabilistic accuracy and calibration at a negligible cost to discriminative ranking. Furthermore, method performance (C-index, IBS, MAE-PO, and X-CAL) is fundamentally governed by dataset characteristics. While DL models scale exceptionally well with larger sample sizes, they suffer notable performance drops in high-dimensional feature spaces. Additionally, we expose a structural vulnerability where heavy censoring artificially inflates absolute calibration metrics, creating overly conservative tests. Ultimately, our findings challenge the utility of global single-number performance summaries and advocate for survival analysis results that transparently report the evaluation setup. Our code is available at: https://github.com/sdening/surv-bench-study

Prognosis Without Prediction: The Intrinsic Geometry of Survival 2026-11-18
📝 Code available at: https://github.com/Atahanka/SOSM
Show abstract

Modeling systemic failure in high-dimensional environments is fundamentally limited by supervised algorithms that attempt to predict sparse, noisy or censored endpoints. To break this reliance on explicit outcome regression, we engineered the Self-Organizing Survival Manifolds (SOSM) framework, a deep learning architecture that decouples unsupervised representation learning from survival labels and treats time-to-event progression as an emergent geometric property. The system projects heterogeneous multi-omics arrays into a unified latent space governed by a pseudotime-weighted, curvature-minimizing graph Laplacian, organizing patient trajectories along low-dissipation geodesic flows. To validate the framework, we applied this architecture to a 184-patient clear cell renal cell carcinoma multi-omics cohort. Across a deterministic 5-fold cross-validation pipeline, SOSM achieved a mean Concordance Index of $0.768 \pm 0.068$, while out-of-fold risk stratification yielded significant survival divergence (log-rank $p < 0.001$) accompanied by a 14.7% increase in local manifold dissipation among high-risk patients. Furthermore, interrogating the network's encoder weights confirmed that the model autonomously recovered canonical metabolic and microenvironmental drivers of disease progression without supervised feature selection. By demonstrating that systemic failure can be mapped as a geometric phase transition, SOSM establishes a generalizable computational engine for prognostic modeling across biological, industrial and financial complex systems.

Show abstract

Parkinson's disease (PD) is the second most common neurodegenerative disease in the world, and 70\% to 75\% of PD patients suffer from speech disorders at some stage. Since PD affects men more frequently than women, previous publications tend to rely on unbalanced datasets that potentially introduce sex biases into trained models. This study investigates sex differences in PD detection from speech using deep learning and traditional machine learning approaches. We evaluated speech-based PD detection using embeddings from three pre-trained speech models, combined with handcrafted acoustic features and different classifiers. Sex-specific experiments were conducted on balanced subsets of four sustained phonation datasets, controlling for sex, disease status, and, where possible, age. Our results highlight the importance of including sex as a variable in the development of PD detection systems.

From Activity Recognition to Shift Detection: Variational Wi-Fi Sensing with Evidential Uncertainty 2026-11-18
📝 17 pages,8 figures, 8 tables
Show abstract

Human motion perturbs wireless channels, making commodity Wi-Fi devices a practical platform for device-free activity recognition. Yet Channel State Information (CSI) also depends strongly on the person, receiver position, recording session, and surrounding environment, so a classifier can continue producing activity labels after its deployment conditions have moved away from those seen during training. We propose Wi-HAR, a multi-antenna architecture in which four Variational Auto-Encoders learn compact distributions from raw Wi-Fi 6 CSI magnitudes and a delayed-fusion classifier combines their means and log-variances. On twelve activities from a public benchmark, Wi-HAR reaches 96.6% median recognition accuracy, compared with 70.8-81.5% for independently optimized single-antenna models, showing that the antennas provide complementary views of the same activity. We then use the same latent backbone to detect deployment shifts without using shifted samples for model selection or calibration. A class-conditional uncertainty score derived from Evidential Deep Learning achieves a macro-average ROC-AUC of 0.897 across eleven datasets that change receiver position, participant, recording session, or room, and detects 82.5% of shifted samples at a realized 5.2% in-distribution rejection rate. Detection performance nevertheless varies substantially across receiver positions, showing that the same physical change does not induce a uniform uncertainty response. The compact representation also supports shallow decision-tree analysis using variables from several antennas.

Minimum Neural Weight Norm = Kolmogorov Complexity 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Why do neural networks generalize better when their parameter norm is small? We prove that, in looped neural networks with constant-precision weights, the smallest weight norm of a network computing a function is asymptotically equivalent to the Kolmogorov complexity of that function, divided by its logarithm. The proof has two reductions: looped neural networks can encode information at a rate of logarithmically many bits per non-zero parameter encoded via the sparsity pattern, while any fixed-precision network can be described by enumerating its non-zero parameters with logarithmic addressing cost. Both bounds have the same leading constant factors, giving an asymptotic equivalence. More broadly, by establishing a theoretical link between norm minimization and algorithmic simplicity, we provide an avenue towards understanding generalization in modern deep learning.

Show abstract

Deep learning systems grade prostate biopsies at the level of pathologists, but most report a grade without the glandular architecture that justifies it. This work presents a grading system built on a sparse code over the tokens of a pathology foundation model. A sparse autoencoder pretrained on biopsies is aligned with multi-reader annotations, so that 69 of its 2048 features are named after Gleason morphologies such as cribriform glands, cords, and comedonecrosis. These results show that a shared sparse representation can support competitive Gleason grading while providing named morphological evidence that transfers across datasets and whose contribution to grading can be measured directly.

Uncertainty-aware damage identification in short-span bridges via physics-informed variational autoencoder 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Vibration-based damage identification in civil infrastructure is a challenging, ill-posed inverse problem due to measurement noise, sparse sensor arrays, and modeling errors. While deep learning is powerful for system identification, deterministic approaches lack reliable uncertainty quantification and can yield physically inconsistent results. This work proposes a probabilistic Scientific Machine Learning framework for structural health monitoring: a physics-informed Gaussian copula variational autoencoder. The methodology introduces two main contributions. First, a numerical eigenvalue solver is embedded directly into the variational autoencoder as its decoder, which replaces data-driven surrogates, ensures that every posterior sample satisfies the governing equations of structural dynamics, and reduces the number of trainable parameters. Second, a Gaussian copula describes the posterior distribution of the stiffness factors, capturing cross-correlations between components with fewer parameters than Gaussian mixtures in real time. We validate the method numerically on a short-span bridge supported on elastic bearings, whose response is dominated by vertical bending, accounting for modeling errors, measurement noise, and a finer finite element discretization for testing. The method estimates the posterior distribution of the damage condition, i.e., the stiffness reduction factors of the deck zones and bearings, and these posteriors closely match the exact ones computed with sequential Monte Carlo sampling. The results reveal that the proposed methodology offers a robust and interpretable decision-making tool, able not only to indicate the presence and location of damage, but also to inform on the uncertainty associated with the diagnosis, for instance, when light damage is masked by the effect of noise or when the measured modal properties present limited sensitivity to damage.

Applying the frozen diffusion model (FDM) [29] to the constrained control of the 2D radiative transport equation presents several challenges. Survival of the Fittest Perturbations: Uncertainty-Aware Evolution of Adversarial Distributions for Robust Generalization 2026-11-18
Show abstract

Despite deep learning's broad successes across diverse scenarios, its vulnerability to adversarial examples impedes deployment, making adversarial training a leading approach to improve robustness. However, pointwise adversarial training maps each clean sample to a single adversary, overlooking surrounding adversarial variability and the associated shifts in feature statistics. To address these issues, we propose Evolutionary Uncertainty-Aware Distributional Adversarial Training (EvoUAD-AT). Specifically, we design an evolutionary adversarial uncertainty modeling module that maintains an instance-wise search distribution over adversarial feature statistics around an adversarially generated anchor. Candidates are evaluated under the current model, selected by their threat to a benign reference, and used to adapt the distribution; strategy parameters are retained across epochs. Extensive evaluations across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in both clean accuracy and robustness, with thorough analyses confirming the effectiveness of each proposed component.

I2I Backdoor: Backdoor Attacks against Image-to-Image Tasks 2026-11-18

Show abstract

With the rapid development of deep learning technology, deep learning-based Image-to-Image (I2I) networks have become the predominant choice for I2I tasks like image super-resolution and denoising. Despite their remarkable performance, the security of I2I networks has not been thoroughly investigated. While some studies have probed their susceptibility to adversarial attacks, none have explored the backdoor attack against I2I networks, which is a more stealthy and severe threat. In this work, for the first time, we comprehensively investigate the vulnerability of I2I networks to backdoor attacks. We propose a backdoor attack against I2I tasks, where the backdoored I2I network behaves normally on clean input images, yet outputs a specific inappropriate image when the backdoor trigger appears on the input image.
