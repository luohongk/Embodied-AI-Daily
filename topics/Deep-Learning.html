Spatiotemporal learning improves quantification of pore-space evolution and fluid displacement 2026-11-12
📝 48 pages, 9 figures, 18 tables, including Supplementary Information
Show abstract

Pore-space evolution and fluid displacement influence how rocks transmit and store fluids, affecting geological carbon storage, underground hydrogen storage and groundwater contaminant transport. A single time-resolved micro-CT experiment may produce tens to hundreds of three-dimensional images, and segmentation is required to quantify physical properties and run image-based simulations. Traditional methods such as thresholding perform poorly on noisy, low-contrast images and need manual adjustment per image; 3D deep learning networks segment each image without temporal information, so their errors vary between images and could be mistaken for physical change. Here we introduce a transformer-based 4D segmentation framework combining spatial and temporal information. It uses sequential images to resolve ambiguous phase assignments and separate physical change from segmentation error. The method is tested on images of mineral dissolution: it measures pore-volume growth and rock boundaries more accurately than an equivalent 3D model that ignores temporal information. It also captures the increase in permeability verified against reference segmentations and flow simulations. The advantage transfers to another dissolution sample without retraining. On two other datasets of fluid displacement, it reduces spurious changes in predicted rock and porosity fluctuations and accurately identifies filling events. Spatiotemporal segmentation turns sequences of hundreds of three-dimensional images into more reliable quantitative measurements of pore-space evolution and fluid displacement without per-image parameter adjustment to support the analysis of time-resolved CT imaging.

A Compact xDAWN-Guided Spatio-Temporal Network with Filter-Bank Convolution for P300 Detection 2026-11-12
Show abstract

P300-based brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) enable communication by decoding stimulus-evoked EEG responses into character selections without requiring voluntary motor output. Reliable P300 detection remains difficult because of low response amplitude and signal-to-noise ratio, while practical spellers require accurate character selection with few stimulus repetitions and low computational cost. We propose a compact deep learning architecture that combines xDAWN spatial filtering for event-related potential enhancement and dimensionality reduction, a fixed frequency-selective filter bank, and convolutional feature learning for spatio-temporal EEG decoding. Depthwise-separable convolutions and progressive average pooling reduce computational complexity while retaining discriminative temporal information. The resulting target/non-target classifier contains only 5,100 trainable parameters. We evaluated the model on two public benchmarks, BCI Competition II Dataset IIb and BCI Competition III Dataset II, and compared it with established P300 decoding methods across different numbers of stimulus repetitions. The model achieved an information transfer rate of 17.8 bits/min with two repetitions on BCI Competition III and 40.5 bits/min on BCI Competition II, demonstrating a favorable accuracy-speed trade-off. These results suggest that combining domain-informed spatial and spectral priors with compact neural networks can support efficient P300 decoding for EEG-based BCI communication.

DPA: Decentralized Primal Averaging with Quasi-Global Momentum for Highly Heterogeneous Data 2026-11-12
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 7 figures. Code available at https://github.com/Diyako754/DPA
Show abstract

Decentralized training of deep learning models keeps training data local and supports on-device learning over networks. In realistic scenarios, heterogeneity across agents' local data poses an optimization challenge and can severely degrade test accuracy, especially on sparse communication graphs. Building on recent advances in centralized primal averaging, we propose Decentralized Primal Averaging (DPA), a decentralized learning algorithm that combines primal averaging with a local quasi-global momentum buffer. DPA maintains three coupled sequences for optimization, gradient evaluation, and output averaging, while using consecutive mixed iterates to construct a network-aware momentum direction without transmitting an auxiliary tracking variable. For smooth nonconvex objectives, we establish a convergence bound for DPA and identify conditions for linear speedup in the number of agents for sufficiently many iterations. We also introduce DPA-1G, a variant that uses one communication round per iteration and matches the per-iteration communication cost of DSGD-style baselines. Experiments following the GUT decentralized image-classification benchmark show that DPA compares favorably with DSGD, gradient tracking, QG-DSGDm, momentum tracking, and QG-GUTm across datasets, architectures, topologies, and heterogeneity levels, with the largest gains under high heterogeneity on sparse rings. At the same total communication cost, both DPA variants achieve higher mean test accuracy than the best baseline on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20. Our code is publicly available.

ActiNet: An Open-Source Tool for Activity Intensity Classification of Wrist-Worn Accelerometry Using Self-Supervised Deep Learning 2026-11-12
Show abstract

The use of accurate and reliable open-source human activity recognition (HAR) models on passively collected wrist-accelerometer data is essential in large-scale epidemiological studies that investigate the association between physical activity and health outcomes. While self-supervised learning has generated considerable excitement in improving HAR, the extent to which these models, coupled with hidden Markov models (HMMs), would make a tangible improvement to classification performance and the effect this may have on the predicted daily activity intensity compositions is unknown. Using up to 24 hours of wrist-worn accelerometer data from 151 CAPTURE-24 participants (aged 18-91, mean age 42, 66% female), we trained the ActiNet model, consisting of HARNet, a self-supervised, 18-layer, modified ResNet-V2 model, followed by hidden Markov model (HMM) smoothing, to classify labels of activity intensity. The performance of this model, evaluated using 5-fold stratified group cross-validation, was then compared to a baseline random forest (RF) + HMM, established in existing literature. Differences in performance and classification outputs were compared with different subgroups of age and sex within the CAPTURE-24 population. The ActiNet model was able to distinguish labels of activity intensity with a mean macro F1 score of 0.80 and a mean Cohen's kappa score of 0.85. This exceeded the performance of the RF + HMM, trained and validated on the same dataset, with mean scores of 0.76 and 0.80, respectively. The improvements in performance were consistent across subgroups of age and sex. These findings encourage the use of ActiNet for the extraction of activity intensity labels from wrist-accelerometer data in future epidemiological studies.

The growing volume of process data generated by screwdriving systems, combined with advancements in computing power, data storage, and algorithms, is facilitating the application of artificial intelligence in this field. Accurately detecting and classifying screwdriving errors is crucial for ensuring product quality, avoiding costly rework in manufacturing, and supporting sustainability by facilitating the maintenance, reuse, and recycling of screw-connected products. In this context, machine learning approaches offer a promising alternative to traditional monitoring techniques, such as window-based methods or envelope curves. This study examines the use of deep learning techniques for anomaly detection and error classification in screwdriving processes. Additionally, real-world data material was examined in order to further demonstrate model robustness. Overall the results indicate strong generalization capability and highlight the practical potential of the developed models for use in industrial screwdriving processes, eliminating the need for expert knowledge in error identification and significantly reducing manual effort and analysis time.

A Dual-Branch Graph and Convolutional Network for Interpretable Movement EEG Classification 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Decoding upper-limb movements from scalp electroencephalography (EEG) is an important problem for brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), but many deep learning models provide limited insight into the brain regions underlying their predictions. We propose a two-branch architecture that combines an LGGNet-inspired local-global graph branch with an EEGNet-style convolutional branch for movement-versus-rest classification. The graph branch groups 61 EEG electrodes into anatomically informed regions and learns their spatial relationships, providing a direct representation of the regions emphasized by the model. We evaluate the proposed architecture using leave-one-subject-out cross-validation on the Ofner upper-limb movement dataset. The model achieves a mean F1-score of 0.74 +/- 0.06 across subjects. Beyond classification performance, we analyze the learned graph structure to identify the regions with the strongest connectivity. Across all held-out subjects, the highest connectivity is consistently observed in the Parietal Left Low region, corresponding to electrodes P1 and P3. This region is opposite to the performed right-arm movements and lies within the posterior parietal area previously associated with upper-limb movement processing. These results show that incorporating a region-based graph into the decoder can provide an anatomically interpretable representation while maintaining effective movement classification, offering a practical approach toward more transparent EEG-based BCIs.

Learning Temporal Dynamics with Derivative Representations for Time Series Forecasting 2026-11-12
📝 20 pages, 3 figures
Show abstract

Time series forecasting aims to predict the future evolution of complex systems by learning their underlying dynamics from past observations. Despite recent advances in deep learning, Transformers in particular still struggle to capture the temporal evolution of system dynamics. Existing approaches have been largely driven by architectural innovation, with comparatively little attention paid to training strategies that explicitly exploit the temporal structure governing system dynamics. In this work, we introduce DeltaTSF, a dynamics-aware forecasting framework based on discrete derivative representations of time series. Specifically, DeltaTSF jointly learns the series and their discrete temporal derivatives across both input and forecast horizons within a unified, model-agnostic framework. By coupling these representations over time, the model is encouraged to maintain temporal consistency, a property we show to induce a form of discrete Taylor consistency during training. This formulation enables the model to better reflect the underlying system dynamics, resulting in more stable and physically coherent forecasts. Notably, DeltaTSF does not require explicit derivative computation and introduces no additional computational overhead at inference time. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and backbone architectures demonstrate that DeltaTSF consistently improves forecasting performance and yields more dynamically coherent predictions than strong SOTA methods. These results highlight the effectiveness of enforcing dynamic coherence through derivative representations as a model-agnostic paradigm that operates alongside and complements architectural design. Source code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.

Multi-Task Self-Evolving Harnesses with the Agent as Its Own Optimizer 2026-11-12
Show abstract

A harness is the code around a language-model agent that organizes prompts, calls tools, manages context, and controls execution. As models grow stronger, recent work has begun to let agents improve their own harnesses, a line of work known as self-evolving harnesses. In most existing methods, a separate proposer running on a human-designed harness modifies the solver's harness, and a separate harness is evolved for each benchmark. Real-world tasks come from many domains, so both the evolution and the evaluation of a harness should cover a diverse range of tasks. We propose a framework close to recursive self-improvement: the same frozen model, on the same version of the harness, first solves tasks as the solver and then, as the proposer, reads the complete run records and directly edits the harness that runs it. Each evolution batch draws tasks from five benchmarks in different domains. To measure generalization, training and held-out tasks are strictly separated, and we additionally evaluate on five out-of-distribution benchmarks never used during evolution. We frame the evolution process as deep-learning training with two stages, multi-task pretraining and continual training. Starting from a 49-line seed harness, the harnesses evolved by GLM-5.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol gain about 5 points on the in-distribution benchmarks, matching or exceeding Claude Code and Codex, and the GPT-5.6 Sol harness is on par with Codex on five out-of-distribution benchmarks. Continued evolution on Claw-Eval in the second stage then lifts GPT-5.6 Sol and GLM-5.3-Flash above Codex and Claude Code. We also provide an in-depth analysis of the mechanisms that emerged during evolution, including output truncation, history compaction, completion checks, independent review, and visual inspection. Code and the evolved harnesses are available at https://github.com/xqkkk/self-evolving-harness.

Density Constraints Overcome the Energy-Density Trade-Off in Exchange-Correlation Functionals 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Exchange--correlation functionals are trained mainly on energies, and their densities often degrade as their energies improve. We present a supervised-learning algorithm that trains functionals on energies and accurate densities without self-consistent-field (SCF) solutions. From one Fock build at a fixed accurate density, it predicts the density that the functional would reach self-consistently and penalizes its deviation from the reference; for functionals linear in their parameters, training is convex. Refitting the MN15 form to 1,500 reaction energies under a constraint on 98 coupled-cluster densities gives the density-constrained hybrid DCH-D4. On 19 held-out energy sets its mean normalized error ratio is 0.96, against 1.76 for MN15, and on 145 densities outside the fit its mean density error is 22 times lower than that of MN15. Deep-learning neural GGA and meta-GGA functionals (NN-GGA and NN-mGGA) trained on only 6 energies and 11 densities lower the atomization-energy error on 103 molecules from 14.6 to 5.6~kcal/mol relative to PBE and from 3.7 to 3.3~kcal/mol relative to SCAN, while roughly halving the density error of each base functional. Accurate densities can thus enter functional training at the scale of modern energy databases.

StraTyper: Automated Semantic Type Discovery and Multi-Type Annotation for Dataset Collections 2026-11-12
Show abstract

Understanding dataset semantics is crucial for effective search, discovery, and integration pipelines. To this end, column-type annotation (CTA) methods associate columns in tabular datasets with semantic types that accurately describe their contents, using pretrained deep learning models or Large Language Models (LLMs). However, existing approaches have important limitations. First, they require users to specify a closed set of semantic types either during training or inference, which hinders their application to domain-specific datasets where predefined type vocabularies often lack adequate coverage and specificity. They also assume that each column is associated with a single semantic type, an assumption that does not hold for many real-world datasets. Lastly, while proprietary LLMs have shown effectiveness for CTA, they incur high monetary costs and produce inconsistent outputs across similar columns, leading to type redundancy that degrades downstream applications. To address these challenges, we introduce StraTyper, a cost-effective method for column type discovery (CTD) and multi-type annotation (CMTA) in dataset collections. StraTyper eliminates the need for predefined semantic labels by employing LLMs to discover types tailored to the target dataset collection. Through strategic column clustering, controlled type generation, and iterative cascading discovery, StraTyper balances type precision with annotation coverage while minimizing LLM costs. Our experimental evaluation, both manual and LLM-assisted, on real-world dataset collections demonstrates that StraTyper discovers accurate types for both numeric and textual data, achieves substantial cost savings over commercial LLMs, and effectively handles multi-typed columns. We further show that StraTyper's annotations improve downstream tasks, including join discovery and schema matching, outperforming LLM-only baselines.

There Will Be a Scientific Theory of Deep Learning 2026-11-11
📝 43 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

In this paper, we make the case that a scientific theory of deep learning is emerging. By this we mean a theory which characterizes important properties and statistics of the training process, hidden representations, final weights, and performance of neural networks. We pull together major strands of ongoing research in deep learning theory and identify five growing bodies of work that point toward such a theory: (a) solvable idealized settings that provide intuition for learning dynamics in realistic systems; (b) tractable limits that reveal insights into fundamental learning phenomena; (c) simple mathematical laws that capture important macroscopic observables; (d) theories of hyperparameters that disentangle them from the rest of the training process, leaving simpler systems behind; and (e) universal behaviors shared across systems and settings which clarify which phenomena call for explanation. Taken together, these bodies of work share certain broad traits: they are concerned with the dynamics of the training process; they primarily seek to describe coarse aggregate statistics; and they emphasize falsifiable quantitative predictions. We argue that the emerging theory is best thought of as a mechanics of the learning process, and suggest the name learning mechanics. We discuss the relationship between this mechanics perspective and other approaches for building a theory of deep learning, including the statistical and information-theoretic perspectives. In particular, we anticipate a symbiotic relationship between learning mechanics and mechanistic interpretability. We also review and address common arguments that fundamental theory will not be possible or is not important. We conclude with a portrait of important open directions in learning mechanics and advice for beginners. We host further introductory materials, perspectives, and open questions at learningmechanics.pub.

Characterizing the Edge of Stability in Variational Training Without Priors 2026-11-11
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 21 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Variational approaches in deep learning promise to deliver improved uncertainty quantification and out-of-distribution generalization by inferring a distribution over weights, regularized by a divergence to a chosen prior. However, prior elicitation presents a significant challenge in deep learning, resulting in the common practice of significantly downweighting the regularization term in the variational objective. Recently, it was proposed to train solely via the expected loss by relying on implicit regularization from the optimizer, rather than explicit regularization from the divergence to the prior. While this approach largely sidesteps the issue of prior elicitation, current theory lacks an understanding of how key optimization hyperparameters impact the implicit regularization. Here, we theoretically and empirically demonstrate that larger learning rates and fewer parameter samples lead to flatter minima of the loss landscape, which are known to generalize better. We show that training on the expected loss operates at a modified edge of stability, characterized by the signal-to-noise ratio of the gradient estimates and the number of Monte Carlo samples. Experiments on out-of-distribution benchmark datasets confirm the practical significance of the theoretical results.

Accurate and reliable near-field multi-source localization is essential for location-aware processing in future wireless systems. Imputing Missing Near-Surface Air Temperature using Graph Attention Networks with Distance-Aware Edge Features 2026-11-11
📝 23 pages, 7 figures; LaTeX
Show abstract

We introduce a graph attention network for reconstructing near-surface air temperature fields from incomplete observations. The model represents each grid cell as a node connected to its $k$ nearest neighbors, with attention weights that depend on node features, distance, and land-ocean transitions. This graph representation avoids the polar distortions associated with treating the globe as a flat latitude-longitude image, as most deep learning reconstruction methods do. This is particularly relevant for historical temperature records, which extend back to the mid-nineteenth century but remain incomplete, with the largest gaps early in the record and at high latitudes. We train our model on CMIP6 CESM2 historical simulations masked with the time-varying HadCRUT5 observational coverage from 1850 to 2014. We then compare our approach with ordinary kriging, GraphEM, and the LaMa inpainting network on a held-out realization. The graph model achieves the lowest spatial RMSE (0.997 K), 10.1% lower than LaMa and approximately 31% lower than kriging and GraphEM. The largest gains occur in the Arctic and Antarctic, while LaMa remains slightly more accurate in the densely observed tropics. The graph model's error also falls faster as coverage increases, so its advantage over LaMa grows by more than a third from the 1850s to the 2000s. An ablation study shows that the model's skill comes mainly from the geometric graph construction rather than from auxiliary physical covariates.

Deep Heteroskedastic Regression: Post-Hoc Variance Estimation from Latent Representations 2026-11-11
📝 Code available at https://github.com/jonasvj/PracticalDeepHeteroskedasticRegression
Show abstract

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) in deep learning regression is of wide interest, as it supports critical applications including sequential decision making and risk-sensitive tasks.
