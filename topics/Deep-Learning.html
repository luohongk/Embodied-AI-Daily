Deep Learning-based Semantic Segmentation of Dental Radiographs: Comparative Evaluation of PSPNet, DeepLabv3, UNet, and UNet++ 2026-10-19
📝 11 pages
Show abstract

Accurate and efficient analysis of dental radiographs has become increasingly important for disease diagnosis and clinical decision making. In particular, dental radiographs segmentation plays a critical role as it is challenging due to several reasons, such as low contrast, noise in the images, variations in the image quality, etc. Previous studies have primarily addressed these challenges using convolutional neural networks (CNN)-based models. However, direct comparison of these segmentation models is still challenging due to the variations among the experimental settings. Therefore, we mainly compared four segmentation models, including Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet), DeepLabV3, UNet, and UNet++, through a fair, controlled experimentation setting under varying conditions such as image sizes and batch sizes. For this, we used 1269 dental radiography images, and prior to training, we implemented a series of preprocessing techniques. To assess the models, we used several metrics, including Dice coefficient (DICE), Intersection over Union (IoU), accuracy, precision, training and validation losses. According to our results, UNet++ demonstrated the highest IoU score of 0.6719, and a DICE value of 0.7869, showing superior results in terms of segmentation, specifically when configured with an image size of 300x300 and a batch size of 16. In addition, we also explored the effect of image resolutions and batch sizes on model performance and accuracy under different architectures. Therefore, this study provides insights into the strengths of different segmentation models and the potential of deep learning-based semantic segmentation methods for improving automated dental radiograph analysis.

Channel-Dependent State Space Model for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF) is critical across many real-world domains. Existing deep learning approaches fall into two paradigms with distinct limitations: channel-independent (CI) methods unconditionally ignore cross-variable dependencies and model only temporal dynamics, while channel-dependent (CD) methods consider both but typically rely on architectural compromises to mitigate overfitting and computational overhead. We therefore propose Chameleon, a specialized CD state space model (SSM) that enables data-dependent, fine-grained interactions across variables while scaling linearly with their number. By connecting selective SSMs with the Kalman filter, we leverage the missing measurement update in the former for cross-variable modeling while preserving the SSM backbone for robust temporal modeling. We further identify favorable inductive biases of GatedDeltaNet for time series, adapt it as our backbone, and improve generalization through additional techniques, including a previously unexplored stochastic perturbation of reversible instance normalization. On strongly dependent ODE and PEMS datasets, Chameleon achieves the best MSE and MAE across all settings, while its CI ablation and prior CD methods incur 61-178% higher MSE on average. Across 28 standard benchmark settings, Chameleon also achieves better MSE and MAE than each baseline in at least 27 and 22 cases, respectively. Training-time and peak-memory analyses on Traffic and ETT further demonstrate competitive efficiency and favorable memory scalability across different variable counts.

Injection of Space Radiation Errors on Low-power NVM using Generative Deep Learning 2026-10-19
Show abstract

We develop an error model for a Non-Volatile Memory (NVM) device that represents the effects of physical faults experienced in a CubeSat mission in Low Earth Orbit (LEO). The CubeSat carries an off-the-shelf low-power Ferroelectric RAM (FRAM) chip and runs repeated read/write tests according to a predefined pattern. We compare every memory snapshot received from the satellite with a reference bitmap to identify bit errors. We visually analyze erroneous values distributions in the memory snapshots and we understand that they are frequently distributed according to regular spatial patterns. We define a generative deep learning technique to automatically learn these spatial patterns and to synthetically generate additional error patterns mimicking the observed ones. The model is capable at producing memory errors for general application-level experimentation. Our preliminary validation indicates that when comparing the model output with the data we collect, 0.92 of the generated bit flips correspond to bit flips in the memory snapshots and the model captures 0.91 of the observed bit flips. A comparison of the spatial distribution of bit flips in 10,000 generated error patterns also provides a Wasserstein distance of 0.073, indicating that the two distributions differ by roughly 7% of the maximum possible displacement of their probability masses.

Toward a Physics of Deep Learning and Brains 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Deep neural networks and brains both learn and share superficial similarities: processing nodes are likened to neurons and adjustable weights are likened to modifiable synapses. But can a unified theoretical framework be found to underlie them both? Here we show that the equations used to describe neuronal avalanches in living brains can also be applied to cascades of activity in deep neural networks. These equations are derived from non-equilibrium statistical physics and show that deep neural networks learn best when poised between absorbing and active phases. Because these networks are strongly driven by inputs, however, they do not operate at a true critical point but within a quasi-critical regime -- one that still approximately satisfies crackling noise scaling relations. By training networks with different initializations, we show that maximal susceptibility is a more reliable predictor of learning than proximity to the critical point itself. This provides a blueprint for engineering improved network performance. Finally, using finite-size scaling we identify distinct universality classes, including Barkhausen noise and directed percolation. This theoretical framework demonstrates that universal features are shared by both biological and artificial neural networks.

Propensity Straight-Through Gradients for Discrete Stochastic Systems 2026-10-19
📝 19 pages, 6 figures, and Supplementary information
Show abstract

We introduce the propensity straight-through (PST) estimator for gradient-based learning through exact simulations of continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs). These models describe discrete stochastic dynamics across physics, chemistry, and biology, but their categorical event choices prevent ordinary differentiation. PST preserves exact forward trajectories and differentiates, in the backward pass, the normalized reaction propensities the simulator already computes. The affine state update makes this rule exact for the one-step conditional-mean sensitivity. We derive the per-step discrepancy for one-step sensitivities composed along nonlinear trajectories in closed form, show that it vanishes for affine downstream dependence, and bound it quadratically in the stoichiometric jump size. Across reversible dimerization, a genetic oscillator, a 50-task repressilator suite, and experimental patch-clamp recordings, PST recovers parameters to within 0.06-1.7% and converges 2.4-3.0-fold faster than the Gumbel-Softmax straight-through estimator at matched computational cost. At deep-learning scale, PST trains all 203,796 parameters of a stochastic reaction network using exact discrete events from initialization. By differentiating an exact conditional mean, PST eliminates reaction-selection temperature tuning and provides a scalable route to learning through exact stochastic dynamics.

Logit Averaging During Training Sets a New State of the Art for Non-Invasive Speech BCI 2026-10-19
Show abstract

Non-invasive brain-computer interfaces are in practice limited by signal-to-noise ratio, and recent state-of-the-art methods address this limitation by aggregating over repeated trials of the same stimulus. Yet multi-trial aggregation has been treated inconsistently across the literature: some work averages brain signals at the model's input, other work averages predictions at the model's output, and neither treats aggregation as an architectural choice in its own right. We provide the first systematic comparison of aggregation strategies for non-invasive neural decoding, evaluating six paradigms (including logit averaging during training and three attention-based variants we introduce) across three datasets, four tasks, and two group sizes. We find that aggregation choice matters substantially: training-time logit averaging, the simplest method we propose, outperforms all others in 6 of 7 settings. On the phoneme classification setting of the 2025 PNPL competition, it sets a new state of the art, significantly outperforming the competition winner by 17.4 absolute percentage points in balanced accuracy. We further draw a theoretical connection between logit averaging and joint ensemble training, which has been shown to underperform independent ensembling in the deep learning literature; our results call into question recent explanations of why joint ensemble training fails. Together, these findings establish multi-trial aggregation as a key design choice for non-invasive neural decoding and identify training-time logit averaging as a simple, effective default.

Superposed analog memory for vector storage in neural networks 2026-10-19
Show abstract

The growing memory demands of artificial neural networks pose a major challenge for energy-efficient computing, particularly for models that rely on large collections of high-dimensional weight and embedding vectors. Conventional memory architectures store these vectors individually, requiring many memory cells per vector and limiting achievable storage density. Here we demonstrate an analog memory architecture that encodes multiple vectors in a distributed superposition within a single memory array. Individual vectors are recovered through in-memory matrix-vector multiplication, allowing the degree of superposition to be tuned to balance storage density against recall fidelity. This approach introduces a new scaling dimension for analog memory, in which effective storage density can be increased without a corresponding increase in the number of physical memory cells. The distributed representation also provides resilience to device-level noise. We implement the architecture on a prototype phase-change memory chip and experimentally evaluate it using embedding tables from deep learning recommendation models and large language models. The results demonstrate inference accuracy comparable to full-precision software baselines, establishing analog superposition as a promising route towards dense, energy-efficient memory for artificial intelligence.

MADGRAV: a multilevel anomaly-detection pipeline for gravitational-wave searches applied to LIGO data 2026-10-19
📝 16 pages, 6 figures, submitted to EPJ C
Show abstract

We present the results of \textbf{MADGRAV}, a deep-learning-based search for high-mass compact binary coalescences, applied to the data collected by the LIGO interferometers during the third observing run and during the first and second part of the fourth observing run. The \textbf{MADGRAV} pipeline consists of a series of sequential convolutional neural networks that perform anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing, and signal ranking. Data from the Hanford and Livingston LIGO detectors are studied (both individually and in coherence) by way of 1 second Q-transform windows. Of the candidates that survive every stage of the pipeline, 48 reach the significance threshold, and we report 47 gravitational wave detections characterised by a false alarm rate below $1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ with a probability of astrophysical origin $p_{\rm astro}>0.9$. Of the 47 detections, 44 are shared with the minimally modelled coherent WaveBurst search. The observed total source-frame masses, extracted from official gravitational wave transient catalogues, are in the $14-236 M_{\odot}$ range with a median of $69 M_{\odot}$, and a median SNR of 16. We note that the recovered fraction of confident detections rises with mass: for LIGO detectors network SNR $>10$ the pipeline recovers $8.1\%$ of confident catalog events below $30 M_{\odot}$, $39.8\%$ between $30$ and $100 M_{\odot}$, and $53.3\%$ above $100 M_{\odot}$, corresponding to $33.3\%$, $45.5\%$ and $53.3\%$ of the events detected by coherent WaveBurst in the same bins. These results suggest that anomaly detection pipelines can serve as an independent detection channel complementary to matched filtering in the high-mass high-SNR regime.

We formulate implicit negative candidate discovery to identify unobserved interactions supported by observed customer behavior. Copy-move forgery detection aims at detecting duplicated regions in a suspected forged image, and deep learning based copy-move forgery detection methods are in the ascendant. These deep learning based methods heavily rely on synthetic training data, and the performance will degrade when facing new tasks.
