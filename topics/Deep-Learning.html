Towards end-to-end LLM-based censoring-aware survival analysis 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Objective: Survival analysis is central to medical prediction, yet large language models (LLMs) are rarely used as end-to-end survival models because censoring prevents straightforward supervised fine-tuning. Here we present LLMSurvival, a framework that enables censoring-aware survival analysis with unmodified LLMs operating directly on tabular clinical data. Materials and Methods: LLMSurvival reformulates time-to-event prediction as pairwise ranking among comparable subjects, and derives test-time risk by aggregating comparisons against anchor individuals from the training cohort. Results: Across two clinical tasks (ICU mortality prediction in MIMIC-IV and fragility fracture prediction in a NewYork-Presbyterian/Weill Cornell Medicine cohort), LLMSurvival improves overall concordance over Cox proportional hazards modeling by 3.1% for ICU mortality and 0.5% for fracture risk, 2.1% on average for ICU mortality and 2.8% for fracture risk over three established deep learning survival models. Discussion: The results show that survival modeling with censoring can be made compatible with LLM fine-tuning through comparison-based reformulation. The framework demonstrates high portability and superior performance over expert curated scores like SAPS-II and FRAX scores across diverse clinical context. Furthermore, the framework supports local deployment, as compact, publicly available base models provide sufficient performance. Conclusion: The LLMSurvival framework serves as a proof of concept for an integrated, censoring-conscious approach to survival analysis via LLMs.

DailyWildfireCAN: A Dataset and Benchmark for Next-Day Wildfire Spread Prediction in Canada 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Wildfires represent a critical ecological and socio-economic challenge in Canada, where vast boreal forests and a warming climate have amplified the frequency and severity of extreme fire seasons. Predicting next-day wildfire spread can support fire management, but requires capturing complex interactions among weather, fuels, and terrain. Deep learning has emerged as a promising approach to learning these relationships from heterogeneous environmental data, yet its development and evaluation depend on representative datasets. Existing deep learning research on wildfire spread relies predominantly on US-focused datasets, leaving Canadian fire environments underrepresented. To address this gap, we present DailyWildfireCAN, a multi-modal, multi-temporal dataset and benchmark for predicting next-day wildfire spread across Canada. The dataset tracks continuous fire progression over multi-day events at a high spatial and temporal resolution of 90-m and 24-hour, respectively. This resolution preserves fine-scale information relevant to fire-edge geometry, local fuel variations, and terrain effects, supporting research toward improved daily wildfire spread forecasting. DailyWildfireCAN covers 2,440 wildfire events across eight fire seasons 2017-2024 and integrates 33 features across diverse modalities, including topography, meteorology, land cover, vegetation and forest structure, human influence indices, and satellite imagery. We establish a comprehensive benchmark comparing mono-temporal and multi-temporal prediction models, accompanied by a multi-dimensional evaluation framework assessing robustness across wildfire characteristics, geographic regions, and temporal conditions, spatial prediction quality, and sensitivity to reference label variations.

Deviation-Aware Pretraining for Cardiovascular Disease Detection from Electrocardiograms 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Electrocardiogram (ECG) signals are critical for cardiovascular disease (CVD) detection. Most current deep learning models rely on supervised, task-specific training, which limits their ability to generalize across diseases, patient populations, and acquisition settings. This paper presents a self-supervised ECG feature extraction framework that learns from nearly one million unlabeled 12-lead ECG recordings. The framework includes two modules: Deviation Localization Embedding (DLE), which detects waveform-level deviations from normal patterns, and Association Reconstruction Embedding (ARE), which models cross-lead associations through deviation-guided reconstruction to capture abnormal patterns expressed across multiple leads. These modules enable the framework to capture subtle deviation-related patterns and their cross-lead context. Embedding visualizations show that the learned features support meaningful disease grouping and anomaly identification. The approach reduces the need for labeled data and can support clinicians in diverse healthcare settings. The framework has potential to improve early detection and monitoring of cardiovascular diseases, contributing to more accessible and equitable heart care.

Show abstract

Multimodal datasets that combine synchronised behavioural and physiological signals with subjective affective and body perception measures are still rare. This limits both the understanding of embodied experience and the development of computational models that infer subjective states from sensor data. We introduce BODYinTRANSIT-Lab, a multimodal dataset that addresses this gap by capturing affective, perceptual, and behavioural responses to the Footsteps Illusion. In this closed-loop paradigm, participants perform walking tasks while receiving real-time manipulated auditory feedback of their own footsteps intended to alter perceived body weight. The dataset comprises synchronised recordings from 101 participants recruited through systematic stratification by eating-disorder symptomatology and physical activity level. BODYinTRANSIT-Lab combines full-body kinematics with electromyography, electrocardiography, respiration, and electrodermal activity. On within-session, segment-level splits, the baseline model achieved mean balanced accuracies of 45.9% for affective valence and 44.5% for perceived weight. The BODYinTRANSIT-Lab dataset provides an experimentally controlled resource for cognitive neuroscience and affective computing research, with potential applications in affect-aware interactive systems, health and well-being technologies, and creative embodied experiences.

Objective: To improve the spatial and temporal resolution of aortic 4D flow MRI through a unified deep learning framework for post-acquisition super-resolution (SR) of reconstructed velocity vector fields. Methods: We propose CURRENT, a recurrent neural network that performs spatial and temporal SR with a single network architecture. Supervised training was conducted using paired low- and high-resolution datasets generated by embedding computational fluid dynamics simulations of aortic flow into in vivo thoracic MRI backgrounds, followed by controlled spatial and temporal downsampling. Furthermore, the proposed model demonstrated promising generalization to in vivo 4D flow MRI data despite being trained exclusively on synthetic datasets. Conclusion: CURRENT provides a unified framework for spatial and temporal SR of 4D flow MRI. The proposed approach has the potential to enhance physics-based analysis and broaden the applicability of accelerated 4D flow MRI.

Adapting a Vision Foundation Model for Fracture Characterization in Acoustic Image Logs 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Natural fractures (NFs) and drilling-induced tensile fractures (DITFs) in acoustic image logs provide insights into rock mass structure and in situ stress, but manual characterization is time-consuming. Deep learning can automate this task, although expert-labeled data are scarce. We investigate whether adapting a pretrained vision foundation model (VFM) outperforms task-specific models trained from scratch with limited annotations. Using 80 annotated acoustic image samples, we fine-tuned DINOv3, pretrained on satellite imagery, and evaluated it on four boreholes excluded from fine-tuning. Eigenvalues of the Hessian in Deep Learning: The Origin of Symmetry and Its Breaking 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Hessian spectra at trained models in deep learning exhibit a persistent pattern: eigenvalues organize into distinct clusters, including a large bulk near zero and a few isolated outliers. This paper shows that a natural account of these spectral phenomena emerges when the original setting is understood as a departure from a nearby, otherwise hidden, highly symmetric reference. Modifications, including changes to the architecture, data distribution, or parameter metric, expose a nearby reference configuration whose Hessian exhibits rich invariances-ones not accounted for by weight symmetries. There, symmetry enables a precise description of the spectra, forcing high-dimensional kernels and eigenvalues of large multiplicity. Returning to the original configuration breaks the Hessian symmetry and thereby produces the observed hierarchy of clusters and outliers. The framework is developed in some generality, with a detailed analysis of three-layer ReLU networks and applications to convolutional, graph, and transformer models, as well as to the NTK. The same mechanism is further shown to yield analogous spectral structures in layerwise Hessians and the Gauss-Newton matrix.

COMO: Closed-Loop Optical Molecule Recognition with Minimum Risk Training 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Optical chemical structure recognition (OCSR) translates molecular images into machine-readable representations such as SMILES strings or molecular graphs, but remains challenging in real-world documents due to wide variations in chemical structures, shorthand conventions, and scanning artifacts. Most existing deep learning approaches rely on teacher forcing with token-level Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). This training paradigm suffers from exposure bias, as models are trained under ground-truth prefixes but must condition on their own previous predictions during inference. Moreover, token-level MLE objectives hinder the optimization towards molecular-level evaluation criteria such as chemical validity and structural similarity. Here we introduce Minimum Risk Training (MRT) to OCSR and propose COMO (Closed-loop Optical Molecule recOgnition), a framework that mitigates exposure bias by directly optimizing over molecule-level, non-differentiable objectives. Experiments on ten benchmarks, including synthetic and real-world chemical diagrams from patent and scientific literature, demonstrate that COMO substantially outperforms existing rule-based and learning-based methods with less training data, consistently achieving superior performance across both reproduced baselines and previously reported results. Ablation studies further show that MRT is architecture-agnostic, demonstrating its potential for broad application to end-to-end OCSR systems.

Show abstract

Recent progress in physics-aware deep learning (DL) has embedded mass conservation within Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture for hydrologic modeling, improving physical consistency and enabling diagnostics of internal states. However, existing mass-conserving neural networks are primarily developed in lumped formulations. Consequently, previous studies provide only fragmented and often indirect evidence of when and why imposed physical constraints may lead to unrealistic hydrologic behavior. To address this limitation, we propose Physics-Enhanced Conservation-Augmented Net (PECAN), a mass-conserving convolutional LSTM-based framework for distributed hydrologic modeling and diagnosing structural limitations of physics-aware DL models. PECAN demonstrates accurate streamflow simulation at gauges and shows diagnostic potential to expose structural limitations in hydrologic process representation, thereby informing future physics-aware neural network development for hydrology.

The Rise of Low Precision Floating Point 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Floating point computing has dominated scientific computations since the 1950s because of its versatile ability to approximate real numbers with both sufficiently high precision and sufficiently great range. Since 1985, two specific formats have become standard across the industry, one with 32 bits which is adequate for many purposes and one with 64 bits which allows numbers with both much higher precision and much greater range for applications where these are needed. However, during the past decade, deep learning has come to dominate computation, and narrower floating point formats with lower precision and reduced range have steadily become more common. In this article we discuss this phenomenon, why it is happening, how various 16-bit, 8-bit and even 4-bit floating point formats have been developed, and efforts to standardize these formats.

Kolmogorov-Chapman Maps: Trajectory-Free Learning for Simulating Molecular Dynamics 2026-11-09
Show abstract

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) describe stochastic processes across a wide range of scientific domains, including physics, biology, and finance. Although individual numerical integration steps are inexpensive, observing meaningful events or transitions can require millions of sequential steps, resulting in substantial wall-clock simulation time and poor hardware utilization. Deep learning offers an alternative by amortizing many integration steps into a single neural network forward pass, potentially enabling much faster simulation. However, existing supervised approaches typically require long simulated trajectory data, which restricts their applicability. In this work, we introduce Kolmogorov-Chapman Maps (KC-Maps), a method for learning the transition kernel of an SDE that relies only on the trajectory-free marginal samples together with the known drift and diffusion coefficients. Given an initial time and state of a process, KC-Maps models its distribution integrated to any given time, corresponding to the weak solution of the SDE. To recover the correct dynamics, KC-Maps satisfies two key properties: (i) it satisfies the boundary conditions, which we incorporate directly into the neural network architecture, and (ii) it respects the Kolmogorov--Chapman equation, which provides an efficient training objective. We empirically demonstrate that KC-Maps learns to integrate the dynamics of peptide molecular systems and outperforms classical molecular dynamics in terms of GPU-hours.
