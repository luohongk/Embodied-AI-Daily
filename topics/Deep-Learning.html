Multi-institutional grading of Barrett's esophagus dysplasia without stain normalization using a pathology foundation model and multiple instance learning 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Barrett's esophagus dysplasia grading suffers substantial inter-observer variability, particularly for low-grade dysplasia, complicating clinical decision-making. We conducted a retrospective multi-institutional diagnostic accuracy study of a foundation model-based deep learning system for dysplasia grading, using 969 H\&E-stained whole slide images from four institutions. Patch-level classifiers trained on expert annotations identified diagnostically relevant regions, followed by attribute-aware multiple instance learning for slide-level classification as non-dysplastic Barrett's esophagus (NDBE), low-grade dysplasia, or high-grade dysplasia. On a held-out test set (n = 130), macro-averaged AUC was 0.96 (95\% CI 0.93--0.98). For binary dysplasia detection, sensitivity was 98\% (80/82) and specificity 81\% (39/48), with a negative likelihood ratio of 0.030 and quadratic-weighted \kap{} of 0.83 against expert consensus. No high-grade dysplasia case was misclassified as NDBE. Performance was consistent across institutions without stain normalization, indicating that stain-robust foundation-model representations may remove the site-specific preprocessing that has constrained prior systems to institution-specific deployment.

DailyWildfireCAN: A Dataset and Benchmark for Next-Day Wildfire Spread Prediction in Canada 2026-11-19
📝 Preprint; under review
Show abstract

Wildfires represent a critical ecological and socio-economic challenge in Canada, where vast boreal forests and a warming climate have amplified the frequency and severity of extreme fire seasons. Predicting next-day wildfire spread can support fire management, but requires capturing complex interactions among weather, fuels, and terrain. Deep learning has emerged as a promising approach to learning these relationships from heterogeneous environmental data, yet its development and evaluation depend on representative datasets. Existing deep learning research on wildfire spread relies predominantly on US-focused datasets, leaving Canadian fire environments underrepresented. To address this gap, we present DailyWildfireCAN, a multi-modal, multi-temporal dataset and benchmark for predicting next-day wildfire spread across Canada. The dataset tracks continuous fire progression over multi-day events at a high spatial and temporal resolution of 90-m and 24-hour, respectively. This resolution preserves fine-scale information relevant to fire-edge geometry, local fuel variations, and terrain effects, supporting research toward improved daily wildfire spread forecasting. DailyWildfireCAN covers 2,440 wildfire events across eight fire seasons 2017-2024 and integrates 33 features across diverse modalities, including topography, meteorology, land cover, vegetation and forest structure, human influence indices, and satellite imagery. We establish a comprehensive benchmark comparing mono-temporal and multi-temporal prediction models, accompanied by a multi-dimensional evaluation framework assessing robustness across wildfire characteristics, geographic regions, and temporal conditions, spatial prediction quality, and sensitivity to reference label variations.

Single-molecule force spectroscopy (SMFS) probes biomolecular mechanics at the single-molecule level, but high-throughput acquisition of force-extension curves creates a data processing bottleneck. Identifying rare specific unbinding events among thousands of noise-dominated traces still relies largely on manual visual inspection. Here, we present a deep learning workflow for the preliminary screening of severely imbalanced SMFS datasets. Using 2D image representations of force-extension curves without system-specific physical priors, we fine-tuned a modified ResNet18 architecture trained with focal loss and evaluated it on the mechanical unfolding pathways of the Ruminococcus champanellensis XMod-Doc/Coh complex. Channel-Dependent State Space Model for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF) is critical across many real-world domains. Existing deep learning approaches fall into two paradigms with distinct limitations: channel-independent (CI) methods ignore cross-variable dependencies and model only temporal dynamics, while channel-dependent (CD) methods consider both but typically rely on architectural compromises to mitigate overfitting and computational overhead. We therefore propose Chameleon, a CD state space model (SSM) that enables data-dependent, fine-grained interactions across variables while scaling linearly with the number of variables. By connecting selective SSMs with the Kalman filter, we adapt the latter's measurement-update formulation for cross-variable modeling while preserving the SSM backbone for robust temporal modeling. On strongly dependent ODE and PEMS datasets, Chameleon achieves the best MSE and MAE across all settings, while its CI ablation and prior CD methods incur 61-178% higher MSE on average. Across 28 standard benchmark settings, Chameleon also achieves better MSE and MAE than each baseline in at least 27 and 22 cases, respectively. Training-time, FLOP, and peak-memory analyses on Traffic and ETT further demonstrate competitive efficiency and favorable scalability across different variable counts.

SCALE: Towards Whole-Slide Counterfactual Explanations in Cancer Histopathology 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Accurate cancer diagnosis depends on histopathological evaluation, in which tumor morphology drives disease classification and therapeutic decision-making. While deep learning models have demonstrated strong performance in predicting diagnostic and prognostic properties from histopathology images, their clinical adoption remains limited by a lack of interpretability. Unlike conventional medical images where diagnostic findings are spatially localized, histopathological images are characterized by complex textural patterns distributed across the entire whole slide image (WSI), making it difficult to explain model predictions through region-based localization alone. Counterfactual explanations offer a promising alternative by asking "what changes in the image could have altered the model prediction." To achieve this, we propose SCALE, a framework for generating such counterfactual explanations for histopathological DL models. In a blinded review by pathologists, SCALE demonstrated superior morphological plausibility compared to baseline methods (win rate of 91.7%) and robust counterfactual effectiveness (target consistency of 96.7%). These results suggest that SCALE generates morphologically plausible and effective counterfactual explanations that can be applied to improve the interpretability of deep learning models for histopathology.

ZoionNET: Low-Power Animal Intrusion Detection Using an Acoustic Deep-Learning Classifier for a Cellular Remote-Sensing System 2026-11-19
📝 Honorary Mention, 2024 IEEE ComSoc Student Competition
Show abstract

Crop raiding and village intrusion by elephants and large felids impose a recurring cost on communities along forest boundaries in southern India, and existing camera-based monitoring has proven difficult to sustain on the power budgets available at such sites. We present ZoionNET, a battery-powered sensing node that detects animal intrusion acoustically rather than visually. Audio from an onboard PDM microphone is sampled at 8 kHz, converted to mel-spectrograms on-device, and classified on an Arm Ethos-U55 microNPU by two int8-quantised YOLOv8 models arranged as a two-state machine: a lightweight always-on model runs in a dormant state and gates a four-class species classifier through exponential-moving-average thresholds, so the heavier model runs only when an intrusion is likely. The two models reach reported accuracies of 0.974 and 0.946 on held-out audio, and a complete node costs USD 133.57 in parts. We report the system as built and deployed, and identify explicitly what has not yet been measured, most notably the energy behaviour that motivates the two-state design.

KernelOPT: Dispatch-Aware Agentic Search for GPU Kernel Optimization 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Deep learning inference and training performance depends critically on GPU kernel efficiency. Modern compilers such as PyTorch Inductor automatically generate GPU kernels from high-level model code, but frequently underperform expert-written implementations by wide margins. Recent LLM-assisted optimizers explore these opportunities through kernel generation and refinement; yet translating isolated kernel improvements into model-level gains remains a challenge. We present KernelOPT, a profiling-guided multi-agent system for GPU kernel optimization. It inspects generated Triton kernels and external library calls in compiler artifacts to guide synthesis and refinement, then assembles and evaluates the resulting model. Four stages of validation combine execution checks, multi-seed numerical comparison, model-level checking with a float64 fallback and end-to-end performance gating ($γ{=}1.03$). The system supports PyTorch nn.Modules, standalone Triton kernels, and Helion kernels. It is evaluated on all 250 KernelBench problems on NVIDIA H200 which includes 100 single-operator problems (Level1), 100 composite operators (Level2), and 50 model architectures (Level3). KernelOPT achieves geometric mean speedups over torch.compile of 1.40$\times$, 1.15$\times$ and 1.07$\times$ respectively across L1, L2 and L3. Among problems exceeding 1.03$\times$ speedup, the geomeans are 2.54$\times$ (36 L1), 1.84$\times$ (23 L2), and 1.37$\times$ (11 L3). These gains include arithmetic-precision changes and task-specific algebraic simplifications under the evaluated numerical tolerances. The evaluation also reveals discrepancies between isolated kernel improvements and end-to-end performance, motivating model-level measurement alongside kernel-level optimization. Code is available at https://github.com/TorchedHat/KernelOPT.

Explainability of genotype embedding models through observed cohort paths 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Deep learning models that combine genotypes with neuroimaging predict neurological diseases more accurately than either modality alone. Their clinical use, however, requires identifying which genetic variants a prediction is based on. Path-based attribution methods like Integrated Gradients address this by integrating the model's gradient from a baseline to the input. These methods assume the model accepts any point along that path as a valid input. Genotype models violate this assumption. They map the three discrete allele dosages \{0,1,2\} to vectors through a learned embedding defined only at those values, so a straight line passes through fractional genotypes at which no embedding, and therefore no gradient, exists. Attribution must therefore proceed in embedding space where the choice of path can affect the resulting attribution. We show that the commonly used per-variant estimator does not generally satisfy completeness. Additive separability guarantees completeness for this estimator, but interactions between variants generally break that property. Instead, we integrate along piecewise-linear embedding-space paths connecting genotype waypoints observed in the training cohort. Each path satisfies completeness in the continuum although the interiors of its segments are not necessarily observed. Our method improves faithfulness over Integrated Gradients and discretized Integrated Gradients under both perturbation protocols, achieves the strongest results for Parkinson's disease classification under support-preserving perturbations, and is competitive with a support-background Shapley baseline under per-variant perturbations. It also shows higher seed stability and better recovery of planted non-additive effects in a synthetic benchmark.

Secure multiparty computation (MPC) lets two servers compute a prediction on a secret-shared drug, without either server learning the drug. However, secure inference of deep models is slow. In this paper, we present our solution to Track~III of the iDASH 2026 privacy and security competition, which asked for fast two-party secure DTI inference on GPUs.
