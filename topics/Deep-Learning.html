The Collection of a Real-World, Large-Scale Dataset for Automated Skill Evaluation in Central Line Dressing Change 2026-10-16
📝 9 pages
Show abstract

Central line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSIs) remain a major source of preventable morbidity and mortality in healthcare settings. Proper execution of the central line dressing change (CLDC) procedure is essential for infection prevention, yet current approaches to competency assessment rely primarily on manual ob- servation by clinical educators, making evaluation time-intensive, difficult to scale, and limited in consistency and granularity. These challenges highlight the need for AI-assisted skill evaluation methods that can support more objective, reproducible, and scalable assessment of clinical performance. However, progress in this area has been constrained by the lack of broad-scale, clinically grounded datasets for developing and evaluating such methods. In this work, we present the collection process of a large-scale video dataset for CLDC skill evaluation, comprising 365 recordings of registered nurses collected during the 2026 annual competency exam at Houston Methodist Hospital. Each video is paired with subtask-level temporal annotations and clinical performance assessments spanning 17 subtasks of the procedure. We describe the data collection and curation pipeline, including pilot testing of record- ing configurations, annotation protocol design, and post-collection processing. We further establish the feasibility of video-based skill assessment through benchmark experiments with spatiotemporal deep learning models. This dataset provides a foundation for future research on automated clinical skill assessment, feedback generation, and AI-assisted training in nursing education.

Accelerated Execution of Bayesian Neural Networks using a Single Probabilistic Forward Pass and Code Generation 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted for publication in ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (TACO)
Show abstract

Machine learning models perform well across domains such as diagnostics, weather forecasting, NLP, and autonomous driving, but their limited uncertainty handling restricts use in safety-critical settings. Traditional neural networks often fail to detect out-of-domain (OOD) data and may output confident yet incorrect predictions. Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) address this by providing probabilistic estimates, but incur high computational cost because predictions require sampling weight distributions and multiple forward passes. The Probabilistic Forward Pass (PFP) offers a highly efficient approximation to Stochastic Variational Inference (SVI) by assuming Gaussian-distributed weights and activations, enabling fully analytic uncertainty propagation and replacing sampling with a single deterministic forward pass. We present an end-to-end pipeline for training, compiling, optimizing, and deploying PFP-based BNNs on embedded ARM CPUs. Using the TVM deep learning compiler, we implement a dedicated library of Gaussian-propagating operators for multilayer perceptrons and convolutional neural networks, combined with manual and automated tuning strategies. Ablation studies show that PFP consistently outperforms SVI in computational efficiency, achieving speedups of up to 4200x for small mini-batches. PFP-BNNs match SVI-BNNs on Dirty-MNIST in accuracy, uncertainty estimation, and OOD detection while greatly reducing compute cost. These results highlight the potential of combining Bayesian approximations with code generation to enable efficient BNN deployment on resource-constrained systems.

ECG-SDI: Single-lead Electrocardiogram Enables Continuous Sleep Depth Index Profiling and Scalable Digital Biomarkers for Sleep Health 2026-10-16
Show abstract

Conventional sleep staging provides a coarse-grained representation that obscures within-stage depth variation. We developed ECG-SDI, a single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) framework for continuous sleep-depth profiling and digital biomarker extraction. Using 12,392 overnight polysomnographic recordings, we trained and validated a two-stage deep learning model supervised by conventional sleep-stage labels. Correlations with ordered stages excluding rapid eye movement (REM) sleep were 0.828 in the pooled internal test set and 0.848 in the external Cleveland Family Study; corresponding REM classification AUROCs were 0.954 and 0.977. Overnight trajectories captured within-stage variation. Biomarker-based phenotyping identified a disturbed subtype associated with poorer subjective sleep quality and higher all-cause mortality in the Sleep Heart Health Study (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.20; 95% CI, 1.05-1.38). Among 507 participants with paired recordings before and after continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), normalized sleep-depth area increased by 6.1% and shallow-sleep burden below 0.3 decreased by 16.0%. Exploratory analyses linked reductions in approximate entropy and depth variability to reduced oxygen desaturation and increased sleep efficiency, respectively (Spearman rho = 0.24 and 0.12); both associations remained positive after covariate adjustment. Mean ECG-SDI within N2 increased by 0.017 (95% CI, 0.004-0.030). A wearable proof-of-concept illustrated the acquisition-to-report workflow. These findings support ECG-based sleep phenotyping and further evaluation of CPAP response monitoring at home.

Neural Expansion: A Unified Mechanism for How Deep Neural Networks Generalize 2026-10-16
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Includes supplementary material. Code: https://github.com/meetchashi/neural_expansion_neurips_2026
Show abstract

Deep neural networks demonstrate remarkable generalization despite being highly over-parameterized, challenging traditional statistical learning theories. While several theoretical paradigms such as margin-based bounds, PAC-Bayesian analyses, and neural tangent kernels provide global explanations, they often lack mechanistic insight into how generalization emerges from training dynamics. We introduce Neural Expansion, a unified geometric framework that characterizes generalization through the expansion of directionally-selective ray coverage in the input space. Using Generalization Intervals (GIs), we quantify direction-specific regions around training samples where the model's predictions remain stable. Our extensive empirical studies across diverse architectures and datasets reveal that training leads to progressive expansion of ray coverage, particularly along directions with low Jacobian singular values. This mechanism helps explain a large fraction of correctly classified test inputs, adversarial examples, and even mislabeled or out-of-distribution samples through the geometry of ray coverage rather than instance-level memorization. By connecting input-space curvature to predictive behavior, our framework provides a mechanistic foundation that complements existing global theories of deep learning.

RTSM: Adapting Recursive Models to Multivariate Time-Series Classification 2026-10-16
📝 33 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Accurate multivariate time-series classification requires extracting temporal features and integrating information across a sequence. Weight-tied refinement offers a way to repeatedly process these features without allocating new parameters at each stage. Applying this principle to time series requires choosing both a temporal representation and a latent-state organization suited to classification. We introduce the Recursive Time-Series Model (RTSM), which combines a learned convolutional temporal tokenizer, single-state weight-tied refinement, and a pooled classification readout. Across all 30 UEA datasets, RTSM achieves 72.81% mean accuracy under validation-based configuration and checkpoint selection, the highest observed mean among the 17 evaluated deep-learning models and 1.60 percentage points above MambaSL. Ablations on eight datasets favor the proposed tokenizer over a simple convolutional embedding and the single-state model over the tested two-state variants. The complete classifier reaches 86.10% mean accuracy versus 84.79% for a separately trained tokenizer-only classifier. These findings identify temporal encoding and state organization as key design choices for adapting recursive architectures to time series. The resulting classifier combines competitive accuracy with a shared refinement module whose parameter count is independent of computational depth.

SETAREH Transform: A Sorting-Assisted Haar-Wavelet Sparsification Method for Approximate Inner-Product Computation 2026-10-16
📝 22 pages, 2 figures, 5 Tables, 21 References
Show abstract

Modern deep learning systems increasingly rely on high-dimensional vector operations, in which large input and parameter vectors are repeatedly stored, transferred, and processed. Although substantial research has focused on reducing neural-network model size through pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and efficient architectures, the storage, memory-traffic, and communication costs associated with repeatedly presented high-dimensional input vectors remain important practical considerations, particularly in distributed and edge-computing environments. This paper proposes the SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets) transform, a Haar-wavelet-based sparsification approach for approximating the inner product between a fixed high-dimensional coefficient vector and successive input vectors. By applying the same sequence of permutations and Haar transforms to both vectors, SETAREH preserves the exact inner product before truncation. It then retains only a subset of the transformed coefficient-input pairs, reducing their representation cost while introducing a controllable approximation error. To evaluate SETAREH, we consider two complementary experimental settings: a synthetic Gaussian-vector baseline and a real apparent-age estimation task using a convolutional neural network on the APPA-REAL dataset. The results show that SETAREH can approximate the evaluated inner products using substantially fewer transformed coefficient-input pairs than the original vector dimension. The findings support its use as a potential structured data-representation method for repeated inner products with fixed weights, while the practical benefit depends on approximation accuracy and the cost of storing or transmitting permutation metadata.

Scalable Levenberg-Marquardt Training Framework for Deep Learning 2026-10-16
Show abstract

The Levenberg-Marquardt (LMA) algorithm is a widely used nonlinear least-squares solver known for its rapid convergence. For small-scale neural networks, LMA provides better convergence and architectural robustness than first-order optimizers like Adam. However, LMA imposes prohibitive memory and computational costs. These costs are primarily from the Jacobian matrix, whose dimensions scale with both the number of samples and parameters. This bottleneck prevents its use in large-scale neural networks. Recent efforts scale LMA using preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) solvers to avoid materializing the Jacobian. However, PCG only approximates the solution, requiring many network propagations and a task-specific preconditioner. Other studies optimize LMA to reduce costs, but they restrict their methods to specific tasks or network architectures. As a result, LMA remains impractical for training general large-scale neural networks. In this paper, we propose LeMA, a distributed LMA algorithm for large-scale neural network training on multi-GPU platforms. Our contributions are twofold: First, an exact dual formulation of LMA removes the memory dependence on the parameter dimension. Second, a Jacobian sharding strategy computes Jacobian shards on the fly and in parallel to avoid materializing the full Jacobian. For large-scale neural networks, LeMA reduces the memory footprint of LMA from quadratic in the number of parameters to quadratic in the batch size. This makes exact LMA steps feasible for billion-parameter models without approximate PCG solves. Furthermore, for a convolutional neural network with 1,000,022,632 parameters on an 8-GPU node, LeMA achieves a 7.97x speedup over a single GPU, corresponding to a 99.7% strong scaling efficiency.

Show abstract

Large language models exhibit misbehaviors that hinder deployment in high-stakes settings. A well-known and efficient class of monitors can detect such failures from internal representations (such as activation probes), but are tied to the model they were trained on, requiring costly re-annotation as new LLMs appear. A natural way to avoid this repeated annotation cost is to develop internal-representation based monitors that transfer zero-shot across LLMs. However, such transfer is challenging because internal representations are not naturally aligned across models; in particular, attention maps, known to contain signals of misbehavior, may differ in head function, ordering, and model depth. LGGS: Longitudinal MRI Reconstruction via Guided Group Sparsity 2026-10-15
📝 10 pages main, 8 figures main, 5 pages supplementary, 7 figures supplementary
Show abstract

Serial MRI is routinely performed in current clinical practice for disease surveillance and applications such as MR-guided radiotherapy. However, long MRI scan times increase costs and limit patient access, making accelerated image reconstruction of broad clinical importance. Previous scans of the same patient contain image information highly correlated with the current exam, yet existing reconstruction methods rarely exploit this information. Major challenges in the effective use of prior scans include inter-visit misregistration, pathological changes, and image phase variation. In this work, we propose Longitudinal Guided Group Sparsity (LGGS), an interpretable deep-learning framework for longitudinally informed MRI reconstruction derived from classical group sparsity. The prior scan is incorporated into the reconstruction regularizer through learned phase-invariant circulant attention that identifies corresponding features across exams. This formulation provides robustness to local in-plane and through-plane misregistration as well as inter-visit phase variation.
