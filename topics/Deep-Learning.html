URSA: Chemistry-Aware Benchmark for Utilitarian Retrosynthesis Assessment 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Synthesis planning aiming to find pathways of reactions for a target molecule is one of the most important and challenging tasks in drug discovery. Recent progress has produced both specialized deep-learning retrosynthesis systems and general-purpose large language models, but objective comparison remains difficult due to the lack of flexible, chemically interpretable benchmarking protocols. In the current study, we are introducing the URSA (Utilitarian RetroSynthesis Assessment) evaluation framework that provides the opportunity to benchmark the synthetic routes not only from a formal perspective, such as convergence to commercially available starting materials, but also from a chemical plausibility perspective, mimicking the way expert chemists evaluate the reactions and routes. The study covers a comprehensive evaluation of both conventional end-to-end retrosynthesis solutions and LLMs for the synthesis planning task on a set of novel, diverse target molecules with undisclosed synthetic routes, which represent realistic tasks in the daily drug design routine. Although LLMs can support high-level strategic planning and even achieve SOTA performance in the OOD set, the Pareto frontier for estimated run cost and chemically plausible route solvability consists entirely of specialized retrosynthesis planners.

ISLA: Ensemble learning and domain adaptation for generalizable acute ischemic stroke lesion segmentation in diffusion MRI with clinical validation 2026-11-13
📝 Substantially revised manuscript, including improvements to the methodology, additional experiments, and an expanded discussion
Show abstract

Accurate segmentation of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) lesions on MRI is essential for clinical decision-making. Deep learning methods offer a promising approach to automating this task. However, they often generalize poorly to unseen clinical sites, and few AIS lesion segmentation methods have been externally validated. In this study, we propose $\mathit{ISLA}$ (Ischemic Stroke Lesion Analyzer), a framework for AIS lesion segmentation built on the design principles of $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$. $\mathit{ISLA}$ combines attention modules, deep supervision, and ensemble learning across different input resolutions, convolutional block types, and loss functions. We also introduce $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$, a variant that leverages unlabeled target-domain images through semi-supervised learning. We trained both approaches on the SOOP and ISLES 2022 datasets and evaluated on a held-out internal validation set of 253 cases, as well as on 590 cases from an independent multicenter clinical cohort. We compared them with three state-of-the-art pre-trained models and with $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$ trained on the same data. $\mathit{ISLA}$ outperformed the pre-trained models on the internal validation set, while both $\mathit{ISLA}$ and $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$ outperformed them on the external cohort. Although $\mathit{nnU\text{-}Net}$ performed slightly better than $\mathit{ISLA}$ internally, both $\mathit{ISLA}$ variants demonstrated better generalization to the external cohort. Furthermore, $\mathit{ISLA\text{-}MT}$ significantly improved performance over $\mathit{ISLA}$ on the external cohort. The proposed models performed particularly well on low-contrast lesions, and qualitative error analyses suggested greater robustness to acquisition artifacts. Both models are fast ($\sim$ 5 s per case), and their code and weights are publicly available at https://github.com/RocaVincent/isla_segmentation.

Parameterization method of reservoir properties for ensemble-based data assimilation using intermediate latent space of StyleGAN 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Ensemble smoothers are the most successful and efficient techniques currently available for history matching. However, because these methods rely on Gaussian assumptions, their performance is severely degraded when the prior geology is described in terms of complex facies distributions (non-Gaussian). In this way, for these methods, we need to apply efficient parameterization techniques. Currently, the most efficient methods for performing parameterization are deep learning models. However, given the variety of existing deep learning models, studies have not identified which is most suitable for use with ensemble-based methods, although some important models had already been evaluated. Based on a recent literature review, the most promising models selected were VAE-GAN, Latent Diffusion, and StyleGAN models. As a novel aspect of this work, data assimilation with the second generation of StyleGAN (StyleGAN2) model was performed using the latent z-space and intermediate w-space, separately. They were applied in two 2D case studies: one categorical (three facies) and the other continuous. Our findings show that performing data assimilation with StyleGAN2 using the intermediate space (w-space) yielded better results than the traditional application in the latent space (z-space). This is due to the fact that ESMDA uses linear updates and the w-space is much more linear and disentangled than the highly entangled z-space, thereby ensuring that the updated vectors remain close to realistic geological patterns. These results were validated using main geostatistical and history matching metrics.

f-Divergence Self-Play for Tabular Anomaly Detection via Large Language Models 2026-11-13
📝 Accepted at ICML 2026. 28 pages, 7 figures
Show abstract

Anomaly detection in tabular data poses significant challenges due to heterogeneous feature types-mixing numerical, categorical, and textual attributes, which complicate learning meaningful representations of normality. Recent work has applied large language models (LLMs) to this problem by serializing table rows as text sequences, yet these approaches rely on one-shot supervised fine-tuning that offers limited signal to tighten the model's description of normality. We propose DiSPaT, a self-play fine-tuning framework that strengthens the model's understanding of normal data. Building on the theoretical foundation of f-divergence minimization, we derive a tight approximation connecting our training objective to reducing the distributional gap between real normal data and model-generated samples. DiSPaT operates through an alternating optimization: at each iteration, the current policy generates synthetic samples that serve as pseudo-anomalies, while a critic discriminator learns to distinguish these from real normal samples; this signal drives policy updates that progressively align the model distribution with the true normal-data distribution. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmarks demonstrate that DiSPaT consistently outperforms prior LLM-based methods, deep learning approaches, and classical unsupervised detectors for tabular anomaly detection.

Causal AI-Driven Secure mmWave Beam Alignment with Defensive Variational Autoencoders 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Efficient and secure beam alignment is essential for mmWave communication systems. However, existing deep learning (DL)-based beam alignment methods often neglect causal relationships between inputs and outputs, resulting in limited interpretability, poor generalization, and excessive beam sweeping. Moreover, these DL solutions are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, where small perturbations in received power measurements can mislead beam classifiers. To overcome these issues, we propose a causally-aware DL framework integrating causal discovery into beam alignment. A two-stage causal beam selection algorithm learns a Bayesian graph capturing dependencies between received signal strength indicators (RSSI) as inputs and the optimal beam index as the target variable, then uses it to select a minimal set of relevant input features for the DL classifier. To mitigate adversarial attacks, we introduce a defensive variational autoencoder (DVAE) that maps potentially perturbed inputs back to their clean versions. End-to-end fine-tuning of the downstream beam classifier on the DVAE-reconstructed RSSI representations further boosts prediction accuracy. Simulation results show that causal beam selection achieves accuracy comparable to conventional methods while providing an \(\mathbf{829\times}\) lower per-sample computational cost than SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations), a widely used explainable AI (XAI)-based feature selection method. It further reduces beam-sweeping overhead by \(\mathbf{59.4\%}\) and \(\mathbf{89.8\%}\), compared to conventional DL-based beam selection and exhaustive search, respectively. The DVAE-based defense effectively denoises adversarial inputs, sustaining 85\% accuracy across different perturbation bounds, retains baseline performance on unperturbed RSSI inputs and surpasses defensive distillation defense scheme by 15--20\% on average under different attack scenarios.

A Variational Latent Equilibrium for Learning in Neuronal Circuits 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Brains remain unrivaled in their ability to recognize and generate complex spatiotemporal patterns. While AI is able to reproduce some of these capabilities, deep learning algorithms remain largely at odds with our current understanding of brain circuitry and dynamics. This is prominently the case for backpropagation through time (BPTT), the go-to algorithm for learning complex temporal dependencies. In this work we propose a general formalism to approximate BPTT in a controlled, biologically plausible manner. Our approach builds on, unifies and extends several previous approaches to local, time-continuous, phase-free spatiotemporal credit assignment based on principles of energy conservation and extremal action. Our starting point is a prospective energy function of neuronal states, from which we calculate real-time error dynamics for time-continuous neuronal networks. With this, as a first contribution we provide a simple and straightforward derivation of the adjoint method dynamical equations, the time-continuous equivalent to BPTT. Importantly, additional approximations are necessary to avoid unphysical temporal non-localities. One such approximation was recently proposed in Generalized Latent Equilibrium (GLE), but enforcing temporal locality of the backpropagating dynamics induces an error in the gain that becomes problematic when processing complex signals. Thus, our second contribution is to design a learning rule for the backward weights to find a fully local (in space and time) set of equations for neuron and synapse dynamics that corrects for this gain difference. With this inclusion of backward weight learning, our theory provides a rigorous framework for spatiotemporal deep learning in the brain, while simultaneously advancing a blueprint for physical circuits capable of carrying out these computations.

BPU-SpikeMM:Exact Matrix Multiplication via Spatio-Temporal Spike Encoding on Real Neuromorphic Hardware 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Matrix multiplication is the dominant computational kernel in deep learning, yet its growing scale strains conventional von Neumann hardware in energy efficiency and bandwidth. Neuromorphic architectures offer a promising alternative, but mathematical equivalence between matrix multiplication and spiking computation does not guarantee direct execution on real neuromorphic hardware, as the specific hardware paradigm may still require additional data recovery, bit-weight reconstruction, or state readout after neuromorphic execution. This paper presents BPU-SpikeMM (Brain Processing Unit-based Spike Matrix Multiplication), an exact spatio-temporal spike encoding framework for matrix multiplication on real neuromorphic hardware. We prove the exact equivalence of spike-encoded matrix multiplication under the BPU execution paradigm, distinguish mathematical equivalence from native direct-readout equivalence on BPU hardware, and build a spatio-temporal offloading pipeline supporting multiple encoding schemes. Furthermore, we extend the framework to a multi-dimensional encoding design space and evaluate the applicability of different encodings on real BPU hardware in terms of correctness, temporal resource cost, spatial resource cost, and event-driven metrics. The results confirm native direct-readout equivalence across all evaluated encoding schemes and show that their applicability to BPU execution depends on joint trade-offs among temporal cost, spatial resource consumption, and event activity.

Causal-prior-constrained deep learning for rapid-intensification warning of western North Pacific tropical cyclones 2026-11-13
📝 34 pages, including 15 pages of supplementary information; 5 main figures and 6 supplementary figures
Show abstract

Rapid intensification (RI) is one of the most dangerous and unpredictable phases of tropical cyclones, and its environmental and structural drivers are difficult to identify and separate in observations. Here we introduce CausalVisionRI, a causal-prior-constrained deep-learning system for 24-h RI warning in the western North Pacific. It integrates infrared remote sensing imagery, atmospheric and oceanic fields, and an observationally discovered causal graph into a unified model. The graph and eight sign-verified effects on 24-h intensity change provide an auditable causal prior for training. Discovered from 522 cyclones in 2000-2017, it identifies causal pathways linking atmospheric and oceanic conditions, the cyclone's dynamical and structural state, and its 24-h intensity change. On a forward holdout covering 2020-2024 and 3,004 records, the five-member ensemble achieves a Brier skill score (BSS) of 0.259 relative to a fixed development climatology, significantly exceeding four reference systems; the three LDA baselines reach at most 0.105. It also attains higher detection rates than a published knowledge-block scheme and a reconstructed contrastive-learning scheme at matched false-detection rates under our USA 30-kt standard and the published RITCF standard. Ablations show that the graph pathway adds 0.032 BSS across the holdout and 0.063 over 2020-2021, indicating that the gain concentrates in the time period adjacent to the estimation window. A deep-learning model constrained by observationally discovered causal structure thus offers a new route to physically informed RI warning.

Random Abstract Cell Complexes 2026-11-13
📝 9 pages, 7 figures (plus appendix). For evaluation code, see https://github.com/josefhoppe/random-abstract-cell-complexes
Show abstract

In machine learning and signal processing, methods based on (abstract) cell complexes (CCs) are being applied successfully in different applications. However, there is currently a lack of random models, which are necessary for a thorough evaluation of these methods, both by generating synthetic data and as null models to compare results to. We fill this gap by defining a random model for abstract cell complexes based on independent inclusion probabilities, similar to the Erdős-Rényi model for graphs. We also discuss how modifications to bias the model to incorporate more structure can be implemented. The model can also be used to lift a graph (or other cell complex) to a cell complex of arbitrary dimension. The main challenge in this is that the number of cycles (possible 2-cells) on graphs is exponential, and the same is true in higher dimensions. We overcome this complexity with an approximate sampling algorithm limited to 2-cells (polygons) that is based on a recent advance in sampling cycles on a graph. Using this algorithm, we analyze the properties of random 2-dimensional CCs and showcase its usage as a performance baseline for both topological signal processing and topological deep learning. The algorithm is available in the package py-raccoon on the Python Package Index.

The Transformer Paradigm: Dynamic Processing and a New Principle of Inference 2026-11-13
📝 v1: 15 pp., 5 figs., 1 table
Show abstract

We argue that the Transformer revolution is not adequately understood as a merely quantitative expansion of deep learning, but as the emergence of a new paradigm based on a different principle of inference. We interpret the Transformer as a system in which token representations are transformed by parameterized transformations, or transforming concepts. During inference, some of these transformations are dynamically constructed from the current input: previously acquired transformations are first adapted and then applied. At the mechanical-structural level, this dynamic processing is realized through output-weight interconnections, whereby outputs of some networks determine the weights of others. The resulting mode of processing, which we call Sequence-level Interactive Dynamic Parallel Processing (SIDPP), provides a framework for explaining strong prompt sensitivity and opens research directions concerning interpretability, control, alternative AI architectures, neuromorphic systems, and possible biological realizations of dynamic processing.

PADSNet: Physics-Aware Dual-Stream Network with Biomechanical Priors for Laryngeal Lesion Classification 2026-11-13
Show abstract

Precise classification of laryngeal lesions is clinically crucial yet highly challenging, as inter-class visual differences are often subtle. While abnormalities in vocal fold vibratory dynamics provide complementary discriminative information, existing deep learning models predominantly rely on visual appearance and lack explicit mechanisms for modeling clinically meaningful vibratory dynamics. To address this, we propose PADSNet, a Physics-Aware Dual-Stream Network for laryngeal lesion classification that integrates visual representations with explicit biomechanical reasoning. Specifically, a Wavelet-Attention Enhancement Module (WAEM) is designed to bridge the spectral-semantic gap, enabling complementary learning of spatial texture and dynamic frequency representations. Meanwhile, a Knowledge Computing Module (KCM) extracts interpretable biomechanical indices from temporal glottal distance waveforms, including indicators of mobility, symmetry, glottal closure, and vibration stability. These results indicate that explicitly integrating biomechanical priors with deep visual representations provides a more accurate, robust, and clinically interpretable framework for automated laryngeal lesion classification.

Modern deep learning commonly relies on AdamW with prescribed learning rate schedules, but recent works challenge both components: Schedule-Free optimization removes explicit schedules via iterate averaging, and Muon improves the update geometry by orthogonalizing momentum for matrix parameters.
