Noisy Issues, Noisy Results: Mislabeling in Information Retrieval-based Bug Localization 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Information Retrieval-based Bug Localization (IRBL) leverages bug reports to identify faulty source files. Evaluations of IRBL approaches primarily rely on labeled issues from issue tracking systems. However, prior research has shown that issue labels are frequently incorrect in open-source projects, threatening the validity of IRBL evaluations. Existing studies have primarily examined traditional IRBL techniques on small datasets, overlooking modern approaches and the partial overlap between noisy and cleaned datasets. In this paper, we conduct a large-scale empirical study to investigate the prevalence and impact of mislabeled issues in IRBL benchmarks. We analyze five benchmark datasets comprising over 55,000 issue reports and find that, on average, 34.82% of issues are mislabeled. We further evaluate six IRBL techniques, spanning traditional, deep learning-based, and large language model-based approaches using the widely adopted Ye et al. benchmark and statistical methods that account for dataset overlap. Our results show that removing mislabeled issues from evaluation datasets significantly reduces measured performance across all six techniques, with Mean Reciprocal Rank decreasing by 4.68%-21.74%. Although mislabeling does not change the relative ranking of techniques, it affects the magnitude of their performance differences. These findings highlight the importance of reliable evaluation data for ensuring valid and meaningful comparisons of IRBL techniques.

Learning curves in natural and artificial systems 2026-11-03
Show abstract

A general mathematical model of experience formation is considered, in which the learning level is defined as the probability that the next value of the uncertainty factor has already been mastered and not forgotten. Within this model the shape of the learning curve is derived, and it turns out to be determined primarily by the distribution of situation occurrence: a uniform distribution yields an exponential curve, whereas a power-law distribution yields a power-law curve whose exponent equals the ratio of the distribution exponent to that same exponent increased by one. Both mechanisms generating power-law curves, established earlier in the analysis of debugging, assembly and repair activities, are shown to have been independently rediscovered in deep learning theory as the "frequency" and "spectral" mechanisms of scaling laws for artificial neural networks, while the exponential curves of machine learning (contraction operators, strongly convex optimization, stationary reinforcement) are generated by the same constancy of the contraction coefficient that was revealed half a century earlier in models of radioactive decay, the first-order lag element, and others. A solution is given for the problem of choosing a training distribution that differs from the deployment distribution. A methodological consequence is discussed: three distinct mechanisms generate curves indistinguishable in shape, so an observed learning curve does not allow one to judge the "internal" structure of the learning system.

Fusing Earth observation embeddings and spaceborne terrain with meteorological forcing for flood peak depth prediction from high-water marks 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Flood peak depth on normally dry land governs damage and risk to life, but is observed only sparsely, by post-event high-water mark (HWM) surveys, and depth derived from satellite flood extent is produced after the event and inherits the vertical error of the digital elevation model (DEM) it is computed from. Deep-learning flood models have in turn been trained on hydrodynamic simulations rather than on observed depths. We ask whether flood peak depth can be learned from field observations and predicted before the peak from spaceborne terrain, geospatial foundation-model embeddings and antecedent meteorological forcing. We assembled 5689 USGS HWM surveys from 39 events (2011-2025) in 24 U.S. states and Puerto Rico, assigned each a peak time from nearby sensors, paired each with a rain-free window at the same site, and linked them to Copernicus GLO-30 terrain, AlphaEarth embeddings and 144 h of hourly forcing masked to the lead time. A cross-modal fusion network trained on these observations predicts depth at the surveyed marks with a mean absolute error of 0.39 m 6 h ahead of the peak. Supervision by surveyed depth corrects the DEM error implicitly: differencing the measured peak water level with the same DEM gives 0.93 m at the same marks, 2.4 times larger, although it uses the measured water level and the model does not. Forcing accounts for 77% of the attributed reduction in depth error; terrain and the embeddings are statistically equivalent to each other and contribute the remaining 23%. On eleven held-out events the model still separates flooded from rain-free conditions but no longer resolves depth at the marks (R2 = 0.03), so the skill reported here is confined to the events and regions sampled. Field-surveyed depths can thus supervise flood depth prediction and correct terrain error that extent-based estimation cannot, while transfer to unseen storms remains the open problem.

Community detection in complex networks via node similarity, graph representation learning, and hierarchical clustering 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Community detection is a significant challenge in analysing real graphs, including social, citation, biological, and many other networks. This article proposes three new, general, hierarchical frameworks to deal with this task. The introduced approach supports various linkage-based clustering algorithms, vertex proximity matrices, and graph representation learning models. We compare 364 module combinations on Stochastic Block Model graphs and real-life datasets. We observe that our best pipelines (Wasserman-Faust and the mutual information-based PPMI proximity, as well as the deep learning-based DNGR representations) perform competitively with the state-of-the-art Leiden and Louvain algorithms. At the same time, unlike the latter, they remain hierarchical. Thus, they output a series of nested partitions of all possible cardinalities which are compatible with each other. This feature is crucial when the number of correct partitions is unknown in advance, and we show that it is obtained at near-zero practical cost in terms of partition quality. On graphs composed of many small communities, our pipelines are more accurate than the reference solutions. Nevertheless, the downside of our approach is its time complexity, which confines the framework to networks of some 10000 vertices on personal machines.

An Investigation on Numerical Bugs in GPU Programs Towards Automated Bug Detection 2026-11-03
📝 Published in Proceedings of the ACM on Software Engineering (PACMSE), ISSTA 2025, associated with the 34th ACM SIGSOFT International Symposium on Software Testing and Analysis (ISSTA 2025)
Show abstract

General-purpose graphics processing unit (GPU) computing has emerged as a leading parallel computing paradigm, offering significant performance gains in various domains such as scientific computing and deep learning. However, GPU programs are susceptible to numerical bugs, which can lead to incorrect results or crashes. These bugs are difficult to detect, debug, and fix due to their dependence on specific input values or types and the absence of reliable error-checking mechanisms and oracles. Additionally, the unique programming conventions of GPUs complicate identifying the root causes of bugs, while fixing them requires domain-specific knowledge of GPU computing and numerical libraries. Therefore, understanding the characteristics of GPU numerical bugs (GPU-NBs) is crucial for developing effective solutions. In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive study of GPU-NBs by analyzing 397 real-world bug samples from GitHub. We identify common root causes, symptoms, input patterns, test oracles that trigger these bugs and the strategies used to fix them. We also present GPU-NBDetect, a preliminary tool designed to detect numerical bugs across six distinct bug categories. GPU-NBDetect detected a total of 226 bugs across 186 mathematical functions in four libraries, with 60 of the bugs confirmed by developers. Our findings lay the groundwork for developing detection and prevention techniques for GPU-NBs and offer insights for building more effective debugging and auto-repair tool.

Zero-Shot Cross-Material Ptychographic Phase Reconstruction Using Deep Learning 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Ptychographic phase reconstruction is commonly formulated as an iterative inverse problem, requiring repeated object-probe updates and resulting in substantial computational cost for large-scale 4D-STEM data. We present a direct local-to-global learning framework that reconstructs full-field phase maps from diffraction measurements without iterative refinement during inference. The proposed network predicts local wrapped-phase patches from individual diffraction patterns using a sine-cosine representation, and the predictions are assembled into a full-field reconstruction using calibrated scan positions and Gaussian-weighted stitching. To evaluate generalization beyond the training domain, the model is trained on one material and directly applied to another in a zero-shot setting without target-domain fine-tuning. Experiments on AuPd and MoS$_2$ demonstrate consistent cross-material transfer in both directions, with the proposed method achieving the best full-field MSE, PSNR, and MS-SSIM among the evaluated learning-based methods. Compared with the iterative ePIE approach, the proposed direct local-to-global pipeline reduces end-to-end reconstruction time by approximately 10$\times$, demonstrating its potential for efficient and transferable ptychographic reconstruction.

TStruct: Learning Shared Temporal Structures for Long-Term Time Series Forecasting 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Long-term time series forecasting requires learning temporal dependencies that remain stable and generalizable under temporal distribution shifts. However, real-world time series are often noisy, non-stationary, and heterogeneous, making deep learning models prone to overfitting incidental temporal interactions in the training data. This raises a question: when observations are difficult to align, could temporal relationships be easier to reuse? To investigate this possibility, we analyze patch-level temporal relationship matrices across commonly used time series datasets, revealing dominant patterns shared across different temporal windows and variables. Comparisons with patch-shuffled and IID Gaussian controls further support the presence of reusable temporal relationship structures. Motivated by these findings, we propose \textbf{T}emporal \textbf{Struct}ure Network (\textbf{TStruct}), a simple yet effective network that learns reusable temporal dependency structures and adaptively combines them for each sample and variable. Specifically, TStruct directly parameterizes a set of shared temporal dependency matrices and generates normalized combination weights using dynamic sample information and static variable priors. These weights represent a soft allocation of each sample-variable pair over shared temporal dependency structures, enabling the model to generate adaptive temporal dependency matrices under shared structural constraints, rather than fitting incidental interactions in a fully unconstrained manner. Notably, TStruct consistently outperforms several strong baselines across all evaluated datasets, achieving superior accuracy while maintaining high computational efficiency. Our code is available at https://github.com/yangzeli/TStruct.

Federated Transfer Learning for Medical Image Classification under Mixed Skew 2026-11-03

Show abstract

Deep learning in medical imaging is constrained by data scarcity and by privacy regulations that make centralizing patient data across institutions impractical. Federated Learning (FL) addresses these constraints by keeping data local, but its distributed nature exposes training to strong statistical heterogeneity. Multicentric medical data is particularly affected, exhibiting a mixed skew that combines feature, label, concept, and quantity shifts across centers. Personalized FL mitigates heterogeneity by tailoring a model to each client, yet most methods assume training from scratch, which is unrealistic given the high dimensionality of medical features relative to the limited data available. We propose the Personalized Aligner Federated Pretrained (PAFP) framework, a personalized FL approach built on a pre-trained backbone. PAFP inserts client-specific Feature Alignment modules before the fine-tuned blocks to realign hidden feature distributions toward a server-aggregated target. On the FedISIC benchmark, PAFP attains the highest unweighted balanced accuracy while reducing inter-client performance variance roughly six-fold relative to the strongest baseline, yielding accuracy and cross-client equity jointly. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/Alexandre-Leclercq/PAFP.

Remaining Useful Life (RUL) estimates guide maintenance in semiconductor manufacturing, where a late intervention can disrupt production but an early one discards usable equipment life. A single prediction does not express the uncertainty needed to choose between these outcomes. We combine Deep Learning (DL) sequence models with Simoultaneous Quantile Regression (SQR) to estimate RUL at selectable quantile levels, and evaluate maintenance decisions using Unexpected Breaks (UB), Unexploited Lifetime (UL), and a cost-weighted objective. Why depth yields a computational advantage over shallow methods remains a central open question in learning theory. How deep is your network? Deep vs. shallow learning of transfer operators 2026-11-03
Show abstract

We introduce RaNNDy, a randomized neural network framework for learning transfer operators and their spectral decompositions. By fixing the weights of the hidden layers and training only the output layer, RaNNDy achieves significant reductions in computational costs -- often by two orders of magnitude -- with similar accuracy compared to state-of-the-art deep learning methods. The method is less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and provides a closed-form solution for the output layer weights that directly represent eigenfunctions of the learned operator. Moreover, it is possible to estimate uncertainties associated with the computed spectral properties via ensemble learning. We demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach using diverse benchmark problems, including stochastic dynamical systems and protein folding processes, highlighting the strengths but also weaknesses of RaNNDy.

Finite-precision arithmetic unavoidably introduces numerical approximation errors. Numerical computations may use insufficient precision or an improper formulation, which leads to numerical instability. In this paper, we introduce a unified software tool for stochastic numerical validation of deep-learning operators. The tool follows CESTAC on supported exposed operations and uses an operator-level data-perturbation approximation for GEMM-like kernels. Our developed software not only enables numerical validation with a single computation pass but also detects the sources of numerical instability and provides numerical stability monitoring during deep learning training and inference. Robust Learning under Non-Gaussian Noise with Adaptive Log-Correntropy Loss 2026-11-03
Show abstract

Robust deep learning under heavy-tailed and impulsive noise remains challenging because conventional losses such as mean squared error (MSE) exhibit unbounded sensitivity to outliers. Although correntropy-based objectives improve robustness, many conventional formulations rely on fixed kernel parameters that must be empirically tuned and remain static during training. To address these limitations, we propose an Adaptive Log-Correntropy Loss (ALCL), a heavy-tailed loss formulation that adaptively learns its robustness geometry during optimization. ALCL introduces a logarithmic residual model whose shape and scale parameters are learned jointly with network weights through differentiable reparameterization. This yields a heavy-tailed robust optimization objective whose psi-function is formally bounded and redescending, allowing the loss geometry to adapt dynamically to evolving residual statistics while suppressing extreme outliers. Comparative experiments on four widely used benchmark datasets spanning grayscale and red-green-blue (RGB) image data under mixed heavy-tailed and impulsive noise demonstrate that ALCL consistently outperforms MSE and optimally tuned generalized correntropy losses in both reconstruction fidelity and downstream classification accuracy. While performance differences remain small under low-noise conditions, under high-noise regimes ALCL improves median accuracy by up to 4.75% on grayscale benchmarks and 4.51% on RGB datasets, with reduced variance across runs. These results demonstrate that adaptive robustness through joint learning of loss parameters provides a computationally efficient alternative to static correntropy-based losses for deep learning in non-Gaussian environments.

Predicting protein structures at any folding stage from sequence 2026-11-03
📝 47 pages, 10 figures. The first five authors contributed equally
Show abstract

Protein structure predictors reliably identify native conformations, but do not describe the continuum connecting unfolded ensembles to folded states. Here we present FoldScape, a sequence-conditioned deep learning framework that generates C$α$ conformational ensembles at a specified folding fraction $Q$. FoldScape reconstructs three-dimensional chains through hierarchical, sliding-window regressions of one-dimensional inter-residue distances, followed by chain assembly and sampling with a rectified-flow decoder. It is trained on multi-scale coarse-grained folding trajectories and all-atom mechanical-unfolding trajectories. Across 20 human proteins (36-857 residues), at matched sample size, FoldScape covers about an order of magnitude more of the folding coordinate than seed-varied AlphaFold 3 ensembles, robustly across proteins, order parameters and binning choices. AlphaFold 3 better anchors the immediate native vicinity, which FoldScape does not reach, making the approaches complementary. FoldScape reconstruction accuracy remains approximately constant along $Q$, from collapsed coil to near-native globule, rather than deteriorating away from the native state. A stride-one crop-and-superpose scheme extends inference to chains several times longer than a single chain-level forward pass. We also show that training trajectories resolve mechanical unfolding at residue resolution, that elongation-conditioned ensembles exhibit reproducible continuous observables across pulling velocities, and that partially unfolded chains form geometrically coherent segments. FoldScape thus combines broad coverage of folding stages with stable reconstruction accuracy, without per-protein simulation.

Photorealistic Style Transfer (PST) aims to transfer the color and tonal style of a reference to a content image while strictly preserving its structural integrity. However, existing deep learning-based methods inherently suffer from semantic entanglement caused by pre-trained image encoders, leading to unnatural spatial distortions. Moreover, current pixel-level mapping paradigms often ignore color gamut topology, resulting in color banding, while also lacking the multimodal capability for precise, tone-specific text-driven control. To address these bottlenecks, we propose StatLUT, an innovative multimodal framework for 3D LUT generation. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks demonstrate that StatLUT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both visual quality and quantitative metrics, pioneering a highly robust and versatile paradigm for multimodal photorealistic style transfer.

Deep-learning-based mass detectors usually downsample mammograms, trading fine image detail for lower computational cost. However, published studies cannot reveal how much accuracy this trade-off sacrifices, since they also differ in architecture, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol. In this work, we isolated the effect of input resolution in a controlled study on the VinDr-Mammo dataset, training 18 detectors at six input resolutions ranging from 512 x 416 to 1600 x 1280 pixels, for 108 model-resolution combinations. Whole mammograms were resized without cropping or tiling. For each model, preprocessing, model initialization, augmentation, and evaluation procedures were kept fixed across resolutions. From the lowest to the highest resolution, median mAP50 rose from 0.626 to 0.660 and the best mAP50 from 0.670 to 0.709. Gains were concentrated in small masses: for the smallest third of lesions, median mAP50 rose by 0.074 (from 0.367 to 0.441), compared with 0.007 and 0.032 for medium and large masses. Median training time increased from 0.45 to 1.40 h, and median inference time from 23.6 to 113.7 ms per image. Therefore, higher input resolution mainly benefits the small masses that are hardest to detect, at a computational cost that remains practical.

What Does 4-Bit Microscaling Cost a Wearable Activity Recognizer? 2026-11-03

Wearable sports sensors can recognize actions on the device itself, where lower-precision numbers reduce model storage and memory traffic. Two 4-bit block-scaled formats, MXFP4 from the Open Compute Project, introduced in 2023, and NVFP4 from NVIDIA, introduced in 2025, are drawing interest. Published evaluations cover general deep-learning benchmarks and large language models; to our knowledge, nobody has yet measured what they cost a small wearable-sensor model. We train a 159,492-parameter 1D CNN to recognize four basketball actions from wrist-accelerometer data in Hang-Time HAR and test it on players it has never seen, using five-fold cross-validation grouped by player. Every comparison between number formats is made fold by fold. In full precision the model reaches 69.26\% macro-F1. Measured TFLite FP16 and INT8 models lose at most 0.16 percentage points. In simulation, 4-bit weights cost 1.45 points with MXFP4 and 0.72 points with NVFP4. When activations are quantized to 4 bits as well, MXFP4 loses 5.14 points and NVFP4 only 0.75, and NVFP4 comes out ahead in all five held-out-player folds. The ranking survives two alternative simulator settings, with NVFP4 leading by 3.49 to 4.40 points. A $2 \times 3$ ablation with 4-bit weights and activations (two block sizes and three scale rules) points to scale selection as the main source of MXFP4's loss. The reference MXFP4 conversion we simulate rounds the scale down, which can pick scales that are too small, so many blocks clip their largest values. Rounding the scale up recovers 3.18 points with 32-value blocks and 3.63 points with 16-value blocks, and switching to an 8-bit floating-point scale, as NVFP4 does, adds another 0.84 and 1.20 points. Block size matters less. Under this round-down rule, 16-value blocks score 0.44 points below 32-value blocks in all five folds; under the other two rules there is no consistent effect.
