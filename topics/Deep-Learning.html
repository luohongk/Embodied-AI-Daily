One, No One and One Hundred Thousand CTs: Testing Anatomical Control in Text-to-CT Generation 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Recent advances in deep learning and the public availability of computed tomography (CT) datasets with paired reports have enabled many Text-to-CT generators. However, their evaluations almost always differ in preprocessing and metrics, which hinders comparison. Moreover, their protocols almost never test how editing the report changes the CT, not even at the same seed, so the report is often assumed to control the anatomy without evidence of how much and how consistently it does. We therefore propose CtrlCT, a toolkit that tests two properties of a generator, Credibility and Control. Credibility tests compare generated CTs, as a population, with real CTs and reports; Control tests are metamorphic and need no reference CT: at a fixed seed, editing one finding should change it as requested, keep negated findings absent and shift distant anatomy no more than a new seed, and the change should hold across seeds. A Report Card summarises all measures and insights for each generator. We benchmark five public generators on 940 chest CTs and 687 edits of pleural effusion and cardiomegaly, holding out a sixth to test the toolkit. Among them, the most credible generators barely respond to an added effusion, while the most responsive produce less realistic and less diverse CTs. The analyses proposed in the literature do not reveal this. We release CtrlCT, its edits, seeds and 30,525 synthetic CTs after acceptance.

Scaling Laws of Ensemble Uncertainty in Language Models 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Many use cases for large language models (LLMs)---from AI safety to exploration and human interaction---can benefit from epistemic uncertainty estimates. We argue that existing Bayesian Deep Learning solutions, which are often restricted to a fixed model family, fail to capture the full epistemic uncertainty at practical LLM scales. This is because the error due to the bounded capacity of the model family remains invisible to disagreement among models supported by the prior. We relate this error to the well-known power-law scaling of LLM loss with model size. We then show that a second power law quantifies the shrinking disagreement within ensembles as model size increases. Its slower decay relative to the capacity law above suggests that ensembles capture a growing share of epistemic uncertainty and become more competitive as an axis for scaling, compared to a single larger model. Finally, a third power law quantifies the loss of ensemble diversity as members share a larger fraction of training before branching, and therefore identifies a further limitation, beyond the capacity error, for LoRA ensembles and optimization-based or second-order methods that approximate uncertainty around a single training solution. At a high level, these findings suggest diverse ensembles can be a promising axis, both for scaling and uncertainty quantification at larger scales.

Deep learning-based image enhancement methods face a fundamental trade-off between computational efficiency and representational capacity. For example, although a conventional three-dimensional Look-Up Table (3D LUT) can process a degraded image in real time, it lacks representational flexibility and depends solely on a fixed prior. To address this problem, we introduce FlowLUT, a novel end-to-end model that integrates the efficiency of LUTs, multiple priors, and the parameter-independent characteristic of flow-matched reconstructed images. Specifically, firstly, the input image is transformed in color space by a collection of differentiable 3D LUTs (containing a large number of 3D LUTs with different priors). Subsequently, a lightweight content-aware dynamically predicts fusion weights, enabling scene-adaptive color correction with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ complexity. Next, a lightweight fusion prediction network runs on multiple 3D LUTs, with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ complexity for scene-adaptive color correction.Furthermore, to address the inherent representation limitations of LUTs, we design an innovative iterative flow matching method to restore local structural details and eliminate artifacts. Finally, the entire model is jointly optimized under a composite loss function enforcing perceptual and structural fidelity. Extensive experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on three benchmarks.

Understanding the Importance of Massive Activations through Adversarial Robustness 2026-11-24
📝 Includes an appendix. Code available at https://github.com/tigranfah/ma-robustness
Show abstract

A common occurrence in Large Language Models (LLMs) is the emergence of so-called massive activations. This phenomenon is well known in the literature and has been connected to attention sinks, representational compression, as well as signal propagation. However, the reasons and consequences for their appearance are not yet well understood. In this paper, we explain the role of massive activations in deep learning by showing that they are connected to the region migration phenomenon in delayed robustness. Under the spline theoretic interpretation of deep networks, we show that massive activations push the representations away from the nonlinearity boundaries of the network, decreasing the complexity of its input-output map around data points. Furthermore, we uncover for the first time that this phenomenon is not unique to transformers and actually appears in a number of common architectures performing simple supervised learning tasks. We validate this connection through extensive experiments on a diverse set of architectures including residual MLP, ResNet18, Vision Transformers and GPT and datasets including MNIST, CIFAR10, Imagenette and Shakespeare Text. We consistently observe that massive activations and region migration co-occur with adversarial robustness. Furthermore, we illustrate that this connection holds in the large-scale pretraining of LLMs by showing that the rise of massive activations in LLMs closely tracks the adversarial accuracy of the model under perturbations of the token embeddings.

On the notion of missingness for path attribution explainability methods in medical settings: Guiding the selection of medically meaningful baselines 2026-11-24
Show abstract

The explainability of deep learning models remains a significant challenge, particularly in the medical domain where interpretable outputs are essential for clinical trust and transparency. Path attribution methods such as Integrated Gradients (IG) rely on a baseline that represents the absence of informative features, a notion commonly referred to as missingness. However, commonly used baselines (such as all-zero inputs) are often semantically meaningless in medical contexts, where intensity values carry clinical significance. In this work, we revisit the notion of missingness for medical imaging, expose the limitations of standard baselines in this setting, and formalize a stricter missingness we term semantic missingness: a baseline must not merely lack signal, but must represent a clinically plausible state in which the disease-related features are absent. This formulation motivates a counterfactual-guided approach to baseline selection, in which a synthetically generated counterfactual (i.e. a clinically normal variant of the pathological input) serves as a principled and semantically meaningful reference. We derive theoretical guarantees showing that counterfactual baselines yield more faithful attributions than standard alternatives, and empirically validate this with two complementary counterfactual generative models, a VAE and a diffusion model, though the concept is model-agnostic and compatible with any suitable counterfactual method. Our experiments show that counterfactual baselines produce consistently more faithful and medically relevant attributions across three diverse medical datasets, outperforming both standard baseline choices and other related methods. Lastly, we also compare against using the counterfactual directly as an explanation and show that employing it as a baseline for IG yields superior results, thereby bridging two separate explainability paradigms.

Which Parameters to Update? An Instance-Specific Adaptation Framework for Real-World Pansharpening 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Pansharpening aims to fuse a single observed image pair, comprising a low-resolution multispectral (LRMS) image and a panchromatic (PAN) image, to obtain a high-resolution multispectral (HRMS) image. Recently, deep learning-based methods have achieved significant performance due to their strong capability of extracting nonlinear features. However, distribution shifts between training data and test images can degrade the performance of pretrained pansharpening networks, limiting their generalization in real-world scenarios. In this work, we propose an instance-specific test-time adaptation framework that uses the observed PAN-LRMS pair to select and update only a subset of the pretrained network's parameters. Specifically, the framework uses cross-scoring to identify parameter groups whose updates improve reconstruction across image patches. It then updates the selected groups by minimizing a loss that combines a spectral consistency term with a newly designed spatial consistency term. Experiments show that the adapted networks perform better than their pretrained counterparts and outperform representative state-of-the-art pansharpening methods on GaoFen-2, QuickBird, and WorldView series datasets.

Spoofing Wireless Sensing with Metasurfase-based Physical Deepfakes 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Wireless communication networks are expanding from mere information transfer to sensing: in the Integrated Sensing and Communication paradigm, existing infrastructure and signals are reused to capture physical information about the surroundings of devices, envisioned to inform high-stakes applications such as healthcare and autonomous vehicles. From a security perspective, wireless sensing inherently trusts physical observations of the radio environment. However, as in wireless communication, the open nature of radio propagation allows attackers to tamper with victim signals. In particular, attackers can use programmable metasurfaces to manipulate wireless channels and thereby mislead sensing systems. Yet, prior metasurface-based manipulations target only individual channel features such as variance or Doppler shifts. Thus, they require knowledge of the victim sensing system and produce implausible channels that are easy to detect. In this work, we show that metasurfaces enable physically plausible manipulation of wireless sensing that is independent of the features used by the victim sensing system. Our attack reproduces genuine pre-recorded events at the physical layer and thereby synthesizes physical deepfakes of wireless channels. To this end, we develop an attack framework that controls the amplitude and phase of victim channels across time, frequency, and antennas. The key challenge here is to build a deep learning model of the interaction between metasurface and victim channel. We demonstrate that attackers can solve this challenge in several settings, e.g., by exploiting Wi-Fi Beamforming Feedback Information or replicating the victim environment. We validate the attack both in a controlled lab study on MIMO channels and with off-the-shelf Wi-Fi sensing. Our results fundamentally challenge the trustworthiness of wireless sensing.

Characterizing Datasets to Anticipate Unsupervised Domain Adaptation Performance for Radiographic Pneumonia Detection 2026-11-24
📝 12 pages, 8 figures. Submitted to IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics
Show abstract

Deep learning models for pneumonia detection from chest radiographs exhibit degraded accuracy when applied across institutions, which motivates Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA). We observe that UDA performance depends on the properties of both the source and target datasets, and that performance gains are asymmetric with respect to dataset pairs. We investigate which structural properties of chest X-ray datasets are the most important predictors of UDA performance, and whether they can be measured before adaptation is attempted. Five chest radiograph datasets were characterized by the class separability of their features, obtained from one forward pass of a frozen DenseNet-121 and summarized by the Silhouette Score, with the Fisher Ratio as a supporting measure. All twenty directed source-target pairs were adapted with a Domain-Adversarial Neural Network (DANN), while direct transfer without adaptation was also evaluated. Seven published transferability estimators were computed on the same representation for comparison. We found that adaptation performance depended on both endpoints in opposite directions. More separable target datasets attained higher AUC scores ($r=0.934$, $p=0.042$), whereas more separable sources produced lower AUC scores ($r=-0.922$, $p=0.017$). The signed difference in separability tracked the signed difference in AUC between the two directions of a pair ($r=-0.932$, $p=0.017$) and identified the better direction in 10 of 10 pairs ($p=0.001$). The proposed descriptor was comparable to the best published estimators on target evaluation. Source and target datasets play opposite structural roles in adaptation: higher class separability makes a dataset easier as a test set but not as a training source. This framework can guide dataset selection for adaptation in low-resource settings without running full experiments.

Unbiased Approximate Vector-Jacobian Products for Efficient Backpropagation 2026-11-24
Show abstract

In this work we introduce methods to reduce the computational and memory costs of training deep neural networks. Our approach consists in replacing exact vector-jacobian products by randomized, unbiased approximations thereof during backpropagation. We provide a theoretical analysis of the trade-off between the number of epochs needed to achieve a target precision and the cost reduction for each epoch. We then identify specific unbiased estimates of vector-jacobian products for which we establish desirable optimality properties of minimal variance under sparsity constraints. Finally we provide in-depth experiments on multi-layer perceptrons, BagNets and Visual Transfomers architectures. These validate our theoretical results, and confirm the potential of our proposed unbiased randomized backpropagation approach for reducing the cost of deep learning.

Relational Bottleneck Models 2026-11-24
📝 14 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

Achieving both interpretable and high-performance inference is a key challenge in deep learning. While Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) provide inherent interpretability, they are constrained by task-specific training and rely on isolated visual concepts that fail to capture higher-order visual semantic relationships. Conversely, Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) demonstrate zero-shot capabilities through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. However, standard CoT lacks a strict causal dependency between the generated rationale and the final output, compromising faithfulness. To bridge this gap, we propose Relational Bottleneck Models (RBMs), a novel training-free framework that transforms any off-the-shelf LVLMs into inherently interpretable models in a plug-and-play manner. RBMs consist of two stages: (i) Structured Rationale Generation (SRG), which explicitly models semantic interactions between visual concepts as subject-predicate-object triplets, and (ii) Rationale-Grounded Decoding (RGD), which enforces a strict causal dependency between the rationales and the final output. RGD aggregates rationale-conditional next-token distributions via an importance-weighted product-of-experts strategy, strictly excluding the image inputs from the decoding context. Extensive zero-shot evaluations across diverse benchmarks and LVLM backbones demonstrate that RBMs achieve superior interpretability while improving task performance over CBM baselines, entirely without task-specific fine-tuning.

Joint Classification and Generation on Tabular Data: an Empirical Study 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Many applications of machine learning on tabular data need both a classifier and a generator of realistic records. The default is to train two separate models, whereas a single joint model promises both from one set of weights. Several families of tabular deep learning models can be turned into such a joint model, which raises the question of which joint model is preferable at which computational budget. In this empirical study, we introduce and compare joint models based on four state-of-the-art tabular data modeling families (energy-based models, probabilistic circuits, tabular foundation models and tabular diffusion models) against measured FLOPs and wall-clock time, on 29 classification datasets. Methods are evaluated based on two defined aggregate scores that take into account different aspects of prediction and generation. We then also compare these joint models with every pair of a separate classifier and generator at different computational budgets, to see when a joint model may be a defendible choice against the regular practice of keeping a separate classifier and generator. We find that the best joint model is a probabilistic circuit at the smallest budgets and, beyond that, a tabular foundation model that generates column by column using a regressor (TabICL). Compared with pairs of separate models, this study finds a joint model is rarely preferable but often very close to the best pair, without the hassle of training and maintaining two separate models.

Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) depicts coronary anatomy and cardiac fat, but segmentation alone does not ensure anatomically consistent measurements. Reconstructing Natural Images from Brain Activity during Natural Vision 2026-11-24
📝 40 pages, 18 figures, 4 tables. Includes supplementary material
Show abstract

Reconstructing images from brain activity has two broad goals. The first is to build brain-computer interfaces. The second is to understand how the brain sees. The eyes deliver incomplete, noisy input, so the brain must fill in the gaps with its expectations of what natural scenes look like. This filling-in relies on the brain's prior over natural images. Image reconstruction lets us test which natural image prior best matches the brain's natural image prior. However, most previous work on image reconstruction relies on an unnatural condition: subjects must hold their eyes still. In reality, primates constantly move their eyes to see the world. We introduce a framework for reconstructing images from neural activity while a primate freely views a static natural scene. We record from five monkeys with high-density electrode arrays in the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) and primary visual cortex (V1). The recorded receptive fields cover only a small part of the image. Thus, we combine neural activity across eye movements, trials, and sessions to reconstruct the full image. We first present a simple linear method with a 1/f prior. We then scale this approach up with modern deep learning. We train a decoder by tokenizing spiking activity and measured gaze; these tokens are fed into a transformer and diffusion model to reconstruct the full scene. Three factors determine reconstruction quality: the amount of neural evidence combined over viewing time, the accuracy of eye tracking, and the choice of image prior. Our method sets a new state-of-the-art performance in reconstructing natural images from brain activity, across brain recording modalities: fMRI, EEG, MEG and electrophysiology.

HEAR, SEE, SPIKE ONCE: Highly Efficient NeuroAI Temporal Neural Networks for Low-Precision Multimodal Audio-Visual Classification and Online Learning 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Spiking neural networks for multimodal audio-visual classification reach high accuracy by adopting deep-learning machinery: multi-stage convolutional or transformer encoders trained end-to-end with surrogate-gradient backpropagation through time. These prior works typically rely on full-precision (32-bit) parameters and do not treat hardware efficiency as a primary design objective. In this paper, we propose NeuTNN-AV, a much simpler hardware-amenable multimodal substrate built on the recently proposed NeuroAI Temporal Neural Network (NeuTNN) and demonstrate that it can match or outperform state-of-the-art SNN baselines with a fraction of their synapse counts. NeuTNN neurons carry multiple active dendritic segments. Each input line spikes at most once (temporal coding) and every weight is learned via a local reward-modulated spike-timing dependent plasticity (STDP) rule. All weights are 4-bit integers and inference uses only comparisons and additions. On CREMA-D, it reaches 78.6% with 2.34M synapses establishing a new state-of-the-art SNN accuracy, against 77.14% for MISNet-XL (31M parameters). On UrbanSound8K-AV, NeuTNN reaches 98.82% with 1.38M synapses, against 96.85% for SMMT (9.99M parameters) and 98.96% for MISNet-XL (31M parameters). Weight memory is 23-106x smaller than the spiking baselines, and the measured synaptic energy per inference is two to three orders of magnitude lower. Furthermore, the proposed NeuTNN learns CREMA-D in a single pass with 73.8% accuracy and demonstrates rapid online continual learning by adapting to unseen actors from a few dozen clips.

A Steerable Deep Network for Model-Free Diffusion MRI Registration 2026-11-24
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Nonrigid registration is vital to medical image analysis but remains challenging for diffusion MRI (dMRI) due to its high-dimensional, spatio-angular dependence. We present a novel, geometric deep learning framework for model-free, nonrigid registration of raw dMRI data. We formulate registration in the native spatio-angular acquisition space under the joint action of 3D roto-translations, $\mathrm{SE}(3)$. Central to our framework is the equivalence between equivariant spatio-angular convolutions and 3D steerable convolutions on spherical-harmonic coefficient fields, which we exploit to construct an $\mathrm{SE}(3)$-equivariant U-Net. A by-product of this design choice is freedom from having to augment the data with roto-translated versions of itself. We also formulate a maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) loss between water-displacement distributions, evaluated in Fourier space as a weighted sum of squared signal differences, and extend it with a local contrast correction that reduces to a local correlation. In controlled comparisons, including a known deformation of real tissue, the raw signal yields markedly more accurate alignment than fractional anisotropy under the same optimizer. On held-out HCP subjects and on a held-out clinical-grade cohort (AOMIC) from a different scanner, our method improves anatomical overlap over ANTs SyN on fractional anisotropy, mrregister on fODFs, DDMReg and SynthMorph, without estimating any derived representation, and the steerable network matches a conventional U-Net with fewer parameters while being unaffected by grid rotations of its inputs. This work establishes a foundation for data-driven, geometry-aware dMRI registration directly in the acquisition space.

Learning Legged MPC with Smooth Neural Surrogates 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Deep learning and model predictive control (MPC) can play complementary roles in legged robotics. However, integrating learned models with online planning remains challenging. When dynamics are learned with neural networks, three key difficulties arise: (1) stiff transitions from contact events may be inherited from the data; (2) additional non-physical local nonsmoothness can occur; and (3) training datasets can induce non-Gaussian model errors due to rapid state changes. We address (1) and (2) by introducing the smooth neural surrogate, a neural network with tunable smoothness designed to provide informative predictions and derivatives for trajectory optimization through contact.
