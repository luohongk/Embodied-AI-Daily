Beyond Ground Truth: A Heuristic Unsupervised Quality Assurance Engine for Diagnosing Calibrational Blindness in Dental Segmentation 2026-10-29
📝 Accepted at 11th IEEE Asia-Pacific Conference on Computer Science and Data Engineering (IEEE CSDE 2026), Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. Copyright 2026 IEEE. 5 pages, 5 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

In real-world clinical settings, dental X-ray segmentation using deep learning methods suffers from reliability issues. Even when the segmentation quality is bad, deep learning models show high confidence in their predictions (calibrational blindness). Moreover, in real use, doctors do not have ground-truth masks to check their accuracy. To address this problem, we introduce a simple quality-checking engine called HUS-QA with three stages. We train it on the PRAD (Periapical Dental X-ray) dataset to build our SimCLR self-supervised ResNet-18 U-Net model, which generates the segmentation mask and then evaluates its quality. Our HUS-QA checks the segmentation mask quality without a ground-truth mask. Prediction confidence, edge sharpness, shape connectivity, and area-size penalty are the simple indicators considered in this research. Results show that standard confidence methods fail (r = -0.1394, p = 1.7864e-3, ROC AUC = 0.4275). By contrast, our HUS-QA shows a positive correlation with actual Dice scores (r = 0.4647, p = 3.7510e-28) and achieves a strong ROC-AUC of 0.8405. Therefore, HUS-QA serves as a practical tool for clinical quality control.

Multistatic ISAC with Deep Learning-Based Clutter Suppression for 5G and Beyond 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) systems using 5G and beyond signals of opportunity enable joint wireless sensing and communication using shared infrastructure and spectrum resources. While multistatic ISAC can increase sensing coverage with spatially distributed receivers, dense and dynamic clutter can significantly degrade bistatic range and Doppler estimation, leading to errors in target localization and velocity estimation. This paper presents a multistatic ISAC framework with clutter suppression for high-fidelity estimation of target position and velocity. Distributed clutter is modeled with randomly located scatterers with varying Doppler spreads and radar cross sections to capture dense and dynamic clutter effects. A broad range of conventional signal processing baselines are evaluated for clutter suppression, showing limited robustness under strong clutter conditions. To address this limitation, deep learning-based clutter suppression methods are introduced to improve robustness against dense and dynamic clutter by employing residual and attention-enhanced deep neural network architectures that directly enhance target responses from clutter-contaminated observations. These methods substantially outperform conventional approaches in target position and velocity estimation across diverse clutter conditions in multistatic ISAC.

Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis: integrating reinforcement learning and conformal prediction for reliable wafer defect classification 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Semiconductor manufacturing is a complex, multi-step process that requires both high precision and low operating costs. Therefore, detecting defects in a high-volume setting is an important component of wafer map analysis for reliable production. However, most state-of-the-art deep learning approaches require a full scan of the surface, which hinders the development of high-throughput inspection systems. To address this problem, we propose Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis (UFLD), a novel algorithm that employs reinforcement learning for adaptive measurement acquisition and provides a prediction uncertainty level after each step, stopping the process once sufficient information is obtained. Unlike existing methods that rely on ensemble models or auxiliary confidence estimation deep learning models, UFLD utilizes a more parameter-efficient conformal prediction framework to determine the optimal stopping time. The proposed UFLD reduces acquisition time by about $40\%$ and achieves $98.4\%$ inspection accuracy, without requiring an additional learned confidence-estimation network. We validated our approach on the MixedWM38 dataset, demonstrating its advantages over existing adaptive acquisition procedures and standard convolutional neural networks.

IMPACT: Inverter-Informed Multimodal Learning for Photovoltaic Generation Forecasting 2026-10-29
Show abstract

This work develops a physics-informed multimodal deep learning framework for 10- to 110-minute-ahead probabilistic photovoltaic power forecasting. The forecasting model is built on a four-branch architecture that separately encodes plant-level temporal history and inverter-level spatial cloud dynamics through a graph attention encoder with a learned adjacency, known-future solar geometry, and the current meteorological state, fused through gated cross-attention. The geometry branch adds two forward-looking physical signals, spatial cloud heterogeneity and inverter ramp-down activity, each empirically weighted toward the horizon where it is most predictive. The decoder anchors its prediction to a smart persistence baseline, learning a horizon-conditioned correction, and the resulting quantile forecasts are calibrated online through a kernel-weighted adaptive conformal calibration step. The proposed model outperforms all 14 baselines, achieving a forecast skill score (FSS) of 0.2950, 0.099 above the next-best model. A branch-composition ablation shows that every branch contributes positively and the model retains substantial skill in challenging overcast and variable sky conditions (FSS 0.198 and 0.255).

OCCAMS: A Multi-Task Learning-Based Investigation of the CMB Power Spectra 2026-10-29
📝 13 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables, plus appendices. Comments are welcome
Show abstract

How many latent variables are needed to fit the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature and polarisation power spectra when a neural network is given no cosmological model, and to what extent do they map onto the standard cosmological parameters? We use multi-task learning with variational autoencoders trained on CLASS-generated spectra (TT, TE, EE) over $2\le\ell\le2500$. No cosmological model is built into the network, and the number of latent variables, $L$, is varied over $L=5$, $6$ and $7$. Their fits are compared to the Planck 2018 data using $\hatχ^2$, AIC, BIC and Bayesian evidence. Their correspondence with cosmological parameters is measured with normalised mutual information post-training. The loss weights are set within a single training run by uncertainty weighting, with the weight of the disentanglement term either fixed (semi-automated, SA) or learned (completely automated, CA). The reconstruction loss is split over multipole ranges in three configurations that separate the roles of temperature and polarisation and of the low- and high-$\ell$ ranges. In four of the six combinations of configuration and strategy, the fit improves from $L=5$ to $L=6$ and not beyond, and $L=6$ is the best, or close to the best, in five of the six cases. So, six latent variables are needed, as many as the free parameters of the standard, model-dependent approach. SA gives clearer disentanglement, while CA needs no a priori choice of the disentanglement weight and has the higher evidence at $L=5$ and $6$: the two strategies are points on a trade-off between interpretability and fit to the data. Notably, the latent variables for $τ$ and $A_s$ share almost no information in any model, consistent with large-scale polarisation separating the two, as in standard analyses. A deep-learning approach can thus give a compact, data-driven parametrisation of the CMB spectra.

Show abstract

This research proposes a two-level keystroke dynamics authentication system that provides defense-in-depth security through continuous behavioral drift monitoring. Level 1 employs fixed-text password authentication using Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) with contrastive learning and population-based training (PBT), achieving 3.77% EER for high-security login verification. Upon successful authentication, Level 2 continuously monitors user typing during natural free-text sessions using a 4-Model Deep Learning Ensemble. If suspicious behavioral drift is detected during monitoring, the system re-authenticates the user by returning to Level 1, providing a feedback loop against session hijacking and behavioral anomalies. The final system achieves 96%+ accuracy for fixed-text authentication (3.77% EER) and 83.53% True Acceptance Rate (TAR) at 5% False Acceptance Rate (FAR) for free-text continuous authentication (10.05% EER), demonstrating superior performance and strong resistance to impostor attacks through continuous behavioral monitoring.

Causal-prior-constrained deep learning for rapid-intensification warning of western North Pacific tropical cyclones 2026-10-29
📝 33 pages, including 15 pages of supplementary information; 5 main figures and 6 supplementary figures
Show abstract

Rapid intensification (RI) is one of the most dangerous and unpredictable phases of tropical cyclones, and its environmental and structural drivers are difficult to identify and separate in observations. Here we introduce CausalVisionRI, a causal-prior-constrained deep-learning system for 24-h RI warning in the western North Pacific. It integrates infrared remote sensing imagery, atmospheric and oceanic fields, and an observationally discovered causal graph into a unified model. The graph and eight sign-verified effects on 24-h intensity change provide an auditable causal prior for training. Discovered from 522 cyclones in 2000-2017, it identifies causal pathways linking atmospheric and oceanic conditions, the cyclone's dynamical and structural state, and its 24-h intensity change. On a forward holdout covering 2020-2024 and 3,004 records, the five-member ensemble achieves a Brier skill score (BSS) of 0.259 relative to a fixed development climatology, significantly exceeding four reference systems; the three LDA baselines reach at most 0.105. It also attains higher detection rates than a published knowledge-block scheme and a reconstructed contrastive-learning scheme at matched false-detection rates under our USA 30-kt standard and the published RITCF standard. Ablations show that the graph pathway adds 0.032 BSS across the holdout and 0.063 over 2020-2021, with a significant contribution in one ensemble member. A deep-learning model constrained by observationally discovered causal structure thus offers a new route to physically informed RI warning.

Self-training for cross-centre coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography using pre-contrast frame subtraction 2026-10-29
📝 7 pages, 2 figures, 3 tables
Show abstract

Deep learning models for coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography often perform poorly on data from centres that were not represented in training. Apart from differences in equipment, the labels of the largest public dataset (ARCADE) are incomplete, as only the segments defined in the SYNTAX score are annotated. In this study, we evaluated whether self-training with pseudo-labels can improve performance on external data without additional manual annotation. Pseudo-labels were generated from two sources, namely the predictions of an nnU-Net model trained on ARCADE and a contrast map obtained by subtracting a pre-contrast frame from the same sequence. The nnU-Net predictions were extended along the contrast map, and pixels detected only in the subtraction image were assigned an ignore label and excluded from the loss. Six models were trained and tested on ARCADE and on two external datasets (XCAD and XACV). All first-round models initialised with the baseline predictions performed better than both the baseline and a control model fine-tuned for the same number of epochs. The model trained on the XCAD pool improved the macro-averaged Dice score by 2.7 points (95% CI 2.3-3.2), while the control decreased it by 0.6 points. A second round of self-training did not lead to further improvement. When an external model trained on 50 fully annotated images was used to generate the initial labels, the XCAD Dice score increased from 0.623 to 0.745 without any XCAD labels, although performance on XACV did not improve because large main vessels were missed. These results suggest that the benefit of self-training depends largely on the sensitivity of the model used to generate the initial pseudo-labels.

Multigrade Neural Network Approximation 2026-10-29
Show abstract

We study multigrade deep learning (MGDL) as a principled framework for structured error refinement in deep neural networks. While the approximation power of neural networks is now relatively well understood, training very deep architectures remains challenging due to highly nonconvex and often ill-conditioned optimization landscapes. In contrast, for relatively shallow networks, most notably certain one-hidden-layer ReLU models, training admits convex reformulations with global guarantees under appropriate settings, motivating learning paradigms that improve stability while scaling to depth. MGDL builds on this insight by training deep networks grade by grade: previously learned grades are frozen, and each newly added grade-wise subnetwork is composed on top of the previously learned grades and trained to fit the residual left by the current approximation, yielding a structured and interpretable hierarchical refinement process. We develop an operator-theoretic foundation for MGDL and prove that, for any continuous target function defined on a hypercube, there exists a fixed-width multigrade ReLU scheme whose residuals are pointwise nonincreasing in magnitude and converge uniformly to zero, with strict $L^p$-norm decay at every nontrivial grade for $p\in [1,\infty)$. To the best of our knowledge, this work provides the first rigorous constructive approximation guarantee showing that a grade-wise residual refinement scheme can achieve vanishing error in a fixed-width multigrade ReLU architecture.

A Lightweight Fiducial-Based Pipeline for 3D Hyperspectral Mapping of ex-vivo Lumpectomy Specimens 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) is a promising modality for intraoperative assessment of resection margins in Breast-Conserving Surgery (BCS), but its clinical translation requires aligning the inherently 2D spectral information onto the 3D shape of the excised tissue so that suspicious regions can be precisely localized for targeted follow-up. We present a fully automated, calibration-free pipeline that produces a 3D hyperspectral point cloud of an ex-vivo lumpectomy specimen from a set of consumer-camera RGB images and a single top-down HSI acquisition. The 3D geometry is reconstructed with a deep-learning Structure-from-Motion backbone, stabilized in a metric reference frame by a custom bundle adjustment that enforces consistency on the corners of four ArUco markers placed around the specimen. These results support the feasibility of integrating HSI-guided spatial localization into intraoperative margin assessment workflows for breast-conserving surgery.

Testing Deep Learning Library APIs via Cross-Framework Differential Fuzzing 2026-10-29
Show abstract

Deep learning libraries underpin many safety- and reliability-critical applications, yet existing API-level testing techniques often rely on intra-library properties or CPU--GPU differential oracles and may miss defects that behave consistently across hardware backends. We present Xamt, a cross-framework differential fuzzing approach for deep learning library APIs. Xamt constructs and tests execution-validated groups of APIs intended to implement equivalent operations across seven libraries. It uses explicit API aliases and parameter-role normalization to construct candidate correspondences and validates them through pairwise execution and a group-level behavioral check on canonical ordinary inputs. The resulting groups are explored using variance-guided differential fuzzing with ordinary, boundary, and non-finite inputs. Among the 72 developer reports, 25 have been confirmed, including 23 that have been fixed.

Riemannian Deep Learning: Modules, Networks, and Geometries 2026-10-29
📝 PhD thesis manuscript, University of Trento; defense pending
Show abstract

Deep neural networks on manifold-valued representations have attracted growing interest, but many basic components remain tied to specific manifolds, rely on Euclidean approximations, or require costly and numerically fragile geometric operations. This thesis develops a unified framework for Riemannian deep learning from three complementary perspectives: reusable neural modules, manifold-specific network architectures, and the design of underlying geometries. It generalizes batch normalization from Euclidean spaces and individual manifolds to broad classes of Lie groups and gyrogroups, and extends multinomial logistic regression from Euclidean space to SPD manifolds and then to general Riemannian manifolds. It further develops neural networks for several important geometric representations, including an unconstrained model of hyperbolic space, Busemann-based hyperbolic learning, and full-rank correlation matrices. Finally, it introduces adaptive and computationally efficient Riemannian metrics on SPD manifolds, including learnable Log-Euclidean geometries and fast, stable Cholesky-based geometries. The proposed methods are supported by theoretical analysis and validated through numerical experiments and applications in vision, signal processing, graph learning, and genomics.

We present \tool, a query-efficient framework for black-box probabilistic verification. Given a fixed, sampleable input distribution and a binary property oracle, \tool assesses the property's violation probability $p$ against a threshold $θ$. Under independent sampling and stateless oracle behavior, acceptance asserts $p\leθ$, rejection asserts $p>θ+η$ for a user-specified separation margin, and an inconclusive verdict indicates insufficient evidence for either conclusion. The probability of each type of erroneous verdict is bounded by its specified error budget. To support this three-valued contract efficiently, \tool uses a single sequential test, eliminating the interval family required by the baseline. The test accounts for repeated sampling checks through either corrected exact binomial confidence intervals or an anytime-valid confidence sequence. We apply \tool to probabilistic local model semantic consistency, where the property oracle detects decision-level inconsistencies between reference and compiled executions under a specified perturbation distribution. Our evaluation covers 24 tasks across TorchInductor and TensorRT and examines sampling efficiency, conclusiveness, and diagnostic behavior. For sampling efficiency, we use paired one-model clean-label radius searches: the baseline requires 3.6-4.2 times as many unique samples under matched numerical parameters while providing a weaker decision contract. Adaptive optimizers are widely used in deep learning, yet their behavior can degrade under heavy stochasticity and label noise where the sign and magnitude of gradients can be unstable. We study MGO-SC (Moment-Guided Optimizer with Sign-Consistency), a lightweight modification of Adam-style methods that derives a coordinate-wise consistency gate from the magnitude of a sign exponential moving average (EMA): coordinates whose gradient sign is stable are trusted, while coordinates whose sign fluctuates are down-weighted. We show that the naive form of this gate, which can only shrink updates, loses effective step size and underperforms when the learning rate is matched to the baseline. We therefore introduce a mean-preserving variant that reallocates trust across coordinates without changing the net per-tensor step magnitude. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, under a matched learning rate and decoupled weight decay, mean-preserving MGO-SC consistently improves over its AdamW baseline across clean, symmetric, and asymmetric label noise (e.g., +1.4 points at 40% symmetric noise; paired bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero; Wilcoxon p<0.01), while adding no second-order information or per-sample filtering. Scanning Transmission Electron Microscopy (STEM) enables the observation of atomic arrangements at sub-angstrom resolution and plays a key role in the discovery and characterization of advanced materials. Raw STEM images are often noisy and blurred due to multiple degradations. Although recent deep learning methods improve STEM images, their performance remains limited under severe degradations. In this work, we propose a spatial-frequency learning framework for STEM image enhancement.
