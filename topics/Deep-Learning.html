Denoising Textures is a Different Ballgame Altogether! 2026-10-15
Show abstract

The human brain effortlessly deciphers colors and textures from visual stimuli and reconstructs the world around us, yet preserving such details remains challenging in image denoising. While significant progress has been made in generic image denoising, the problem of texture denoising has remained relatively under-explored. Fine details, such as intricate patterns and repetitive structures, often suffer from over-smoothing even in deep learning based approaches for denoising. In this paper, we analyze the behavior of state-of-the-art (SOTA) denoisers on texture datasets and show that performance improvements are possible only by fine-tuning the denoisers on the texture datasets. Surprisingly, fine-tuning on texture datasets result in the models catastrophically forgetting the original dataset. These findings highlight the need for specialized treatment of textures in denoising. Since denoising quality directly impacts downstream tasks such as generative modelling, we argue that dedicated texture aware denoisers are essential for faithful texture reconstruction and synthesis.

Accurate and efficient analysis of dental radiographs has become increasingly important for disease diagnosis and clinical decision making. In particular, dental radiographs segmentation plays a critical role as it is challenging due to several reasons, such as low contrast, noise in the images, variations in the image quality, etc. Previous studies have primarily addressed these challenges using convolutional neural networks (CNN)-based models. However, direct comparison of these segmentation models is still challenging due to the variations among the experimental settings. Therefore, we mainly compared four segmentation models, including Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet), DeepLabV3, UNet, and UNet++, through a fair, controlled experimentation setting under varying conditions such as image sizes and batch sizes. Therefore, this study provides insights into the strengths of different segmentation models and the potential of deep learning-based semantic segmentation methods for improving automated dental radiograph analysis.

Neuro-Relational Programs: Unifying Queries and Neural Computation over Structured Data 2026-10-15
📝 55 pages; Accepted at PODS 2026

The conventional approach to deep learning over relational databases applies neural models, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), to a graph representation of the database. Recent approaches instead operate on databases directly, associating tuples with embeddings and extending query mechanisms to jointly process embeddings and relational content. Inspired by these developments, we introduce Neuro-Relational Programs (NRPs), a declarative query language for relational databases whose facts carry numeric vector embeddings. NRPs extend Datalog-style rules with operations that combine, aggregate, and transform embeddings, thereby interleaving relational reasoning and learnable neural components within a single formalism. This yields a general approach to neural computation over relational data: an NRP can be read both as a query plan with trainable components and as a neural architecture with relational structure built in. Natural syntactic fragments of NRPs recover existing architectures and query formalisms. Zero-ary NRPs correspond to non-adaptive query algorithms; monadic NRPs generalize GNN-style message passing and precisely capture Deep Homomorphism Networks, a connection that we extend to frontier-guarded NRPs over databases with row-ids. We characterize the expressive power of unrestricted NRPs with ReLU-FFN transformations by FOCQ, an extension of first-order logic with counting interpreted over real-weighted structures, yielding a precise connection with uniform TC$^0$ over ordered databases. Together, these results establish NRPs as a broad declarative framework for querying and neural computation over relational data.

Stateful Testing for Deep Learning Compilers 2026-10-15
📝 27 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Modern deep learning compilers compile models lazily and cache artifacts across invocations. Retained compiler state can make a later invocation incorrect even when it is correct in isolation. An analysis of 26 publicly reported issues in PyTorch, JAX, and TensorFlow shows that detecting such bugs requires sequences that first establish compiler state and then expose a failure. We present Rondo, a stateful testing method that represents each test case as a model paired with an invocation sequence. Rondo mutates invocation sequences and uses a Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler guided by compilation behavior to favor rarely observed behaviors. Its three-way differential oracle compares execution without compilation, compiled execution with compiler state reset, and compiled execution with state retained. It confirms stateful bugs by replaying the same invocation with state retained and reset while preserving inputs and program state, then shortens sequences while preserving the original bug. We evaluate Rondo on four deep learning frameworks using 24 common model architectures. Rondo detects 117 bugs, including 35 stateful bugs. We submit 39 bug reports, including 18 for stateful bugs; developers confirm 35 reports, including 16 for stateful bugs, and fix 18 bugs, including 11 stateful bugs. Rondo achieves the highest behavior coverage and compiler Python source-line coverage among the compared methods, with 99.26% alarm precision. It shortens 44 of 117 invocation sequences, reducing the lengths of these 44 sequences by 83.34% on average.

CNet is a C++/CUDA framework for deep complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs), trained by gradient descent with explicit Wirtinger (CR-calculus) derivatives. Complex models are underexplored yet natural where data is intrinsically complex -- RF/IQ, MRI k-space, radar/SAR, audio spectra -- and phase carries information real networks discard. CNet is physics-native: a network is a cascade of complex (often unitary) operations on an amplitude vector, and classification is a Born-rule measurement p_k = |z_k|^2 / ||z||^2 rather than a softmax over real logits. Every layer ships a CPU reference and a finite-difference-verified CUDA kernel; the static, batch-cloned computation graph captures cleanly into CUDA Graphs, outperforming eager and compiled torch.complex64 on an H100. We present three studies. (1) A fully complex causal language model, built on a new causal (triangular-masked) Fourier mixer with an O(N log N) Bluestein evaluation of the forward pass and its Wirtinger adjoint, matches a parameter-matched real causal FNet on character-level tiny-shakespeare in under half the steps. We introduce Born-rule attention, a content-based causal mixer whose weights are the quantum-measurement probabilities ||^2 -- to our knowledge the first classical, complex-valued attention to replace softmax with the squared-magnitude Born score; with rotary embeddings and per-token complex normalization it reaches 1.52 nats/char, matching softmax self-attention, and transfers unchanged to byte-level (1.31 nats/char) and sub-word WikiText-103. (2) On radio-modulation classification (RML2016.10a/RML22) we trace the pooled-power accuracy ceiling to discarded temporal structure and recover it with a learnable cyclostationary conjugate-lag operator. (3) A bottleneck analysis of the Fourier phase problem in coherent-diffraction imaging isolates where complex-valued networks still need new operators.

Model-Driven Deep Learning for Distributed Detection with Low-Bit Quantization 2026-10-15
📝 16 pages, 10 figures. Accepted by IEEE JSAC
Show abstract

Distributed detection plays a fundamental role in communication-efficient collaborative inference over large-scale networks, where spatially distributed nodes must cooperate using severely quantized messages due to bandwidth constraints. While low-bit quantization is essential for reducing communication overhead, it can significantly degrade detection performance, particularly when prior knowledge of observation noise is unavailable. To address this challenge, we propose a model-driven deep learning framework for distributed detection with low-bit quantization. For fixed local quantizers, we first derive the exact finite-sensor maximum a posteriori (MAP) detection error and prove that, under conditionally independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations, independent local randomizations, and a common quantization-level constraint, identical quantizers preserve the supremum of the average network Chernoff information. Guided by these results, we develop a binary probabilistic quantizer with a trainable neural probability controller and design a tree-structured probabilistic quantizer for scalable multi-bit quantization. The exact finite-sensor MAP error provides the training objective for the binary quantizer, while we derive a computationally tractable Bhattacharyya surrogate objective for training the multi-bit quantizer. For both cases, we derive analytical MAP fusion rules based on the class-conditional probability mass functions (PMFs) of the quantized messages. When the observation statistics are unknown, these PMFs are estimated from held-out labeled data, yielding plug-in MAP detectors. Simulation results show that the proposed method approaches centralized-oracle performance at higher quantization resolutions and remains effective under changes in network size and test-time signal amplitude.

Product Interaction: An Algebraic Formalism for Deep Learning Architectures 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Modern neural architectures are largely developed as distinct empirical constructions, despite recurring principles across convolutional, attention, state-space, and equivariant models. We introduce Product Interactions, an algebraic formalism that unifies these architectures under a common construction principle and turns their design into a systematic procedure. The core shift is representing data as algebraic elements and treating multiplication, rather than linear transformation alone, as a primitive architectural operation. Architectures are generated by composing multiplication operators, with input dependence organized by self-interaction order. Within this formalism, we derive convolutional and equivariant networks as symmetry-constrained first-order product interactions, recover Mamba from state-space models as an increase in self-interaction order from one to three, and establish attention as a third-order interaction from composing the same operator. Equivariance is incorporated by constraining the algebraic product via symmetry: translation equivariance yields convolution, while SO(3)-compatibility produces the Clebsch--Gordan product and tensor-field interactions. This perspective recasts architecture design as an algebraic construction problem: the choice of algebra defines admissible interactions, symmetry constrains their structure, and composition generates higher-order architectures. The framework is thus generative. Following these principles, we construct a simplified-algebra dynamical layer that improves both accuracy and throughput over Mamba on CIFAR-10. Experiments show that interaction order affects accuracy non-monotonically and symmetry constraints boost learnability. Product Interactions provides a mathematical methodology for analyzing existing architectures and systematically designing new ones.

Infrared small target detection (IRSTD) aims to identify long distance small targets from complex infrared backgrounds, and is a fundamental task in remote sensing. Extensive experiments on four public benchmarks demonstrate that LCPNet achieves accurate and robust detection with low false-alarm rates, together with competitive efficiency among high-accuracy deep unfolding methods. Learning to Forecast on Multiple Spatiotemporal Hierarchies 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Hierarchical forecasting exploits aggregation structures that expose multiple views of the same underlying spatiotemporal forecasting problem, while ensuring coherence among forecasts. Individual series provide fine-grained information, while progressively coarser aggregates reveal temporal and spatial patterns at different scales. In the associated deep learning architectures, the hierarchy determines which aggregate series are available and how they are processed together with individual series (e.g., through reconciliation or message-passing operators). Different hierarchies thus provide distinct learning signals and inductive biases for forecasting the same collection of time series. Nonetheless, existing architectures are typically trained on a single, often fixed, structure. In this work, we propose a graph deep learning framework that leverages variations across hierarchies as multi-task regularization. We show that jointly learning across these structures can reduce over-specialization to any single hierarchy and improve forecasting performance. Indeed, training across hierarchies provides an inductive bias toward representations that are useful across different structures, and as such, generalize better. Making forecasts coherent with the aggregation constraints for each sampled hierarchy is computationally expensive: to complement the framework, we propose a novel operator-based on a top-down approach-that produces coherent forecasts with linear time and space complexity w.r.t. the hierarchy size. Experiments show that our approach improves forecasting accuracy over training on a single structure and is competitive with current state-of-the-art methods.

Beyond Confidence: Rethinking Uncertainty Calibration in Deep Code Models 2026-10-15
Show abstract

Deep learning models have shown significant promise in code-related tasks. While confidence calibration techniques have been proposed to improve model reliability, their effectiveness in real-world tasks with strict accuracy requirements remains uncertain. In this paper, we investigate two key questions: first, whether existing measurement metrics, including confidence scores and uncertainty metrics, are effective for guiding selective prediction; and second, whether existing calibration techniques can improve these metrics to better identify cases where model predictions are likely to succeed or fail. We conduct a systematic study on defect prediction and vulnerability detection using seven code models, evaluating thirteen uncertainty signals and three widely used post-hoc calibration methods. Our analysis reveals three major findings: (1) even strong uncertainty signals can remain poorly calibrated and provide limited separation at the high-confidence end, constraining selective prediction in practice; (2) standard post-hoc calibrators often improve calibration scores but do not consistently translate into better selective prediction behavior; and (3) commonly used calibration metrics can disagree with selective prediction performance, underscoring the need to evaluate both jointly. Moreover, our findings motivate a decision-oriented view of reliability in deep code models, where calibrated scores must be evaluated together with selective-prediction quality and retained-region risk before they can support accept--defer deployment.

The rapid advancement of deep learning has presented significant energy efficiency challenges to the conventional von Neumann architecture.
