Reva: Real-Time Vulnerability Detection through Contextual Probing of Large Language Models 2026-10-26
Show abstract

A vulnerability costs more to fix the later it is discovered. As coding agents generate code faster than developers can review it, software needs a first line of defense screening each new function in real time. LLM agents have recently become capable of finding real-world vulnerabilities by working as a security expert would: they run analysis tools, explore the surrounding code, test hypotheses and weigh the evidence before forming their own judgement. This reasoning, however, spans many model calls, so that each verdict takes minutes of compute, far too long for real-time screening. Faster detectors, which fine-tune deep learning models or prompt an LLM for a direct verdict, are less accurate: they judge a function without the context an agent gathers, and an LLM's generated verdict is unreliable even when given some of it. We present REVA (REal-time Vulnerability Analysis), an AI vulnerability detector that brings the security context and code understanding components behind agentic detectors into a single forward pass of an LLM. For each new function, REVA gives the LLM a pre-computed threat model of its module, lightweight static analysis findings and call-graph information. REVA leverages a trained probe to read the LLM's internal representations of the new function in this context. On PrimeVul's held-out test set, REVA on GLM-5.3-Flash reaches 60.0% accuracy in about one second per function. This matches VulTriage (59.2%), the state-of-the-art agentic detector, run on the same LLM, while being over 250 times faster and less than a tenth as costly. On the same prompt, the probe is 14% to 17% more accurate than the LLM's own direct verdict. Across three open LLMs, REVA is at least as accurate as VulTriage, and about 10% more accurate on two of them. An LLM's internal representations thus encode much of what agentic detectors obtain through reasoning.

On the Architectural Complexity of Neural Networks 2026-10-26
📝 69 pages, 54 figures, 11 tables
Show abstract

We introduce a unified theoretical framework for the rigorous analysis and systematic construction of deep neural networks (DNNs). This framework addresses a gap in existing theory by explicitly modeling the structure of tensor operations -- lower level information that is often abstracted. Our framework enables two novel objectives: (1) analysis of the evolution of architectural complexity over deep learning history, and (2) automatic construction of novel architectures based on new types of tensor operations. Our study of DNNs introduced over the past 40 years reveals a connection between groundbreaking architectures and increases in different types of architectural complexity. Moreover, we identify several large classes of higher complexity architectures that have not yet been explored. We then collect a dataset of 3,000+ higher complexity architectures, which we publicly release at: https://github.com/combinatoriallabs/ArchitecturalComplexity.

Evaluating Bayesian deep learning for robust biomedical time-series classification under distribution shift: A sleep scoring case study 2026-10-26
📝 28 pages (8 pages main text), 8 figures, 15 tables
Show abstract

In biomedical time-series classification, machine learning models often suffer from deteriorating accuracy and calibration due to distribution shift. We investigate to what degree Bayesian-inspired deep learning improves robustness through Bayesian model averaging (BMA). Using rodent sleep scoring as a case study, we evaluate BMA under distribution shifts in genotype, laboratory, and species. We systematically compare nine approximate inference methods spanning single- and multi-basin approaches across two neural network backbones on in-distribution (ID) data and five out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets. In our results, multi-basin methods perform best, outperforming baselines and single-basin methods. Compared with point estimates, BMA generally improves OOD accuracy and calibration across both architectures, while yielding only modest ID gains. It also often improves selective prediction and OOD and artifact detection, but, interestingly, not inter-rater disagreement detection.

Toward Clinical Translation of Deep Learning-Based Intracranial Super-Resolution 4D Flow MRI Using Multi-Cohort Transfer Learning 2026-10-26
📝 28 pages, 6 main figures, 4 main tables; supplementary material includes 6 sections, 2 figures, and 1 table
Show abstract

We evaluated synthetic pretraining and multi-cohort transfer learning for deep learning-based super-resolution of intracranial 4D Flow MRI across heterogeneous neurologic cohorts. This mixed prospective-retrospective study analyzed 18 participants (12 healthy participants, 3 with moyamoya disease, and 3 with multiple sclerosis) with paired 1.2-mm and 0.6-mm 4D Flow MRI scans. A convolutional neural network pretrained on synthetic cerebrovascular data was fine-tuned using cohort-specific or pooled multi-cohort in vivo data. High-resolution-derived inputs enabled voxel-wise evaluation; native standard-resolution scans were used for angiographic and hemodynamic analyses. Synthetic pretraining reduced mean artery-wise velocity-normalized root mean square error (vNRMSE) versus interpolation (0.147 vs 0.166). Pooled transfer learning yielded the lowest mean vNRMSE (0.119 vs 0.127 for cohort-specific models and 0.147 for synthetic pretraining), with narrower 95% limits-of-agreement widths than synthetic pretraining across all test cases (0.109-0.222 vs 0.154-0.278 m/s). Super-resolved phase-contrast angiography achieved higher precision-recall area under the curve than native standard-resolution imaging in all cohorts (0.848-0.896 vs 0.486-0.805). These results indicate that pooled transfer learning improves cross-cohort reconstruction agreement and that super-resolution can improve vessel depiction, supporting further multi-center validation for cerebrovascular imaging.

ACSAN: All-Convolutional Self-Attention Network Integrating Convolutional Operations with Vision Transformer 2026-10-26
Show abstract

In the past few years, Vision Transformer (ViT) has achieved excellent performance in the field of computer vision. However, there still some problems remain. For instance, ViTs heavily rely on large-scale training data since they simply contain a large number of trainable parameters, which slows down their learning efficiency and limits their applicability in resource-constrained scenarios. In this paper, we propose a novel deep learning model, namely All-Convolutional Self-Attention Network (ACSAN), which applies convolution operations to replace fully-connected matrices multiplications in the ViT framework. To implement ACSAN, we firstly design Dynamic Convolutional Self-Attention (DCSA) as the basic module, which employs multi-scale dilated convolutions to generate Query, Key, and Value. We defined spatial self-attention and channel self-attention to generate attention features respectively. The two attention features are finally merged as the output of the DCSA module. Based on DCSA, we then construct the basic ACSAN block. Comparing with ViT, we use DCSA to replace the regular self-attention operations, and use convolution layers to replace MLPs. To evaluate the performance of the proposed method, we train ACSAN on three widely-used image classification databases, i.e. CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1k, from scratch. Experimental results show that ACSAN achieves excellent classification accuracy comparing with standard ViT with comparable or fewer parameters, as well as other state-of-the-art methods. Ablation studies verify that each component contributes positively to the final performance.

Low-rank MERA Decomposition of 3D OCT Image Volume for Compression, Denoising, Feature Extraction and Diagnosis of Retinal Diseases 2026-10-26
📝 10 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables
Show abstract

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is a non-invasive imaging modality used for visualization of retinal tissue. However, OCT volumes are affected by speckle noise, which degrades image contrast and obscures fine structural details. Furthermore, modern clinical practice requires large field-of-view, high-resolution OCT volumes, whose storage and transmission are demanding for archiving and teleophthalmology applications. To address aforementioned challenges, we propose a low-rank multi-scale entanglement renormalization ansatz (MERA) tensor network approximation of 3D OCT image volume, where the image volume is represented as contractions of a top core factor encoding global features and multiple interacting low-rank orthogonal or semi-orthogonal factors modeling inter- and intra-B scan correlations. This representation jointly enables compression, denoising, and feature extraction. Unlike deep learning approaches, the proposed approach is training-free and processes each OCT volume independently. We evaluate the proposed framework on OCT image volumes acquired by two different scanners and demonstrate improved visual quality and reduced retinal layer segmentation errors in comparison with several competing methods. We also extract MERA features from two clinical OCT volume datasets. They are subsequently classified using a Grassmann-manifold nearest-subspace classifier. With the subspace dimension as its only hyperparameter, the classifier is theoretically shown to be robust in small-sample clinical scenario. We demonstrate competitive accuracy of classification of retinal diseases in these small-sample settings, where deep learning methods are often limited by the scarcity of labeled data. The proposed structure-aware model-driven representation learning for high-dimensional data under limited supervision provides good generalization performance while maintaining high interpretability.

Position: Adopt Constraints Over Fixed Penalties in Deep Learning 2026-10-26
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Position Paper Track). 10 pages of main text plus appendix; 3 figures, 4 tables. Code: https://github.com/merajhashemi/constraints-vs-penalties
Show abstract

Deep learning models are increasingly subject to explicit requirements, or constraints, such as safety levels, fairness limits, and sparsity budgets. In practice, such requirements are often handled through fixed weighted-sum penalization: the constraints are added to the task loss with fixed coefficients, and the resulting scalarized objective is minimized. This position paper argues that fixed penalization is often ill-suited to deep learning problems with non-negotiable requirements, for three main reasons. First, in non-convex settings, the penalized and constrained problems are not, in general, equivalent, so a minimizer of the penalized objective need not solve the constrained problem. Second, a penalty coefficient sets an objective-constraint trade-off rather than the constraint level, so finding an acceptable coefficient often takes trial and error, each trial a full training run. Third, fixed penalization weakens hard requirements into soft penalties to be traded off against task performance. As a result, compliance is no longer built into the problem, and requirements can be quietly relaxed. We therefore argue that, when a deep learning problem specifies non-negotiable requirements, one should solve the constrained problem itself instead of a surrogate defined by fixed penalization.

WADBERT: Dual-Channel Web Attack Detection via Permutation-Invariant Parameter Modeling 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Web attack detection is the first line of defense for securing web applications, designed to preemptively identify malicious activities. Deep learning-based approaches are increasingly popular for their advantages: automatically learning complex patterns and extracting semantic features from HTTP requests to achieve superior detection performance. However, existing methods are less effective at representing irregular HTTP requests and introduce unnecessary sensitivity to payload parameter order. To tackle this issue, in this paper, we propose WADBERT, a structure-aware dual-channel framework for web attack detection built on two complementary designs. First, we introduce \textit{Hybrid Granularity Embedding (HGE)}, which combines WordPiece embeddings with token-aligned character representations to capture fine-grained patterns in symbol-dense URLs and payload parameters. Second, we develop \textit{structure-aware feature extraction}, with separate branches for the sequential URL and the unordered collection of payload parameters. Each parameter is encoded independently, and position-free multi-head attention followed by mean pooling produces a payload representation invariant to parameter reordering. The URL and payload features are fused for request classification, while the learned parameter-level attention weights support inspection of potentially malicious parameters. Experiments on CSIC2010 and SR-BH2020 validate the effectiveness of our method: WADBERT achieves F1-scores of 99.64\% and 99.59\%, respectively, outperforming the evaluated state-of-the-art baselines.

A Machine Learning API for Earth Observation Data Cubes Based on openEO 2026-10-26
📝 35 pages, 12 figures
Show abstract

Earth Observation (EO) data are increasingly organized as spatio-temporal data cubes, while machine learning (ML) methods operate on tabular feature matrices or structured tensor inputs. This mismatch forces platform-specific transformations that are difficult to reproduce or transfer across cloud infrastructures. The openEO specification provides a unified interface for EO data access and processing across heterogeneous backends, but lacks a standardized approach for ML integration. We propose a process-level ML specification for openEO structured into three stages: model initialization, model actions (training, tuning, inference, validation), and model management. It supports classical algorithms such as Random Forest and SVM, as well as deep learning architectures for time-series and spatial patch-based modeling, including TempCNN, Temporal Attention Encoders, and foundation model inference. Three prototype implementations in R and Python demonstrate feasibility across diverse technology stacks. Addressing both through explicit backend conformance profiles represents the most important near-term direction. The specification advances the reproducibility, portability, and accessibility of ML workflows on EO data cubes across cloud platforms.

A-UTE: Advection Informed, Uncertainty Aware Temperature Emulator 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Physics-based Earth system models (ESMs) are essential for attributing climate change and generating scenario projections, yet their reliance on high-resolution numerical integration makes multi-decadal experiments expensive. In parallel, deep learning has delivered strong gains in short-range weather forecasting; however, auto-regressive roll-outs can accumulate error and become unstable when extended to decade-scale climate emulation. We introduce A-UTE: Advection Informed, Uncertainty Aware Temperature Emulator, aimed at stable multi-year emulation across heterogeneous climate models and grid resolutions. A-UTE is trained on various physics-based models at varying spatial resolutions to emulate temperature fields over a 10-year horizon. A-UTE formulates climate emulation as a forward-time stochastic dynamical system. We propose an auto-regressive ODE-SDE surrogate in which transport dynamics are constrained by an advection consistent ODE component, while a learned neural SDE term models coarse-grained variability and cross-model discrepancy at monthly resolution. We train A-UTE under negative log-likelihood objective for principled uncertainty estimates and probabilistic evaluation. Experiments across 20 climate models show that A-UTE improves long roll-out stability and accuracy relative to relevant baselines, advancing data-driven climate emulation with explicit physical structure and uncertainty-aware predictions.

Streaming Recurrent Memory 2026-10-26
Show abstract

Natural intelligence learns from a continuous stream of sensorimotor experience: data can arrive at high bandwidth, and behavior adapts to feedback as soon as it arrives, even when it is delayed. In contrast, most modern deep learning systems rely on stored datasets, replay buffers, and retained contexts through which credit is later assigned. At high data rates, storing and revisiting past experience can be impractical under tight memory and computational constraints. This paper studies recurrent memory under a streaming learning constraint: the learner learns from experience as soon as it arrives without storing raw experience. We ask whether a streaming learner can maintain a state that remains useful for future prediction and control from delayed feedback. We introduce Streaming Recurrent Memory (SRM), a recurrent memory designed for the streaming setting which preserves information that may matter later while remaining trainable with one-step backpropagation through time (BPTT). SRM provides a streaming-compatible recurrence that is designed to recall past information by content or time, track elapsed steps, and compose transformations whose outcome depends on the order in which inputs are observed. Across controlled memory tasks and streaming applications, SRM learns effectively with one-step BPTT, outperforming recurrent baselines in associative recall and online language modeling while achieving near-perfect success in associative T-Maze with delayed rewards. These results suggest that fixing how inputs are stored in the recurrent state, while learning how that state is read out, enables effective streaming learning without long-range backpropagation.

Conditional Diffusion Generation of DVL and Inertial Signals for Underwater Navigation 2026-10-25
Show abstract

Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) navigate without satellite positioning and rely on Doppler velocity log (DVL) and inertial sensors for motion estimation. Deep learning methods for processing these sensors require training data covering the motion the AUV encounters, commonly obtained through costly sea trials. To overcome this limitation, we present a conditional diffusion framework that generates synthetic DVL and inertial signals to replace real recordings in training. The generator employs an Elucidated diffusion model conditioned on three per-channel motion descriptors. Three configurations are evaluated: DVL beams alone, inertial channels alone, and all channels jointly from one model. Across thirteen trajectories of the real dataset, the generated signals followed the requested conditions with Pearson correlations between 0.72 and 0.997, transferred to trajectories withheld from generator training, and did not replicate any training window. A BeamsNet velocity estimator trained on generated signals alone, with no real recording retained, matched the velocity error obtained with the full real training set. Replacing up to all real windows with generated ones caused no measurable loss. Thus, this approach reduces the dependence of learned AUV navigation on sea-trial data, enables training when recordings are scarce, and facilitates developing and evaluating deep learning methods for underwater navigation.

Is Oracle Pruning the True Oracle? 2026-10-25
📝 TMLR 2026, Webpage: https://fscdc.github.io/Oracle-Pruning-Sanity-Check/
Show abstract

Oracle pruning, which selects unimportant weights by minimizing the pruned train loss, has served as the foundation for most neural network pruning methods for over thirty-five years, while few (if any) have thought about how much the foundation really holds. This paper, for the first time, attempts to systematically examine its validity on deep neural networks through empirical correlation analyses and provides meta-framework reflections on the field of neural network pruning. Specifically, this paper focuses on the pruning algorithms with three stages: training, pruning, and retraining. We analyze the correlation in model performance before and after the retraining stage. Extensive experiments (37K models are trained) across a wide spectrum of models (LeNet5, VGG, ResNets, ViT, MLLM) and datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10/CIFAR100, ImageNet-1K, MLLM data) are conducted. For large-scale experiments, we adopt approximate oracle pruning due to the prohibitive cost of exact oracle pruning. The results point to a counterintuitive conclusion: for deep learning models of nontrivial size (already at the scale of ResNet56 on CIFAR-10), pre-retraining performance is negligibly correlated with post-retraining performance. In other words, the weights identified by oracle pruning can scarcely guarantee strong performance following retraining. This further suggests that existing works that derive pruning criteria from oracle pruning may rest on a questionable foundational premise. Further studies suggest that rising task complexity is a primary factor behind the invalidity of oracle pruning nowadays. Finally, given the evidence, we argue that the retraining stage in a pruning algorithm should be accounted for when developing pruning criteria.

Our primary contribution is a combinatorial constraint enforcement technique that ensures fractional feasibility by construction, bypassing the need for penalty-based approximations. We introduce CAGraph, a novel graph-based architecture designed to capture dependencies between items and bidders, alongside CANet and CAFormer as deep learning baselines, for combinatorial settings. We also provide a Matching Pursuit-inspired decomposition that maps fractional allocations to feasible discrete allocations and empirically evaluates the resulting discretization gap. In synthetic benchmarks and an airport slot-divestiture case study, respectively, the learned mechanisms achieve up to 73.5% and 180% higher expected revenue than VCG, under small empirical regret. Finally, we visualize the Pareto frontiers for revenue, welfare, and fairness, providing regulators with a decision-support tool to design infrastructure auctions that balance profitability, efficiency and equity.
