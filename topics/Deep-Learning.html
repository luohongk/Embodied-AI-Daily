Point2SSM++: Self-Supervised Learning of Anatomical Shape Models from Point Clouds 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Correspondence-based statistical shape modeling (SSM) stands as a powerful technology for morphometric analysis in clinical research. SSM facilitates population-level characterization and quantification of anatomical shapes such as bones and organs, aiding in pathology and disease diagnostics and treatment planning. Despite its potential, SSM remains under-utilized in medical research due to the significant overhead associated with automatic construction methods, which demand complete, aligned shape surface representations. Additionally, optimization-based techniques rely on bias-inducing assumptions or templates and have prolonged inference times as the entire cohort is simultaneously optimized. To overcome these challenges, we introduce Point2SSM++, a principled, self-supervised deep learning approach that directly learns correspondence points from point cloud representations of anatomical shapes. Point2SSM++ is robust to misaligned and inconsistent input, providing SSM that accurately samples individual shape surfaces while effectively capturing population-level statistics. Furthermore, we present extensions of Point2SSM++ tailored for dynamic spatiotemporal and multi-anatomy scenarios, showcasing the broad versatility of the framework. Through extensive validation across diverse anatomies, evaluation metrics, and clinically relevant downstream tasks, we demonstrate Point2SSM++'s superiority over existing state-of-the-art deep learning models and the traditional approach. Point2SSM++ substantially enhances the feasibility of SSM generation and significantly broadens its array of potential clinical applications.

Network of Theseus (like the ship) 2026-11-25
📝 NeurIPS 2026. 23 pages, 10 figures, 10 tables
Show abstract

A standard assumption in deep learning is that the inductive bias introduced by a neural network architecture must persist from training through inference. The architecture you train with is the architecture you deploy. This assumption constrains the community from selecting architectures that may have desirable efficiency or design properties due to difficulties with optimization. We challenge this assumption with the Network of Theseus (NoT), a method for progressively converting a trained, or even untrained, guide network architecture part-by-part into an entirely different target network architecture while preserving the performance of the guide network. At each stage, components in the guide network architecture are incrementally replaced with the target architecture modules and aligned via representational similarity metrics. This procedure preserves most of the functionality of the guide network even under substantial architectural changes-for example, converting a convolutional network into a multilayer perceptron, or GPT-2 into a recurrent neural network. By decoupling optimization from deployment, NoT expands the space of viable inference-time architectures, opening opportunities for better accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs and enabling more directed exploration of the architectural design space.

Comparative Study of Deep Learning Architectures for Brain Tumour Classification 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Accurate and early classification of brain tumours from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is essential for treatment planning, yet many published deep learning studies are trained and tested on small, single-source datasets, reporting accuracies that may not hold outside these datasets. This study compares six deep learning architectures for four-class classification (healthy, meningioma, glioma and pituitary tumour): a baseline convolutional neural network (CNN), an attention-based CNN, ShuffleNet, and EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-50 and Xception augmented with the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM). The models were trained on a corpus assembled from four publicly available MRI datasets. Because these datasets carry no patient identifiers, a leakage-aware pipeline was developed that removes exact duplicates using MD5 hashing (550 images) and near-duplicates using perceptual hashing (8,024 images), and assigns visually similar images to the same fold during 5-fold group cross-validation. A leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) test on a fully held-out dataset was used to measure cross-dataset generalisation. Under a conventional random split, ShuffleNet achieved 97.1% accuracy with 1.2M parameters. Under leakage-aware group K-fold cross-validation, ShuffleNetV2 (98.41%) and EfficientNet-B0 (98.23%) were statistically indistinguishable (paired t-test, p = 0.19), and both significantly outperformed the custom CNNs. On the held-out dataset, accuracy dropped for every model: EfficientNet-B0 generalised best (78.4%), while ShuffleNetV2 fell to 66.2%. Glioma-meningioma confusion was the dominant error mode, and Grad-CAM maps showed that predictions were driven by tumour regions. These results indicate that in-distribution accuracy alone overstates real-world performance and that leakage-aware and cross-dataset evaluation should be reported alongside it.

BridgeHammer: DNN Backdoor Attack via CPU-iGPU Shared-memory Rowhammer 2026-11-25
📝 7 pages, 3 figures. Accepted at DATE 2027
Show abstract

Heterogeneous systems-on-chips (SoCs) share physical memory between CPUs and integrated GPUs (iGPUs), creating a path for CPU-side memory corruption to compromise the integrity of deep learning models used for iGPU inference. Implanting a backdoor that causes targeted misclassifications on trigger-bearing inputs while retaining accuracy on normal inputs requires carefully selected weight changes. However, realizing these changes presents two challenges: where to induce faults, since vulnerable DRAM locations may not contain the targeted weights, and when to intervene, since memory placement and fault injection must align with different stages of the model deployment lifecycle that involve two distinct computation domains. We present BridgeHammer, a Rowhammer-based backdoor attack that addresses these challenges by coordinating targeted weight placement before zero-copy binding for the iGPU and fault injection before inference. The iGPU then uses the corrupted model for subsequent inferences. We evaluate BridgeHammer on a heterogeneous Intel CPU-iGPU platform using OpenVINO, targeting ResNet-20, VGG-16, and ResNet-50 at both INT8 and FP32 precision. Across all six configurations, BridgeHammer achieves an 88.9% attack success rate (ASR) and 86.3% clean accuracy on average, with reliable target-page placement and bit-flip reproduction. These results demonstrate a new integrity vulnerability on heterogeneous CPU-iGPU SoCs, motivating cross-domain memory integrity protections.

Investigating the efficacy of vortex beam speckle patterns for the recognition of atmospheric turbulence and fractional topological charge with a deep learning model 2026-11-25
📝 10 pages, 6 figures
Show abstract

Fractional topological charge (TC) enlarges the usable mode set of orbital angular momentum (OAM) links, but reading it reliably after atmospheric turbulence remains difficult. Interferometric and diffractive methods need an undistorted beam and a careful alignment, while most learned approaches treat integer or coarsely spaced charges and do not include turbulence. Here we ask whether the charge of a scalar vortex beam can be regressed directly from a single speckle image, and at which turbulence strength this stops working. We propagate vortex beams over 1500~m through 150 von Kármán phase screens, scatter them with a fixed random diffuser, and record $256\times256$, 16-bit speckle images for TC from 0 to 10 in steps of 0.01 and turbulence strengths $D/r_0$ from 0 to 50; the results reported here use a subset of 20\,000 images, and a data set of $10^{6}$ images is in preparation. A convolutional neural network estimates the TC and, through an auxiliary head, the turbulence category. On charges withheld from training, the mean absolute error is 0.031 without turbulence (with a common intensity scale) and rises to 0.78 at $D/r_0=10$ and 1.85 at $D/r_0=50$, against 2.5 for a constant prediction, and the turbulence category is identified correctly in 78\% of the images. The results show that, at the present training size, the charge is read accurately only without turbulence, and that the charge and the turbulence oth act on the speckle grain size, which may limit the readout. Experimental validation and the extension to vector vortex beams are left for future work.

Interpretable identification of constitutive parameter field for three-dimensional heterogeneous and graded hyperelastic materials from full-field displacements 2026-11-25
Show abstract

The inverse problem of identifying spatially varying material properties of heterogeneous solids using experimentally available displacement fields and boundary reaction forces alone remains challenging. Being able to characterize heterogeneous and graded solids would be advantageous in the design and manufacturing of functionally graded materials and composites. Additionally, an efficient technique for identifying heterogeneous material properties from in vivo measurements would be useful in biomedical contexts to characterize heterogeneous biological tissues. Although several techniques developed over the past two decades have addressed inverse identification for homogeneous materials, including linear elastic, hyperelastic, viscoelastic, and elastoplastic solids, comparatively fewer studies have considered heterogeneous and graded materials. Furthermore, several recent approaches to characterizing heterogeneous and graded materials employ parameter-heavy, deep learning-based frameworks trained to enforce force equilibrium at specified collocation points within the material. In this work, we propose an interpretable and time-efficient framework that predicts the three-dimensional relative spatial variation of a constitutive parameter field for multiple parameters of a heterogeneous hyperelastic material, using only displacement-field data. In this work, we validate our framework on 3D synthetic and 2D experimental benchmarks involving heterogeneous, graded, and anisotropic hyperelastic materials. By leveraging the linear nature of the inverse problem for hyperelastic materials and directly optimizing for the constitutive parameters at the finite-element level, we achieve acceptable prediction accuracy with significantly lower computational time compared to recently reported deep-learning-based frameworks.

Weakly Conditioned Diffusion Model Augmentation for Prostate Segmentation 2026-11-25
📝 15 pages, preprint
Show abstract

Deep learning models for prostate MRI segmentation often struggle to generalize when trained on limited heterogeneous data, leading to boundary errors and over-segmentation. We present an augmentation framework using mask-conditioned synthetic and diverse T2-weighted MRI volumes to improve boundary precision and reduce over-segmentation. We employ a 2.5D weakly mask-conditioned denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) via channel concatenation. The diffusion model conditions on stacks of adjacent binary masks from training folds. The model generates T2 volumes consistent with the conditioning anatomy while diversifying tissue appearance. We evaluate the proposed approach on two cohorts of data: (i) the LUND probe cohort (n=432) for strategy selection and training nnU-Net from scratch with a 23% synthetic data mix, and (ii) the Medical Segmentation Decathlon (MSD) prostate cohort (n=32) evaluated with a 20% synthetic data mix. Experimental results on both these cohorts suggest that mask-conditioned DDPM augmentation can reduce over-segmentation errors and improve boundary adherence in data-constrained settings.

Change Point Detection with Generative Models 2026-11-25
📝 Neuroinformatics 2026 (Oral)
Show abstract

Change point detection (CPD) methods aim to identify abrupt changes in the properties of time series, which is a key problem in numerous fields. A major class of CPD approaches relies on computing a CPD score, which is either a discrepancy or divergence between two subsequent samples, called windows. A fundamental limitation of window-based CPD is the 'sample size bottleneck.' To ensure temporal precision, windows must be small; however, small samples provide insufficient statistical evidence for the classifiers and deep-learning models currently used to compute CPD scores. This leads to high variance in the scores and frequent mispredictions. Our study tackles this issue by exploring generative models for window augmentation, effectively expanding available data to improve CPD quality. Experiments on synthetic and real data demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly enhances CPD. Specifically, we show that this approach allows simple classifiers to outperform complex deep-learning baselines by stabilizing the CPD score distribution.

A Tensor Neural Network for Interpretable and Predictive Representations 2026-11-25
📝 35 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables
Show abstract

Tensor regression is challenged by the exponential dimensionality of tensor-valued covariates and their complex multi-mode dependence. Existing methods therefore often rely on simple models, such as linear tensor regression and tensor additive models, which are computationally tractable but impose restrictive structural assumptions and may limit predictive accuracy. To address these limitations, we propose a tensor neural network that learns low-dimensional, interpretable, and prediction-sufficient representations while preserving the multi-mode structure of tensor data. The model captures nonlinear effects for accurate response prediction while retaining the interpretability of classical linear tensor regression. Theoretically, we establish selection consistency for the minimal representation dimension $K^*$ necessary for prediction and show that the estimator attains a representation-adaptive non-asymptotic error rate. Specifically, the rate improves from the ambient-dimensional order $n^{-\fracβ{\mathcal{D}+2β}}$ to $n^{-\fracβ{K^*+2β}}$, where $\mathcal{D}=\prod_{j=1}^M P_j$ is the ambient dimension. This demonstrates that recovering the low-dimensional representation is crucial for mitigating the curse of dimensionality in tensor regression. Moreover, minimizing the derived generalization error bound yields explicit tuning-parameter choices for computationally lightweight tuning. Extensive simulations and real-data analyses show consistent performance across settings of varying complexity. Unlike black-box deep learning models, the framework supports direct visualization and interpretation of learned tensor representations without external post hoc tools.

Counter-DECEIVR: Counterfactual Explanations for Deepfake Detectors using Detector-Guided Rectified Flow Inversion 2026-11-25
📝 Under review
Show abstract

Deep-learning-generated media are increasingly passed off as real, driving an arms race between image generators and deepfake detectors. Trust in the latter is limited by their often black-box nature; and, while region-based explanations can sometimes be extracted, these become less useful as generation quality improves. Counterfactual examples, which show the minimal change that flips a detector's decision, reveal what evidence drives a verdict, helping analysts decide whether to trust it. This work introduces Counter-DECEIVR (DEtector-guided reCtifiEd flow InVeRsion for counterfactuals), a novel training-free framework that generates counterfactual examples. Counter-DECEIVR steers latents in the velocity field of a rectified flow model using gradients from off-the-shelf deepfake detectors, without training a generator or adapting the detector; unlike prior methods, it requires no per-instance or per-detector fitting and no diffusion training on the target data. It also leverages the detector's explanation maps to concentrate editing to salient regions, turning low-level feature importance into localised counterfactual edits. A single parameter controls the strength of the edits. Across two deepfake detectors and two widely used forensics datasets, Counter-DECEIVR outperforms prior counterfactual generation methods, including training-based ones. Code will be made available upon acceptance.

Spoofing Wireless Sensing with Metasurface-based Physical Deepfakes 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Wireless communication networks are expanding from mere information transfer to sensing: in the Integrated Sensing and Communication paradigm, existing infrastructure and signals are reused to capture physical information about the surroundings of devices, envisioned to inform high-stakes applications such as healthcare and autonomous vehicles. From a security perspective, wireless sensing inherently trusts physical observations of the radio environment. However, as in wireless communication, the open nature of radio propagation allows attackers to tamper with victim signals. In particular, attackers can use programmable metasurfaces to manipulate wireless channels and thereby mislead sensing systems. Yet, prior metasurface-based manipulations target only individual channel features such as variance or Doppler shifts. Thus, they require knowledge of the victim sensing system and produce implausible channels that are easy to detect. In this work, we show that metasurfaces enable physically plausible manipulation of wireless sensing that is independent of the features used by the victim sensing system. We validate the attack both in a controlled lab study on MIMO channels and with off-the-shelf Wi-Fi sensing. Our results fundamentally challenge the trustworthiness of wireless sensing.

Random Voronoi Networks 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Nearest-neighbor methods are local, simple, and interpretable, but their non-parametric form makes them memory-intensive, expensive at inference, and difficult to integrate into differentiable neural architectures. We introduce Random Voronoi Networks (RVNs), a family of structured three-layer MLPs that express nearest-neighbor geometry through sampled Voronoi comparisons. The first layer computes Random Voronoi Features (RVF) from affine pairwise separators between selected training samples, the second layer aggregates these comparisons into Voronoi cells or top-k neighborhood scores, and the readout layer produces classification or regression predictions. We show that, when all samples and pairwise comparisons are included, step RVNs recover exact Euclidean k-NN, while ReLU RVNs provide a violation-score realization for 1-NN that can be relaxed into a differentiable soft weighting. Subsampling yields approximate RVNs with explicit control over the number of cells and features. We study several unsupervised sampling methods that trade off random coverage, directional diversity, coordinate sparsity, and empirical feature variance. Empirically, RVFs produce a highly effective data encoding strategy, providing performance boosts to downstream methods ranging from linear to modern tabular deep learning models. Finally, we showcase that RVF provides an interpretable feature map due to its k-NN based construction and ties to pairwise local directions. These results position our Random Voronoi formulation as a bridge between nearest-neighbor rules, random features, and modern tabular models.

📝 22 pages, 5 figures, 6 tables

Deep learning models often struggle with sharp local optima and overfitting in limited-data regimes, and exhibit limited capacity for capturing high-order, complex probability distributions. Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models, by operating in exponentially large Hilbert spaces, can efficiently represent such distributions and provide favorable inductive biases for learning, offering a promising pathway to overcome these limitations. Bridging Transferability and Discriminability via Positive-Unlabeled Domain Adaptation for Building Damage Mapping Across Disaster Events 2026-11-25
Show abstract

Mapping the damage to buildings following natural and human-caused disasters is essential for disaster response and humanitarian assistance. However, differences in hazard types, geographic environments, building characteristics, and imaging conditions cause cross-event distribution shifts that limit the generalization of deep learning models to new disaster events, even for geospatial foundation models (GFMs) pretrained on extensive Earth observation data. Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) can mitigate these shifts, but adaptation remains challenging when damage-proportion and damage-appearance shifts coexist and interact.
