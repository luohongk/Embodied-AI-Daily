A Methodologically Rigorous Benchmark of Linear Regression,RNN, and LSTM for S&P 500 Forecasting:Identifying Sources of Performance Inflation in Financial DeepLearning 2026-11-20
Show abstract

This study examines one-day-ahead forecasting of the S&P 500 closing level using a 2006-2025 dataset that includes fundamental price data, macroeconomic indicators, and engineered technical features. We benchmark three model families, namely linear regression, vanilla recurrent neural networks (RNN), and long short-term memory networks (LSTM), within a unified preprocessing and chronological evaluation pipeline. A central contribution of this work is a methodological audit of common practices in the deep-learning-for-stock-prediction literature. We identify three sources of result inflation that frequently appear in published methodologies: wavelet-denoised evaluation targets, the absence of a naive persistence baseline, and best-of-N replicate reporting. We then re-evaluate all model families under a corrected protocol that controls for each. Performance is reported using root mean squared error, mean absolute percentage error, and the Pearson correlation coefficient, averaged across N = 30 independent replicates per configuration. We compare models on accuracy, stability across replicates, and architectural depth sensitivity, using the naive persistence forecast (predicting today's close as tomorrow's close) as a primary baseline against which all candidate models must demonstrate improvement.

Real-time neuromorphic audio and vision perception for autonomous robotic applications: Integration and experimental evaluation on a rover platform 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Neuromorphic sensing and computing offer a promising alternative to conventional deep learning pipelines for mobile robots, where perception must run continuously under tight latency and energy constraints. However, most neuromorphic solutions are still evaluated offline or on isolated benchmarks, and their integration into complete robotic systems remains largely unexplored. In this technical report, a four-wheel rover platform is presented that combines three real-time perception pipelines: a classical audio tagging pipeline based on Pretrained Audio Neural Networks (PANNs) running on an NVIDIA Jetson GPU, a neuromorphic audio pipeline in which a spiking neural network (SNN) is deployed on the SynSense Xylo Audio~2 chip, and a neuromorphic vision pipeline in which events from an event-based camera are processed by the SpikeYOLO spiking detector for traffic cone detection. On the Xylo chip, the whole processing chain---from the microphone signal to the classification of low- and high-pitched sounds---is performed on-chip, while the host only configures the device and reads the output neurons. All models are deployed as containerised services and integrated through ROS~2, and a configurable action dispatcher maps the detected classes to robot behaviours, closing the loop between perception and actuation. The platform is demonstrated in a proof-of-concept scenario in which the robot reacts to sound classes detected by both audio pipelines and detects traffic cones with the event camera, showing the feasibility of integrating neuromorphic hardware into a multimodal robotic platform operating in real time.

Multi-Task Self-Evolving Harnesses with the Agent as Its Own Optimizer 2026-11-20
Show abstract

A harness is the code around a language model agent that organizes prompts, calls tools, manages context, and controls execution. As models grow stronger, recent work has begun to let agents improve their own harnesses, a line of work known as self-evolving harnesses. In most existing methods, a separate proposer running on a human-designed harness modifies the solver's harness, and a separate harness is evolved for each benchmark. Real-world tasks come from many domains, so both the evolution and the evaluation of a harness should cover a diverse range of tasks. We propose a framework close to recursive self-improvement: the same frozen model, on the same version of the harness, first solves tasks as the solver and then, as the proposer, reads the complete run records and directly edits the harness that runs it. Each evolution batch draws tasks from five benchmarks in different domains. To measure generalization, training and held-out tasks are strictly separated, and we additionally evaluate on five out-of-distribution benchmarks not used in the first stage. The design borrows from deep learning training and has two stages, multi-task pretraining and continual training. Starting from a 49-line seed harness, the harnesses evolved by GLM-5.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol gain about 5 points on the in-distribution benchmarks. They are on par with Claude Code and Codex on these benchmarks, and the GPT-5.6 Sol harness is also on par with Codex on five out-of-distribution benchmarks. The weaker model of each family benefits less: GLM-5.3-Flash gains less than 2 points, and the harness evolved by GPT-5.6 Luna scores below the seed. Continued evolution on Claw-Eval in the second stage further improves GPT-5.6 Sol and GLM-5.3-Flash on its multimodal tasks. We also analyze where the gains come from and the mechanisms that emerged during evolution. Code: https://github.com/xqkkk/self-evolving-harness.

Redesigning Neural Networks to Not be Confidently Wrong even to Strong Adversarial Attacks 2026-11-20
📝 Code: https://codeberg.org/mwspratling/STEAL/
Show abstract

Early deep learning models attempted to simulate visual perception using a neural hierarchy that alternated between two types of layer: one modelled on cortical simple cells (that was designed to increase selectivity), and one modelled on cortical complex cells (that aimed to increase tolerance). We show that such a neural network (NN) architecture, when implemented with novel components that are better able to learn selective and tolerant representations, is capable of accurate and robust image classification and improved alignment between the behaviour of the model and the perceptual abilities of humans. Firstly, the proposed models are shallow, fewer than ten layer-pairs, which is more consistent with the rapid feedforward object categorisation performed by the biological visual system. Secondly, they are far less prone to producing classification predictions with high confidence for samples from unknown classes or for image consisting of pure noise, and hence, like humans are not easily fooled into perceiving objects that are not present in the image. Thirdly, for the proposed NNs adversarial samples cause predictions with low confidence, enabling them to be accurately identified. In contrast to humans, our networks still do not correctly classify adversarial samples. However, the behaviour of our networks is a significant step towards human-like behaviour compared to standard DNNs that produce highly confident, but incorrect, classification predictions for adversarial samples. The increased robustness to adversarial and unknown samples is achieved while maintaining competitive performance in terms of generalisation to novel samples from the training categories. This enables models of this type to be competitive with state-of-the-art DNNs across multiple different evaluation metrics and benchmark tasks.

MorphoHELM: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Evaluating Representations for Microscopy-Based Morphology Assays 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Microscopy images contain rich information about how cells respond to perturbations, making them essential to applications like drug screening. To quantify images, researchers often use representation extraction methods, and recent years have seen a proliferation of deep learning methods. While measuring the quality of these representations is essential, evaluation remains fragmented, with each proposed model evaluated on different tasks and datasets, using custom pipelines and metrics, making it difficult to fairly compare models. Here, we introduce MorphoHELM, a comprehensive open benchmark for evaluating feature extraction methods for Cell Painting, the most widely-used morphological profiling assay. MorphoHELM consolidates evaluation standards in the field, extends and corrects them to be more robust, and evaluates on the widest range of methods to date. We show that no existing model outperforms classic computer vision analytic strategies across all settings, which remain the strongest general use-case representations. All datasets, code, and evaluation tools are publicly available at https://github.com/microsoft/MorphoHELM.

Ultrasound time-harmonic elastography (THE) is a non-invasive imaging technique that measures soft-tissue stiffness. Relying on the same principle of time-harmonic shear wave excitation as established magnetic resonance elastography (MRE), THE offers full field-of-view stiffness mapping at short acquisition times and sub-second temporal resolution across organs. However, reconstruction of shear wave speed maps from acquired tissue wavefields remains challenging due to speckle noise and sparse spatial coverage of the lateral component. Deep learning methods have shown promising results for image reconstruction in transient-based ultrasound elastography modalities but remain unexplored in THE. In MRE, MF-ElastoNet, a 2D multifrequency neural network-based wave reconstruction method trained on synthetic physics-based wave data, has shown high generalizability across dynamic ranges and resolutions. MF-ElastoNet was successfully adapted to THE with in vivo liver testing. Compared with k-MDEV, the state-of-the-art wave inversion method for THE, Bland-Altman analysis showed a mean bias of -0.02 m/s (95% limits of agreement: -0.36 to 0.33 m/s).

SVarM: Linear Support Varifold Machines for Classification and Regression on Geometric Data 2026-11-20

Show abstract

Despite progress in the rapidly developing field of geometric deep learning, performing statistical analysis on geometric data--where each observation is a shape such as a curve, graph, or surface--remains challenging due to the non-Euclidean nature of shape spaces, which are defined as equivalence classes under invariance groups. Building machine learning frameworks that incorporate such invariances, notably to shape parametrization, is often crucial to ensure generalizability of the trained models to new observations. This work proposes \textit{SVarM} to exploit varifold representations of shapes as measures and their duality with test functions $h:\mathbb{R}^n \times S^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. Equivariant Deep Learning in Direct-Product Space for Electronic-Structure Hamiltonian Prediction 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Most existing equivariant architectures for Hamiltonian prediction propagate features in direct-sum spaces of irreducible spherical tensors, whereas the target Hamiltonian is naturally an operator block in a direct-product representation over local orbital and spin bases. This representation mismatch fragments contiguous Hamiltonian blocks, obscures direct manipulation of orbital couplings, and organizes features into irregular angular-momentum components that require numerous transformations and are poorly aligned with efficient hardware execution and memory management. To address both the representation mismatch and computational bottleneck, we propose DP-Learner, an equivariant learning framework that performs message passing directly on Hamiltonian-like operator blocks in the direct-product space. Theoretically, we propose Lie-generator-based directional embedding and invariantly weighted left--right products and contractions that preserve equivariance to SO(3), SU(2), and spatial inversions, without explicit Clebsch--Gordan decomposition or inverse reconstruction. Methodologically, we instantiate this principle as a Transformer architecture, in which Hamiltonian-like feature matrices or tensors evolve through equivariant operators in the direct-product space, while non-linear invariant scalar routing controls structured gating over matrix units, directional propagation, attention, and environment-embedded context interactions. We further construct MP-HAM-110K, a large-scale Hamiltonian dataset comprising more than 110,000 material structures covering 68 elements with both Hamiltonian and wavefunction data. Experiments on MP-HAM-110K and Materials-HAM-SOC demonstrate that DP-Learner dramatically improves Hamiltonian prediction accuracy and inference efficiency over the state-of-the-art method.

Fractal basin geometry and the limits to predictability and reproducibility in deep learning 2026-11-20
Show abstract

Fundamental limits to predictability are central to our understanding of many physical and computational systems. In deep learning, training outcomes are often difficult to predict and reproduce even when extrinsic sources of randomness are controlled, yet the underlying mechanism remains unclear. Here we propose riddled basin geometry as a unifying dynamical systems mechanism: every initialization leading to one solution lies arbitrarily close to initializations leading to different solutions, forming a fat fractal with structure at arbitrarily fine scales. Using a minimal neural network model, we analytically establish the classical route to riddling and derive a near-zero uncertainty exponent: even sharp increases in initialization precision yield only marginal gains in outcome predictability. In deep networks, intertwined training destinations and near-zero parameter-space exponents provide robust signatures consistent with riddling across the tested architectures, optimizers and training protocols, offering a common dynamical account of otherwise disparate empirical observations. We further introduce functional uncertainty exponents, extending these diagnostics of scale-insensitive irreproducibility from parameter space to the learned functions themselves. Together, our results support riddled basin geometry as a candidate organizing principle for irreproducibility in deep learning, with practical implications for optimization and the reliable deployment of artificial intelligence.

Beware of complexity bias: A hindrance to conceptual model generalization and interpretability 2026-11-20
Show abstract

In the field of rainfall-runoff modeling, lumped conceptual models have been shown to be powerful representations of catchment filtering functions. They demonstrate fairly strong predictive performance in gauged catchments and have the ability to learn important intermediate latent variables and flow paths which increases their value as interpretable models. During calibration, these conceptual models may fit streamflow just as well as deep learning models, however, their performance often sharply degrades after deployment, suggesting the presence of overfitting. We hypothesize that complexity (or flexibility) bias is one of the causes of such overfitting. Complexity bias can appear within conceptual models when the number of effective degrees of freedom is parameter or flow path dependent. Then, using real rainfall-runoff data from CAMELS-US, we investigate if overfitting can be reduced by adding regularization terms that prevent complexity bias from forming. Our results suggest that conceptual and hybrid modeling can be further improved if hydrologists become aware of this pervasive bias.

Free Decompression with Algebraic Spectral Curves 2026-11-20
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Oral)
Show abstract

Tools from random matrix theory have become central to deep learning theory, using spectral information to provide mechanisms for modeling generalization, robustness, scaling, and failure modes. While often capable of modeling empirical behavior, practical computations are limited by matrix size, often imposing a restriction to models that are too small to be realistic. This motivates the inference of properties of larger models from the behavior of smaller ones. Free decompression (FD) is a recently proposed method for extrapolating spectral information across matrix sizes, but its utility is currently limited by strong assumptions that preclude its implementation on more realistic machine learning (ML) models. We use algebraic spectral curve theory to provide a general FD methodology for spectral densities whose Stieltjes transform satisfies an algebraic relation, a modeling assumption that is more likely to hold in practice. This recasts FD as an evolution along spectral curves which can be readily integrated. Our framework enables the expansion of spectral densities that have multiple or multi-modal bulks, that exist at multiple scales, and that contain atoms, all characteristic of real-world data and popular ML models. We demonstrate the efficacy of our framework on models of interest in modern ML, including Hessian and activation matrices associated with neural networks and large-scale diffusion models.

A Scale-Adaptive Framework for Joint Spatiotemporal Super-Resolution with Diffusion Models 2026-11-19
Show abstract

Deep-learning video super-resolution has progressed rapidly, but climate applications typically super-resolve (increase resolution) either space or time, and joint spatiotemporal models are often designed for a single pair of super-resolution (SR) factors (upscaling spatial and temporal ratio between the low-resolution sequence and the high-resolution sequence), limiting transfer across spatial resolutions and temporal cadences (frame rates). We present a scale-adaptive framework that reuses the same architecture across factors by decomposing spatiotemporal SR into a deterministic prediction of the conditional mean, with attention, and a residual conditional diffusion model, with an optional mass-conservation (same precipitation amount in inputs and outputs) transform to preserve aggregated totals. Assuming that larger SR factors primarily increase underdetermination (hence required context and residual uncertainty) rather than changing the conditional-mean structure, scale adaptivity is achieved by retuning three factor-dependent hyperparameters before retraining: the diffusion noise schedule amplitude beta (larger for larger factors to increase diversity), the temporal context length L (set to maintain comparable attention horizons across cadences) and optionally a third, the mass-conservation function f (tapered to limit the amplification of extremes for large factors). Demonstrated on reanalysis precipitation over France (Comephore), the same architecture spans super-resolution factors from 1 to 25 in space and 1 to 6 in time, yielding a reusable architecture and tuning recipe for joint spatiotemporal super-resolution across scales.
