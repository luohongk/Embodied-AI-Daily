Structured Visual Landscape: Generating Preferred Representations in Biological and Artificial Neural Networks 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Understanding how neurons respond to visual stimulus is an important question in both deep learning and neuroscience. It has significant implications for enhancing the interpretability of black-box artificial neural networks and for understanding the visual representation in the human brain. We propose a structured visual representations landscape and design an activation-based prior that allows us to effectively regularize the landscape with activations from brain regions or neural network units. Our model Vis-Lens integrates a variational autoencoder and a diffusion model into an image generation framework. It enables the generation of natural, realistic preferred images by directly modifying the activation-regularized latent codes without per-image iterative optimization. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework on both artificial neural networks and human visual cortex using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data. Our framework improves activation modulation and image realism compared with a recent state-of-the-art method for generating preferred visual representations of these neural targets.

Nonparametric Intensity Estimation for Covariate-Driven Poisson Processes Using Deep Learning 2026-11-02
📝 59 pages, 13figures
Show abstract

We consider nonparametric maximum likelihood estimation of the intensity function of a Poisson process, allowing the intensity to depend on observed covariates. Such models describe how event rates vary with external conditions, including conditions under which the intensity becomes arbitrarily small or vanishes. However, risk analysis based on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence typically requires the true intensity to be bounded away from zero, a restrictive assumption in applications. We develop a unified approach that avoids this assumption and derive an oracle inequality for the covariate-averaged Hellinger distance between the true intensity and the nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator (NPMLE). As an application of the oracle inequality, we establish convergence rates for NPMLEs based on deep neural networks (DNNs). Our results show that, as in nonparametric regression, the ability of DNNs to capture underlying structure leads to improved convergence rates. Specifically, DNN-based NPMLEs mitigate the curse of dimensionality when the true intensity has a compositional structure and automatically adapt to a low-dimensional Riemannian manifold structure of the event space. For intensity functions with a compositional structure, DNN-based NPMLEs achieve nearly minimax-optimal convergence rates without explicitly incorporating this structure into the network architecture. Simulations demonstrate that DNN-based estimators outperform kernel estimators in most settings, while manifold kernels remain competitive in some designs. Finally, we analyze stimulus-evoked neural spiking using data from the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding Neuropixels project. DNN-based NPMLEs provide better out-of-sample performance than a conditional kernel estimator, as evaluated by held-out log-likelihood.

EmbryoProfiler: A Visual Clinical Decision Support System for IVF 2026-11-02
Show abstract

In-vitro fertilization (IVF) has become standard practice to address infertility, which affects more than one in ten couples in the US. However, current protocols yield relatively low success rates of about 20% per treatment cycle. A critical but complex and time-consuming step is the grading and selection of embryos for implantation. Although incubators with time-lapse microscopy have enabled computational analysis of embryo development, existing automated approaches either require extensive manual annotations or use opaque deep learning models that are hard for clinicians to validate and trust. We present EmbryoProfiler, a visual analytics system collaboratively developed with embryologists, biologists, and machine learning researchers to support clinicians in visually assessing embryo viability from time-lapse microscopy imagery. Our system incorporates a deep learning pipeline that automatically annotates microscopy images and extracts clinically interpretable features relevant for embryo grading. Our contributions include: (1) a semi-automatic, visualization-based workflow that guides clinicians through fertilization assessment, developmental timing evaluation, morphological inspection, and comparative analysis of embryos; (2) innovative interactive visualizations, such as cell-shape plots, designed to facilitate efficient analysis of morphological and developmental characteristics; and (3) an integrated, explainable machine learning classifier offering transparent, clinically-informed embryo viability scoring to predict live birth outcomes. Quantitative evaluation of our classifier and qualitative case studies conducted with practitioners demonstrate that EmbryoProfiler enables clinicians to make better-informed embryo selection decisions, potentially leading to improved clinical outcomes in IVF treatments.

Autonomous mobility is essential for sustained lunar surface operations, as communication delays and line-of-sight occlusions limit continuous teleoperation. Although recent traversability estimation methods increasingly rely on deep learning, the domain gap between terrestrial analogue environments and real lunar terrain can limit their generalisation, and real lunar traversability labels are scarce, making fully supervised training difficult. To address these challenges, we build a weakly supervised framework trained on sparse pairwise annotations and study whether augmenting visual features with depth-derived geometric information: depth, surface normals, and combined geometric descriptors. Temporal Island Sparse Autoencoders for Interpreting Clinical Time-Series Models 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Deep learning models for electronic health records achieve strong predictive performance but provide little insight into what clinical concepts they represent internally. Post-hoc attribution methods produce per-cell importance scores that lack temporal coherence, cross-variable structure, and reusability across patients. We introduce the Temporal Island Sparse Autoencoder (TI-SAE), which learns a backbone-specific, patient-shared clinical concept dictionary from a frozen predictive model's representations. Each dictionary entry (latent) is defined by learned temporal Gaussian islands (specifying when the concept is active) and a sparse variable-composition vector (specifying which variables are involved). TI-SAE produces structured clinical concepts that go beyond transient attribution heatmaps while achieving strong performance on standard faithfulness benchmarks.

Empirical Ciphertext Distinguishability Testing of KEMs and Hybrid Encryption via Deep Learning 2026-11-02
Show abstract

We model indistinguishability under chosen-plaintext attack (IND-CPA) games as binary classification tasks for deep neural networks (DNNs). We apply this methodology to the public-key encryption (PKE) schemes used to construct PQC key encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs) such as ML-KEM and HQC, and to cascaded symmetric algorithms, demonstrating methodological generality. Moreover, we present a novel extension of this approach to empirical distinguishability testing of hybrid KEMs. For IND-CPA-secure schemes and their combinations, no DNN achieves accuracy significantly different from random guessing (measured via Bonferroni-corrected binomial tests), consistent with theoretical security guarantees. For insecure schemes, such as plain RSA, AES-CTR and AES-CBC with IV or counter reuse, or RC4, the DNNs classify the ciphertexts better than random guessing. Thus, this framework is shown to serve as a practical, scheme-agnostic tool for flagging distinguishable ciphertexts in both individual and composed constructions.

End-to-End NTT on FPGA for FHE Acceleration 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) enables privacy-preserving computation on encrypted data without decryption. Despite its strong security guarantees, FHE incurs substantial computational and memory overhead. In FHE-based deep learning inference, on average 63% of the CPU time is spent on the number theoretic transform (NTT) and its inverse (INTT), which reduce the complexity of polynomial multiplication from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N \log N)$. Prior FPGA works follow two approaches: accelerating entire FHE schemes, which yields significant speedups but ties them to specific parameters and algorithms, or accelerating only the NTT, which applies across schemes, algorithms, and libraries. Most FPGA NTT designs require interconnects that grow with parallelism and utilize significant resources, which complicates routing on multi-die FPGAs. Additionally, to the best of our knowledge, end-to-end NTT acceleration within an FHE context has so far been left unexplored. We design a fully unrolled, delay-commutator streaming NTT/INTT kernel, and floorplan it across the three dies of an AMD Alveo V80. At 400 MHz, the kernel transforms a polynomial of $2^{16}$ coefficients every 5.12 $μ$s, the highest throughput among FPGA designs at this size. Offloaded end to end, it is up to 22x faster than the CPU per transform. We integrate the kernel into the Lattigo FHE library and evaluate it end to end. With 128-bit secure parameters, it accelerates core FHE operations, such as key switching and bootstrapping, by 1.8-2.2x, and neural network inference by 1.8-2.1x.

PaddleMaterials: An End-to-End Deep Learning Toolkit for Materials Science 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Machine learning supports materials design, atomistic simulation and experimental characterization, yet models and workflows remain fragmented. We present PaddleMaterials, an open-source framework integrating heterogeneous materials-learning tasks while preserving task-specific representations and scientific semantics. It unifies data processing, training, evaluation, pretrained-model management and workflows across six task classes: property prediction, structure generation, machine-learning interatomic potentials, electronic-structure fields, spectrum elucidation and microscopy image processing. Task interfaces record physical quantities, units, aggregation conventions and references, making cross-task composition verifiable. A shared compiler backend enables switching from dynamic-graph to compiled execution through one setting, using graph optimization, operator fusion and hardware-targeted code generation without changing model definitions. Across 65 model instances in 11 groups, compiled execution accelerated every instance in eight groups, with group median speedups of 11%-224% and group maximum speedups of 11%-336%. Case studies on LiMnO2 and toluene link structure generation or spectrum elucidation, interatomic-potential relaxation, property prediction and electron-density prediction, using same-geometry density functional theory (DFT) calculations as references. For LiMnO2, predicted formation energies for generated and relaxed structures differed from same-geometry PBE+U references by at most 7 meV per atom, while the computational cost of model steps was less than one-twentieth of a DFT single-point calculation. PaddleMaterials extends unified materials workflows beyond conventional property, potential and structure-generation tasks while supporting verifiable cross-task operations and compiled execution.

Networked AI: Architecture, Theory, and Applications 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly large-scale deep learning, have been driven by monolithic or offline training on massive curated datasets. While successful, these approaches face structural limitations: escalating data and computational demands, limited online adaptability, and a lack of system-level intelligence in open, non-stationary environments. This paper presents Networked AI - a networked intelligent system composed of interacting, heterogeneous, and continuously evolving AI agents. We establish a hierarchical layered architecture comprising an Agent Layer for heterogeneous capability construction, an Interaction Layer for signal-processing-guided online data extraction, and a Regulation Layer for stability-constrained collective evolution. Application frontiers are discussed for IoT spaces, embodied, spatial, and industrial intelligences, as well as the cellular infrastructure and beyond. Key open challenges are identified towards realizing locally autonomous, collectively intelligent, continuously evolving, globally regulated Networked AI.

The ability to estimate invertebrate biomass using only images could help scaling up quantitative biodiversity monitoring efforts. Computer vision-based methods have the potential to omit the manual, time-consuming, and destructive process of dry weighing specimens. We present two approaches for dry mass estimation that do not require additional manual effort apart from imaging the specimens: fitting a linear model with novel predictors, automatically calculated by an imaging device, and training a family of end-to-end deep neural networks for the task, using single-view, multi-view, and metadata-aware architectures. Leakage-Aware Encrypted Semantic Communication with Key-Conditioned Signal Perturbation 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted for presentation at IEEE GLOBECOM 2026
Show abstract

Encrypted semantic communication systems protect transmitted messages by inserting key-conditioned modules into a deep-learning semantic communication pipeline, but existing designs rely on a fixed adversarial weight and operate mainly at the semantic-feature level. We propose two changes to the encrypted semantic communication system (ESCS): a key-conditioned signal-domain perturbation layer, called learned adversarial perturbation injection (LAPI), and an information noise-contrastive estimation (InfoNCE)-guided adaptive training rule that {adds an adaptive leakage penalty alongside the fixed adversarial objective} using an InfoNCE-based proxy for mutual information leakage. Learning Steadily: Accumulating Relative Point Margin Scores for Face Image Quality Assessment 2026-11-02
📝 Accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Biometrics, Behavior, and Identity Science
Show abstract

Face Image Quality Assessment determines the suitability of captured face images for automated face recognition (FR), a critical capability for reliable biometric systems. Existing state-of-the-art FR-integrated FIQA methods suffer from temporal instability: as the feature space evolves during training, single-epoch quality estimates fluctuate, creating a moving target that undermines reliable quality prediction. We introduce CARPM-FIQA, a stabilization strategy for FR-integrated FIQA that accumulates relative point margin measurements, the ratio between intra-class compactness and inter-class separation, across the entire training trajectory rather than relying on single-epoch estimates. This cumulative averaging approach provides theoretically grounded advantages: reduced variance in quality estimates, improved mean squared error, and enhanced ranking stability with convergence guarantees as training progresses. Through controlled experiments on the SynFIQA dataset with labeled quality groups, we demonstrate that cumulative averaging achieves superior discriminative ability, and ablation studies across different training configurations confirm consistent improvements. Evaluated against twelve FIQA methods on eight challenging benchmarks with four FR models at two FMR thresholds, CARPM-FIQA places 4th (CARPM-FIQA(L)) and 6th (CARPM-FIQA(S)) of 17 compared methods by pAUC-EDC and AUC-EDC averaged across FR models and, after per-benchmark normalization, across benchmarks, staying within a few percent of the best method's normalized average for every FR model, providing a principled solution to training instability while maintaining the performance benefits of FR integration. More broadly, our work demonstrates that temporal aggregation strategies can stabilize training objectives in deep learning systems where target values inherently fluctuate due to evolving feature representations.

Show abstract

Effective polyp segmentation in colonoscopy images is crucial for early detection of colorectal cancer, a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide. While current deep learning models perform well on traditional Dice benchmarks, border segmentation remains difficult. Most methods use boundary information only as an additional loss signal during training, which prevents the network from explicitly learning border-guided feature selection or mask refinement. This paper introduces BGD-SF PolySegNet, a boundary-guided encoder-decoder architecture that controls three phases of the segmentation pipeline: dynamic multi-scale context aggregation, selective skip-feature fusion, and final mask refinement. The architecture comprises a pretrained EfficientNet-B4 backbone and three proposed modules: Boundary-Guided Dynamic Cascaded Multi-Scale Context Aggregation (BGD-CMSCA), Boundary-Guided Selective Attention Fusion (BG-SAGF), and a Multi-Level Boundary Guidance Head (MBGH). On the internal test set, BGD-SF achieves a Dice of 0.887, IoU of 0.839, mask-derived Boundary F1 of 0.531, HD95 of 30.32, and ASSD of 18.10, improving Boundary F1, HD95, and ASSD by 15.7%, 27.5%, and 34.6% over the strongest CNN baselines. Deep learning models have emerged as the standard computational tool for a wide range of applications in genomics. Yet, uncertainty quantification (UQ) --- and more specifically, the reliability of different uncertainty estimates in this domain --- has received little systematic attention. This work presents an empirical analysis of UQ in deep learning models, focusing on genomics applications. In a series of experiments, we contrast Deep Ensembles, Bayesian Neural Networks, and Monte Carlo-dropout methods. We assess their ability to quantify uncertainty in different scenarios, accounting for common dataset characteristics in two genomic application areas and modalities: sequence-to-activity models and single-cell expression analysis. Structured channel pruning for convolutional neural networks (CNNs) plays an important role in pattern recognition and deep learning. In the emerging paradigm of holistic AI-orchestrated software maintenance and evolution, large-scale app stores continuously generate exponentially vast Software Change Requests (SCRs) streams through user feedbacks. These streams enable intelligent software evolution. Automating SCR extraction and classification is crucial with rapid growth of Mobile Apps (MA). However, significant challenges exist such as scare large-scale datasets, inconsistent classification schemes, limited data sharing and insufficient research. This study presents a first and thorough systematic survey of SCR identification from MA Reviews (MAR). In particular, we investigated how Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) enhanced identification and classification of SCR. This study has found pertinent datasets varying sizes from 5,000 to more than 18 million reviews. The key findings highlight the dominant use of Random Forest and Support Vector Machines, limited adoption of deep learning techniques, inconsistent F1-score results ranging from 0.08 to 1 and partial automation in existing SCR tools such as SURF and ChangeAdvisor. Finally, we have performed a synthesis of the studies to presents a comparative taxonomy of SCR methodologies, identify research gaps and annotation frameworks. We contend that classification schemes, shared datasets and annotation frameworks are essential for advancing SCRs and thereby improving the maintenance of mobile applications.

SATFRONT: Detecting Atmospheric Fronts with Satellite Data using Machine Learning 2026-10-30

Atmospheric fronts are associated with much of the high-impact weather in the mid-latitudes, yet their identification and classification remain largely manual and subjective. Existing deep learning approaches rely primarily on gridded reanalysis or forecast data, limiting their applicability to satellite observations available for time-critical applications. We introduce SATFRONT, a machine learning framework for detecting cold, warm, and occluded fronts over Europe from geostationary satellite observations, with reanalysis data as an optional additional input. The model is trained on frontal analyses from five European national meteorological services and evaluated against UK Met Office analyses for 2024. Using satellite observations alone, SATFRONT achieves a Critical Success Index (CSI) of 0.428 at a 150 km tolerance, within the range of agreement between analyses from different meteorological services (0.277-0.501) and higher than that of an operational rule-based front detector that uses satellite data (0.133). Adding ERA5 reanalysis increases the CSI to 0.591. ERA5 substantially improves the placement of individual fronts, although its effect on their annual spatial distribution varies by front type. These results demonstrate that cold, warm, and occluded fronts can be detected directly from geostationary satellite observations, providing a basis for automated frontal analyses at the high temporal frequency required for time-critical applications.
