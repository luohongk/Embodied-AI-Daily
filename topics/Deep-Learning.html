SATFRONT: Detecting Atmospheric Fronts with Satellite Data using Machine Learning 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Atmospheric fronts are associated with much of the high-impact weather in the mid-latitudes, yet their identification and classification remain largely manual and subjective. Existing deep learning approaches rely primarily on gridded reanalysis or forecast data, limiting their applicability to satellite observations available for time-critical applications. We introduce SATFRONT, a machine learning framework for detecting cold, warm, and occluded fronts over Europe from geostationary satellite observations, with reanalysis data as an optional additional input. The model is trained on frontal analyses from five European national meteorological services and evaluated against UK Met Office analyses for 2024. Using satellite observations alone, SATFRONT achieves a Critical Success Index (CSI) of 0.428 at a 150 km tolerance, within the range of agreement between analyses from different meteorological services (0.277-0.501) and higher than that of an operational rule-based front detector that uses satellite data (0.133). Adding ERA5 reanalysis increases the CSI to 0.591. ERA5 substantially improves the placement of individual fronts, although its effect on their annual spatial distribution varies by front type. These results demonstrate that cold, warm, and occluded fronts can be detected directly from geostationary satellite observations, providing a basis for automated frontal analyses at the high temporal frequency required for time-critical applications.

Integrating AI for Human-Centric Breast Cancer Diagnostics: A Multi-Scale and Multi-View Swin Transformer Framework 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Despite advancements in Computer-Aided Diagnosis (CAD) systems, breast cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths among women worldwide. Recent breakthroughs in Artificial Intelligence (AI) have shown significant promise in development of advanced Deep Learning (DL) architectures for breast cancer diagnosis through mammography. In this context, the paper focuses on the integration of AI within a Human-Centric workflow to enhance breast cancer diagnostics. Key challenges are, however, largely overlooked such as reliance on detailed tumor annotations and susceptibility to missing views, particularly during test time. To address these issues, we propose a hybrid, multi-scale and multi-view Swin Transformer-based framework (MSMV-Swin) that enhances diagnostic robustness and accuracy. The proposed MSMV-Swin framework is designed to work as a decision-support tool, helping radiologists analyze multi-view mammograms more effectively. More specifically, the MSMV-Swin framework leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to isolate the breast lobe, reducing background noise and enabling comprehensive feature extraction. The multi-scale nature of the proposed MSMV-Swin framework accounts for tumor-specific regions as well as the spatial characteristics of tissues surrounding the tumor, capturing both localized and contextual information. The integration of contextual and localized data ensures that MSMV-Swin's outputs align with the way radiologists interpret mammograms, fostering better human-AI interaction and trust. A hybrid fusion structure is then designed to ensure robustness against missing views, a common occurrence in clinical practice when only a single mammogram view is available.

Binary Sparse Codes: Where are we on Energy Efficient Representations? 2026-10-30
📝 38 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

Biological neural systems achieve impressive computational capabilities under tight metabolic constraints, relying on sparse cortical representations that minimise total neural activity. This combination of efficiency and representational power has motivated growing interest in sparse coding as a computational strategy for artificial systems. Artificial models designed to produce binary sparse codes span three broad paradigms, theoretical methods grounded in efficient coding, deep learning methods trained via backpropagation, and biologically plausible methods that learn through local Hebbian rules, each yielding codes whose quality depends not only on achieving low activation density but also on preserving the informational structure of the input. The latter property, however, lacks an agreed-upon quantitative definition, leaving the field without a principled basis for comparing methods. We propose a set of metrics that formalise information preservation as similarity preservation, measuring how faithfully a binary sparse code reproduces the relational geometry of the input space, and apply this framework to compare four representative methods under matched conditions. The results reveal a consistent trade-off in which models that achieve high information preservation do so at the cost of sparsity, while models approaching the uniformity of an optimal random sparse code tend to sacrifice it. Competitive Sparse Codes, a biologically plausible approach, is the only architecture to occupy a region of near-maximal sparsity while retaining meaningful information preservation. These findings frame the simultaneous satisfaction of both factors as an open challenge for the field and provide a reusable evaluation framework for measuring progress toward it.

Iterated Integrals as Lexical Fingerprints: Path Signature Embeddings for Lexical Similarity 2026-10-30
📝 accepted in NeurIPS 2026 LP4FM Workshop
Show abstract

While sentence embeddings are primarily designed for semantic generalization, their latent geometry implicitly encodes surface-level lexical properties, a relationship that remains poorly understood. Shallow pooling methods discard word order, while deep Transformer models obscure these lexical alignments behind computational complexity and uninterpretable spaces. We propose constructing sentence embeddings via the path signature of Word2Vec trajectories, leveraging the iterated integrals of rough path theory to create representations that explicitly capture sequential and lexical structure in a geometrically tractable manner. Through extensive ablations spanning Word2Vec dimensions, signature depths, and PCA compression, we correlate embedding distances against five lexical string metrics: Levenshtein, Jaro-Winkler, word Jaccard, phoneme Levenshtein, and character TF-IDF. We reveal that while average pooling almost entirely fails to capture character-level edit distances, path signatures strongly align with them, with depth 2 signatures optimally balancing character and word-level sensitivities. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the critical lexical variance is concentrated in a highly compact PCA subspace, and unlike pooling, signatures structurally preserve and quantify word order. These findings establish path signatures not as semantic generalizers, but as rigorous mathematical lenses that explicitly encode discrete lexical structures and word order, offering an interpretable alternative to opaque deep learning for lexical tracking.

Sample-wise Constrained Learning via a Sequential Penalty Approach with Applications in Image Processing 2026-10-30
📝 https://openreview.net/forum?id=Xi1UWSFooI
Show abstract

In many learning tasks, certain requirements on the processing of individual data samples can arguably be formalized, in an intelligible way, as strict constraints of the underlying optimization problem, rather than by means of arbitrary penalties that often require heavy trial-and-error tuning. In this paper we show that, in these scenarios, learning can be carried out exploiting a sequential penalty method that allows to explicitly deal with constraints. For the proposed algorithm we show that, under classical assumptions and in the considered learning scenario, penalty subproblems can provably be solved in finite time with suitable guarantees of approximate stationarity in expectation; we also show that, when the latter condition is guaranteed for the subproblems, the overall sequential scheme possesses almost sure convergence properties to KKT-stationary points of (general) constrained problems. The results of experiments on sandbox and real-world image processing tasks show that the method is indeed viable to be used in practical deep learning scenarios.

Exploring Bad Minima in Transfer Learning 2026-10-30
📝 57 pages, 13 figures, 3 tables. MPhil thesis, University of Cambridge, 2022. Supervised by Ferenc Huszár
Show abstract

Transfer learning is a highly successful learning paradigm in deep learning which leverages knowledge from one task and applies it to another. Despite its success, the necessary conditions for knowledge transfer are unclear and require further research. In this dissertation, our aim is to understand transferability a little better by answering the following question: Does good generalization on a source task necessarily imply transferability to a related target task? We hypothesize the answer is negative, and attempt to find counterexamples via adversarial attacks on the transfer learning procedure. Over three types of adversarial attacks, our empirical results show a strong relationship between generalization on the source task and transferability. These results do not rule out generalization on the source task as a sufficient condition for successful transfer.

Backpropagation-free Deep Learning for Time-Series Classification 2026-10-30
📝 16 pages, 12 figures
Show abstract

Deep neural networks for time series classification (TSC) are trained almost exclusively by error backpropagation, which imposes update locking and the weight-transport problem. Backpropagation-free learning rules such as Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA), Direct Random Target Projection (DRTP), and the diffusion-inspired NoProp relax these constraints but have been evaluated almost exclusively on 2D image benchmarks. We adapt these three learners to univariate TSC with 1D convolutional architectures and present the first systematic study of them across 112 datasets from the UCR time series archive, with one hyperparameter-optimized configuration per method. The optimized learners reach mean F1 scores of 0.76 to 0.79, below ROCKET (0.83) and InceptionTime (0.84). We propose an analysis framework that relates the per-dataset gap to InceptionTime to dataset properties and tests convolutional feature learning by replacing the flatten readout with global average pooling, measured as a difference-in-differences (DD) against a backpropagation control on the identical architecture. Intra-class temporal variability is the dataset property most consistently associated with the gap for all three learners (partial Spearman $ρ$ from $0.28$ to $0.40$). Pooling harms DFA and DRTP more than backpropagation (DD $-0.025$ and $-0.055$), consistent with near-random convolutional features, whereas NoProp tolerates pooling as backpropagation does (DD $+0.013$). Relative to backpropagation on the same architectures, DRTP reduces training floating-point operations (FLOPs) by 33% and trains faster only on series longer than about 700 time steps, DFA yields no computational reduction, and NoProp reduces peak memory by 38% at the cost of 48% longer training.

Accurate and Auditable ECG Classification via Visibility Graph Neural Networks 2026-10-30
📝 43 pages, 10 figures, 21 tables, including Supplementary Information
Show abstract

Physicians read electrocardiograms (ECGs) from waveform shape and timing, yet accurate deep-learning classifiers rarely show which waveform regions drive their predictions. We introduce LYNX-ECG, a graph neural network that represents each heartbeat as a sparse visibility graph of timestamped turning points. On CODE-Test, LYNX-ECG reaches a macro F1 score of 0.889, outperforming five baselines including the ECGFounder foundation model, while its graphs need about 14% of the storage of graphs built from every sample. Because each node maps to a specific ECG time, we can delete clinically defined waveform regions and alter beat timing to audit predictions. For conduction disorders, the clinically expected region was the strongest evidence in more than 90% of correct predictions. Bradycardia and tachycardia calls follow heart rate with conduction intervals held fixed, and atrial fibrillation calls vanish when beat-to-beat variation is removed. Sparse visibility graphs thus pair accurate ECG classification with clinically grounded, testable explanations.

Beyond IID: How General Are Tabular Foundation Models, Really? 2026-10-30
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 Datasets and Benchmarks Track. v2: NeurIPS camera-ready version

Show abstract

Foundation models for predictive machine learning on tabular data have recently gained significant traction in academia and industry. Research communities across disciplines are increasingly evaluating tabular foundation models on diverse datasets and tasks. However, these task- and discipline-specific evaluations remain largely inaccessible to model researchers because benchmark software and evaluation protocols are fragmented. As a result, model researchers rely on standard benchmarks, which are mostly defined for tasks where tabular foundation models already excel. The most challenging scenarios are excluded, limiting meaningful progress in the field by focusing on marginal improvements on IID data rather than on broader, more demanding challenges. To overcome this, we introduce BeyondArena, the first unified holistic benchmark for tabular data that supports diverse task types (IID, temporal, grouped), across sample size and feature dimensionality scales, with diverse feature types (with text, with high cardinality) from a broad range of disciplines. To enable unified benchmarking beyond standard benchmarks, we introduce DataFoundry, a Python framework and metadata schema for curating tabular datasets for predictive machine learning. Our results across 11 models and 142 curated datasets show that current open-source tabular foundation models are competitive or excel on datasets up to 100k rows, while traditional tree-based and deep learning models still dominate on grouped or large data. BeyondArena guides model research for the most demanding challenges in tabular data, enabling progress towards truly foundational tabular models.

Show abstract

Satellite-derived bathymetry (SDB) is often proposed as a low-cost complement to multibeam surveys of ports, but its accuracy is seldom measured on an area the model has never seen. We test SDB in the turbid, shallow and tidal waters of the Abu Dhabi coast against 285,619 multibeam soundings from twelve surveys at six port areas, reduced to Lowest Astronomical Tide. The Stumpf ratio, the Lyzenga transform, a monotonic gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) and a U-Net ensemble were trained on 72 Sentinel-2 images and tested in transfer to an unseen site and with local control (about 20% of a site's soundings, spatial blocks beyond a 225 m buffer). The seabed could not be read beyond 11 m, and summer images were five to six times more turbid than winter ones. ICESat-2 ATL24 agreed with the surveys to 0.25 m after a depth-dependent offset was removed. A 60 m reconnaissance mosaic of 17,200 km2 of the UAE coast, calibrated cell by cell on ICESat-2 without any vessel survey, reached 1.42 m RMSE on held-out blocks over half of the product. Patch-Based Deep Kernel Learning for Data Assimilation and Bias Correction with Extremely Sparse Observations 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Data Assimilation (DA) reconstructs the state of a spatio-temporal system by combining sensor observations with the output of a numerical model. Since model states are high-dimensional while observations cover only a small fraction of the domain, DA is a challenging large-scale estimation problem, central to weather forecasting and oceanography. Classical variational methods such as 3D-Var rely on a hand-crafted background error covariance that assumes unbiased errors, and therefore cannot capture state-dependent correlations or systematic model biases. Moreover, their cost grows with the size of the domain. Deep learning (DL) methods can perform DA and bias correction (BC) non-linearly, but typically offer limited interpretability and no calibrated uncertainty estimates. We propose a hybrid framework that couples a convolutional neural network (CNN) with a learnable-kernel Gaussian Process (GP) to jointly perform DA and BC from extremely sparse observations. The model is trained end-to-end by maximizing the marginal log-likelihood, learns a data-driven covariance over the whole grid, and provides both the Maximum A Posteriori analysis and its uncertainty by design. A patch-based strategy reduces the $O(N^3)$ training cost of exact GPs over $N$ grid points to $O(N)$ for a fixed patch size. Inference remains exact and only requires solving a linear system whose $O(m^3)$ cost depends on the number of observations $m \ll N$, not on the domain. The learned covariance can also replace the hand-crafted one in 3D-Var, improving its accuracy without changing the variational formulation. Experiments on sea surface temperature and salinity, with both synthetic and real, extremely sparse in-situ observations, show that the framework outperforms 3D-Var, a classical GP and a competing DL model, while reducing inference time by more than 98% with respect to 3D-Var.

When Everyone Can Build a Model: Mechanistic Inference in the Age of AI 2026-10-30
📝 8 pages, 2 figures
Show abstract

Modern deep learning and AI-assisted code generation are lowering the technical barrier to building mathematical models of biological systems that reproduce complex observations. This lets researchers with domain expertise build semi-mechanistic, hybrid models constrained by prior knowledge and augmented with learned structure without extensive training in modelling. However, easier construction does not make interpretation easier: predictive success of such so-called grey-box models does not establish that an inferred mechanism corresponds to the one that generated the data, and the learned component can absorb errors in the mechanistic part. Many biological modelling problems are underdetermined, so several, possibly infinitely many, models may reproduce the same data equally well. This ambiguity enters at two modelling choices: which variables can affect others, and which response function describes the underlying mechanism. For the first choice, causal inference provides a formal framework stating which causal relationships and intervention effects are identifiable from the data and available knowledge. For the second, ensemble analyses quantify the uncertainty and the range of behaviours still allowed by models that fit equally well. Together, they turn a single good fit into an explicit account of what is supported, what remains open, and which experiment would decide.

A Lightweight Fiducial-Based Pipeline for 3D Hyperspectral Mapping of ex-vivo Lumpectomy Specimens 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) is a promising modality for intraoperative assessment of resection margins in Breast-Conserving Surgery (BCS), but its clinical translation requires aligning the inherently 2D spectral information onto the 3D shape of the excised tissue so that suspicious regions can be precisely localized for targeted follow-up. We present a fully automated, calibration-free pipeline that produces a 3D hyperspectral point cloud of an ex-vivo lumpectomy specimen from a set of consumer-camera RGB images and a single top-down HSI acquisition. The 3D geometry is reconstructed with a deep-learning Structure-from-Motion backbone, stabilized in a metric reference frame by a custom bundle adjustment that enforces consistency on the corners of four ArUco markers placed around the specimen. Evaluated on two ex-vivo lumpectomy specimens, the pipeline achieves a median 3D registration error below 1 mm and a 2D reprojection error below 0.02 mm, with a total per-specimen processing time under 4 minutes on accelerated hardware. These results support the feasibility of integrating HSI-guided spatial localization into intraoperative margin assessment workflows for breast-conserving surgery.

Fusion or Confusion? Multimodal Complexity Is Not All You Need 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Multimodal learning has become a prominent research area, with the potential of substantial performance gains by combining information across modalities. At the same time, model development has trended toward increasingly complex deep learning architectures, motivated by the assumption that multimodal-specific methods improve performance. We challenge this assumption through a large-scale empirical study by reimplementing 19 high-impact multimodal methods across nine diverse datasets with up to 23 modalities. Under standardized experimental conditions, including hyperparameter tuning, weight initialization, cross-validation, and statistical testing, increased multimodal complexity often yields confusion rather than effective fusion of data modalities. A Controlled Comparison of Deep Learning Architectures for Multi-Horizon Financial Forecasting: Evidence from 918 Experiments 2026-10-30
Show abstract

Reliable comparison of deep learning architectures for financial forecasting requires controlled evaluation that separates architectural effects from hyperparameter tuning, random initialization, forecast horizon, and asset characteristics. We present a five-stage benchmark using equal-budget Bayesian hyperparameter optimization, category-level configuration freezing, three-seed final training, held-out metric aggregation, and rank-based statistical validation. Nine architectures from Transformer, MLP/linear, convolutional, and recurrent families are evaluated with direct multi-step prediction from five OHLCV features across 12 assets (four cryptocurrency, four foreign exchange, four equity-index instruments), using chronological 70/15/15 splits of 30,000 hourly windows per asset at 4- and 24-hour horizons. The benchmark includes 918 runs (270 optimization trials, 648 final training runs). Across 24 asset-horizon points, ModernTCN has mean rank 1.333 and the lowest RMSE in 18 points (75.0%), followed by PatchTST (mean rank 2.000). Global ranking differences are strongly supported by an Iman-Davenport test (F(8,184)=101.36, p<1e-15), with 33 of 36 Holm-adjusted pairwise comparisons significant. Rankings are consistent across horizons for all 12 assets (rho>=0.683; Stouffer p=3.47e-10), while errors increase at the longer horizon. Architecture explains 99.90% of raw error variance and 68.33% after within-slot normalization excluding LSTM; seed variance contributes at most 0.04% (0.02% without LSTM). Mean directional accuracy is 50.08%. These findings provide a statistically validated comparison of nine architectures, identify ModernTCN and PatchTST as leading performers under the evaluated conditions, and offer a reproducible benchmark for multi-asset financial time-series forecasting.
