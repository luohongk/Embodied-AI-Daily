Rennala NSGD: Asynchronous Stochastic Optimization Beyond Euclidean Geometry 2026-10-28
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 111 pages, 2 algorithms, 3 tables
Show abstract

The empirical success of matrix-aware optimizers, such as Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) and Shampoo (arXiv:1802.09568), highlights the potential of non-Euclidean geometry for deep learning, including large language model (LLM) training. Despite this progress, the theoretical understanding of stochastic optimization in general normed spaces remains limited, particularly in distributed settings. We analyze minibatch stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for smooth nonconvex optimization with respect to an arbitrary norm on a finite-dimensional space. Using the framework of $κ$-regular normed spaces (arXiv:0809.0813), we derive high-probability oracle complexity bounds under a light-tail condition stated directly in the dual norm induced by the update norm. The bounds make the dependence on geometry explicit through the regularity constant $κ$ of the dual space. In the Euclidean case, our bound recovers the standard minibatch SGD rate up to logarithmic factors. For some non-Euclidean geometries and noise models, it is sharper than bounds obtained by converting a Euclidean noise assumption, reducing a linear dimension dependence to a logarithmic one. Building on this framework, we introduce Rennala NSGD, a semi-asynchronous distributed algorithm, and establish wall-clock time complexity guarantees under fixed, heterogeneous worker computation times. In the Euclidean case ($κ=1$), our bound matches the parameter dependence of classical Rennala SGD up to logarithmic factors.

Quantum-Inspired Pairwise Ranking Aggregation via Active LLM Feedback 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Ranking aggregation from multiple retrievers is a powerful yet under-explored technique in modern retrieval and RAG systems. We introduce LLM-IRA, the first extension of the quantum-inspired interactive ranking aggregation framework to document reranking. By leveraging large language models (LLMs) solely for pairwise judgments on high-disagreement document pairs, our method learns fusion weights in a principled, interactive way. The final ranking is a simple, ultra-fast pointwise linear fusion of scores, no full pairwise matrix or expensive listwise prompting is required. Experiments on the BEIR benchmark and TREC Deep Learning tracks with an open-weight instruction-tuned judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) show that LLM-IRA matches or numerically exceeds strong static fusion baselines on 5 of 6 evaluated benchmarks. We report two weight-update variants: momentum blending and a full accumulated-history refit, which together reach statistical significance (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p<0.05) over the best unsupervised aggregator on 4 of 6 benchmarks (DL19 under momentum; DL20, DBpedia, and NFCorpus under accumulated refit, which is the stronger of the two configurations overall). On the benchmarks we profiled (DL19, DL20), it already clears the best unsupervised baseline within as few as 5 LLM calls per query, well before exhausting its typical ~25--30 call budget. LLM-IRA also achieves cost-effectiveness comparable to state-of-the-art zero-shot LLM rerankers at an order of magnitude fewer LLM calls, and requires no training data or model fine-tuning.

REVBRAIN: Real-time Probabilistic EEG Forecasting Using Residual Vector Quantization 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Real-time forecasting of resting-state EEG signals is required by applications like brain-state-dependent stimulation, where computer generated actions are coupled with ongoing neural oscillations. Recently, the deep learning model WaveNet was proposed for the forecasting task. It shows superior accuracy compared to the prevailing method, the linear autoregressive (AR) model, and provides a built-in uncertainty measure due to its probabilistic modeling framework. However, inference using WaveNet is too slow for real-time use due to its sample-by-sample autoregressive operation: a single forecast requires evaluating the model tens or hundreds of times depending on the length of the prediction window. In this paper, we present REVBRAIN, a probabilistic deep learning method that produces forecasts in real time by generating the forecast over the whole prediction horizon at once. This is achieved by tokenizing the output space using residual vector quantization (RVQ) and generating forecasts over the items in the tokenized space. We tested REVBRAIN for forecasting 200 ms of EEG time series in the alpha-frequency band (8-13 Hz) and compared our results against WaveNet model. We found that REVBRAIN achieved real-time inference on a laptop GPU and an over $50\times$ speedup compared to WaveNet, while reaching superior prediction accuracy. By achieving real-time inference speed, REVBRAIN enables incorporating accurate and probabilistic EEG forecasting into BCI applications, opening up new ways for investigating the functioning of the human brain.

oRANS: Online optimisation of RANS machine learning models with embedded high-fidelity data generation 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Deep learning (DL) has shown promise for flow simulations, but progress is constrained by the scarcity of high-fidelity training data, which is costly to generate and limited to few flow conditions. Closures trained offline on such data tend to overfit and fail to generalise to new regimes. We introduce an online optimisation framework for DL-based Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) closures. Training data is generated by a simulation at direct numerical simulation (DNS) resolution embedded in a subdomain of the RANS domain. The RANS solution supplies boundary conditions to the embedded simulation, which provides the mean velocity and turbulence statistics used to update the closure, which thereby adapts to the target flow without precomputed datasets. The approach is developed on the stochastically forced Burgers equation and on turbulent channel flow at $Re_τ=180$, 270, 395 and 590 with embedded domain lengths $1\leq L_0/L\leq 8$, and is then applied to spanwise-rotating channel flow at $Ro_b=0.15$ and $0.5$, a flow in which the Coriolis force vanishes from the equations of a linear eddy-viscosity closure. Online-optimised RANS models outperform offline-trained and literature-calibrated closures with modest embedded subdomains, and in the rotating channel recover the rotation-induced asymmetry from non-rotating inflow turbulence and a rotation-blind closure. The learned coefficients are interpretable, recovering near-wall damping and, under rotation, the collapse of the eddy viscosity on the stabilised side. Performance degrades when boundary contamination dominates or domains are too short to capture low-wavenumber modes. This framework provides a route to data-adaptive machine learning closures without large precomputed training datasets.

Physics-Inspired Optimizers beyond the Edge of Stability: Period-Two Orbits and Delayed Instability 2026-10-28
📝 34 pages, 14 figures. v2: adds an analytical derivation of the symmetry-breaking bifurcation of the VR map, previously found numerically (new Proposition 8); clarifies the scope of the slow-sweep and noise approximations in Sec. 4; minor editorial corrections
Show abstract

Gradient descent with momentum can be viewed as a particle moving with friction in the loss landscape. In deep learning the learning rate is usually large, so training is a discrete dynamical system: in many full-batch experiments the sharpness grows to the optimizer's stability limit, the edge of stability (EoS), and training continues there. Physics-inspired optimizers change this motion; Velocity-Regularized Adam (VRAdam), for example, shrinks the step at high speed. We study kinetic maps, in which the step is the gradient of a kinetic energy of the momentum, and ask what they do beyond the EoS. (i) In a reduced model, a kinetic map creates a symmetric period-two orbit beyond the edge, strictly linearly stable if and only if the map's elasticity (the log-log slope of step length versus momentum) lies between 0 and 1; on it, training sits at an effective edge of stability. VRAdam's damper fails above twice the critical learning rate, where its kinetic energy is not convex; the Legendre dual of VRAdam's own Lagrangian is convex and passes at every learning rate. (ii) Because the sharpness rises gradually, the loss of stability is delayed, as in Neishtadt's theory of slowly swept bifurcations. In a network with a controlled sweep, the dynamics match this theory without fitted parameters, including the memory of where the sweep started and its erasure by noise. (iii) With momentum, reaching the oscillation also requires elasticity at large momentum: maps that bound the step have competing period-four and chaotic attractors already at beta1 = 0.9, while the cubic-Legendre map reached the oscillation from every sampled start up to beta1 = 0.99. Experiments on an exact quadratic problem, a full-batch MLP and ResNet-32 support the theory; on ResNet-18, much of the apparent benefit of the maps is reproduced by matching the effective learning rate.

Detection of trade in products derived from threatened species using machine learning and a smartphone application 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Illegal and unsustainable wildlife trade remains a major conservation challenge and is increasingly shifting to online marketplaces where monitoring is difficult. To address this gap, we used real-world images from online platforms, enforcement seizures, and market inspections to develop a deep-learning object detection framework to identify products derived from three highly traded species: elephant, pangolin, and tiger. Our dataset of 39,941 images included both wildlife products and visually similar non-wildlife items, reflecting real enforcement complexity. We used cross-entropy and asymmetric focal loss functions to train Faster Region-based convolutional neural network (Faster R-CNN) models and compared species-specific and multispecies training strategies. The best-performing model, trained with cross-entropy on all species combined, achieved an overall accuracy of 84.2%, with high performance for pangolin (93.6%) and tiger (90.3%) and moderate performance for elephant (71.1%). Although asymmetric focal loss increased recall, it substantially reduced precision. We deployed the optimized model in a prototype smartphone application, potentially usable in real-world settings for monitoring online images. With an independent test set, the application achieved 91.3% accuracy and an F-score of 0.86. These results demonstrate the potential of object detection models to support scalable, real-time monitoring of wildlife trade and enable the rapid triage of suspicious posts, regulatory compliance, and conservation decision-making.

Tracking the Discriminative Axis: Dual Prototypes for Test-Time OOD Detection Under Covariate Shift 2026-10-28
Show abstract

For reliable deployment of deep-learning systems, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is indispensable. In the real world, where test-time inputs often arrive as streaming mixtures of in-distribution (ID) and OOD samples under evolving covariate shifts, OOD samples are domain-constrained and bounded by the environment, and both ID and OOD are jointly affected by the same covariate factors. Existing methods typically assume a stationary ID distribution, but this assumption breaks down in such settings, leading to severe performance degradation. We empirically discover that, even under covariate shift, covariate-shifted ID (csID) and OOD (csOOD) samples remain separable along a discriminative axis in feature space. Building on this observation, we propose DART, a test-time, online OOD detection method that dynamically tracks dual prototypes -- one for ID and the other for OOD -- to recover the drifting discriminative axis, augmented with multi-layer fusion and flip correction for robustness. Extensive experiments on a wide range of challenging benchmarks, where all datasets are subjected to 15 common corruption types at severity level 5, demonstrate that our method significantly improves performance, yielding 15.32 percentage points (pp) AUROC gain and 49.15 pp FPR@95TPR reduction on ImageNet-C vs. Textures-C compared to established baselines. These results highlight the potential of the test-time discriminative axis tracking for dependable OOD detection in dynamically changing environments.

Rosemary: Efficient and Adaptable In-Storage Acceleration for Fully Homomorphic Encryption 2026-10-28
Show abstract

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables computation on encrypted data and is promising for privacy-preserving computing. However, FHE deployment remains constrained due to massive computational, memory, and data-movement overheads. Existing acceleration strategies often overlook the storage I/O bottleneck caused by the enormous size of FHE data in large-scale applications, as most prior works focus on small benchmarks that fit in main memory. Our studies show that state-of-the-art FHE systems spend 71\% to 85\% of their execution time moving data between storage and compute units rather than performing useful computation. To address the I/O bottleneck, we present Rosemary, a heterogeneous FHE acceleration system that incorporates a novel in-storage processing (ISP) architecture with compiler-level and algorithmic optimizations. Our key innovations include 1) an adaptable hardware-aware FHE engine inside a NAND flash-based solid-state drive (SSD) for efficient and cost-effective in-storage FHE processing, 2) a Throughput-Data-Aware Program Allocator (TDAP) that partitions FHE workloads into heterogeneous programs, and 3) a Compiler-Level Ciphertext-Aware Optimizer (CLCAO) that exploits ISP-friendly FHE kernels for low-cost ISP execution. Across a diverse set of FHE workloads, including encrypted deep learning inference and cloud database queries, Rosemary achieves geometric-mean speedups of $185.7\times$ over state-of-the-art CPU/GPU implementations and $3.7\times$ over prior FHE accelerators, while improving energy efficiency by up to $3.9\times$ and Energy-Delay-Area-Product (EDAP) by up to $16.5\times$.

Data Amount Heterogeneity Improves Performance in Federated Learning for Neuroimaging 2026-10-28
📝 SIPAIM 2026
Show abstract

Federated Learning (FL) allows for secure analysis of distributed data without data sharing. We and others have successfully applied FL to neuroimaging tasks, such as Alzheimer's disease classification from structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). FL trains a deep learning model locally at each site, encrypts the local model parameters and sends them to a federation controller that aggregates the local models under homomorphic encryption, the resulting community model is sent back to the sites for further training, and the process repeats for a number of rounds. FL often achieves learning performance (e.g., accuracy) close to centralized training, but no data leaves the sites and model parameters are protected through encryption and other measures. In this paper, we show that federations composed of sites with heterogeneous amounts of data (1 large site plus small sites) perform better than homogeneous (same data per site) federations, particularly in low data regimes, while reaching performance close to centralized training. Having a big site often results in faster convergence and higher final accuracy. Heterogeneous federations better exploit sites with small amounts of data, a common situation in large consortia such as ENIGMA.

Show abstract

Motivated by the observational incompleteness of intermediate-to-long-period Earth-size planets, we present TransitNet, a compact attention-augmented deep-learning framework for low-SNR transit blind searches. To enable realistic method development and objective threshold calibration under blind-search conditions, we develop a unified dataset construction, benchmarking, and threshold-selection framework. On recovery benchmarks constructed from unseen Kepler targets, TransitNet attains 95.2 percent accuracy in the challenging SNR range of 6 to 8 and outperforms both TLS and BLS, achieving ROC-AUC and PR-AP values of 0.974 and 0.982, respectively. In an injected Earth-size and sub-Earth-size transit recovery experiment, TransitNet achieves a recovery rate of 93.0 percent, substantially exceeding those of TLS (63.1 percent) and BLS (60.0 percent). Applied to real Kepler observations, the model successfully recovers all 34 selected confirmed Kepler planets, with a mean absolute transit midpoint error of 1.24 hours. The model combines a compact footprint of about 1.5 MB with high inference efficiency, yielding speed-ups of about 12 to 25 times relative to CPU-TLS and about 4 to 5 times relative to CPU-BLS. These results demonstrate that TransitNet provides an accurate, scalable, and computationally efficient framework for low-SNR transit blind searches in the tested regime and motivate its extension to longer-period Earth-size planet searches.

Measuring Deep Learning Performance - An Empirical Study of Performance Distributions Across Architectures and Tasks 2026-10-27
Show abstract

Context: Non-determinism in deep learning algorithm design and implementation leads to performance variation, meaning model performance is not a single value, but rather a distribution. These model performance distributions are underexplored despite their impact on robustness. Objective: We investigate the robustness of deep learning performance to sources of non-determinism, specifically focusing on how performance distributions differ across various architectures and tasks. Method: We conducted 186 experiments on image classification and time series forecasting architectures. Each experiment was run 100 times with different random seeds to generate performance distributions, resulting in 18,600 runs. Robustness was quantified using metrics for spread, symmetry, and tail risk. Results: Performance distributions are frequently non-Gaussian, particularly in time series forecasting. Model size does not systematically affect robustness -- larger image classification models show fewer outliers but not lower spread, while smaller time series models show lower spread but more extreme underperformers. Training duration does not scale linearly; early stopping effectively balances performance and robustness. Mean performance does not predict robustness - time series forecasting shows moderate correlation while image classification shows none. Time series models produce nearly three times more underperforming outliers than image classification models, indicating substantially higher tail risk. Conclusions: Tail risk poses serious concerns for Trustworthy AI in high-stakes applications. Models performing well on average may exhibit long tails and extreme outliers revealed only through distributional analysis. Mean performance alone should not guide model selection; assessment of spread, symmetry, and tail risk is essential for reliable model assessment where consistent performance is critical.

Deep learning for non-transvenous ICD screening: tailored neural network architectures, extended experiments, and explainability 2026-10-27
Show abstract

This paper studies two non-transvenous implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs), namely the Subcutaneous ICD (S-ICD) and the Extravascular ICD (EV-ICD). Both avoid the complications of transvenous leads but are prone to oversensing, which can trigger inappropriate shocks associated with increased mortality. Oversensing risk is predicted by the ratio of T- or P-wave amplitude to R-wave amplitude (T:R, P:R), and these ratios vary over a 24-hour period. Yet S-ICD screening uses a single 10-second electrocardiogram (ECG) segment, and the EV-ICD has no formal screening protocol whatsoever. An explainability analysis against interpretable classical peak labelling algorithms shows that AmpRatioNet-SE's increase in error on oversensing-prone segments is up to 14 times smaller than theirs. Segment-level predictions are aggregated into a 24-hour timeline, extending S-ICD screening beyond 10 seconds and offering a plausible EV-ICD screening pathway pending device-level validation.

EvoPS: Evolutionary Patch Selection for Whole Slide Image Analysis in Computational Pathology 2026-10-27

Show abstract

In computational pathology, the gigapixel scale of Whole-Slide Images (WSIs) necessitates their division into thousands of smaller patches. Analyzing these high-dimensional patch embeddings is computationally expensive and risks diluting key diagnostic signals with many uninformative patches. Existing patch selection methods often rely on random sampling or simple clustering heuristics and typically fail to explicitly manage the crucial trade-off between the number of selected patches and the accuracy of the resulting slide representation. To address this gap, we propose EvoPS (Evolutionary Patch Selection), a novel framework that formulates patch selection as a multi-objective optimization problem and leverages an evolutionary search to simultaneously minimize the number of selected patch embeddings and maximize the performance of a downstream similarity search task, generating a Pareto front of optimal trade-off solutions. We validated our framework across four major cancer cohorts from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) using five pretrained deep learning models to generate patch embeddings, including both supervised CNNs and large self-supervised foundation models. The results demonstrate that EvoPS can reduce the required number of training patch embeddings by over 90% while consistently maintaining or even improving the final classification F1-score compared to a baseline that uses all available patches' embeddings selected through a standard extraction pipeline. The EvoPS framework provides a robust and principled method for creating efficient, accurate, and interpretable WSI representations, empowering users to select an optimal balance between computational cost and diagnostic performance.

Earth Observation (EO) data are increasingly organized as spatio-temporal data cubes, while machine learning (ML) methods operate on tabular feature matrices or structured tensor inputs. Streaming Recurrent Memory 2026-10-26

Natural intelligence learns from a continuous stream of sensorimotor experience: data can arrive at high bandwidth, and behavior adapts to feedback as soon as it arrives, even when it is delayed. In contrast, most modern deep learning systems rely on stored datasets, replay buffers, and retained contexts through which credit is later assigned. At high data rates, storing and revisiting past experience can be impractical under tight memory and computational constraints. This paper studies recurrent memory under a streaming learning constraint: the learner learns from experience as soon as it arrives without storing raw experience. We ask whether a streaming learner can maintain a state that remains useful for future prediction and control from delayed feedback. We introduce Streaming Recurrent Memory (SRM), a recurrent memory designed for the streaming setting which preserves information that may matter later while remaining trainable with one-step backpropagation through time (BPTT). SRM provides a streaming-compatible recurrence that is designed to recall past information by content or time, track elapsed steps, and compose transformations whose outcome depends on the order in which inputs are observed. Across controlled memory tasks and streaming applications, SRM learns effectively with one-step BPTT, outperforming recurrent baselines in associative recall and online language modeling while achieving near-perfect success in associative T-Maze with delayed rewards. These results suggest that fixing how inputs are stored in the recurrent state, while learning how that state is read out, enables effective streaming learning without long-range backpropagation.
