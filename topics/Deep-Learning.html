Quaternion Approximate Networks for Enhanced Image Classification and Oriented Object Detection 2026-10-27
📝 IROS 2025 Oral, 8 pages, 5 figures
Show abstract

This paper introduces Quaternion Approximate Networks (QUAN), a novel deep learning framework that leverages quaternion algebra for rotation equivariant image classification and object detection. Unlike conventional quaternion neural networks attempting to operate entirely in the quaternion domain, QUAN approximates quaternion convolution through Hamilton product decomposition using real-valued operations. This approach preserves geometric properties while enabling efficient implementation with custom CUDA kernels. We introduce Independent Quaternion Batch Normalization (IQBN) for training stability and extend quaternion operations to spatial attention mechanisms. QUAN is evaluated on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), object detection (COCO, DOTA), and robotic perception tasks. In classification tasks, QUAN achieves higher accuracy with fewer parameters and faster convergence compared to existing convolution and quaternion-based models. For objection detection, QUAN demonstrates improved parameter efficiency and rotation handling over standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) while establishing the SOTA for quaternion CNNs in this downstream task. These results highlight its potential for deployment in resource-constrained robotic systems requiring rotation-aware perception and application in other domains. Comparing Temporal Deep Learning Architectures for Discrete Cross-User Myoelectric Control 2026-10-27
Show abstract

The recognition of dynamic gestures via discrete myoelectric control is becoming an attractive input modality for event-based human-machine interaction. While recent work suggests that discrete cross-user models are possible (i.e., no calibration data is required from the end user), few have evaluated techniques to optimize these systems and their generalizability to new randomly prompted data simulating real-world discrete control is relatively unknown. Using the 612-user EMG-EPN dataset, this work benchmarks five temporal deep-learning approaches, evaluates the tradeoff between performance and model complexity, and compares hand-crafted and machine-learned feature extraction techniques. The CNN-GRU achieved the best accuracy of 93% when tested on 184 previously unseen users. These models were then evaluated on a different, more realistic, unsegmented dataset, wherein users were randomly prompted to elicit discrete gestures during a continuous data collection. This dataset was recorded five years apart and in a separate country from the data used to train the original model. Results show that the cross-user results scale to this new dataset (accuracies of 96%) even though no feedback was given to users during the elicitation of gestures. These findings highlight the exciting potential of discrete zero-shot cross-user myoelectric control and offer valuable insights for the continued advancement of this promising technology.

The Geometry of Manifold Learning: Singular foliations via Deep Learning models 2026-10-27
📝 18 pages, 4 figures, 2 appendix
Show abstract

Deep learning algorithms have become widely used and successful across a wide range of tasks. A key challenge is determining whether these algorithms truly capture the intrinsic geometry of their training data in order to better understand what they learn from data. In this theoretical work, we explore a new geometric structure defined on the data space by a trained neural network through the Data Information Matrix (DIM), a variation of the Fisher Information Matrix. This new geometric structure is a foliation and the main focus of this work is to explore the non-smooth case of ReLU activation functions, where traditional geometric tools often fail. The foliation is defined through a distribution spanned by the columns of the DIM. We first prove that this distribution is non-smooth in the sense of distributions, rendering the existence of an associated foliation much more complex. To circumvent this issue, we introduce a smoothed version of this distribution that differs from the original only on a set of measure zero. Finally, we prove that this smoothed distribution induces a singular foliation on the data space, which is based on the data the network has been trained with. Our theoretical contributions provide a geometric framework to better understand how neural networks organize their input space in non-smooth settings, bridging differential geometry and deep learning theory.

CraftSPH: A composable framework for differentiable smoothed particle hydrodynamics implemented in PyTorch 2026-10-27
📝 The code is publicly available at https://github.com/GenMNL/CraftSPH 43 pages, 14 figures
Show abstract

Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) is well suited to large-deformation fluid problems, and the growing use of automatic differentiation in engineering applications has motivated interest in differentiable SPH solvers. In this study, a composable differentiable SPH framework, CraftSPH, has been developed. In CraftSPH, major computational operations are implemented as independent modules that users can combine to construct solvers for different purposes. This design supports high-accuracy discretization and both explicit and implicit computation within a common framework, while allowing individual components to be maintained and extended. Selected numerical operations also support automatic differentiation (AD) for physical-parameter estimation and potential coupling with deep learning models. These capabilities were demonstrated through forward and inverse analyses. Forward analyses using an implicit SPH scheme with high-accuracy discretization showed good agreement with analytical and reference solutions for Poiseuille flow and a rising bubble, while capturing the key experimental trends in two- and three-dimensional dam-break problems. In inverse analyses, explicit and implicit SPH schemes were used to estimate kinematic viscosity in Taylor-Green vortex flow and Reynolds number in lid-driven cavity flow. The results also showed that the choice of operations included in the computational graph affects the computed gradients. Together, these findings demonstrate that CraftSPH enables users to assemble problem-specific solvers for forward and inverse analyses within a common framework.

diff-soup: GPU-native Differentiable Simulation and Inference for Large-scale Stiff Dynamical Systems 2026-10-27
Show abstract

Learning physical models from spatiotemporal data requires simulating systems of partial differential equations (PDEs) and differentiating through their solution. Upon spatial discretization, these models give rise to stiff dynamical systems that require repeatedly solving large, sparse linear systems. Traditional scientific computing frameworks are largely CPU-based and require problem-specific tuning to accelerate, scale, and differentiate. In this work, we introduce diff-soup, a differentiable, sparse-oriented, unified PDE suite for GPU-native simulation and inference of large, stiff dynamical systems. Built in JAX, diff-soup provides 1) a fully differentiable design for problems with sparse structure, 2) scaling to large problems through the NVIDIA cuDSS sparse direct solver with model and data multi-GPU parallelism, and 3) end-to-end PDE-constrained optimization, from classical statistical inference to embedded deep learning. On stiff PDE benchmarks, diff-soup outperforms established CPU- and GPU-based frameworks by one to two orders of magnitude in both forward solves and gradients. We demonstrate both a classical Bayesian inference workflow, identifying constitutive relationships, and a modern deep learning workflow, training a neural field for the catalyst activity of a three-dimensional packed bed reactor. We provide diff-soup as an open-source repository to facilitate research in computational science and engineering.

Deep learning optimizers are optimization algorithms that enable deep neural networks to learn. The effectiveness of learning is highly dependent on the optimizer employed in the training process. Alongside the rapid advancement of deep learning, a wide range of optimizers with different approaches have been developed. This study aims to provide a review of various optimizers that have been proposed and received attention in the literature. From Stochastic gradient descent to the most recent ones such as Momentum, AdamW, Sophia, and Muon in chronological order, optimizers are examined individually, and their distinctive features are highlighted in the study. In addition, insights are offered into the open challenges encountered in the optimization of deep learning models. Thus, a comprehensive resource is provided both for understanding the current state of optimizers and for identifying potential areas of future development.

Retrospective macromolecular pool fraction mapping from conventional clinical MRI: a physics-guided self-supervised deep learning framework 2026-10-27
📝 38 pages, 24 figures
Show abstract

Macromolecular pool fraction (MPF) is a quantitative imaging biomarker sensitive to myelin integrity and other changes in tissue macromolecular content, but specialized acquisitions and reconstruction methods limit its use in large-scale studies. We present a physics-guided, self-supervised deep learning framework for retrospectively estimating MPF directly from conventional clinical brain MRI ($T_1$-weighted, $T_2$-weighted, and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery [FLAIR]) without paired quantitative reference scans. The framework embeds an analytical two-pool magnetization transfer signal model that related sequence-averaged radiofrequency transmit power to apparent longitudinal relaxation. To accommodate heterogeneous clinical protocols, the network conditions intermediate features on acquisition parameters via adaptive instance normalization. It was trained and evaluated on a multisite, multivendor, multiprotocol dataset of 7,705 MRI sessions (3 T). Across 1,237 test sessions, mean MPF was $0.182 \pm 0.007$ in white matter and $0.144 \pm 0.009$ in gray matter, with limited variation across vendor-by-protocol groups (between-group coefficient of variation: 2.65% and 5.63%, respectively). In 70 sessions with demyelinating-disease labels, MPF was 35.6% lower in segmented lesions than in normal-appearing white matter. MPF maps from two different clinical protocols acquired in a healthy volunteer showed a mean voxel-wise absolute percentage difference of 7.7% and a concordance correlation coefficient of $0.888$, indicating good cross-protocol consistency. These results suggest that magnetization transfer-informed self-supervised learning enables stable MPF mapping from conventional clinical MRI, providing a scalable approach to investigate demyelinating pathology in both existing imaging archives and prospectively acquired conventional clinical scans without dedicated qMT acquisitions.

CalArena: A Large-Scale Post-Hoc Calibration Benchmark 2026-10-27
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026, Evaluations & Datasets track. Camera-ready version with a new ablation study on using log probabilities rather than logits, and a new multiclass post-hoc calibration method: Platt scaling on the logits (one-versus-rest)

Reliable probability estimates are critical in many machine learning applications, yet modern classifiers are often poorly calibrated. Post-hoc calibration provides a simple and widely used solution, but the large number of proposed methods, combined with small-scale and inconsistent evaluations, makes it difficult to determine which approaches are truly effective in practice. We introduce a large-scale, standardized benchmark for post-hoc calibration, covering nearly 2000 experiments across tabular and computer vision tasks, including binary, multiclass, and large-scale classification settings. Our benchmark aggregates predictions from a diverse set of classical models, modern deep learning architectures, and foundation models, and provides unified, reproducible implementations of dozens of calibration methods within a common evaluation framework. We argue that Post-Hoc Improvement (PHI) in proper scoring rules offers a principled alternative to traditional calibration error estimators for comparing post-hoc methods, capturing both calibration quality and potential degradation to the model's predictive performance. Using this framework, we conduct the most comprehensive empirical study of post-hoc calibration to date. Our results reveal consistent patterns across domains: smooth calibration functions outperform binning-based approaches, dedicated multiclass methods are essential in high-dimensional settings, and generic machine learning models are not competitive without calibration-specific design. To facilitate future research, we release all data, code, and evaluation tools, providing a plug-and-play benchmark for developing and comparing calibration methods.

Physics-Guided Flow Matching: A Generative AI Framework for Global Total Electron Content Forecasting 2026-10-27
Show abstract

Accurate forecasting of global total electron content (TEC) is important for space weather monitoring and the reliable operation of satellite communication and navigation systems. We propose a physics-guided generative artificial intelligence (AI) framework for day-ahead global TEC forecasting. The framework builds on flow matching, which learns to gradually transform samples from an initial distribution into new samples that share the statistical characteristics of the target data. The proposed model incorporates ionospheric dynamics described by a partial differential equation (PDE) into this transformation process, guiding the changes between initial and target samples using physical knowledge. The model further combines the physical dynamics with statistical patterns captured from data to learn this transformation, adaptively adjusting their relative contributions at each location. The model is trained and evaluated using one year of global TEC data generated by the Thermosphere Ionosphere Electrodynamics General Circulation Model (TIEGCM). The results demonstrate that our model improves both spatial and temporal forecasting accuracy over representative benchmarks, including statistical, deep learning, and generative AI methods at all lead times and across latitudes. The average root mean square error (RMSE) is 39.2% lower than that of ConvLSTM and 23.8% lower than that of the same framework without physical guidance. The learned contribution weights vary systematically with latitude and across geographic locations, showing how the physical contribution changes across the forecast domain. These findings demonstrate that explicit physical guidance improves generative forecasts of global TEC and highlight the value of incorporating physical dynamics into generative AI modeling.

Label-free cell counting and viability prediction with brightfield imaging and deep learning 2026-10-27

Cell viability assessment is a core requirement in cell culture systems, with critical applications in biopharmaceutical manufacturing and drug development. Conventionally, it is measured by adding membrane-impermeable dyes to a sample (a process called staining), which allows compromised cell membranes to be distinguished from intact ones. However, staining has several limitations: (a) chemical agents can perturb normal cellular processes of the cells being measured, (b) it is often ambiguous to assign viability to individual cells whose membrane integrity is only partially compromised, (c) photobleaching can undermine measurement accuracy over time when using fluorescent stains, and (d) staining cannot be performed in situ or in real time. Here, we show that (1) stained cells captured under brightfield imaging contain sufficient information to distinguish live and dead cells, and (2) cells captured under unstained brightfield imaging exhibit similar image features to their stained counterparts, enabling models trained on stained cells to generalize to unstained ones. ViabiLens combines a cell detection model for localizing individual cells with a convolutional neural network (CNN) classifier for live/dead prediction, paired with an interactive UMAP-based viewer for visualizing and exploring individual cells across the sample. Evaluated on Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells spanning a wide range of viability conditions, ViabiLens achieves a mean absolute error of 2.33\% on unstained samples against fluorescence-based reference measurements. The demo of ViabiLens software is available at https://amirrezavazifeh.github.io/ViabiLens-Project-Page/.

Kähler landscapes for complex neural network descents and guarantees including a search and destroy of the Calabi-Yau manifold 2026-10-27
Show abstract

We study landscapes for complex-parameterized networks. Our approach is motivated with an information-theoretic manifold perspective of the parameter and via classical optimization guarantees although of complex geometric variety such as through Dolbeault asymptotics. The descent path admits a Kähler information metric under a cross-entropy via the Wirtinger Hessian on the log-likelihood potential. We restrict attention to a descent update rule with natural gradient descent via a differentiated loss scaled by the inverse metric, so the descent path remains in the holomorphic tangent bundle. We emphasize Calabi-Yau information manifolds which profane theoretical guarantees via an ill-curvature-conditioned, or lack thereof, landscape. We focus on Calabi-Yau metrics specifically in a non-compact setting with a global potential, so defined geometrically rather than invoking the topological requirements of the Calabi conjecture. In non-compact settings, we can write the metric determinant with respect to a background in terms of a function, pluriharmonic or real. Using Yau's Liouville theorem, we get a partial eigenvalue blow-up effect.
