A Time-Frequency-Condition Multimodal Fusion Foundation Model for Universal Rotating-Machinery Fault Diagnosis under Multi-Element Heterogeneous Scenarios 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Intelligent fault diagnosis models are typically developed for a single machine under a specific set of operating conditions, such that each new machine or condition requires retraining from scratch. This one-machine-one-model paradigm scales poorly. Although transfer learning and domain generalization can reduce the labeling burden, they primarily seek domain-invariant commonality, potentially discarding machine-specific characteristics that are critical for diagnosis. This paper proposes TFC-MMFD, a time-frequency-condition multimodal fusion foundation model for rotating-machinery fault diagnosis under multi-element heterogeneous scenarios. Statistical indicators, time-frequency images, vibration sequences, and textual machine and condition descriptions are tokenized into a unified language-like space, allowing inputs of different physical origin to be represented as a single sequence. A LLaMA backbone, together with cross-modal bidirectional attention and a Q-Former, then distills this sequence into fault-mode queries. Diagnosis is performed by matching these queries against natural-language fault-mode anchors rather than fixed class indices, while regression-adversarial condition disentanglement and condition-conditioned masked pre-training improve robustness to changing conditions. Across four progressive tasks on ten public bearing datasets spanning diverse bearing types, speeds, and loads, TFC-MMFD attains 99.40% accuracy and 99.16% macro-F1, maintains over 80% accuracy on most interpolated unseen conditions without adaptation, and improves from 20.26% to 95.63% accuracy on an unseen machine with only five labeled samples per class. It outperforms the strongest of eight domain-generalization baselines by 10.57 percentage points and compares favorably with a recent bearing foundation model while using less than half of its fine-tuning data.

Modality vs. Morphology: A Framework for Time Series Classification for Biological Signals 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Techniques for classifying biological signals are largely developed within specific modalities rather than the underlying signal structure (i.e., morphology), leading to disjointed methodologies and limited guidance across diverse signals. This work introduces a novel morphology--modality framework that connects waveform structure to a principled design of preprocessing, feature extraction, and modeling techniques for biological time-series classification. We analyze evidence across biological signals including electroencephalography, electromyography, electrocardiography, photoplethysmography, and ocular modalities including electrooculography, pupillometry, and eye-tracking, and organize according to their primary morphologies: spikes, bursts, oscillations, slow drift, and hierarchical rhythms. Across these categories, we examine how morphology influences the analytical pipeline from preprocessing to modeling and evaluation. Localized architectures favor spikes and bursts, whereas frequency and temporal models capture oscillations, and hybrid approaches integrate local and global dependencies in hierarchical signals. The associations are not absolute as biological signals often contain multiple morphologies where relevance is task dependent. The proposed framework positions morphology as an inductive biases are useful for modeling. We further identify challenges in cross-subject generalization, morphology-preserving data augmentation, multimodal fusion, and evaluation practices. By linking modality-specific constraints with waveform structure, this review provides guidance for selecting and evaluating pipelines across biological signals. We argue that morphology provides a useful cross-modal lens for developing biological time-series classification systems that are more generalizable, interpretable, and physiologically grounded.

LUMIR: Decoupled Multimodal Representation Learning from Heterogeneous Data for Next-POI Recommendation 2026-11-10
📝 16 pages. Accepted at the DaQuaMRec Workshop, co-located with ACM RecSys 2026
Show abstract

As multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources has become increasingly available, learning effective representations has attracted attention for improving recommendation performance. Existing approaches often tightly couple multimodal fusion with the architecture and optimization objective of a specific downstream model, which limits the generality and reusability of the learned representations. To address these limitations, we propose LUMIR, a decoupled multimodal representation learning framework based on contrastive learning. Multimodal information collected from heterogeneous data sources is first semantically unified into a shared textual representation space using large language models (LLMs). An interaction-aware fusion mechanism is then designed to model cross-modal interactions, while sequential signals are incorporated as a structural constraint to guide representation learning. To evaluate the learned representations, experiments are conducted on a multimodal point-of-interest (POI) dataset under the next-POI recommendation setting across multiple sequential recommendation backbones. The results show that LUMIR consistently outperforms existing multimodal fusion baselines in both accuracy-oriented and beyond-accuracy metrics, demonstrating that decoupling representation learning from downstream optimization provides an effective paradigm for learning reusable multimodal representations. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/wzehui/LUMIR.

REVELIO: Universal Multimodal Task Load Estimation for Cross-Domain Generalization 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Adaptive interactive systems that respond to user mental state require task load estimation that stays reliable outside its training context. Models built in one setting rarely transfer, limiting real-world use. This work presents Robust Estimation Via End-to-End Learning of Multimodal Observations (REVELIO), a unified framework, dataset and evaluation protocol for cross-domain task load estimation. Cardiac, electrodermal, muscle, respiration, skin temperature, eye tracking and facial signals were recorded in 96 sessions, spanning a laboratory working memory task, a driving simulator and two games under consistent hardware and annotation. Multimodal fusion reached up to 0.87 area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, above single input eye tracking (0.83), facial action units (0.74) and biosignals (0.62). Multi-domain training stayed calibrated on domains seen in training, whereas removing the target domain caused the classifier to collapse in some applications. REVELIO provides a standardized foundation for measuring modality contributions and cross-domain generalization toward deployable workload-aware systems.

In this paper, we propose CM3AE 2.0, a unified RGB Frame and Event-Voxel/-Frame pre-training framework. Extensive experiments on four diverse downstream tasks demonstrate that CM3AE 2.0 consistently improves performance, validating the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed pre-training framework.

Conditional Diffusion-Based Proxy Representation Recovery for BCVA Prediction with Missing SLO Modality 2026-11-08
📝 8 pages, 3 figures, 3 tables. Accepted at IEEE BIBM 2026
Show abstract

Preoperative prediction of postoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) can support prognosis assessment and individualized decision-making in cataract surgery. Multimodal learning can integrate preoperative optical coherence tomography (OCT) images, scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (SLO) images, and clinical text to characterize patient status. However, SLO images are often missing in real-world clinical data due to acquisition or data-management issues, resulting in incomplete multimodal inputs. To address this problem, this paper proposes a conditional diffusion-based method for SLO proxy representation recovery and fusion prediction. Instead of reconstructing raw SLO images, the proposed method formulates SLO missingness as proxy representation recovery in the latent feature space. Specifically, OCT image features and clinical text features are first fused to construct an OCT-text conditional representation, which guides the reverse denoising process of the diffusion model to estimate sample-specific SLO proxy representations. The recovered SLO proxy representation is then used as a substitute for the unavailable SLO modality and incorporated into the downstream multimodal fusion prediction network for postoperative BCVA prediction. Experiments were conducted on 2,141 preoperative multimodal cataract samples, with an SLO missing rate of 58.80%. The proposed method achieved an accuracy of 0.9714 under the criterion of an absolute prediction error within 0.1 LogMAR and a mean absolute error of 0.0423, outperforming representative postoperative visual acuity prediction and missing-modality learning methods. These results indicate that representation-level proxy recovery can alleviate the impact of SLO modality missingness on postoperative BCVA prediction.

Guided Co-Attention with Adaptive Gating for Multimodal Medical Data Fusion 2026-11-07
📝 IEEE BIBM 2026
Show abstract

Multimodal fusion requires not only exchanging information across modalities but also determining which representations should participate in the interaction and how complementary cross-modal views should be combined. We introduce Guided Co-Attention with Adaptive Gating, a fusion framework that integrates token-wise guidance, bidirectional cross-attention, and sample-specific fusion. The proposed method constructs two directional representations: an image-conditioned text representation, obtained by allowing textual tokens to query visual features, and a text-conditioned image representation formed in the reverse direction. Token-wise guidance modulates the representations before cross-attention, while an adaptive gate learns the relative contribution of the two resulting directional representations for each sample. We evaluate the approach using paired chest radiographs and radiology reports as an image-text test bed for binary (normal vs. abnormal) classification. The two cross-attention directions exhibit distinct performance characteristics, demonstrating that reversing the query and source modalities does not produce interchangeable representations. Ablation experiments further show that bidirectional interaction, adaptive gating, and token guidance provide complementary benefits. The proposed model achieves 96.05% accuracy, 96.10% F1-score, 98.37% AUC, 95.60% Sens@95Spec, and 96.51% Spec@95Sens, yielding the highest mean performance across all evaluated metrics. These findings support explicitly modeling interaction direction, token relevance, and sample-dependent integration when constructing multimodal representations.

MM-DyGraph: A Dataset, Benchmark, and Model for Multimodal Dynamic Graphs 2026-11-06
Show abstract

Multimodal dynamic graphs are pervasive in real-world applications, where nodes and edges are accompanied by multimodal signals, including text and images. Despite their practical importance, benchmarks and methods that explicitly study multimodal learning in dynamic graphs remain scarce, hindering the full understanding and effective utilization of multimodal information for downstream tasks. To address this gap, we introduce MM-DyGraph, to the best of our knowledge, the first benchmark for multimodal dynamic graph learning, consisting of diverse real-world datasets with fine-grained temporal graph evolution and rich multimodal attributes. MM-DyGraph provides three evaluation tasks to comprehensively assess model performance in dynamic settings. Extensive experiments over diverse datasets and tasks show that TST-MF delivers robust and competitive performance, achieving clear gains in multimodal settings.

HMMF-Net: A Lightweight Hierarchical Multi-Frame Multimodal Fusion Network for Accurate Fish Feeding Intensity Assessment 2026-11-06
📝 47 pages, 10 figures, 15 tables
Show abstract

Automated fish feeding intensity assessment (FFIA) is important for precision aquaculture and feed cost optimization. However, unimodal methods are vulnerable to acoustic noise and visual disturbances, while existing multimodal frameworks often rely on computationally intensive 3D convolutions or Transformers, limiting their use on edge devices. To address these limitations, we propose HMMF-Net, a lightweight multimodal architecture that combines early multi-frame visual encoding and multi-axis acoustic modeling through a hierarchical bidirectional messenger cross-modal fusion (BMCF) mechanism. The visual branch captures temporal variations in feeding behavior by stacking temporally distant frames as a multi-channel input to an adapted 2D EfficientNetB0 backbone. The acoustic branch employs adaptive multi-axis residual units (AMARU) to extract temporal, frequency, and local time-frequency representations from log-mel spectrograms. BMCF progressively exchanges information between the two modalities at intermediate representation levels through compact messenger tokens, reducing the computational overhead of dense cross-modal attention. Evaluated on the AV-FFIA dataset using five-fold cross-validation, HMMF-Net achieves 96.77% accuracy and 96.88% F1-score on the held-out test folds, with 3.987M parameters and 1.002 GFLOPs. Compared with the best-performing visual configuration (95.49% accuracy), HMMF-Net reduces the classification error by 28.38%. Deployment on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano using TensorRT with FP32 precision maintains comparable accuracy, with an inference time of 11.54 ms per 2-second sample and a model size of 18.81 MB. These results demonstrate that HMMF-Net combines effective multimodal representation learning with computational efficiency, enabling accurate and practical FFIA on resource-constrained edge platforms.

📝 15 pages, 3-page supplementary material provided as an ancillary file. Accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI)
Show abstract

Multimodal self-supervised learning is challenged by heterogeneity, unequal informativeness, and complex information interactions across multimodal data sources such as images, text, audio, and tabular data. While contrastive methods effectively align shared semantics, they typically rely on strong multi-view redundancy assumptions, overlooking modality-specific and synergistic information, thus leading to suboptimal representations. We propose the Multimodal Information Interactions Bottleneck (MIIB), a principled framework grounded in Partial Information Decomposition that explicitly encodes redundant, unique, and synergistic information within multimodal interactions. MIIB employs a dual-path model architecture for modality-specific encoding and multimodal fusion, optimized using surrogate tractable entropy-based objectives that enforce minimal sufficient representations without requiring explicit mutual information estimation. FuseAdapt: Parameter-Efficient Multimodal Fusion for Semantic Segmentation with Missing Modalities 2026-11-03
📝 NeurIPS 2026. 26 pages, 6 figures, 20 tables
Show abstract

Multi-modal semantic segmentation benefits from complementary sensors, but this benefit is fragile when some modalities are degraded or missing at test time. Many existing methods rely on dedicated fusion modules or multi-branch cross-modal encoders, and the fusion often becomes unreliable when modalities are absent, leading to substantial performance drops. We propose FuseAdapt, a parameter-efficient framework that performs fusion within the adaptation space of a frozen vision foundation model (VFM), exploring whether VFM priors can serve as an effective shared backbone for multi-modal segmentation. Instead of attaching a standalone fusion network, FuseAdapt assigns each modality a lightweight adaptation branch and performs adaptation-space fusion by keeping Q/V low-rank adaptation private to each modality and routing only FFN low-rank residual activations across modalities inside the frozen backbone. The pretrained VFM remains fixed while modality-specific corrections and explicit cross-modal interaction are learned through the adaptation paths. To handle missing modalities, we further introduce Predictive Modality Modeling (PMM), a direct representation-level objective for cross-modal redundancy. During training, PMM masks a target modality and predicts its embedding from the visible ones, supervised by a teacher target computed under full-modality input. PMM is used only during training and adds no inference-time overhead. Across three multi-modal segmentation benchmarks (MCubeS, DELIVER, MUSES), FuseAdapt consistently improves both modality-complete and modality-incomplete segmentation with a small trainable budget, yielding up to +3.01 mIoU under full modalities and +11.25 mIoU under missing modalities over prior state-of-the-art. Calibrating Multimodal Test-Time Adaptation under Reliability and Availability Modality Shifts 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Multimodal test-time adaptation (TTA) enables pretrained multimodal models to accommodate distribution shifts during inference using only unlabeled test data, without recourse to the original training data. Compared with unimodal adaptation, multimodal adaptation is further complicated by the coexistence and interaction of multiple representation perspectives. This complexity is particularly pronounced under modality reliability shifts, which preserve modality availability but degrade information quality, and modality availability shifts, which render the corresponding modality entirely inaccessible. Category-conditional Gaussian modeling offers a natural starting point for handling such distributional variations, having proven effective in unimodal test-time adaptation for characterizing representation distributions, yet its extension to multimodal settings requires a more elaborate formulation. To this end, we propose Adaptive Probabilistic Gaussian Calibration (AdaPGC), which extends category-conditional Gaussian modeling across modality-specific and multimodal fusion representations for probabilistic calibration under multimodal distribution shifts. AdaPGC continuously updates category-conditional Gaussian models over modality-specific and fusion representations, using the resulting probabilistic predictions to calibrate source model predictions and guide reliability based rectification, while exploiting their joint distributions to recover unavailable fusion representations. Extensive experiments under diverse modality corruption and missingness conditions demonstrate that AdaPGC effectively improves robustness under both modality reliability and availability shifts. Our source code will be released at https://github.com/XuJinglinn/AdaPGC-plus.

Hybrid Federated Learning for Multimodal Fusion under Missing Modalities and Site Heterogeneity 2026-11-02
Show abstract

Multimodal fusion is studied under hybrid federation, where data are partitioned across samples and feature blocks. The proposed two-dimensional federated smooth multiple-kernel large-margin learning method (2D-FedSMKL) integrates availability-corrected modality scores, group-kernel regularization, and smooth worst-site risk aggregation. For fixed maps and propensities, the objective is convex and smooth. Curvature bounds and exact synchronous block-gradient equivalence are established for binary and multiclass scores, giving a conditional accelerated rate. An oracle-propensity binary risk bound and a propensity-perturbation result are also derived. Simulations and two public activity-recognition benchmarks assess site heterogeneity and modality absence.
