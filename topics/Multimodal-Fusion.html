AGF-Net: A Robust Multimodal Emotion Recognition Model for Asynchronous Audio-Visual Signals Based on Predictive Dynamic Fusion with Negative Learning 2026-10-22
Show abstract

This study develops an audio-visual emotion recognition system that integrates automatic utterance segmentation with multimodal fusion. Unlike conventional methods that rely on manually annotated utterance boundaries, the proposed system automatically generates candidate segments from continuous dialogue and extracts the corresponding audio and visual features for emotion classification. To address the challenges introduced by automatic segmentation, the proposed AGF-Net integrates Audio-Guided Fusion (AGF), Multimodal Negative Learning (MNL), and Predictive Dynamic Fusion (PDF) to improve cross-modal fusion, modality balance, and robustness to varying modality quality. Experimental results show that AGF-Net achieves 64.92% Accuracy and 65.47% F1-score under clean conditions, 62.13% Accuracy and 63.08% F1-score under moderate noise, and 58.19% Accuracy and 59.41% F1-score under higher noise. Overall, the proposed framework reduces reliance on manually annotated utterance boundaries and maintains competitive performance under automatically segmented and noisy audio-visual inputs.

DeliMF: Gated Cross-Attention Fusion of Structured Time Series and Clinical Text for Early Intensive Care Delirium Prediction 2026-10-20
📝 Preprint
Show abstract

Delirium affects a large fraction of intensive care unit (ICU) patients and foreshadows longer stays, lasting cognitive decline, and higher mortality. Flagging the patients who will become delirious within the first day of admission would give clinicians a window to intervene. Yet state-of-the-art predictors rely only on the structured electronic health record (EHR) of vital signs, laboratory values, and medications, while overlooking the free-text clinical notes written in the same window, which carry information the numbers cannot encode. To this end, we present DeliMF, a multimodal framework that fuses the structured time series with clinical text. It encodes the structured signals with a temporal Transformer and the time-stamped radiology reports with a clinical text encoder, and fuses the two through a gated cross-attention module. The gate learns a per-patient weight on the text; when a patient has no note the gate closes and DeliMF reduces to its structured-only form, giving robustness to missing text by construction. On an incident-only MIMIC-IV cohort, DeliMF improves significantly over the baselines, particularly on patients who carry clinical text, while losing nothing on those without. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of multimodal fusion and gated cross-attention, and a case study further shows that fusing clinical text with the structured stream recovers delirium cases that the structured data alone misses. We hope DeliMF encourages early ICU prediction to make fuller use of the overlooked clinical text and contributes to the early identification and timely intervention of delirium.

GAMF-Net: Grouped Availability-Aware Multimodal Fusion for Smartphone Skin Lesion Classification 2026-10-20
📝 14 pages, 4 figures. Accepted at MMM 2027
Show abstract

Smartphone clinical images provide a low-cost data source for computer-aided skin lesion classification in resource-constrained settings, but images alone cannot fully capture patients' medical histories and lesion symptoms. To address the lack of structured organization among heterogeneous clinical variables, the ambiguity between observed and imputed values, and the risk that clinical information may interfere with visual evidence, we propose GAMF-Net, a grouped availability-aware multimodal fusion network. Ten clinical variables are divided into three semantic groups---demographics, history-related risk, and lesion presentation---and each group jointly encodes field values and field-level availability masks. The image branch produces the base classification logits, while each clinical group generates image-conditioned class-level residual evidence. Only available groups participate in availability-normalized equal-weight fusion; when all clinical information is unavailable, the model reduces exactly to the image-only pathway. We evaluate the method on PAD-UFES-20 under a patient-disjoint five-fold protocol with separate training, validation, and test partitions. GAMF-Net achieves an Accuracy of $74.17\pm2.85\%$, a balanced accuracy (BACC) of $70.11\pm2.11\%$, a Macro-F1 of $68.85\pm0.85\%$, and a Macro-AUROC of $93.42\pm1.20\%$, outperforming all image-only and multimodal baselines reimplemented under a unified protocol. Compared with the strongest baseline, MetaBlock, the corresponding improvements are 2.37, 2.59, 4.14, and 1.02 percentage points, respectively. Ablation results show that clinical information substantially improves class-balanced performance, constrained equal-weight fusion is more effective than dynamic fusion, and the lesion-presentation group provides the most informative clinical complement.

HypTI: Hyperbolic Geometry-Aware Multimodal Feature Fusion Framework for Dementia Detection Based on Tabular-Image Data 2026-10-20
Show abstract

AI-assisted dementia detection usually requires multimodal fusion of diverse data, such as clinical tabular assessments and neuroimaging data. However, most existing approaches perform feature fusion in Euclidean space. This overlooks the geometric structure of representation, including hierarchical organization in clinical assessments and multi-scale anatomical patterns in neuroimaging data. Recent studies have proved hyperbolic space provides a natural geometry for modelling hierarchical and multi-scale structures due to its exponentially expanding space. Motivated by these, we propose HypTI, a hyperbolic geometry-aware multimodal fusion framework for dementia detection based on clinical tabular assessments and MRI scans. HypTI extends hyperbolic representation learning to an underexplored tabular-MRI setting, jointly modelling clinical tabular, MRI scans, and cross-modal interactions in hyperbolic space. Specifically, clinical tabular variables are represented through learnable Lorentz prototypes to capture hierarchical relationships among assessments, while MRI scans are encoded using ROI-guided multi-scale representation learning to capture anatomical patterns across brain regions and spatial scales in Lorentz space. The multimodal representations are integrated through an adaptive fusion module generates subject-specific scale and shift coefficients to dynamically modulate and integrate the multimodal embeddings, enabling effective patient-level tabular-image representation learning for dementia detection. Experiments on OASIS-3 dataset show that the proposed framework achieves strong performance compared with representative approaches.

MuteBench: Modality Unavailability Tolerance Evaluation for Incomplete Multimodal Fusion 2026-10-19
🤖 AI 深度总结（DeepSeek 全文阅读）· 点击展开
Show abstract

Multimodal physiological data powers clinical AI systems from intensive care units to wearable devices, but sensors routinely fail in practice. Two failure modes are common: modality missing, where an entire channel is absent, and within-modality missing, where a contiguous time segment is lost. No existing benchmark evaluates multiple fusion architectures under both failure modes at controlled severity levels across diverse clinical datasets. We present MuteBench, a benchmark covering 9 datasets from 7 clinical domains, 6 fusion architectures, and 2 missing-data modes over 125,000 samples. Through this benchmark, we find that architecture family is the strongest predictor of robustness, outweighing parameter count. Channel-independent models tolerate modality missing well but can be sensitive to within-modality missing, especially on short sequences. Curriculum modality dropout protects reliably only up to the maximum dropout rate used in training. We also find that channel count, sequence length, and modality alignment jointly determine which failure mode poses the greater threat. Finally, a PTB-XL case study suggests that diffusion-based imputation can improve downstream classification under within-modality missing, with the largest gains for models whose expert routing is most sensitive to corrupted inputs, though broader validation across datasets remains an open direction. MuteBench provides practitioners with concrete guidance for both selecting existing architectures and informing the design of future robust multimodal fusion methods.

EDMA: Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering 2026-10-19
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Multimodal question answering (MMQA) is based on integrating heterogeneous data sources, selectively leveraging relevant modalities, and ignoring distractors. Existing approaches based on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) improve performance by splitting the task into intermediate steps, but do not quantify how each modality contributes to reaching the final answer. We argue that quantifying the effect that each modality has on the answering process improves process traceability and final performance. Thus, we propose Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering (EDMA), which uses logical entropy to formalise multimodal QA as an iterative process of entropy reduction and to quantify the contribution of each modality in distinguishing correct from incorrect candidate answers. This enables the construction of quantifiable answering trajectories, where each step is associated with a measurable entropy reduction. EDMA introduces (i) modality-conditioned partitioning to estimate unimodal entropy reduction, (ii) entropy-driven multimodal fusion to capture complementary information across modalities, and (iii) entropy-based selection of the answering trajectory that minimises the most logical entropy. Evaluated across standard MMQA benchmarks, EDMA outperforms competitive prompting baselines, with average gains of 12.8 F1 and 4.6 LLM-J points over Planning Prompting and 18.6 F1 and 8.3 LLM-J points over Chain-of-Thought. Moreover, EDMA outperforms self-consistency baselines and is competitive with the multi-agent system baseline. Moreover, we show that higher entropy correlates with more false positives and, through controlled trajectory selection, demonstrate that logical entropy serves as an effective, monotonic selector for correct reasoning paths, validating entropy level as a reliable proxy for performance.

CheXFuse: Guided Differential Joint Attention Fusion for Robust Multimodal Chest X-Ray Classification in Data-Scarce Medical Settings 2026-10-19
📝 IEEE ICDM DMBIH 2026
Show abstract

A central challenge in multimodal learning is how to fuse modalities that differ substantially in predictive strength. When one modality provides a much stronger representation than another, indiscriminate cross-modal interaction can introduce weak, redundant, or unstable signals, limiting or even degrading performance relative to the strongest unimodal representation. To address this problem, we propose CheXFuse, a multimodal fusion framework based on Guided Differential Joint Attention (GuDJA). CheXFuse combines image and text token representations within a joint multimodal sequence, where learned token guidance emphasizes informative representations and differential attention reduces less useful attention responses. We evaluate CheXFuse on the Indiana University (IU) Chest X-ray dataset, using binary (normal vs. abnormal) classification as the downstream task. Experiments are conducted under both original-report and masked-report settings, with the latter serving as a controlled stress test under reduced textual informativeness. CheXFuse with GuDJA achieves the strongest mean overall performance across the evaluated baselines and ablations, reaching 95.73% accuracy, 95.79% F1-score, 96.54% recall, and 95.28% sensitivity at 95% specificity (Sens@95Spec) with original reports, and 95.58%, 95.60%, 95.60%, and 94.34%, respectively, under masked reports. These results suggest that GuDJA provides a promising fusion strategy under asymmetric or partially reduced modality informativeness.

Trust Before Fusion: QIMG-7 and Source-Aware Resolution for Polluted Multimodal RAG 2026-10-18
📝 23 pages, 6 figures, 23 tables. Preprint under review

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is often evaluated with clean evidence, yet real retrieval can return topically relevant but unreliable content: false text and misleading images from corrupted metadata, entity swaps, typographic overlays, semantic edits, adversarial patches, blends, or style transfer. We introduce QIMG-7, a controlled benchmark for multimodal retrieval pollution in multi-sentence factual QA, spanning four datasets, seven image-attack families, and 16 paired clean/polluted regimes, for 1,760 evaluation rows per method. Across four generator/gate stacks, naive multimodal fusion is brittle: in the main gpt-4o-mini stack, Full-MM support drops from 0.908 with clean text to 0.490 with polluted text, often making Parametric fallback safer than retrieval. We propose source-aware trust resolution (SATR), a training-free approach that compares Parametric, Text-only, and Full-MM candidate answers and selects among candidate answers or falls back based on source reliability. The Field-Selector variant achieves the best balanced score, 0.816, improving over Full-MM by 11.7 points and over the Cascaded Router by 2.7 points. Ablations show that, in this text-first setting, explicit text-reliability modeling is the dominant driver of these gains. Overall, in text-first factual QA with multimodal retrieval conflict, our results support selective trust rather than unconditional fusion. Label-Space Mismatch Distorts Cross-Cohort Evaluation in Breast Cancer Molecular Subtyping 2026-10-18
Show abstract

Cross-cohort evaluation of breast cancer subtype classifiers is unreliable when source and target cohorts use different annotation standards: METABRIC labels six classes, PAM50 defines five, and a claudin-low prediction cannot be correct under a five-class standard. A classifier transferred from 1,369 METABRIC patients (train n=958) to TCGA-BRCA (n=987) and GSE21653 (n=266) separates three evaluation operations routinely conflated in cross-site comparisons. (1) Label-space restriction - an evaluation-rule change, not a retraining gain - is the dominant correction (73.76%->79.74% on TCGA-BRCA; 63.16%->68.42% on GSE21653); (2) EM prior correction on the reconciled space is an optional calibration step (Brier fidelity up, macro F1 down; to 80.65% and 74.06%); and (3) the target label space is not recoverable from unlabelled predictions (EM retains prior 0.0751 on the absent class; calibrated detectors fail), so compliance must come from cohort documentation. The protocol exposes why internal validation overstates cross-cohort value: multimodal fusion gains do not transfer (no configuration beats mRNA alone, n=694; McNemar p=0.13-1.00), and the genomic panel adds nothing to pathological complete response prediction over clinical variables (DeltaAUC = +0.0045). Clinically, without label-space compliance, cross-site accuracy comparisons misattribute annotation-standard differences to model skill; Luminal A/B and claudin-low boundary errors map to distinct treatment decisions; and subtype accuracy is a weaker portable endpoint than survival discrimination (external C-index 0.577-0.584). Reporting the cohort's allowed label set and applying label-space compliance are therefore prerequisites before any cross-cohort performance claim.

Show abstract

Nutrition estimation is a fundamental task in consumer diet tracking, clinical dietetics, chronic disease management, sports and hospital nutrition, and broader food computing systems. The existing approaches have progressed along two largely separate axes, vision models that rely on calibrated RGB-depth captures and ingredient-aware methods that use textual cues but use limited multimodal fusion. These results demonstrate that ingredient-conditioned prediction and frequency-aware RGB-depth fusion provide measurable gains for single-image nutrient estimation. More broadly, NutriVision offers a practical route toward nutrition-assessment systems that exploit geometric and semantic cues without requiring specialized depth-sensing hardware

Imaging-anchored multiomics in cardiovascular disease: integrating cardiac imaging, bulk, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomics 2026-10-15

Cardiovascular disease arises from interactions between inherited risk, molecular programmes, and tissue-scale remodelling that are observed clinically through imaging. Cardiac MRI (CMR), computed tomography (CT), and echocardiography are integral to routine cardiovascular care, while bulk RNA sequencing, single-cell RNA sequencing, and spatial transcriptomics are providing increasingly detailed molecular characterization of cardiac tissue. A Multimodal Emotion Recognition System: Integrating Facial Expressions, Body Movement, Speech, and Spoken Language 2026-10-15

Psychological evaluation relies on clinicians' observation and interpretation of a patient's emotional expression, which is susceptible to subjectivity, bias, fatigue, and inconsistency. This paper presents a multimodal emotion recognition system designed to support, rather than replace, the evaluator. The system combines facial expression recognition, speech emotion recognition, text-based emotion recognition of transcribed speech, and body movement analysis, and fuses their outputs into an emotional profile of class probabilities that is presented to the evaluator together with the overall prediction. FuseAdapt: Parameter-Efficient Multimodal Fusion for Semantic Segmentation with Missing Modalities 2026-10-15
📝 25 pages, 6 figures, 19 tables. Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Show abstract

Multi-modal semantic segmentation benefits from complementary sensors, but this benefit is fragile when some modalities are degraded or missing at test time. Many existing methods rely on dedicated fusion modules or multi-branch cross-modal encoders, and the fusion often becomes unreliable when modalities are absent, leading to substantial performance drops. We propose \textbf{FuseAdapt}, a parameter-efficient framework that performs fusion within the adaptation space of a frozen vision foundation model (VFM), exploring whether VFM priors can serve as an effective shared backbone for multi-modal segmentation. Instead of attaching a standalone fusion network, FuseAdapt assigns each modality a lightweight adaptation branch and performs \emph{adaptation-space fusion} by keeping Q/V low-rank adaptation private to each modality and routing only FFN low-rank residual activations across modalities inside the frozen backbone. The pretrained VFM remains fixed while modality-specific corrections and explicit cross-modal interaction are learned through the adaptation paths. To handle missing modalities, we further introduce \textbf{Predictive Modality Modeling (PMM)}, a direct representation-level objective for cross-modal redundancy. During training, PMM masks a target modality and predicts its embedding from the visible ones, supervised by a teacher target computed under full-modality input. PMM is used only during training and adds no inference-time overhead. Across three multi-modal segmentation benchmarks (MCubeS, DELIVER, MUSES), FuseAdapt consistently improves both modality-complete and modality-incomplete segmentation with a small trainable budget, yielding up to +3.01 mIoU under full modalities and +12.56 mIoU under missing modalities over prior state-of-the-art. Selection, Not Fusion: Radar-Modulated State Space Models for Radar-Camera Depth Estimation 2026-10-14
📝 Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 16 pages, 3 figures, 9 tables
Show abstract

Radar camera depth estimation turns a very sparse but metric radar signal into a dense depth map. Existing methods combine radar and image features outside the sequence operator of the backbone, through concatenation, confidence gating, sparse supervision, or graph based extraction. Mamba variants for multimodal fusion also leave the selection mechanism itself unimodal. We argue that selection is the right place for radar to enter. We introduce Radar-Modulated Selection (RMS), which injects radar inside the selective scan of Mamba. Radar adds learned perturbations, initialised at zero, to the step size $Δ$ and the readout $\mathbf{C}$. The input projection $\mathbf{B}$ and the state matrix $\mathbf{A}$ never see radar. At initialisation the block equals a pretrained image Mamba, so radar gains influence only where it improves accuracy. The construction also couples the two modalities at linear cost and falls back to the image backbone when radar is missing. We deploy RMS in a Multi-View Scan Pyramid (MVSP) that matches the fusion operator to the reach of radar at each scale. On nuScenes, SemoDepth reduces MAE by 34.0%, 29.9%, and 29.9% over the previous best within 50, 70, and 80 m. It also has the lowest single frame latency among methods with a public release (26.8 ms). A feature blend placed before the scan adds no accuracy on top of RMS, which supports the view that selection inside the scan can replace fusion outside it. When twice as many spurious radar returns as true ones are injected, the MAE@80 of SemoDepth rises by 1.4%, against 33.6% for a Mamba baseline that fuses radar before the scan.

GeoMER: A Joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic Geometry Framework for Multimodal Emotion Recognition 2026-10-13
📝 Accepted at ICONIP 2026 (33rd International Conference on Neural Information Processing)
Show abstract

Multimodal Emotion Recognition (MER) aims to understand human emotions by integrating information from multiple modalities such as speech and text. Psychological studies suggest that emotions exhibit latent hierarchical structured relationships in their semantic representations. However, most MER approaches rely on Euclidean representations, which capture fine-grained cross-modal interactions but do not explicitly provide a geometric prior for organizing hierarchical emotional structures. To mitigate this gap, we propose GeoMER, a joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic modeling framework that introduces hyperbolic emotion-core routing to capture higher-level, abstract emotional representations for MER. The proposed framework jointly models token-level cross-modal semantic interactions and latent emotion representations in two complementary geometric spaces. Specifically, GeoMER consists of a Euclidean Multimodal Fusion Module (EuclFusion) for multimodal representation fusion and a Hyperbolic Geometric Core Routing (HypGeoRoute) module that performs geometry-aware core assignment and aggregation in a hyperbolic manifold to capture more abstract feature representations. The HypGeoRoute module complements the Euclidean token-level modeling. We experiment on the IEMOCAP multimodal MER benchmark and demonstrate that the proposed method achieves the best performance, with 83.54% weighted accuracy and 83.35% unweighted accuracy, outperforming Euclidean baselines, including MemoCMT.

Focus on What You Need: Global-to-Local Dynamic Attention for Multimodal Sarcasm Detection 2026-10-13
📝 10 pages, 8 figures
Show abstract

Multimodal sarcasm detection has attracted increasing attention due to the widespread use of images and text on social media platforms. Existing studies generally treat the semantic or emotional inconsistency between images and text as a key cue for identifying sarcastic expressions. However, images in real-world scenarios often contain multiple visual objects, many of which are irrelevant to sarcasm-related semantics. Such redundant visual information may introduce noise during cross-modal interaction, distracting models from focusing on critical sarcasm cues and potentially leading to inaccurate predictions. To address this issue, we propose GLDA (Global-to-Local Dynamic Attention), a multimodal sarcasm detection framework that models cross-modal interactions from a global-to-local perspective. Specifically, GLDA first employs a global multimodal fusion module to capture high-level semantic interactions between visual and textual modalities, enabling the model to identify coarse-grained cross-modal inconsistencies. To further reduce the influence of irrelevant objects, GLDA introduces a local gated cross-modal attention mechanism that dynamically filters redundant visual and textual features and refines multimodal representations at a finer granularity. In addition, a multi-branch learning objective is designed to jointly optimize unimodal and multimodal representations.
