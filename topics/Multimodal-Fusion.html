Resource-Efficient Multimodal Fusion for Early Rice Blast Forecasting in Sustainable Agriculture 2026-11-24
Show abstract

Resource-efficient artificial intelligence has become increasingly important for sustainable agriculture, where timely disease forecasting must be achieved under computational and energy constraints. Early estimation of rice blast can enhance food security by optimizing crop management practices while reducing excessive pesticide use. However, existing frameworks often suffer from high computational overhead and limited capability to model the complex spectral-temporal dependencies present in heterogeneous meteorological and remote sensing data. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a lightweight multimodal fusion framework comprising an attentive positional encoding-based Transformer and a convolutional neural network (CNN)-embedded gated recurrent unit (GRU) for efficient spectral-temporal representation learning. The attention-enhanced positional encoding assigns adaptive importance to sequential meteorological tokens, enabling effective modelling of temporal characteristics. In parallel, the lightweight CNN extracts discriminative spectral features, while the GRU captures temporal dynamics from remote sensing imagery acquired over two time-instances. A multimodal fusion layer integrates these heterogeneous representations to forecast rice blast risk 5-10 days before symptom manifestation across diverse agro-climatic zones. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving a validation accuracy of 96.93% and an F1-score of 95.79%. In addition to its predictive performance, the lightweight multimodal architecture substantially reduces computational complexity, inference latency, and carbon emissions, making it an energy-efficient solution for large-scale disease forecasting. The proposed framework provides a practical foundation for resource-efficient artificial intelligence in sustainable agriculture.

Can Multimodal Fusion MakeSense for Embodied Vision in Degraded Sensing? 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Embodied vision systems rely on complementary sensors to perceive and act as conditions change. Even models that accept arbitrary sensor subsets can exhibit negative fusion: adding a sensor increases prediction error. We propose MakeSense, a multimodal fusion framework that preserves sensor-specific evidence in private streams and combines information through shared bottleneck tokens. Training pairs full and subset views of the same degraded observations, penalizing higher error with more sensors. Where geometry permits, the distance readout uses range measurements alone, while the rest of the prediction draws on all available inputs. Paired tests across five datasets reveal helpful and harmful sensor additions. MakeSense reduces negative fusion on MM-Fi. With lightweight adapters and per-dataset training, the same design improves UAV localization on MMAUD, cooperative 3D detection on V2X-Real, few-shot pose transfer to RELI11D, and command prediction on Humanoid-Everyday, where a world model trained on the frozen core's outputs forecasts future fused states more accurately than a linear baseline at longer tested horizons. MakeSense supports embodied sensing across human activity, aerial systems, robotics, and autonomous driving.

LIFT: Lifting Language-Controlled Image Fusion to Free-Viewpoint 3D Scenes 2026-11-23
Show abstract

Multimodal image fusion traditionally generates a single fused image from paired observations at a specific viewpoint, limiting comprehensive scene perception and adaptability to diverse perceptual objectives. To overcome these limitations, we present LIFT, a novel framework for our proposed language-controlled 3D image fusion (L3D-IF) paradigm that extends multimodal image fusion from 2D images to free-viewpoint 3D scenes, enabling language-guided fusion tailored to diverse perceptual objectives. First, LIFT formulates Semantic Modality Binding (SMB), which interprets language instructions to identify semantic targets and their associated modality preferences and grounds these targets in corresponding regions of a shared Gaussian scene, establishing persistent semantic-to-spatial correspondences for consistent fusion control across viewpoints. Second, Language-Conditioned Gaussian Appearance (LCGA) is developed to achieve scene-wide multimodal appearance modeling by combining adaptive multimodal blending with language- and viewpoint-conditioned appearance refinement, enabling spatially adaptive realization of diverse fusion appearances while preserving source fidelity at pure-modality endpoints. Finally, we construct a Multi-View Language-Supervision (MVLS) dataset that associates language instructions and calibrated viewpoints with modulated fused images, providing effective supervision for learning instruction-dependent fusion from finite multi-view observations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LIFT achieves coherent multimodal fusion across continuous viewpoints, accurate semantic target localization, and consistent regional fusion control under diverse language instructions.

MusicRec: Audio, Lyrics, and Engagement Feature Enrichment for Music Recommendation 2026-11-22
📝 Accepted at IEEE BigData 2026. Extended version: 18 pages, 5 figures, 14 tables

Show abstract

Music recommendation systems typically treat songs as opaque tokens, relying on interaction histories that overlook semantic or acoustic content. Prior LLM-augmented, multimodal, and text-enhanced sequential recommenders partially combine semantic, acoustic, or engagement signals, and none jointly model all three within a unified LLM-based framework grounded in song content. In this work, we propose a multimodal framework for session-based music recommendation that enriches the LastFM-1K dataset with three complementary signals: (1) audio and lyric embeddings from pretrained music and text models, (2) LLM-generated semantic metadata using the MGPHot schema, and (3) listening completion ratios. We validate the generated metadata against human MGPHot annotations across all 58 attributes. We extend the E4SRec framework with multimodal features, ItemID encoder backbones including SASRec, BERT4Rec, and GRU4Rec, and LLM backbones including LLaMA-2-13B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and LLaMA-3-70B across zero-shot and fine-tuned settings. In addition, we evaluate four multimodal fusion strategies, benchmark five audio and five lyric encoders, and compare Spotify audio attributes with LLM-generated MGPHot metadata. Our experiments show that content-based features improve over ID-only baselines by up to 95% in Recall and 79% in NDCG, with the largest gains when the ItemID baseline is weakest. Fine-tuning substantially improves over zero-shot, after which the best audio or lyric setup outperforms the ID-only baseline for every backbone. Naive multimodal fusion does not always yield additive improvements, highlighting challenges in cross-modal integration, while structured LLM-generated metadata proves more useful than Spotify audio attributes. We release a large-scale multimodal benchmark for music recommendation research.

Time-MAGNET: Federated Multimodal Graph Neural Expert Transformer for Human Activity Recognition 2026-11-21

Show abstract

Human Activity Recognition plays a vital role in applications such as fitness tracking, smart homes, and healthcare monitoring. Single modality HAR systems rely on a single source of sensory information and can therefore be affected by sensor specific noise and signal degradation, which may limit their ability to distinguish between activities. To address these limitations, this work proposes Time-MAGNET, a novel multimodal learning framework that adaptively fuses heterogeneous sensing modalities to learn discriminative representations for HAR. It adaptively models interactions among heterogeneous modalities using graph attention and a Mixture-of-Experts architecture. To capture complex temporal dependencies, we employ a lightweight encoder-only T5 architecture customized for multimodal HAR and efficiently trained using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). In real world applications, multimodal HAR data are typically distributed across devices or users and cannot always be centrally collected due to privacy, data ownership, and communication constraints. However, the distributed nature of HAR introduces statistical and system heterogeneity across clients, which can challenge conventional federated aggregation methods. To address this issue, we propose Federated Proximal Adaptive Weighting (FedPAW), a novel federated aggregation algorithm for stable and effective model aggregation under heterogeneous client settings. Extensive experiments on the MEx dataset show that Time-MAGNET achieves accuracies of 0.9555 and 0.8997 in centralized and federated settings, respectively, outperforming the considered baseline methods. The results indicate that adaptive multimodal fusion and LoRA based temporal modeling improve centralized HAR performance, while FedPAW provides effective aggregation under heterogeneous federated clients.

DynFlux: Learning to Dynamically Balance Consensus and Conflict in Multimodal Emotion Recognition 2026-11-21
Show abstract

Multimodal emotion recognition remains challenging due to the intrinsic heterogeneity of different modalities. Effectively leveraging both cross-modal consensus (consistency) and conflict (inconsistency) is essential, as they jointly provide complementary cues that enhance emotion understanding from diverse perspectives. Traditional multimodal methods often suffer from semantic ambiguity and rarely explicitly model the interplay between cross-modal consensus and conflict. Moreover, existing approaches struggle to capture and exploit the dynamic interactions between these two complementary factors. To overcome these limitations, we propose DynFlux, a model guided by a temporal heterogeneous graph that encodes both consensus and conflict relationships across modalities. DynFlux introduces a dedicated graph branch that explicitly captures cross-modal dependencies through sparse temporal graphs, and modulates the conventional multimodal emotion recognition pipeline at three hierarchical stages: adaptive feature modulation during feature extraction, guided aggregation modulation during feature integration, and fusion strategy modulation at the multimodal fusion stage. Through this design, DynFlux dynamically balances cross-modal consensus and conflict, enabling richer and more robust emotion understanding. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets confirm that DynFlux consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, and its generality is further validated on multimodal intent recognition and multimodal sarcasm detection tasks.

ERIT: A Responsible Multimodal Affective Computing Benchmark for Older-Adult Emotion Recognition in the Wild 2026-11-21
📝 Accepted at the 4th International Workshop on Multimodal, Generative and Responsible Affective Computing (MRAC '26), ACM Multimedia 2026, 5 pages
Show abstract

We introduce a controlled evaluation setting for image-text fusion in older adult emotion recognition, instantiated in the ERIT benchmark. Many existing video-based emotion datasets rely on frame sampling during evaluation, and differences in how frames are sampled can substantially affect results - yet such details are seldom disclosed, limiting reproducibility and fair comparison. ERIT removes this source of variability by pairing representative annotated frames with corresponding transcribed speech, so that each sample has fixed visual and textual inputs. The dataset covers seven emotions (anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, surprise, and neutral), and is designed primarily as a controlled evaluation resource for the underrepresented population of older adults. We evaluate ERIT in zero-shot settings with LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2-VL, GPT-4V, and GPT-4o, and also fine-tune LLaVA-NeXT with LoRA, comparing results using strict, multi-label, and balanced accuracies. We show that multimodal fusion improves ambiguity-aware metrics but remains visually dominated under strict evaluation, exposing limitations of current VLMs for older-adult affect recognition. Robustness and label agreement experiments show how age-related facial morphology introduces misclassifications, and how cross-modal label disagreement (e.g., happy text / sad image) influences fusion results.

UNILARN: Latent Action Pretraining with Dedicated RGB, Depth, and Unified RGB-Depth Supervision 2026-11-20
📝 https://manishgovind.github.io/unilarn/
Show abstract

Depth provides critical geometric supervision for robot manipulation, yet existing depth-aware policies typically inject it through fused representations or auxiliary objectives, leaving its role in action learning implicit. We introduce UNILARN, a latent-action pretraining framework that explicitly preserves three complementary supervisory targets from RGB-D transitions: RGB-specific, depth-specific, and unified latent actions. RGB and depth transition embeddings are first discretized through a shared codebook, then fused and re-quantized through a separate unified codebook, preserving modality-specific dynamics while modeling their shared action semantics. These three latent-action targets provide separate pseudo-action supervision during VLA pretraining, avoiding both inconsistent independent learning and premature multimodal fusion without requiring robot action labels. The pretrained VLA is subsequently fine-tuned on executable actions and requires only RGB observations and language instructions at inference. Experiments in simulation and the real-world show improvements over RGB-based latent-action pretraining, particularly on geometry-sensitive manipulation tasks.

Accurate differential diagnosis of neurodegenerative disorders remains challenging due to overlapping clinical manifestations and heterogeneous, often incomplete multimodal observations. CEVA: Complementary Evidence Verification via Frame-Level Reorganization for Short-Video Fake News Detection 2026-11-17
Show abstract

Short-video platforms are now a common way for the public to follow unfolding events. Their rapid, visually persuasive format also makes fake news easier to spread. Unlike text-image posts, short-video news interleaves multiple modalities and changing scenes, making it difficult to identify which frames verify a claim. Existing detectors either pool sampled frames homogeneously or select them by text-frame similarity. Both overlook that aligned frames may repeat a misleading claim, whereas less dominant context may challenge it. We present CEVA (Complementary Evidence Verification Approach), to our knowledge the first short-video fake news detection framework to explicitly organize frames by their evidential roles before multimodal fusion. Its Dual-Branch Complementary Reorganization (DBCR) module separates explicit-alignment frames from candidate complementary-context frames, and cross-modal fusion with bidirectional contrastive regularization operates on the reorganized evidence. On FakeSV and FakeTT, CEVA achieves the best overall results among the compared methods. Under fixed frame budgets and backbones, DBCR outperforms uniform, random, and similarity-based selection and improves two structurally different detectors. Together, these results establish pre-fusion evidence organization as an effective and transferable component of short-video fake news detection.

Wildfire-SSR: Geolocation-Aware Multimodal Fusion for Structure-Level Wildfire Risk Assessment 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Structure-level wildfire vulnerability assessment is critical for reducing losses in wildland-urban interface communities, yet existing methods often fail to combine parcel-scale ignition pathways with community-scale geographic coverage. This study introduces Wildfire-SSR (Satellite and Street-view for wildfire Risk), a geolocation-aware multimodal framework that integrates macro-scale satellite context with micro-scale street-view evidence. A Vision-Language Model extracts wildfire-relevant building and site attributes from pre-event street-view panoramas and converts them into structured semantic text. These features are spatially aligned with satellite imagery through a decoupled cross-attention fusion decoder, while a frozen satellite-pretrained DINOv3 encoder captures overhead visual context. Alternative geolocation embedding strategies are evaluated to anchor text-derived features within each satellite patch. The framework is tested on approximately 37,000 structures affected by the 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires in Los Angeles, using wildfire risk labels derived from relative post-event damage patterns to better represent inherent structural vulnerability. Rotary Positional Embedding provides the strongest spatial grounding, achieving a building localization F1 score of 0.6735 and a harmonic mean F1 score of 0.2476 for five-class pixel-level risk classification, outperforming unimodal baselines and an unanchored multimodal configuration. The model also shows a conservative bias in higher-risk classes, reducing severe-risk underestimation, and completes end-to-end assessment of the study area in 37 minutes on a single GPU. Wildfire-SSR reframes cross-view fusion as localized semantic alignment and provides a scalable tool for mitigation prioritization, resilience planning, and insurance-oriented wildfire risk assessment.

Beyond Token Stacking: Multi-scale Visual Modulation and Adaptive Fused-feature Calibration for MLLMs 2026-11-16
Show abstract

Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have advanced rapidly and follow two main paradigms: one involves concatenating visual and textual tokens for input into Large Language Model (LLM), while the other injects visual knowledge into Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) of the LLM. However, the former introduces a large number of redundant visual tokens, substantially increasing computational overhead. The latter reduces sequence length but removes images from the input stage entirely, which may hinder the activation of image-related knowledge. Moreover, naively injecting visual knowledge into the FFNs could induce activation distribution shifts due to cross-modal semantic discrepancies. To address these challenges, we develop Beyond Token Stacking (BTS), a novel visual enhancement framework for MLLMs. Specifically, we propose a Multi-scale Visual Modulation (MVM) mechanism that injects multi-scale visual information at the input stage via modulation without increasing the token sequence length. Furthermore, we develop an Adaptive Fused-feature Calibration (AFC) module that performs per-channel affine calibration to decouple visual injection from distribution alignment in the FFN layers, improving multimodal fusion stability while preserving language priors. Experiments show that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance.

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) prediction from histology aims to infer spatially resolved gene expression profiles directly from tissue images. This is a challenging problem that is currently often addressed with complicated model architectures, additional inputs, and complicated training procedures, while remaining limited to fixed, small gene panels. Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding 2026-11-12
📝 Project page: https://unbundling.github.io
Show abstract

How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.

In contrast, large language models (LLMs) provide a natural-language interface for flexible transaction reasoning, yet naively serializing transaction histories into text representations is inefficient and costly. A Time-Frequency-Condition Multimodal Fusion Foundation Model for Universal Rotating-Machinery Fault Diagnosis under Multi-Element Heterogeneous Scenarios 2026-11-11
Show abstract

Intelligent fault diagnosis models are typically developed for a single machine under a specific set of operating conditions, such that each new machine or condition requires retraining from scratch. This one-machine-one-model paradigm scales poorly. Although transfer learning and domain generalization can reduce the labeling burden, they primarily seek domain-invariant commonality, potentially discarding machine-specific characteristics that are critical for diagnosis. This paper proposes TFC-MMFD, a time-frequency-condition multimodal fusion foundation model for rotating-machinery fault diagnosis under multi-element heterogeneous scenarios. Statistical indicators, time-frequency images, vibration sequences, and textual machine and condition descriptions are tokenized into a unified language-like space, allowing inputs of different physical origin to be represented as a single sequence. A LLaMA backbone, together with cross-modal bidirectional attention and a Q-Former, then distills this sequence into fault-mode queries. Diagnosis is performed by matching these queries against natural-language fault-mode anchors rather than fixed class indices, while regression-adversarial condition disentanglement and condition-conditioned masked pre-training improve robustness to changing conditions. Across four progressive tasks on ten public bearing datasets spanning diverse bearing types, speeds, and loads, TFC-MMFD attains 99.40% accuracy and 99.16% macro-F1, maintains over 80% accuracy on most interpolated unseen conditions without adaptation, and improves from 20.26% to 95.63% accuracy on an unseen machine with only five labeled samples per class. It outperforms the strongest of eight domain-generalization baselines by 10.57 percentage points and compares favorably with a recent bearing foundation model while using less than half of its fine-tuning data.

Modality vs. Morphology: A Framework for Time Series Classification for Biological Signals 2026-11-10
Show abstract

Techniques for classifying biological signals are largely developed within specific modalities rather than the underlying signal structure (i.e., morphology), leading to disjointed methodologies and limited guidance across diverse signals. This work introduces a novel morphology--modality framework that connects waveform structure to a principled design of preprocessing, feature extraction, and modeling techniques for biological time-series classification. We analyze evidence across biological signals including electroencephalography, electromyography, electrocardiography, photoplethysmography, and ocular modalities including electrooculography, pupillometry, and eye-tracking, and organize according to their primary morphologies: spikes, bursts, oscillations, slow drift, and hierarchical rhythms. Across these categories, we examine how morphology influences the analytical pipeline from preprocessing to modeling and evaluation. Localized architectures favor spikes and bursts, whereas frequency and temporal models capture oscillations, and hybrid approaches integrate local and global dependencies in hierarchical signals. The associations are not absolute as biological signals often contain multiple morphologies where relevance is task dependent. The proposed framework positions morphology as an inductive biases are useful for modeling. We further identify challenges in cross-subject generalization, morphology-preserving data augmentation, multimodal fusion, and evaluation practices. By linking modality-specific constraints with waveform structure, this review provides guidance for selecting and evaluating pipelines across biological signals.
