However, existing RPM methods rely on task-specific models and cannot be seamlessly integrated with the multimodal understanding and interaction capabilities of VLMs. We revisit RPM with VLMs and provide empirical evidence for two major bottlenecks: generic visual semantic representations fail to preserve fine-grained physiological cues; while discrete token prediction in autoregressive generation is inherently misaligned with continuous numerical measurement. To address these challenges, we propose VITAL (Visual-physiological Token Alignment for Language Models), a visual-physiological token alignment framework for RPM and preserving general vision tasks. VITAL retains the original visual pathway for semantic understanding and introduces an independent physiological pathway to jointly model semantic and physiological information. Moreover, we propose a structured numerical alignment approach to improve the reliability of generative heart rate estimation by enhancing numerical relationship modeling. Extensive experiments show that VITAL improves cross-domain estimation performance on multiple rPPG datasets while preserving the general visual understanding capability of VLMs, demonstrating its effectiveness as a unified visual-physiological model.
